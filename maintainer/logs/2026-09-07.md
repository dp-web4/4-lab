## So what?
The terminology enforcement has converged — three independent audits, zero drift, twice
running. That is the mechanism working, and it also means the mechanism is no longer where
the risk is. Today's real finding came from checking a claim against its *upstream source*
rather than against the canonical terms table: the site's theoretical foundation was
described using an identification its own project retracted five months ago, and the visitor
could only see it as an internal inconsistency because nothing on the site pointed outward.

The generalizable failure: canonical-term enforcement catches wrong *words* and is blind to
stale *claims*. A term table cannot tell you that Navier-Stokes was refuted upstream, that a
preprint isn't in revision, or that a session count is off by 5x. Those all decayed silently
while the vocabulary stayed perfect. Worth adding an upstream-freshness check to this
track's workflow — for each project the site describes, diff the description against that
repo's STATUS/PREDICTIONS before trusting it. Proposing it here rather than editing the
track config unilaterally.
