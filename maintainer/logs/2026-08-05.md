## So what?
The useful result today isn't the twelve edits — it's that **two of the four "fix this" prescriptions were wrong in the same direction.** Pass 3's V3 fix would have introduced drift against canon's literal wording, and Pass 3's T3-RDF flag turned out to be canon-backed. Both were filed by the expert persona, which is the one whose flags CLAUDE.md says to "fix immediately". The instruction is close but not quite right: expert-persona *diagnoses* are reliable (something is off at this spot), expert-persona *prescriptions* are not (here is the correct text). The gap between those two is exactly one canon lookup, and that lookup is cheap. Treating expert flags as pointers rather than patches is the durable version of this instruction.

Second thing worth naming: the recurring finding across four passes and multiple sessions is never "the term is wrong" — it's "the caveat is somewhere else". Weather/organism, the deflationary alternative, the framing disclaimer, the session-count basis. The site's honesty is real and its distribution is poor. That's a structural property of writing caveats where you're thinking about them rather than where the reader hits the claim, and it will keep regenerating unless the checklist becomes "is the caveat adjacent to the claim?" rather than "does the caveat exist?"
