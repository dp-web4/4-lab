## So What?

The expansion-drift enforcement job is **done** — three clean audits running. The failure
mode has moved: definitions and numbers now drift while expansions hold, because
expansions are string-matchable and definitions are not. Two of this session's four Tier 1
fixes were pages disagreeing with each other rather than with canon, and the single
most-found item (Synchronism) was a correction that had been applied to one page and not
propagated — meaning the site's own dated-correction convention is the thing that failed,
twice over, before I caught it.

The sharper result is that **the visitor loop's most confident flags were its least
reliable ones for the second session running.** The expert-persona MRH prescription would
have introduced drift into the glossary row — the most-copied text on the site — and the
"unexpanded acronym" flags pointed at terms that were absent from the page entirely. A
cross-page consistency checker can localise a disagreement but never resolve it, and it is
blind to anything wrong on every page at once. Both today's `/context` §26 discoveries that
no persona found (the stale basis-split claim, the dead `T246` citation) were only visible
by reading the source against `/fleet`, not by comparing rendered pages.

That's the standing lesson to carry forward: **this track's value is verification, not
application.** A maintainer that applies the visitor log as a work queue is a drift
*vector*, not a defense.
