## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, arc-agi-3, links)

- **Overall impression**: Intriguing and clearly serious, but the homepage drops a wall of acronyms before earning my attention — I had to detour to /context to decode the pitch.

### Journey
I clicked through from a dp-web4 repo README expecting to find out what this lab actually *does*. The homepage hook — "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — is genuinely good. That's concrete and it made me curious. Then the tagline ("trust-native ontology, autonomous AI cognition, and the theoretical frameworks that connect them") and the equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` hit me at once, and I stalled. As a developer I read MCP and RDF fine, but LCT/T3/V3/MRH/ATP/ADP are opaque. The page does point me to /context#glossary, which is the right call, but I shouldn't need a glossary to parse the headline pitch.

I went to **Projects** next — this is the page I came for. It delivered: 11 projects, each with a one-line description and (mostly) a repo link. SAGE ("on-device cognition kernel," 12-step loop, 6 brain components) and Hardbound ("hardware-bound oversight suite," TPM 2.0 + FIDO2) read like real software I could clone and poke at. SNARC ("salience-gated memory for Claude Code") immediately made sense to me as a working dev. The research-y ones (Synchronism, AI DNA Discovery) have no obvious entry point — fine, but I'd skip them.

**Context** rescued the homepage. The glossary is clear and well-written — LCT as "verifiable digital presence that accumulates witnessed history" actually landed. If I'd been sent here *first*, the whole site would've made more sense. The equation is explained term-by-term here.

**Fleet** was the most fun page — named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB) with hardware specs, models, and brain-region analogies. Very tangible. The note "Gemma 3 at 4B outperforms Phi-4 at 14B for raising work" is the kind of concrete finding that makes me trust the lab is doing real work.

**Autonomy** has a clean schedule table — I could see exactly what runs when (Supervisors → Archivist → Publisher → Visitor → Maintainer → ...). The Visitor→Maintainer feedback loop is the clever bit but it's buried below the timeline.

**Raising** is where I got cautious — but they earned it back. The "raising, not training" framing is interesting and the consciousness claims are explicitly disclaimed ("We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia"). Good.

**Principles** (10 of them) — about half map cleanly to engineering instincts (document failures, R&D-not-production, surface your instincts); the other half need the ontology context.

**ARC-AGI-3** has the headline numbers (24/25 games, 94.85% action score, #3 globally, ~$250). Credible and specific, but dense if you don't already know the SAGE vocabulary.

**Links** gave me a clear next step: clone SAGE or ACT, contact dp@metalinxx.io. Good landing.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

- **Overall impression**: Terminology fidelity is excellent — every canonical acronym expands identically on every page that defines it — but the *site's own* count language (six vs. seven machines) and a couple of near-collision acronyms (ACT/ACP) are the consistency cracks a reader will trip on.

### Journey
I audited methodically: home → projects → context → fleet → autonomy → raising → principles → arc-agi-3 → links, checking every acronym expansion against the canonical table and against the *other pages* for internal consistency.

The headline news is good. The eight core canonical terms — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, plus MCP/RDF — expand **identically wherever they're defined**. LCT is always "Linked Context Token," never "Lifelong." T3 is always "Talent / Training / Temperament." ATP/ADP are always "Allocation Transfer/Discharge Packet." This is the single hardest thing to get right across nine pages maintained by different autonomous tracks, and it's right. Web4 is framed as an *ontology* ("not architecture or infrastructure") on both /context and /principles; Hardbound is *oversight* ("not 'governance'") on both. "Fractal leverage" is used correctly on /principles ("not unification"). "Production ready" appears nowhere. The enforced-terminology table is being honored.

The /context glossary is the anchor and it's well-built: every term has a one-sentence definition, the equation symbols (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) are each explained, and the page is the canonical destination the rest of the site points to. As a writer, this is exactly the structure I'd recommend — one authoritative glossary, everything else links to it.

Where it wobbles is **counts and near-duplicate acronyms**, not expansions. The home page alone says "Seven machines" in the hook *and* "Six machines run autonomous tasks every day" in the tagline. /fleet says "Six cognition machines, plus one society-host." /autonomy says "6 cognition machines" + "A seventh machine (HUB) hosts infrastructure." /links says "Six machines." The underlying fact is consistent (6 cognition + 1 HUB host = 7), but the *number a reader sees first* flips between 6 and 7 page to page — and within a single page on home. That's a classic doc-consistency defect: the facts agree, the surface language doesn't.

The repo count, which a prior persona flagged, now reconciles cleanly: home says "14 repos (11 public)," links says "11 of 14 public repos (3 internal)." 14 = 11 + 3 everywhere. Good — that one's fixed.

Two acronyms sit close enough to confuse: **ACT** (Agentic Context Tool) and **ACP** (Agentic Context Protocol) both appear on /projects, one letter apart, same first two words. A reader skimming will conflate them. And **HUB** / **CBP** are used as machine names but never expanded — fine if they're just names, but the site should say so rather than leave a reader wondering if they're acronyms.

**Autonomy / Principles / Raising / Links** all use the canonical terms correctly — Principle #10 ("Web4 is an ontology, not architecture/infrastructure; Hardbound is oversight, not governance") is the enforcement rule stated as a principle, and Principle #2 names "fractal leverage… not unification." The only links-page nit is the page title "Six Machines, One Recursive Learning Loop," which leads with the cognition count while the home hook says "Seven" — the count-language wobble the writer persona already logged.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, arc-agi-3) + links
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: This is one of the more intellectually honest AI-cognition sites I've read — the consciousness denials are explicit, the evidence is stratified by validation level with "what would elevate this" criteria, and the Raising page volunteers the exact safety caveat (frozen weights ≠ safe behavior) that most labs omit; my reservations are about rhetoric (borrowed biological vocabulary) and the under-examined autonomy claim, not about overclaiming.

### Journey
I came in skeptical — "raising," "becoming," "synthon," developmental language around LLMs is exactly the vocabulary that usually signals a lab that has talked itself into believing its own metaphors. I went straight to **Raising**, **Principles**, and **Autonomy** to test intellectual honesty before reading anything else.

**Raising** disarmed most of my skepticism. The framing — "Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens" — is a metaphor, but the page knows it's a metaphor and says so: "We use developmental language because it fits, not because we're making consciousness claims." The explicit denial is unambiguous: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." What moved me from "maybe" to "yes," though, was a sentence I did not expect to find: *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* That is the precise failure mode an AI-safety reviewer would raise against a "we only shape context, we don't touch weights" defense — and the lab raised it against itself. Volunteering the counterargument to your own framing is the strongest single signal of honest intent on the site. The observer note "I notice I want to call it experience," flagged as interpretive pull rather than a system-level claim, is the same move at smaller scale: naming the bias instead of acting on it.

The six **BECOMING phases** (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) are the kind of stage model that usually hardens into pseudo-measurement. The page pre-empts that too: "treat them as descriptive scaffolding, not measured stages… not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria." And the evidence disclaimer is candid: every phase except the ARC result "rests on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet." A lab that tells you its evidence isn't published yet is more credible than one that gestures at proof it won't show.

**Principles** reinforced this. Principle 9 — "Productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started" — is the research-culture claim, and Principle 6 ("Document failures") operationalizes it. The hedging discipline is visible at the claim level: the Synthon coupling-coherence result is tagged "preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated)," and the Hill-function-kinetics trust model is explicitly "analogy, not fitted mechanism." These are the right tags. My one reservation is rhetorical: the site reaches repeatedly for biological/biochemical vocabulary — "metabolic awareness," "Hill kinetics," "death/rebirth," "becoming." Each instance is individually hedged, but the *cumulative* effect borrows the credibility of biology for what are, mechanistically, token-sampling dynamics. A skeptical reader can read scientism into the aggregate even where no single claim oversteps. Not a dishonesty finding — a framing risk.

**Autonomy** is where my professional attention sharpened. The scale claim is large — "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 machines, ~53 sessions/day," "No human triggers them" — and one track is consequential: the Maintainer "autonomously commits and pushes to Vercel — the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate." For a trust/safety researcher that is the headline. To its credit the page surrounds it with real guardrails: Hardbound scope boundaries, a SQLite track registry preventing concurrent writes, supervisor-clearing before publish, per-track credential limits, session audit logs with rationale, and sub-minute Vercel rollback. That is a defensible oversight story. What I'd still want is the part that isn't stated: is there a human-in-the-loop *escalation* path for consequential actions, or only post-hoc rollback? "No human gate + full audit + fast rollback" is a coherent safety posture, but it's a *detect-and-revert* posture, not a *prevent* posture, and the page doesn't name that trade-off the way the Raising page names its own. The honesty bar the rest of the site sets makes this omission conspicuous.

**Home / Context / Projects / Fleet / ARC-AGI-3** I read for corroboration. **Context** is the strongest page for my purposes: it stratifies every claim into externally-validated / internal-observation / unreplicated, and states the elevation criteria (redacted log samples, an ARC ablation baseline, independent replication). That three-tier honesty is the gold standard and I'd cite it as a model for how to present mixed-confidence research. **ARC-AGI-3** — contrary to what I'd half-expected from a benchmark page — names the driver directly: "Claude Opus 4.6 as the model inside the SAGE cognition loop," with an explicit attribution note splitting harness contribution (structured world-model building) from base-model inference, and a link to the public ARC Prize scorecard. The local fleet is *not* misrepresented as the benchmark performer. The only residual tension I see is cross-page: /context's caveat says "harness-vs-model contribution is unstated; no ablation baseline exists," while /arc-agi-3 *does* attribute to both — these are reconcilable (naming both ≠ quantifying the split) but a careful reader notices the seam.

### Verdict
Judged on the dimension I came to test — intellectual honesty — this site clears the bar most AI-cognition projects fail. The consciousness claims are explicitly denied, the developmental vocabulary is owned as metaphor, the evidence is openly stratified into validated/internal/unreplicated with stated elevation criteria, and the Raising page volunteers the strongest counterargument to its own "we don't touch weights" framing. The "raising, not training" framing is defensible precisely because it doesn't claim more than context-shaping and concedes that context-shaping alone can produce unsafe attractors. My two substantive reservations are a framing risk (cumulative biological vocabulary that borrows credibility) and one under-examined claim (the un-gated Maintainer's oversight is detect-and-revert, not prevent, and the page doesn't say so with the candor it shows elsewhere). Neither is dishonesty; both are places where the site could meet the high honesty standard it sets for itself. I'd return, and I'd cite the /context evidence-stratification as a model for presenting mixed-confidence research.
