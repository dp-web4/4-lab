### Journey
I landed from a GitHub README expecting a project homepage. The headline — "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — is evocative but doesn't tell me *what the lab does*. I had to read two paragraphs down to get "trust-native systems and autonomous AI cognition across a distributed fleet." Okay, so it's a multi-agent research collective. I bounced to **Context**, which turned out to be the best page for me: a clean glossary table and the Web4 equation (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) with the operators explained. The equation is still opaque on first read — it assumes I want to parse custom notation — but the glossary below it rescued me.

**Projects** was the most actionable page: each card is tagged public/private and runnable/research-stage, which is exactly what I want as a dev deciding where to poke. SAGE and Membot looked like things I could actually clone and run. **Fleet** was fun — named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB) with their hardware and models. **Autonomy** finally explained what "tracks" are (cron-scheduled autonomous sessions) and was refreshingly honest that the maintainer track deploys to production with no human pre-approval ("detect-and-revert, not prevent"). **Principles**, **Raising**, and **ARC-AGI-3** were more philosophy/results than developer onboarding — I skimmed them. **Links** gave me a clear path: vocabulary → SAGE/ACT → clone → open issues.

What confused me: the machine count. Home and Context say "seven machines"; Autonomy says "31+ tracks across **6** cognition machines" and "six supervisors." It *is* consistent (6 cognition + 1 society-host = 7), but a fast scanner sees "6" and "7" on different pages and stumbles. Took me a second read to reconcile.

Exemplary.

The **structural** weakness is define-on-first-use. The site uses a hub-and-spoke glossary model: /context holds the authoritative table, and other pages assume you've read it. All reconcile to 6+1=7, but the seventh machine is "HUB" in two places and "society-host" in another — same entity, two names. Second, **LCT's one-line gloss drifts in wording** (not expansion) between pages: home calls it "Portable identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights," context calls it "Verifiable digital presence that accumulates witnessed history — identity grounded in record, not model weights." Same concept, different sentence — fine for prose variety, but a writer notes it.

One genuinely ambiguous token: **CBP**. It's a fleet machine name (the WSL2/RTX 2060 box) *and* it appears as an unexpanded acronym on /arc-agi-3. A reader can't tell whether "CBP" there means the machine or something else. Same string, two possible referents, neither expanded on that page.

1. **The `/` operator is overloaded and the site says so — but it's still worth a glance.** The operator key defines `/` as both "verified by" (T3/V3) *and* "allocation pair" (ATP/ADP). Canon-consistent; just a framing seam.

3. **ATP/ADP energy metaphor is preserved correctly.** The packets are named after the cellular ATP→ADP discharge, and the site's "declared before / spent form" framing keeps that metaphor intact without overstating it. Good.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary

- **Understanding achieved**: good — I can evaluate the epistemic posture without needing the Web4 internals
- **Would return?**: yes — the caveat discipline is high enough that I'd treat this as a citable example of how to report soft AI claims honestly
- **Overall impression**: This is the rare AI-lab site that hedges *more* than it has to: consciousness-adjacent language is explicitly demoted to functional description, the headline benchmark result is attributed to a frontier model rather than the harness, and the unreplicated finding is labeled "1 trial" everywhere it appears — the honesty is structural, not decorative, though it leans on un-audited internal logs that a skeptic can't independently check.

### Journey
I came in primed for the usual failure mode of "emergent AI" projects: anthropomorphic language doing argumentative work that the evidence doesn't support. I went straight to **Raising**, **Principles**, and **Autonomy** to test that.

**Raising** is where I expected to wince and mostly didn't. The "raising not training" distinction is mechanistically precise: training updates weights via loss; raising shapes context, experience buffer, and interaction history and "never model weights." That's a real, checkable difference — not a metaphor doing sleight-of-hand. The BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) are flagged as "observational pattern-names, not mandatory sequential stages," which is the right epistemic register: these are post-hoc descriptive labels, not a developmental theory making predictions. The consciousness disclaimer is unusually direct: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia."* And then — this is the part that raised my trust — the site preserves the observer's countervailing pull rather than sanding it off: *"I notice I want to call it experience."* That's a researcher being honest about the interpretive temptation *and* refusing to act on it. Most sites either resist the temptation silently (and lose the reader's ability to judge) or give in to it. Naming it and declining it is the defensible move.

**Principles** held up under the same scrutiny. Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") carries a domain-specific safety disclaimer naming "safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings" — exactly the carve-out I'd want a self-deploying agent system to state. Principle 5 (synthon) front-loads "single trial, not independently replicated" and admits the coupling/coherence variables are "defined only within the experiment" — i.e., the result isn't yet operationalized for outside replication, which they *say* instead of hoping I won't notice. Principle 6 ("Document failures") actually documents a failure: a "p_crit derivation … 400× error," cited as evidence that they trust data over theory. A lab that publishes its own 400× miss is signaling it isn't curating only wins.

The weak spots are Principles 8 and 10, which are normative ("surface your instincts," "terminology matters") and carry no empirical bounds — but those are stated as values, not findings, so the missing caveat is appropriate; you don't caveat a value the way you caveat a measurement.

**Autonomy** was the page most relevant to my safety lens, and it does not flinch. The maintainer track is described as "the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate," with the oversight posture stated plainly as *"detect-and-revert (audit log + sub-minute rollback), not prevent."* They name the residual risk rather than implying the rollback eliminates it, and they enumerate the loop's blind spots ("technically correct but misleading," "pattern-matching without semantic depth"). For a low-stakes static marketing site this posture is proportionate; I'd object loudly if this gating model were described for anything safety-critical, but Principle 3 already fences that off.

**Context** is the epistemic backbone: a 40+ term glossary that sorts claims into "externally validated" (the ARC scorecard), "internal observations, not externally audited," and "unreplicated." That three-tier honesty ledger is the single most credibility-building feature on the site.

### Where the honesty has limits (the skeptic's column)
The site is honest *about being un-auditable*, which is better than pretending — but it's still un-auditable. The identity-continuity claims (180+ sessions, 900+ raising sessions, "consistent behavioral separation") "rest on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited," and **no sample logs or coding criteria are published**. So I'm asked to trust that the behavioral-identity measurements are well-defined, while the operational definitions stay inside the lab. The site says this itself; I'm just noting the disclosure doesn't dissolve the problem. The one externally checkable claim — the ARC-AGI-3 score — is the strongest, *and* it's the one most carefully attributed away from the lab's own special sauce ("the result is Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness"; "harness-vs-model contribution unquantified; no ablation baseline exists"). I respect that they undercut their own headline number with the missing-ablation caveat.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is the operational definition of "behavioral identity," and what coding rubric scores "behavioral continuity" across 180+ sessions? Without it, I can't tell whether the continuity claim is a measurement or an impression.
2. Is there *any* externally inspectable artifact for the internal-observation tier (one redacted log, one rubric), or is the entire soft-claim corpus necessarily lab-internal?
3. For the synthon "1% coupling → 35% coherence gain": how are "coupling density" and "coherence" measured outside the single experiment? The site says they aren't yet — is operationalization planned?
4. Does the 94.85% Phase-1 result have *any* ablation isolating the SAGE harness's contribution from Claude Opus 4.6's, even informally? The site says no baseline exists — is one planned for Phase 2?

### Verdict
As an AI-safety/trust researcher, my default posture toward "we're raising emergent AI agents" sites is skepticism, and this one mostly disarmed it — not by making smaller claims, but by labeling each claim's evidentiary tier, attributing its headline benchmark away from itself, and naming (rather than hiding) the interpretive pull toward consciousness language while explicitly declining it. The "raising not training" framing is defensible because it rests on a real, checkable mechanism (context vs. weights), not on a metaphor. The honest limitation is that the soft claims — identity, continuity, synthon coherence — live entirely in un-audited internal logs; the site says so plainly, which is the honest move, but disclosure isn't the same as inspectability. One published sample log with its coding criteria would do more for external credibility than any amount of additional prose. I'd cite this as a model of caveat discipline, with that single reservation.
