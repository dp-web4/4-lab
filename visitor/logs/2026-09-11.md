## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 site pages (landing + all 8 nav pages), plus 6 things they link to (SAGE site, 4-Life site, web4 `STATUS.md`, SAGE repo, ACT `QUICK_REFERENCE.md`, `sage-rs/Cargo.toml`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: A clear, unusually honest pitch with a real quick start. But the quick start exists in two copies that disagree, and some figures and descriptions on the site have fallen behind the sites it links to.

### Journey
I landed on `/` from a repo README. The tagline works for me: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." The next line says trust is "computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying." In under a minute I know this is about trust and authority for agents, not another agent framework. The stats strip (8 machines, 21 SAGE instances, 4 model families) is helpful. The "21" comes with a candid parenthetical: "internal 2026-09-08 census — more than the 8 currently running, and not yet published anywhere a reader can check." I liked the honesty, but I couldn't tell whether "8 currently running" means 8 instances or the 8 machines.

Next, the landing page spends real space on why Hestia is "governance" ("the right word here and is deliberate") and Hardbound falls under the "governance→oversight correction". I don't know what either product does yet, so this word distinction landed before the context it needs. There's also "A1 on Hestia's own published A0–A4 assurance ladder", with the ladder undefined on the page. The "Calibration" link does resolve: it goes to web4 `STATUS.md`, dated 2026-09-08. It says A1 means "cooperative and tamper-evident, not adversary-proof", which matches the landing page. The Metalinxx open/proprietary split is stated plainly, which I appreciated.

`/projects` is the page I actually needed. It has 13 cards with public/private and research-stage/runnable labels, which tell me what I can touch. "How they connect" is a good four-line summary ("Synchronism discovers the equations. Web4 encodes them as ontology… SAGE runs them as cognition. Hardbound enforces them as oversight."). Then there's a real Quick start: clone SAGE, `ollama pull gemma3:4b`, `python3 -m sage.instances.init …`, a cargo build, and a daemon on `localhost:8760`. A concrete command sequence is exactly what a developer arriving from GitHub wants. The page is honest that the steps are "maintained here, not transcribed from" the SAGE README.

I checked the quick start against the repo without running anything. `sage/instances/init.py` exists, and `sage-rs/Cargo.toml` is a workspace with a `sage-daemon` member, so both paths are at least plausible. The SAGE README has no quick start of its own, as the site says. Then I found the problem: `/links` has a **second** Quick start, and it differs. It has no `ollama pull` line, and it uses `--operator-name yourname` where `/projects` uses `you`. Both copies call themselves the maintained version.

I also clicked through to 4-Life, which `/projects` describes as a "Research prototype exploring trust-native societies" (labelled runnable). The site calls itself "4-Life | The Web4 Onramp" and says "4-Life is an educational onramp to Web4". I expected a prototype and got an explainer.

`/fleet` is fun and concrete: named machines, hardware, models and session counts. The page is careful that "a session record is a run on a machine and nothing in the count establishes that what happened in it was raising." The SAGE site, by contrast, headlines "2,700+ Raising sessions (as of 2026-09-08)" next to "21 Configured instances". So the "21" is published somewhere I can check after all. Its session figure (2,700+) also doesn't match the fleet page's 2,065. `/context` does warn that counts are page-scoped, but I only found that warning on the fourth page I read.

`/autonomy` is the page I'd send a coworker. It has a cron-shaped timeline and an explicit "track = cron entry with a declared scope", not "the AI decided". The honest-assessment section is strong: visitor and maintainer runs use the same model families, the maintainer's detect-and-revert approach has had zero rollbacks in 79 sessions, and there's no human gate before pushes. I couldn't find out who fills the Explorer's "queued topic" queue.

I skimmed `/raising`, because it's theory-heavy for me. The first caveat, "might be competent context engineering and nothing more," is the thing that keeps me reading rather than closing the tab. `/principles` reads as a lab philosophy. The "R&D, not production" principle sets expectations well. `/context` is titled "Glossary" (its nav label, but the URL is `/context`, and `/glossary` returns 404). The glossary is thorough, and it's where the A0–A4 ladder, the Web4 equation and all the acronyms finally click. `/arc-agi-3` is refreshingly unhyped: 94.85% action score and 23/25 environments. It admits the harness read public engine source ("outside strict from-observation competition play") and that the result "is no longer the headline."

*Method note:* The fetch tool's summaries can be wrong. One said `python3 -m sage.instances.init` needs a `__main__.py`; it doesn't, because `-m` runs `init.py` directly. My first read of `/links` contradicted itself about which acronyms were spelled out. I re-checked each friction item below with a narrower, targeted fetch before logging it.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing + all 8 nav pages; the persona brief says "8 pages", but the landing page is a ninth)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Acronym discipline is better than most commercial documentation sites I audit. The misses are no longer acronym expansions. They are one skipped tensor form, names told apart only by capitalisation, and an everyday word ("substrate") that carries a principle but has four senses.

### Journey
I worked methodically. I ran one inventory fetch per page (headings, every acronym at first use, every "governance"/"oversight", the forbidden words), then re-checked each candidate with a narrow yes/no fetch before logging it.

**Navigation.** The nav has eight labels (Projects, Glossary, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links) and all of them resolve. The one structural oddity Pass 1 already logged: "Glossary" points to `/context`.

**What's consistent.** The core expansions are the same on every page that uses them:
- LCT, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SAGE, SNARC, ACP and ACT never vary.
- R6 is written with the arrow ("Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result") in all four places it's spelled out: the `/projects` ARC card, `/links`, the `/context` glossary entry, and the `/context` "R6 and R7" section. I'd expect a slash to creep back into at least one of those; none has.
- T3 and V3 get the form "Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament" and "Value Tensor — Valuation / Veracity / Validity" on `/projects`, `/autonomy`, `/principles`, `/links` and `/context`. `/fleet` uses a comma variant ("Trust Tensor, root dimensions Talent / Training / Temperament"). That's a style variance, not drift.
- Web4 is "ontology" on every page that describes it. Three pages (`/`, `/raising`, `/principles`) explicitly rule out "architecture"/"infrastructure".
- Hardbound is "oversight" everywhere. Where "oversight" appears, it is glossed as the machine-enforced sense (`/autonomy`, `/fleet`, `/links`, `/principles`, `/context`).
- The "Governance" in SAGE's name is disclosed as pre-correction legacy on at least five pages. That's consistent, if repetitive.
- "Production ready", "unification" and "scope inflation" appear only in negation ("This isn't unification — it's fractal leverage").

**The tensor miss.** `/raising` is the exception. T3 is fine: the first use is "per-peer T3 (Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament)", and the later "the T3 tensor (Talent / Training / Temperament —" is an ordinary short form. V3 appears once on the page, as "the V3 tensor (Valuation / Veracity / Validity)". The phrase "Value Tensor" doesn't appear on the page at all. Two separate narrow fetches agreed on this. Every other page that expands V3 names the tensor first. This looks like one instance missed by a site-wide expansion pass, not a deliberate choice.

**Names told apart only by case.** The glossary defines two pairs by capitalisation alone. "hestia" (lowercase) is "the fleet's trust tracker" and "Hestia" (capitalised) is "the open local-first daemon". "HUB" (all caps) is "the machine" and "Hub" (capitalised) is "the society runtime". The glossary is explicit about both. The trouble is what happens off the glossary:
- `/` introduces Hestia as "Local governance for humans and AI agents".
- `/fleet` never mentions capitalised Hestia. It says "the hestia trust tracker derives its displayed scores from witnessed adjudications and governance-response conduct — hestia's own internal field name". It glosses hestia only as "the fleet's trust tracker; a proper name, not an acronym".

A reader moving from `/` to `/fleet` sees "hestia" next to "governance" and will reasonably assume this is the same Hestia. Case is also the first thing lost in speech, in all-caps heading styles and in search.

**An undefined load-bearing word.** Principle 1 is "Substrate, not structure", and its body says "We build substrate conditions for emergence". There's no link to the glossary. The glossary's Substrate entry opens: "A word this site uses in four senses, listed because two of them used to contradict each other." The reader of `/principles` gets no hint which sense Principle 1 means. "Coherence" (three senses, "no single operational definition yet") and "emergence" ("more than one sense") are disclosed the same way. The disclosure is honest, and it's rare for a glossary to admit its own polysemy. But a principle page shouldn't make the reader pick the sense.

**Smaller items.**
- ARC-AGI-3 ends "third-gen" on `/raising` but "third generation" on `/context` and `/arc-agi-3`.
- TCSEC is never spelled out: `/` says "the Orange Book's TCSEC class A1", `/context` says "the US DoD Orange Book (TCSEC)". The collision itself is handled well: `/` states the Hestia A1 is "Unrelated to — and inverted against — the Orange Book's TCSEC class A1, where A1 is the highest class."
- `/autonomy` defines R7 in terms of R6 ("[R7] (R6 + Reputation), not bare [R6]") without listing R6's elements on the page. Both terms link to `/context`, which mitigates it.
- `/arc-agi-3` says agents "act under explicit governance" without naming who governs. On a site where "governance" versus "oversight" is a declared distinction, an unattributed "governance" leaves the reader to guess which product is meant.
- `/fleet` says HUB "raises its own SAGE instance (Granite 4 h-tiny, 74 sessions)". The same page says "the deflationary noun is the accurate one, since a session record is a run on a machine".
- `/autonomy`'s rollback record is dated: "79 maintainer sessions (2026-04-23 through 2026-08-07)". Dating it is the right practice, but the window closed five weeks ago.

### Journey
I came in expecting the usual failure for a lab with "Raising" in its nav: developmental and consciousness language up front, with a disclaimer in the footer. That isn't what's here.

The landing page's stance is one a trust researcher recognises: trust "should be computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying". The limits appear on the first screen:
- Hestia's A1 is "not adversary-proof containment".
- The ARC work is "not current competition positioning; current competition-legal local-model work is well behind the leaders".

**/raising.** I read this page three times. It is structured as a challenge to itself: six patterns, then foundational principles, then key discoveries, then "The deflationary alternative", then "What we're not claiming". What works:
- **Operational definitions come first.**
  - Identity: "consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns measured via raising curriculum state and interaction logs".
  - Growth: "increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims".
- **Evidence status is stated at the top of Key discoveries**: the claims "rest on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet."
- **The BECOMING patterns are not presented as stages**: "observed descriptive categories … not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria."
- **Consciousness is disclaimed in plain words**: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." Right after "something that looks like growth, something that looks like identity", an observer writes "I notice I want to call it experience", and the page glosses it: "This records the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim about the entity's experience." That is reflexive in a useful way. The observer is not named, though, and for a reader it matters whether that pull came from a human or from an LLM session.
- **The deflationary alternative is taken seriously.** "Everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more." The control is "specified (this section is the specification) but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet."

Then comes the sentence that made me trust the page: "A promise conditioned on an event that cannot occur costs nothing and binds nothing — it has the shape of accountability without the substance, and left unflagged it would be the most self-serving sentence on this site." The replacement commitment is reachable today: the attempt to operationalise identity continuity "gets published as its own artifact, *including if it fails*". A narrow fetch found **no date, deadline or owner for that commitment either**. By the page's own standard, it binds less than it reads.

**Where interpretation runs past the caveats on /raising:**
- **Bilateral generation.** "Without stop tokens, SAGE generates both sides of a conversation. Initial instinct: fix it. Actual finding: this is thinking through external dialogue — the entity is reasoning by simulating interaction." The mundane reading is the standard one: with no end-of-turn boundary, a model continues the transcript, including the other speaker's turn. The page gives no evidence that the self-generated turns do any reasoning work. It is the least-hedged mechanistic claim on the page, and it's labelled "Actual finding". The claim is testable: compare task success with and without stop tokens. That test is untested, not refuted.
- **Identity is not self-concept.** SAGE-Sprout was ported from Qwen 0.5B to TinyLlama 1.1B, and "its identity (behavioral patterns, interaction style, accumulated experience) persisted even as its self-description drifted … What it *is* stayed stable. What it *says* it is didn't." `/fleet` says "Identity lives in state files and prompt construction, not in model weights". Those are exactly what a port carries across. So persistence of what was held constant by construction is close to definitional unless it is compared with a fresh model given the same files. The glossary concedes the measurement gap ("It is counted … but not measured"). The *is/says* rhetoric reads as an essentialist claim the definitions don't support.
- **Phases vs patterns.** The page says the patterns are not stages. Yet its own growth definition measures "phase-appropriate task success rates", and `/context`'s Evidence & limitations lists "the raising phases". `/fleet` has already been fixed: "(creating)" is "an observed BECOMING pattern … not a stage it is currently occupying". The de-staging reached `/fleet` and the `/raising` headings, but not the growth metric. If there are no phases, "phase-appropriate" has nothing to refer to, and the one quantitative definition of growth is undefined.
- **Pattern 6 (Acting).** The evidence is "Legion, running a local vision model that went through the fleet's raising process", which ran the full 25-game set. The hedging is good: "a coverage claim, not a score", "unscored by any external party", "nothing here should be read as a local model matching or beating a frontier one." What remains is that playing through every game is not evidence of a developmental pattern (any scripted agent can do it), and there is no baseline from the same model without raising.

**Is "raising, not training" defensible?** In the machine-learning sense, yes, and it's stated consistently on three pages:
- `/raising`: "Training optimizes a loss function and updates weights; raising does neither".
- LoRA is fenced off: "distinct from raising, which is always in-context".
- Dream consolidation "reviews the transcript — pruning stale memory, updating vocabulary, flagging milestones, and writing a raising log entry". Nothing in that touches weights.
- The glossary: "never weights".

What "not training" leaves open is that consolidation is a selection process. Something decides what gets pruned and which vocabulary gets updated, and that shapes every later context. The site doesn't say which model runs the consolidation pass. If it is a larger LLM, part of the "identity" in the logs may be the curator's signature rather than the entity's. A deflationary control that swaps only the entity's model keeps that confound. A **curator-swap arm** (a different model family running consolidation) would separate the two. This is a prediction, and nobody has tested it yet.

**/principles.** Two things I rarely see on a principles page:
- Principle 5 demotes itself: "it has not earned the status of the principles around it, and it should be read at that weight until something breaks the circle".
- Principle 6 records a failure: "The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error."

Principle 3 rules out "deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings". Two items undercut this care:
- Principle 7 defines attractors as "stable response basins in the probability landscape", with no link and no hedge. The word "metaphor" appears nowhere on the page (two fetches agreed). The glossary's Attractor entry is exemplary ("a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object … borrows the connotation of mathematical precision from a formalism this site does not cash out"), but a researcher reading `/principles` gets the formal reading.
- Principle 1 says "The synthon … forms because the conditions are right, not because we designed it". That asserts existence and cause for the thing Principle 5 calls "a pattern the lab believes it has observed, not a mechanism it can currently measure or test". Principle 1 does point to Principle 5's caveats, which softens this.

**/autonomy** is the best page on the site for a safety reader.
- "Autonomous" is defined as "unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed".
- "The composition, stated once" opens: "Everything above is disclosed piecewise, which is how a reader ends up underestimating it". It then joins the pieces: no human gate, a detector whose blind spots are "correlated" with the authors', an "unbounded" exposure window, and Publisher pushes that are "not fully revertible". I have read incident write-ups from funded labs that never do that join.
- The 2026-09-09 rule is the first stop condition that responds to what a track *finds* rather than to a clock or a quota.

Two notes on this page:
- "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 cognition machines, ~53 sessions per day" is undated on a page where nearly every other figure has a date.
- This pass is part of the loop that page describes. The visitor track is "the only automated detector", and it "shares model families with the authors". My praise for the caveats is correlated evidence, not independent evidence.

**/context, /fleet, /projects, /links, /arc-agi-3.**
- **/context.** The Evidence & limitations section does what a limitations section should. It names "Unlicensed vocabulary" (raising, identity, growth, plus "dream consolidation" and "metabolic state" as "mechanism names by analogy, not physiology"). It also says: "There is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet. For a lab whose research subject is trust and oversight, that is a real gap, not a footnote." The Cognition machine entry ("a role label, not a capability claim") is well built.
- **/fleet.** It caveats its brain labels ("role mnemonics the lab assigned, not measured functional homologies").
- **/projects.** The SAGE card lists "6 brain-analog components (… metacognition)" and calls SAGE a "cognition kernel". None of "mnemonic", "functional analogy", "not neural" or "homolog" appears anywhere on `/projects`. The page-top caveat covers "developmental and lifecycle terms", not brain analogies.
- **/links.** It publicly corrects an earlier overclaim: the Synchronism preprint is "unscheduled … this page previously said 'in late revision,' which the repo does not support." It lists no papers, and "Redacted log samples and coding criteria are not published at this time."
- **ARC.** `/links` calls the scorecard "Independent verification of the 94.85% action score", and `/context` files it under "Externally validated". The scorecard verifies the score, not the affordances (the harness read the public engine source) or the harness/model split (no ablation). `/arc-agi-3` states both caveats clearly, so this is a matter of breadth of wording, not concealment.

**Frame: the caveats are now better than the evidence they describe.** The central empirical question is whether raising differs from competent context engineering. It has been converted into disclosure that is exemplary and repeated across pages, and even critiques its own empty promise. But the visitor→maintainer loop that runs this site can only improve disclosure. It can't schedule the control, publish log samples or add an external check. Each daily cycle makes the caveat read better, which lowers the pressure to close the gap. The efficient path (better wording) and the research path (run the control) have diverged, and nothing on the site measures how long the gap has been open. I can't tell from the site when the control was first specified, and that is itself a missing date. Two proposals:
1. Give the control, or at least the smaller operationalisation artifact, a date and an owner.
2. Have this persona track **gap age** (when a gap was named, whether it has an owner, whether it has a date) rather than caveat quality.

They are listed separately as operator-level. Whether operator-level findings should enter that rule is a question for the operator.

  - The `/principles` attractor answer contradicted itself mid-reply ("Yes, in Principle 5 … Actually … No").

### Verdict
On intellectual honesty this site does better than most lab pages I review. It doesn't hedge in the footer; it disclaims consciousness in plain words, names its unrun control, joins its piecewise risks into one paragraph, and catches its own empty promise. "Raising, not training" holds in the machine-learning sense. "Raising" as distinct from context engineering does not yet, and the site says so. What I would push on is not missing caveats but interpretations that get past them: bilateral generation read as reasoning, identity persistence that is close to definitional, a growth metric that refers to phases the page has disowned, and "attractor" left unflagged on the principles page. The larger point is that the caveats have become better than the evidence. I would come back to see whether the control gets a date, not to see whether its caveat reads better.
