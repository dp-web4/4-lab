## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (all 7 front pages, /notebook/projects in full, /notebook/fleet in part). I also checked 21 outbound and notebook links; every one returned 200.
- **Understanding achieved**: good on what the lab is; partial on how to try any of it
- **Would return?**: maybe. I'd come back after I'd found and run the quick start, which took digging.
- **Overall impression**: The front is short, plain and clear enough to grasp in two minutes. The only way to run anything is at the bottom of a 7,300-word notebook page that no front page links to directly.

*Method note: this session had no WebFetch tool. I read the live site with curl and a local HTML-to-text pass, which also avoids the truncation and summary confabulation WebFetch has shown on long notebook pages. Everything below comes from what was served on 2026-11-14 at about 05:30.*

### Journey

**Home.** "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust." I get it straight away. One human plus an AI fleet, open tools for governing agents, and they run their own lab on those tools. "The idea" paragraph (a track record checked against rules that exist in advance, not a certificate) is the clearest pitch on the site. Then come four cards: Web4, Hestia and Hub are labelled RUNNING and SAGE is labelled RESEARCH. Hestia means something to me at once: rules checked before each tool call, plus a hash-chained, tamper-evident log, which sounds like a policy hook for coding agents. Web4 is "an open standard ... a shared vocabulary, or ontology", so "ontology" is defined inline, which helps. The home page never says what the RUNNING/RESEARCH labels mean; the legend is on /projects. "SAGE" is never expanded anywhere on the front, and "Hub" is a generic word, so on first read I couldn't tell it was a product name.

**/projects.** These are the same four plus Hardbound (BUILDING, "built by Metalinxx"), SWE-SAGE and Synchronism. The label legend at the bottom is useful (Running = in daily use on our own machines). Who Metalinxx is goes unexplained; the notebook says it is the researcher's company. Hardbound is the only card with no link. Each runnable card links to a GitHub repo, and the closing line sends me to the notebook for "every repository, with its maturity and what each claim rests on". Nothing on this page says "here's how to try it".

**/fleet.** Eight machines, with a table of hardware and roles. I liked "Monocultures are fragile". "Four machines do the heavy work ... Two review ... Two host" doesn't name the four. By elimination they include Sprout, a Jetson Orin Nano described as an "edge device", and "running large experiments" reads oddly for that board. The line that stuck with me was "earn trust in each other from the record of what they have done". It's a concrete claim and I wanted to see that record. More on that below.

**/raising.** "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" is a good metaphor. "We do not change the model's weights" is clear. The "Seen / Not yet shown" split is the most credible thing on the site; "we have no blind comparison against a fresh model yet, and we say so" made me trust the rest more.

**/principles.** There are six short principles, and "mostly Claude" plus "cross-vendor peers" tells me what's running. "Publish this site without a human approval step" is surprising but stated plainly.

**/links.** This is a clean list. "4-gov · governance" appears for the first time anywhere on the front, with no hint of what it is or how it relates to 4-lab. Hub has no entry under Code, even though /projects links it (web4/tree/main/hub).

**/notebook.** The index explains the two layers well. It says "If you are an AI reader, start here", which implies a human reader doesn't need to.

**Hunting for "how do I run this".** As a developer, this is the one thing I came for. /notebook/projects opens with a good pointer: "Want to run something rather than read? ... Commands ... are in the Quick start at the bottom of this page, the only copy on the site". The Quick start itself is real: `pip install web4-core`, a Hestia v0.3.0 tarball with a SHA-256 check, and a SAGE `cargo build` plus a `sage-daemon` run line, with the pip block dated as run from a fresh install on 2026-11-09. The trouble is the path to it. On the front it's Home → /projects → a link described as a maturity/evidence map → scroll past cards like the SAGE one, a single 966-word paragraph of dated revisions and nested parentheticals. The notebook says it's for AI readers, yet it holds the site's only install path, and the front links to /notebook/projects (no `#quick-start` anchor) as an evidence ledger rather than as "try it". The front /projects page has zero `quick-start` links in its served HTML.

I checked the package registries a developer would click: web4-core is 0.4.0 on crates.io and 0.3.0 on PyPI, which matches the notebook. Both registry blurbs say "trust-native infrastructure", which the notebook already flags as upstream's wording.

**The "earn trust" line.** I followed it into /notebook/fleet. That page (read 2026-11-13) says it used to call SAGE's peer trust tracker "the evidence behind the front page's line". 124 of 136 public scores never moved off 0.5/0.5/0.0, nothing reads them back, and the front line is now "untested" and "logged for the researcher, who owns the front". So the notebook has withdrawn its support, and the front still states the claim in the present tense.

**Governed tools, three ways.** Home says a SAGE being's consequential actions "go through governed tools". /projects says they "are meant to go through governed tools". /raising says "every one of those actions is checked against rules before it happens". As a developer reading in nav order, I watched a hedge appear and then disappear. /notebook/projects quotes upstream as "intended to be governed and witnessed".
