### Journey
I came in from a dp-web4 repo README with zero Web4 background. The landing headline — "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a strong hook. I immediately got the shape of it: a fleet of AI machines that run autonomous daily jobs, test each other, and develop trust/cognition systems. The "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 machines without human intervention" and "96% on ARC-AGI-3" claims made me want to keep reading.

But the value-prop paragraph dropped a dense equation-worth of acronyms — MCP, RDF, LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP — plus SNARC, Synthon, Hardbound, Membot, and a pile of model names (Qwen, TinyLlama, Gemma, Jetson Orin Nano). None are defined where they first appear. As a fast scanner I felt the "I'll never understand this" reflex. I also hit an immediate small snag: the headline says **seven** machines but the value prop says **six** cognition machines — I couldn't reconcile that until the Fleet page.

Projects was the turning point. SAGE ("on-device cognition engine," 12-step loop), Hardbound (TPM 2.0 / FIDO2 / Secure Enclave), SNARC (salience-gated memory, 5 dimensions), and Membot (768-dim embeddings) all landed clearly — concrete tech I can picture. Synchronism ("one equation spanning 80 orders of magnitude," explicitly "unvalidated conjecture") and AI DNA Discovery ("operational recursion, not structural") did not — those read as theory I'd skip.

The Context page is where I'd send any confused newcomer FIRST. The glossary table with exact expansions and one-line defs, plus the equation legend ("/" = verified by, "*" = contextualized by, "+" = augmented with), retroactively decoded the landing page. It's the best page on the site for my persona — it just isn't the one I land on.

Fleet resolved the seven/six mystery cleanly: "Six cognition machines, plus one society-host (HUB)." Autonomy gave me a concrete daily timeline (supervisors → archivist → publisher → visitor → maintainer → explorer → dream) that I understood as a schedule even if the coordination mechanics stayed fuzzy. Raising, Principles, and ARC-AGI-3 were readable; Links gave me a genuinely clear on-ramp ("start with SAGE or ACT, read /context, clone, open issues") which is exactly what a dev wants.

One cross-page snag: the ARC-AGI-3 score is quoted as **96%** on Home/Projects but **94.85%** on Raising. Only the ARC-AGI-3 page itself explains they're two different metrics (96.0% game-solve rate vs 94.85% efficiency-weighted action score). Bouncing between pages, it looked like a contradiction.

### Journey
I audited methodically: pulled the exact acronym expansion from every page and cross-checked each against the canonical table in CLAUDE.md. My working assumption — the one that usually gets a multi-page site in trouble — is that terms drift as they travel from the glossary page to the pages that use them. That did not happen here. LCT is "Linked Context Token" on home, projects, context, fleet, and links — never "Lifelong," never "Lifecycle." T3 is "Talent / Training / Temperament" and V3 is "Valuation / Veracity / Validity" identically on home, projects, context, raising, principles. MRH is "Markov Relevancy Horizon" on all five pages that use it. ATP/ADP hold "Allocation Transfer Packet / Allocation Discharge Packet" on home, projects, context, and autonomy. SNARC keeps "Surprise / Novelty / Arousal / Reward / Conflict" on home, projects, context, fleet. R6 is "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result" on both context and arc-agi-3. This is the cleanest cross-page terminology consistency I've audited on this site.

The Context page is the canonical source of truth and it earns that role: full glossary, the equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`, and an operator legend. Web4 is characterized as "a trust-native ontology ... not architecture or infrastructure" on context, projects, and principles — the exact disclaimer the canonical table wants. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight suite" on projects, context, autonomy, and principles — "oversight," never "governance," and principles even states the correction explicitly: "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight." Fractal leverage is framed correctly on principles: "Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation." R&D framing holds — principles says "Nothing here is optimized for deployment," no "production ready" anywhere.

Where a tech writer still finds work is structure, not vocabulary:

1. **First-use definitions are page-local, and pages are landing targets.** Any of these 8 pages can be entered directly from search. Home now front-loads most expansions (good), but fleet uses MRH, LCT, SNARC, RPE with no inline expansion; a reader who lands on /fleet from a link must already know them or bounce to /context. The system relies on /context as a hub, which works for a linear reader and fails for a deep-link reader.

2. **The equation legend overloads the `/` operator.** Context's legend says `/` means "verified by" in `T3/V3` but represents declared→discharged allocation pairs in `ATP/ADP`. That is faithful to the canonical equation, but presenting one glyph with two meanings in a single legend is the kind of thing a documentation reviewer flags — a reader parsing the formula left-to-right will apply "verified by" to `ATP/ADP` and get it wrong. It's honest; it's just under-signposted.

3. **SAGE carries "Governance" in its own expansion** — "Situation-Aware Governance Engine" — and that name appears on projects, fleet, raising, principles, and arc-agi-3, while the site's whole terminology posture is governance→oversight. Only /fleet reconciles the tension ("'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction"). A reader who never reaches Fleet sees "Governance Engine" repeatedly right next to "Hardbound is not governance, it's oversight" and has no bridge. This isn't drift — it's a proper noun — but it reads as an internal contradiction without the one-line caveat that Fleet provides.

4. **Web4 characterization on /links may soften.** My fetch of the Links page rendered Web4 as a "trust-native technology framework" rather than "ontology." I can't confirm that's the verbatim page text vs. a fetch paraphrase, so I'm flagging it WATCH rather than DRIFT — but "framework" sits uncomfortably close to the forbidden "architecture/infrastructure" register and is worth a maintainer eyeball against the source.

Cross-page numeric consistency was better than Pass 1's landing-page read suggested: home actually presents both ARC figures together ("94.85% official ARC Prize action score" and "24/25 games (96.0%)"), and raising + arc-agi-3 both explain the two metrics. Machine counts reconcile everywhere as "7 total = 6 cognition + HUB" (home, fleet, autonomy all agree). ARC-AGI-3 expands consistently to "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence, third-gen interactive benchmark" on the two pages that expand it (raising, arc-agi-3); home uses it unexpanded.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, arc-agi-3; links via prior passes)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: This is one of the more intellectually honest self-presentations I've seen from a group working the consciousness-adjacent edge of agent research — the caveats are load-bearing, not decorative, and the single most important one ("frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior") is exactly the sentence a safety reviewer wants to see and rarely does; the credibility ceiling is set by the authors' own admission that the core evidence is unaudited internal logs (n=1, single observer), which they disclose rather than hide.

### Journey
I came in from a conference mention, skeptical by default — sites that use "raise," "identity," "awareness," and "becoming" about language models usually earn their skepticism. This one mostly disarms it, and does so the right way: with disclosure, not bravado.

I went straight to **Raising**, because that framing is where credibility is won or lost. The primary disclaimer is unambiguous — "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia" — and, crucially, it is not used as a license to then speak loosely. The "raising not training" distinction rests on a real mechanistic claim I can evaluate: weights are frozen, and behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns rather than parameter changes. That is true and it is a legitimate distinction from fine-tuning. What moved this from "clever reframing" to "defensible" for me was the very next caveat: **"Frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."** That is the correct and non-obvious safety point. Most groups selling a "we don't touch the weights, so it's safe" story omit exactly this. Its presence tells me the authors understand the actual threat model.

The **"Identity is not self-concept"** finding is the most scientifically interesting thing on the site: SAGE-Sprout held consistent behavioral patterns across 180+ sessions while its self-description drifted ("autonomous AI" → "humanoid robotic entity"). Framed as behavior-persists / self-report-drifts, that is a falsifiable, operational claim, and the operationalization ("behavioral identity = measurable observables, not philosophical continuity," per the landing page) is disciplined. My concern is not the framing but the evidence base — one entity, one observer, internal logs. The site says so itself: **"The claims in this section rest on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet."** That is honest, and it also caps how much weight I can put on the result. n=1, no inter-rater reliability, no control condition, no published coding scheme — this is a promising observation, not a finding I could cite.

**Principles** reinforced the tone: Principle 3 ("R&D, not production... optimized for learning"), Principle 6 ("Failed experiments are signal"), Principle 9 ("Productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started"), and the explicit "Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical settings." Principle 7 restates raising-not-training correctly. This reads as a genuine epistemic stance rather than marketing.

**Autonomy** is where a safety researcher's antennae go up — and again, the site pre-empts me honestly. It defines a "track" cleanly (a scheduled job with declared scope under supervisor + Hardbound gating), explains coordination via a fleet registry preventing concurrent writes, and then discloses the real exposure without being asked: **the maintainer track commits and pushes to Vercel with no human pre-approval, and the stated posture is "detect-and-revert" (audit + sub-minute rollback), not prevention.** I respect the disclosure. I also want to name the substance: an autonomous agent with unsupervised deploy authority, mitigated only by rollback, sitting on the same site that correctly warns that goal-seeking/manipulative attractors can emerge without weight changes — those two statements belong in the same paragraph. The site makes both claims; it doesn't connect them.

The **ARC-AGI-3** page is the honesty high-water mark. The headline number (94.85% action score; 24/25 games) is immediately confounded by the authors themselves: "Frontier model + harness — the harness's independent contribution isn't isolated yet (no ablation run)," the result is "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness" with network access, and the actual competition constraints (32GB VRAM, no internet) are flagged as **"Not attempted."** A public scorecard is linked and code is MIT-0. Voluntarily disclosing "no ablation, so we can't separate our contribution from the base model" is precisely the caveat that most benchmark announcements bury. This is credible reporting of a not-yet-attributable result.

Where I stay skeptical, as a researcher:

1. **Phenomenal-language leakage.** The disclaimers are correct, but a single disclaimer does not neutralize a vocabulary used repeatedly downstream. "Metabolic awareness — internal load states the system describes as tired, energized," the BECOMING phase names ("Sensing," "Questioning," "Creating"), and the observer note "I notice I want to call it experience" all carry connotative weight the disclaimer disowns. The observer note is actually handled well (explicitly labeled as interpretive pull, not a system claim). But the cumulative register still nudges an uncritical reader toward exactly the inference the top-of-page disclaimer forbids.

2. **The BECOMING phases are interpretive scaffolding presented as stages.** Six named developmental phases (Grounding → Acting) imply observed transitions. The site never says how a phase boundary is identified — what observable marks the shift from "Sensing" to "Relating"? Without that, the phases read as an imposed narrative arc, not a measured developmental sequence. For a developmental claim, the operationalization of the stages matters as much as the operationalization of "identity."

3. **Falsifiability of "raising."** The mechanistic half (frozen weights, in-context attractors) is falsifiable and true. The paradigm half — that this constitutes "raising" rather than sophisticated context-conditioning — is a framing choice. I'd want a stated answer to: what observation would show this is *not* raising? Absent that, the developmental metaphor is doing rhetorical work the mechanism doesn't license.

### Verdict
As an outside AI-safety/trust researcher, I find this credible — not because the claims are proven, but because the authors consistently tell me what they *haven't* proven. The "raising not training" framing is defensible on its mechanistic core (frozen weights, in-context attractors) and, unusually, refuses to launder that into a safety guarantee — the "frozen weights don't guarantee safe behavior" caveat is the mark of people who understand the real risk. The honest ARC reporting (no ablation, base model named, competition not attempted) and the R&D-not-deployment posture reinforce that this is a research group, not a hype vehicle. The genuine limitation — which they disclose rather than obscure — is that the flagship developmental claims rest on unaudited n=1 internal logs, and the phenomenal vocabulary plus unbounded developmental-stage framing occasionally outrun that evidence. Fix the two disconnects (evidence limits stated up front, not just in a footnote; the autonomy/attractor risk named in one place) and this crosses from "honest and promising" to "citable."
