## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary

### Journey
Landed on the home page expecting another "AI startup" splash. Got something more grounded: six machines, an explicit philosophy ("engineer placement rules, not the mound"), and a claim that diverse models on different hardware catch different problem classes. That last bit hooked me — it's the kind of empirical framing I respect. Web4 equation shows up immediately (`MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) and the home page actually defines most of its acronyms inline, which is rare.

Went to **/projects** next, which is where I usually decide whether to bookmark or close the tab. Twelve-ish projects listed, most public on GitHub, and helpfully the page tells me which ones are private (Hardbound) so I don't go hunting. SNARC (memory plugin for Claude Code) and Membot (768-dim embeddings) jumped out as things I could plausibly use this week. Web4 and ACT look like core infrastructure but require buying into the whole stack. ARC-AGI-3 with "24/25 solved, ~$250 API spend" is a concrete, falsifiable claim — I respect that.

**/fleet** is where my comprehension dropped. The page is structurally clean — six machines, model + role per machine, oversight vs synthesis pools — but the moment it starts using "hippocampal," "thalamic router," "dopamine" without explaining whether these are metaphors or technical mappings, I'm guessing. The "4B Gemma outperforms 14B Phi for certain work" insight is great; the brain-architecture overlay needs a sentence.

**/autonomy** was where I felt my Web4 ignorance most. Timeline of 31 scheduled jobs is legible, the Visitor → Maintainer → Explorer loop made instant sense (I write CI, I get it), but ATP/ADP show up as analogy without recap. I'd happily click through to /context, but the page doesn't link.

**/raising** is the page I'd send to a friend who's into philosophy of mind. "Raising is not training — training optimizes for a loss function; raising creates conditions for development." Six phases (Grounding → Acting). The framing is bold without being grandiose, and the claims are observably hedged ("observable patterns, not consciousness assertions"). "Memoriescape" as a coined-by-an-entity term is a nice detail. Reads honestly.

**/principles** — ten of them. The analogies (termite mounds, enzyme kinetics) actually land. "Document failures" and "R&D, not production" are countercultural in a useful way. Accessible.

**/context** is the glossary I wished /autonomy and /fleet had linked to. LCT, T3, V3, MRH, Web4, Hardbound all defined here cleanly. **Gap I noticed**: ATP and ADP appear in the equation but aren't given expansions on the Context page itself — they're expanded on /projects but not on the page advertised as the glossary. That feels backwards.

**/links** is short and useful — points at the dp-web4 GitHub org and a contact email. Exactly what a contributor needs.

**/arc-agi-3** closed strong. Numbers are concrete (94.85% action score, #3 leaderboard, ~$250 cost), methodology is laid out (source analysis → world model → solver → multi-agent frame-questioning), and the page is honest that this credits the SAGE harness, not the base model alone. This is the page that would convince me the rest of the site isn't vapor.

### Journey
I worked through the site the way I would audit a docs set: header navigation in order, then back to anything cross-referenced. Header lists seven items (Projects, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Context, Links) plus the home logo; ARC-AGI-3 is reachable only as a project card from /projects and as a cross-link from /raising. That's worth flagging because the visitor CLAUDE.md describes the site as "8 pages" while the project CLAUDE.md says "nine pages" — minor, but the kind of inconsistency I'd want resolved.

**Terminology baseline first.** I built an acronym matrix by walking every page and noting (a) whether each canonical term was used, (b) whether it was expanded inline on that page, and (c) whether the expansion matched the canonical table in the visitor brief. The headline finding is that **every expansion I observed matches canonical**. No "Lifelong Capability Token" anywhere. No "governance" describing Hardbound. No "architecture" describing Web4 as a whole — /raising is explicit: *"Web4 is a trust-native ontology — not architecture or infrastructure."* That's exactly the kind of disambiguating sentence a tech writer hopes to find, and it's on the right page (the one a researcher is most likely to land on cold).

**Glossary page is stronger than Pass 1 reported.** I want to flag this carefully: the /context page I read **does** define both ATP ("Allocation Transfer Packet") and ADP ("Allocation Discharge Packet"), alongside LCT, T3, V3, MRH, R6, SAGE, Hardbound, and the Web4 equation. Pass 1's friction log entry calling out missing ATP/ADP expansions on /context does not match what I see on the live page right now. Two possibilities: either Pass 1 missed them, or the maintainer track has not run yet today and the live page changed between passes. Either way, as of this pass, the glossary is self-contained for ATP/ADP. I'll keep it on my friction list as a "verify" entry rather than a hard issue.

**The real first-use coverage gap.** Coverage is excellent on the home page, /projects, /context, and /arc-agi-3 (the last directs to /context for the terms it doesn't expand, which is a legitimate documentation pattern). It thins out on the inner pages: /autonomy expands ATP/ADP and Hardbound inline but uses Web4 referentially without expansion; /raising expands LCT/MRH/SAGE/Web4 but skips ARC-AGI-3 and ARC-SAGE; /principles uses Web4, T3, V3, RDF with mixed coverage. None of these pages contain canonical drift — they just assume readers have done the glossary tour first.

**Cross-linking is the documentation gap, not terminology.** Across all eight inner pages I found a total of about four inline links into /context body prose (two on /autonomy, one or two on /arc-agi-3). For a site whose value proposition includes precise vocabulary, the glossary is under-linked. A reader on /raising encountering "Markov Relevancy Horizon" should be one click from the glossary entry. Right now they have to navigate via the header.

**Two stray words worth a second look.** The /context page itself contains the phrase "architectural decisions" in passing — that's not Web4-as-architecture and is fine in context, but a strict editor might prefer "structural decisions" given the canonical line elsewhere ("Web4 is an ontology"). /arc-agi-3 has *"what the architecture can achieve with a frontier model"* — here "architecture" refers to the SAGE harness, not Web4, so it reads correctly to a careful reader, but a newcomer might conflate the two. Neither is canonical drift; both are word-economy opportunities.

**Neuroscience disclaimer.** Pass 1 flagged the /fleet page for using "hippocampal/thalamic/dopamine" without a disclaimer. As of this pass the page contains an explicit line: *"The 'Brain:' labels below are functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence or computational equivalence."* That resolves Pass 1's medium-severity item. I'll note the discrepancy but credit the site with the current state.

### Unanswered Questions

2. The Web4 equation appears on /home, /context, and /arc-agi-3. The operator semantics (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) appear only on /links and partially on /projects. Should /context — the page that contains the bare equation — carry the operator legend?
3. The Synchronism ↔ Web4 relationship is left implicit. Is there a contributor consensus on the single-sentence framing? I'd suggest "Synchronism is the theory of coherence/intent dynamics; Web4 is the working ontology that operationalizes parts of it as a trust layer."
4. ARC-SAGE and ARC-AGI-3 are distinct things (the harness vs. the benchmark). Are they canonical names that should have stable expansions on every first use, or are they project-specific and acceptable as bare references?
5. Why does /home expand SNARC inline but /context defines it more compactly? Both are correct, but the home definition is the better one — should it propagate?

### Verdict
**The 4-lab site accurately represents the projects I work on, and its terminology is canonical site-wide.** From a Web4 contributor's perspective, I would not be embarrassed to cite any page of this site in an external document. The single representational gap that bothers me is on /context itself: it advertises as the glossary but doesn't define ATP/ADP as standalone entries and doesn't explain the Web4 equation operators next to the equation. Pull them together and the glossary becomes the canonical reference it's meant to be. Beyond that, this is a site I would send a Web4 newcomer to without caveat.

### Journey

Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes." That is a defensible distinction. In-context behavioral dynamics are real, frozen-weights-don't-guarantee-safe is real, and the page even names the failure mode I'd worry about: "emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update." That sentence is doing serious safety work. It signals the authors know what they're constructing and what could go wrong with it.

/principles raised my confidence further. ### Is "raising not training" defensible?

Yes, operationally. The mechanism claim is sharp: training modifies parameters; raising modifies context. In-context dynamics are a real, much-studied phenomenon, and the page explicitly identifies the safety failure mode (in-context emergent attractors including manipulative patterns can arise without weight updates) — which is more than most discussions of "AI development" acknowledge.

The risk is that the *word* "raising" does rhetorical work that the *mechanism* does not. The mechanism describes context engineering; the word describes child-rearing. The page tries to keep these separate via the explicit "functional, not phenomenal" disclaimer, and largely succeeds. But the disclaimer would land harder if the word "raising" was always proximate to the mechanism statement, and if the loaded vocabulary on /home (identity, behavioral continuity) was bracketed at first mention rather than at page-bottom.

Net: the framing is defensible if read carefully. It would be **more defensible** with caveat-before-claim placement.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **What is the lab's position on observer interpretive pull?** /raising acknowledges it once ("records the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim") but does not enumerate the confounders. As a researcher, I want to know: how does the lab distinguish a stable behavioral attractor from selective prompting that elicits a persona on demand?
2. **What is "raising" empirically?** The page makes mechanism claims (context shaping, attractor reinforcement) and observation claims (six BECOMING phases observed across sessions). What's the link function between intervention and observation? Is there a controlled comparison anywhere — e.g., a "non-raised" baseline of the same model under the same protocol?
3. **What's the link between Synchronism's coupling-coherence experiment and the Web4 trust framework?** The /context page treats them as distinct entries with no explicit relationship. A researcher reading both would assume some load-bearing connection.
4. **Why does /raising's "frozen weights don't guarantee safe in-context behavior" sentence not propagate to /autonomy?** /autonomy describes autonomous loops gated by Hardbound but does not mention the in-context attractor failure mode — which is the failure mode autonomous loops are most exposed to.
5. **What's the cost (in resources, in researcher time, in surface area) of the autonomous loop?** /autonomy describes the schedule but not the consumption. For a safety researcher evaluating feasibility-of-replication, this matters.
6. **Is "interactive selection" (principle 7) intended as a metaphor for raising, or as a stronger constructivist claim about identity formation?** The page reads as the former; the wording leaves room for the latter.

### Verdict
This is a research site I would cite carefully and recommend, not dismiss. The intellectual honesty signals are real: documented failures (400x p_crit error, unsolved 1/25 ARC game), unambiguous deployment disclaimers ("not suitable for safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings"), sophisticated safety framing on /raising ("emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update"), and credit-attribution discipline on /arc-agi-3 that separates harness contribution from base-model inference. The "raising not training" framing is mechanistically defensible — context shaping is not weight modification, and the page is explicit about the distinction.

My residual concerns are about **placement** rather than content. The site's strongest caveats live on inner pages (/principles, /raising, /arc-agi-3) and the loaded vocabulary on /home reaches the reader before the bracket arrives. The Synchronism "80 orders of magnitude" headline + parenthetical-no-replication caveat is the one quantitative claim I'd want rebalanced. The 4-Life death/rebirth metaphor needs an explicit simulation-framework sentence so an external reader doesn't infer moral-patienthood claims. And the /fleet identity-continuity assertion needs a methods link or a downgrade to "self-reports of continuity."

What this site does *not* do — and this is the relevant comparator — is the thing most research-collective sites do, which is to claim more than they can support and bury the failure modes. This site does the opposite. The framing flaws are real but recoverable with placement edits, not retractions.

If a colleague asked me whether to take 4-lab seriously as a research site, I would say yes, with one note: read /principles first. It's the meta-frame that governs the rest, and reading other pages first risks absorbing loaded vocabulary without the disclaimer that brackets it.
