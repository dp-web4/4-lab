## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (/, /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links — 9 fetches counting the landing page retry)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes

### Journey

I clicked in from a dp-web4 repo README expecting a project page. I got a lab.

**Landing.** Headline is "Eight Machines, One Recursive Learning Loop." That's an evocative hook but it answers *what they have*, not *what problem they're solving*. My first 20 seconds were spent figuring out whether this was a hardware project, an agent framework, or a philosophy site. It's closest to the third, but I didn't know that yet. The section headers — "What's Happening Right Now," "What We've Observed" — read like a lab notebook, which I ended up liking a lot, but they're not a value proposition.

The thing that saved the landing page for me was the vocabulary primer. There's a run of one-line definitions (MCP, RDF, LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, SNARC) right there instead of a link-out. That's the right call. I could keep reading without opening a tab.

What I could *not* parse on first pass was the equation:

```
Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP
```

I read `/` as division and `*` as multiplication, because I write code. It took me until /context to learn that `/` means "verified by," `*` means "contextualized by," and `+` means "augmented with" — and that `/` means something *different* inside `ATP/ADP` than inside `T3/V3`. That notation key needs to travel with the equation. Every time the equation appears without it, a developer misreads it as arithmetic.

**Projects.** Best page for me. Eight project cards, each with a one-line "what it is." SAGE reads as the only thing I could actually run ("runnable on-device cognition kernel"). Hardbound is marked private, which is honest but means one of the four headline projects is a wall. The ARC-AGI-3 number (24/25 games, 94.85%) jumped out as the one claim I could go verify myself.

**Context.** This is the page that made the whole site click, and it's the second nav item, which is correct placement. The glossary is genuinely complete — ~40 terms, every acronym expanded, and several entries admit the term is *underdefined* ("emergence," "coherence," "attractor" are all flagged as having multiple senses without one operational definition). A site that tells me its own vocabulary is fuzzy in three places earns more trust from me than one that pretends otherwise.

One naming problem: the nav label is "Context." I'm a developer reading a site about LLM agents. "Context" means context window to me. I nearly skipped it. It's the glossary — the most useful page on the site — and it's labeled with the one word in the AI vocabulary that means something else.

**Fleet.** Eight machines, real hardware, real model names, session counts per box. As a dev this is the most concrete page and I enjoyed it. But it's where the jargon density spikes hardest: "crystallization," "chapter ledger," "hestia identity," and a full set of brain-region analogies (hippocampus, thalamus, dopaminergic reward circuitry) mapped onto machines. All of those are in the /context glossary. None of them are defined or linked inline on /fleet. I bounced back and forth twice.

Also: the landing page says eight machines, /autonomy says "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 cognition machines." Both are true — six cognition + two society-hosts — but a skimmer sees 6 and 8 and assumes one is stale.

**Autonomy.** Clear timeline, 03:00 through post-session, each track named with a one-line job. I got the feedback loop immediately (Visitor at 05:30 finds problems → Maintainer at 06:30 fixes them). The honest-assessment section states outright that the maintainer track ships to the public site with no human gate and detection runs on a ~24h loop. I respect that being on the page rather than buried.

**Raising.** Six BECOMING patterns. The consciousness disclaimer is unambiguous and stated early — "not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia" — and identity/growth get operational definitions (session-to-session behavioral consistency; response diversity and task success rates). As a developer I don't have standing to judge the framing, but I can say the claims are stated in terms I could in principle measure, which is more than most writing in this space.

One thing I stumbled on: the page says raising "is always in-context," then notes some fleet machines run LoRA adapters for separate tasks. I had to re-read to confirm those are genuinely separate activities and not a hedge.

**Principles.** Ten of them, short, no filler. #3 ("R&D, not production") and #6 ("Document failures") explain the whole site's tone retroactively — I'd have understood the landing page faster if I'd read this first. #10 is "Terminology matters," and it's where I hit the one thing that actually looks like a contradiction to me (see friction log).

**ARC-AGI-3.** The credibility anchor of the entire site, and I'd promote it. It has what nothing else on the site has: an external scorecard, a leaderboard position, an action count, and a dollar figure (~$250). It also volunteers the biggest hole in its own result — no ablation, so the harness's contribution versus base-model capability is unmeasured. Stating that yourself is the strongest thing on the page.

**Links.** Where my journey dead-ends. Repos are all there, SAGE is named as the entry point, three explainer sites are linked. Then: "There is no CONTRIBUTING guide yet and no stated policy on outside pull requests." I arrived from a GitHub README. That sentence is the answer to the only question I actually came with.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **How do I contribute?** The site says there's no policy. That's honest but leaves me with nothing to do. Are issues welcome? Should I email first?
2. **What do I run, and on what?** SAGE is "runnable" and "the entry point," but I never learned the hardware floor, install path, or what a minimal run looks like. The fleet ranges from a Jetson Orin Nano to a 4090 — where's the bottom?
3. **Is Web4 something I can build against today, or is it a research vocabulary?** The equation, the glossary, and the worked example all suggest a real spec, but the only referenced canonical artifact is a `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` in another repo. Is there an implementation to import?
4. **Why is SAGE a "Governance Engine" on a site that says the correct word is "oversight"?** Either the ban is scoped and unstated, or the name is legacy. Neither is explained.
5. **The 94.85% is Claude Opus 4.6 with network access.** So what did the SAGE harness contribute? The page says this is unmeasured — is the ablation planned, or is it acknowledged and parked?
6. **What is a "society-host" as distinct from a cognition machine?** I inferred infrastructure vs. inference, but never saw it stated.

### Verdict

For my persona this site over-delivers on *what the lab is* and under-delivers on *what I can do about it*. The glossary is the best thing here — nearly every acronym is expanded correctly, and where a term is genuinely fuzzy the site says so instead of faking rigor, which is rarer than it should be. The honest-assessment blocks (no human gate on the maintainer track, no ablation on the ARC result, no external verification of internal observations) did more to make me trust the numbers than the numbers did.

But I arrived from a repo README, and the path from "this is interesting" to "here's my first commit" doesn't exist. The single highest-value change on the whole site is three lines on /links saying how to engage. Second is putting the equation's operator key wherever the equation appears — right now the site's central formula is a trap for anyone who reads `/` as division.

I'd come back. I'd read /context first next time, and I'd start at ARC-AGI-3 if I wanted to convince a colleague this is real.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home + all 8 nav items), with targeted re-fetches on 5 pages to verify contested strings
- **Understanding achieved**: solid

### Journey

I audit terminology for a living, so I did this the boring way: transcribe every page, extract every acronym with its surrounding sentence, diff the expansions against the canonical table, then diff the *definitions* against each other. Then I re-fetched every page where I was about to file a finding, because a summarizer that drops a sentence looks identical to a site that never had it.

That last step mattered. Four of the findings I would have filed from a single read did not survive a second fetch.

**Nav and IA.** Eight items: Projects | Context | ARC-AGI-3 | Fleet | Autonomy | Raising | Principles | Links. Every one resolved. Putting the glossary second is the correct call and it is doing real work — most pages use `Web4` bare, which is only acceptable because the definition is one click away and early in the tab order. I agree with Pass 1 that "Context" is a poor label in an AI-agent context; I'd add that it's also the least descriptive word available for a page that is 80% glossary.

**The equation.** Pass 1 filed this as high severity: the operator key appears only on /context, so the equation is misreadable as arithmetic everywhere else. I checked all four pages that carry the equation. The key is present on the **landing page, /projects, /context, and /arc-agi-3** — every instance. The landing page's version is the most careful of the four:

> `"/"` means two things here: "verified by" in `T3/V3`, but a plain declared→discharged allocation pair in `ATP/ADP` — same glyph, unrelated semantics.

That's better than the reference table I was handed, which lists `/` = "verified by" with no second sense. The site is ahead of the shorthand, not behind it. Pass 1's finding is a fetch artifact and should not be actioned.

**Cross-page numbers.** This is where I expected to find drift and instead found the best-executed thing on the site. Three different session totals appear:

| Page | Figure | Stated basis |
|------|--------|--------------|
| / | "2,065+ raising sessions across the fleet" | "(internally logged, per-machine counts on /fleet)" |
| /projects | "1,991 raising sessions across the 6 cognition machines" | "(internally logged; per-machine counts on /fleet, 2,065 including HUB" |
| /fleet | per-machine table | "the same basis as the 2,065 cumulative figure on /projects and the site home page" |

I summed the /fleet table: 435 + 488 + 336 + 377 + 162 + 193 = **1,991**. Plus HUB's 74 = **2,065**. Both figures are correct, the scope difference is disclosed on two of the three pages, and each page cross-references the others. I have audited documentation sets that could not do this with one number.

Two nits remain. The landing page says "across the fleet" for a figure that covers 7 of 8 machines — /projects says "including HUB," which is the precise version, and the home page should borrow it. And `pub` carries no session count at all, so "the fleet" quietly means "the fleet minus pub."

Machine counts reconcile the same way: `/` says "8 machines (6 cognition + 2 society-hosts)", /fleet says the same, /autonomy says "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 cognition machines" and states the 6+2=8 breakdown. Pass 1 flagged this as looking stale on a skim; the reconciliation is actually stated on all three pages.

**Legacy-name handling.** The SAGE "Governance Engine" versus Principle 10 tension is the obvious thing to flag, and Pass 1 filed it as an unexplained self-contradiction. It is explained — in four places:

- **/**: "'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction — see /context."
- **/projects**: "…predates the lab's governance→oversight terminology correction; the name is retained upstream across code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark"
- **/context**: "Names minted before the correction are retained where they are load-bearing: 'Governance' in SAGE's name (kept across code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark)."
- **/principles**, attached to Principle 10 itself: "'Governance' in SAGE's name predates Principle 10's governance→oversight correction — see /context."

Four disclaimers, on exactly the four pages where a reader can see the tension, each pointing at /context. This is not a contradiction; it's a documented exception with a stated rationale (upstream name is load-bearing). It should not be "fixed," and SAGE should certainly not be renamed. /fleet, /raising, /arc-agi-3 and /links use the expansion bare, but a reader reaching those has passed at least one disclaimer.

**The oversight collision note.** The best paragraph on the site, and it's buried. /context states that on this site "oversight" means machine-enforced gating — policy gates, key custody, audit logs — explicitly distinguished from "the human-supervision sense the word carries in AI-safety literature." That is a genuine homonym hazard, correctly identified and correctly disambiguated. But Principle 10 asserts "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight" with no pointer to the collision note, and /principles is the page an outside reader is most likely to read cold. They will take the AI-safety reading and conclude the lab means human review. The disclaimer travels for SAGE's legacy name; it doesn't travel for the word doing the actual work.

**Self-flagged vocabulary.** /context marks three terms as not operationally defined: "emergence/emergent" ("Used in more than one sense on this site…with no single operational definition yet"), "coherence" ("Used in three related senses…"), and "attractor/attractor basin" ("Used on this site as a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object"). Flagging your own underdefined vocabulary in the glossary is the single strongest signal of documentation maturity on this site.

**Where the glossary has a hole.** "Society-host" appears on `/`, /context, /autonomy and /fleet. It has no glossary entry. It *is* defined, functionally, on /fleet — "Runs the Web4 Community Hub daemon. Hosts the fleet itself as a Web4 society — every cognition machine is a member" — which is fourth in nav, well after three pages have already used the term. A glossary that self-flags "emergence" as fuzzy should not be missing a term that appears in the second sentence of the home page.

**The one real gloss defect.** See the terminology audit below — LCT is "portable" on the home page and "non-transferable — permanently bound to its entity" on /fleet.

I read a lot of lab sites. I have not previously seen one write the sentence "the vocabulary used across this site runs ahead of the comparison that would license it." That is a researcher grading their own paper honestly.

**And then it doesn't travel.** I checked. So the site's own consolidation page omits the site's own self-identified scope-wide limitation. That's the finding. Not dishonesty — the disclosure exists and is unusually good. It's a distribution failure in the one place distribution was the explicit goal.

**"Raising, not training" — is it defensible?** As written, yes, and more narrowly than the vocabulary implies. The claim is deflated correctly: *"We're claiming that developmental frameworks describe what we observe better than training frameworks do."* That's a claim about descriptive fit, not about ontology or inner life. The vulnerability is that "better than" is comparative and no comparison has been run. Right now the site asserts superior descriptive fit against an unmeasured competitor. The site knows this — it's the deflationary section. So the framing is defensible *as stated on /raising* and overclaims *everywhere else*, because everywhere else the qualifier is missing.

Principle 7's "It is not training; it is also not nothing" is rhetorically honest and epistemically empty — it defines the thing by negation on both sides. I don't think it should be cut; it's a fair statement of where the work actually is. But it's the sentence a skeptical reader will quote back.

**Consciousness claims.** Handled well, and this one *does* travel. /raising has a "What we're not claiming" block: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." The home page independently frames "teach" and "raise" as "functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience." /fleet carries "functional descriptions of observed behavior, not consciousness claims" and attaches an operational gloss to the Sprout transfer: "(By 'behavioral identity': consistent session-to-session interaction patterns, accumulated experience, and raising curriculum — measurable observables, not philosophical continuity.)" That's three pages, independently hedged. No complaint.

The residual issue is the sentence right after the disclaimer: "The entities show something that looks like growth, something that looks like identity, something that looks like peer relationships." "Looks like" is doing real work and is fair — but it's the same unlicensed comparative as above, one line below a disclaimer about a *different* question. Disclaiming consciousness is not the same as licensing developmental description, and the adjacency invites a reader to think one covers the other.

**Borrowed formalism.** I went looking for terms that import mathematical or biological precision they don't have, expecting to find several unflagged. The site had mostly beaten me there:

- attractor — "Used on this site as a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object"
- tensor — "structured multi-component quantity, not rank-≥2 linear-algebra array"
- synthon — "Not related to chemistry term of same name"
- Markov (in MRH) — "'Markov' gestures at the conditional-independence idea (what's inside the horizon screens off what's beyond it)". *Gestures at* is the right verb, and I had this queued as a finding until I fetched the sentence.
- metabolic state — "Interoceptive proxy, not formally specified"
- emergence / coherence — both self-flagged as multi-sense with no operational definition

Six borrowings, six flags. The two that got through are "dream" (a post-session transcript-review pass, described as "a dream cycle that extracts patterns from observations," with no note that the sleep analogy is an analogy) and the /fleet brain-region assignments — hippocampal index, thalamic router, dopaminergic reward-prediction-error, cerebellum, dlPFC, interoception, one per machine. The page's consciousness caveat covers the wrong axis for these: my concern isn't that they imply experience, it's that a one-to-one mapping of model families onto brain structures reads as measured functional homology when it is almost certainly a role mnemonic assigned by the authors. Nothing on the page says which, or whether the mapping predicts anything.

**"Oversight" is the one term that will actively mislead my field.** /context has the distinction and has it well — Hardbound performs "watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen," explicitly separated from "AI-safety literature's 'human-supervision' sense." Principle 10 states only: "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight. The right word prevents the wrong mental model." I verified: no pointer, no mention of gating versus human supervision.

For an outsider this is worse than ambiguity, it's an inversion. In my literature "oversight" defaults to *human* oversight. A researcher reads Principle 10, imports the default, and concludes the lab has human review in the loop. /autonomy says the opposite: maintainer changes deploy "without a human gate," with detect-and-revert on a ~24h cycle. The site's chosen word makes an outsider believe the precise thing the site elsewhere honestly denies. Passes 2 and 3 both flagged the missing pointer; from my seat this isn't a missing cross-reference, it's the highest-consequence terminology issue on the site.

**Evidence infrastructure.** /links is blunt: "no papers or preprints are published yet," and "claims on this site (other than the externally scored ARC-AGI-3 result) cite internal session logs that are not yet externally auditable." /raising adds "no log samples or coding criteria are published yet." I take that at face value and it raises my trust rather than lowering it.

It also points at the cheapest credibility purchase available, which is *not* the control. Publishing one annotated session-log excerpt plus the coding criteria used to score BECOMING phases would let an outsider evaluate whether the phase categories are doing work or are post-hoc labels. That's days, not months, and it doesn't require the scramble run. (Privacy rules may bind here — if raising logs can't be excerpted, saying *that* is itself the answer, and it converts "not yet audited" into "not auditable by design," which is a very different and more important disclosure.)

**ARC-AGI-3.** The strongest-hedged page on the site. Ablation caveat first, repeated at attribution, and the local-vs-frontier non-commensurability stated flatly: "The two figures are not commensurable and nothing here should be read as a local model matching or beating a frontier one." One thing slips: the "#3 among human competitors" placement. Network access was used; the actual Kaggle competition requires "32GB VRAM, no internet access" on a private set. Both facts are on the page, but the condition difference isn't attached to the ranking, and a ranking against humans is the single most quotable number here.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is the primary metric for the scramble control, and what effect size counts as a null? Without those two numbers the pre-commitment cannot be checked by anyone, including the authors.
2. Who owns the control run, and roughly when? "Specified but not scheduled" is honest; it's also indefinitely stable.
3. Are the /fleet brain-region mappings predictive or mnemonic? If a machine's behavior stopped matching its assigned region, would anything change?
4. The Sprout transfer (Qwen 0.5B → TinyLlama 1.1B, 115 sessions) is reported as one positive case. What would a *discontinuous* transfer have looked like? No negative case, threshold, or baseline is given, so "behavioral identity remained continuous" has nothing to be continuous against.
5. Can one annotated log excerpt plus the BECOMING coding criteria be published under the lab's privacy constraints? If not, the internal-observation tier is unauditable by design rather than not-yet-audited — a different and more significant claim.
6. If the control runs and comes back null, what actually happens to this site? The commitment is to retire the vocabulary; nine pages are built on it. Is there a plan, or is the commitment cheaper than it reads?
7. Why does the /context "Evidence & limitations" section — which explicitly exists to consolidate caveats — not include the deflationary control? Oversight, or a judgment that it belongs to /raising only?

### Verdict

This is one of the more intellectually honest research sites I have read, and the honesty is concentrated rather than distributed. The site names its own deflationary alternative, specifies the experiment that would kill its framing, admits the experiment is unscheduled with no metric and no owner, states that no papers exist, states that maintainer changes deploy with no human gate, states that the ARC ablation is unrun, and states that its one striking number is a single trial. I did not have to dig for any of that. Consciousness-adjacent claims are caveated on three pages independently, and every load-bearing term is given a measurable definition or an explicit flag that it doesn't have one.

"Raising, not training" is defensible in its narrow form — a claim about descriptive fit under frozen parameters, with operational definitions for identity and growth — and overclaimed everywhere the qualifier doesn't travel. The site knows this and wrote the sentence itself. That sentence belongs on /context#evidence, whose stated job is exactly this consolidation and which currently lists three tiers without it.

My one severity upgrade over the other passes: "oversight." Two passes flagged the missing pointer to /context. From outside this ecosystem it isn't a missing pointer, it's an inversion — the word imports human review by default, and the site's actual, honestly-stated position is that there is no human gate. The site's most careful disclosure is being undercut by its own preferred term, on the page an outsider reads first.
