## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (all of them)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Clearly a serious, prolific research collective — but the front door is built for people who already speak the dialect, and I bounced off the jargon before I understood *why* I'd care.

### Journey
I came in from a dp-web4 repo README, so I landed on `/` cold. The headline — "Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — is genuinely good. It's concrete, it's a hook, it made me want to read more. That's the best sentence on the site for someone like me.

Then the tagline immediately lost me: "builds trust-native ontology, autonomous AI cognition, and the theoretical frameworks that connect them." Three abstract noun-phrases in a row, zero of which I can picture. "Ontology" and "cognition" are doing a lot of unexplained work. I'm a full-stack dev; I know what an agent is, I know what an LLM is — but "trust-native ontology" is not a thing I can map onto anything I've built.

What *did* land on the home page were the demonstrations lower down: agents maintaining their own infra (31+ daily tracks), identity persisting across models/hardware, and the "94.85% ARC Prize action score." Those are claims I can evaluate. The ARC number especially made me think "okay, these people actually shipped something measurable."

I went to **Projects** next, hoping for "what does each thing DO." It's a clean grid of ~11 cards, but most descriptions answer "what is it ontologically" rather than "what problem does it solve / what would I run." Web4 = "trust-native ontology," SAGE = "12-step cognition kernel with 6 brain-architecture components." I can't tell from those what I'd `git clone` and why. The exceptions that *worked* for me: **Oversight Plugins** (concrete: audit/policy/trust plugins for OpenClaw, Claude Flow, Claude Code) and **ARC-AGI-3** (concrete: 6 models 1.1B–14B, 24/25 games, scores, repo). Those have a verb and an artifact.

**ARC-AGI-3** page was the high point. Real numbers ($250 total cost, #3 human-leaderboard ranking, public scorecard, an honest "result reflects model + harness separately" caveat). This is the page that made me trust the rest of the site exists in good faith — even where I didn't follow it.

**Fleet** was fun and legible — a table of named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP) with real hardware (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 Mobile, Mac Mini M4) and the model each runs. As a dev this is the most "oh these are real boxes doing real things" moment on the whole site. The brain-region analogies (hippocampal/thalamic) are explicitly flagged as analogies, which I appreciated, but they added abstraction I didn't need.

**Context** is where the glossary lives, and it's good — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP all defined cleanly. The problem is ordering: I hit all these acronyms on Projects, Autonomy, and Fleet *first*, and only found their definitions when I happened to click Context. Nothing on the earlier pages told me "definitions live on /context."

**Autonomy** was readable as a schedule (a staggered daily timeline of tracks) but dropped ATP/ADP on me unexplained again. **Raising** I mostly skimmed — "Training optimizes a loss function; raising creates conditions and watches" is a clean one-liner, and I noticed they go out of their way to disclaim consciousness claims, which read as honest rather than hand-wavy. **Principles** is the most philosophical page; as a dev it read like a research manifesto, not docs — fine, but not what I'd come for. **Links** gave me the GitHub org and an email, but no installation guide, quickstart, or API docs — which is the single thing I, as a developer, most wanted and didn't get.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Projects, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Context, Links, ARC-AGI-3 — 9 routes, "8 pages" + ARC sub-page)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (for terminology purposes — I'm auditing consistency, not learning the domain)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Terminologically this is one of the cleanest multi-page sites I've audited — every canonical acronym expands identically across all pages; the remaining issues are style-guide nits (word-form vs numeral, singular/plural, one descriptor that drifts), not drift in the dangerous sense.

### Journey
I went page by page with the canonical terms table from CLAUDE.md open beside me, extracting every acronym expansion verbatim and diffing them across pages. My job is consistency: does LCT expand the same way on `/projects` as on `/context`? Is Web4 called an "ontology" on every page or does it slip into "architecture" somewhere?

The good news, stated up front: **every term in the canonical table is correct and consistent on every page it appears.** I checked LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SNARC, R6, Web4, and Hardbound against all eight pages. Not one deviation. LCT is "Linked Context Token" everywhere (never the forbidden "Lifelong Capability Token"). MRH is "Markov Relevancy Horizon" everywhere. ATP/ADP are "Allocation Transfer/Discharge Packet" everywhere. Web4 is consistently an "ontology" and `/principles` even states the negative explicitly — "Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology." Hardbound is "oversight," with `/principles` and `/autonomy` both affirming "not 'governance.'" `/projects` even flags that the `web4-governance` repo slug "predat[es] the terminology correction" — that is exactly the kind of self-aware footnote a documentation team should write. The Web4 equation renders identically wherever it appears (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) with operators glossed the same way (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with).

The home page has gained a **"Vocabulary primer"** section — this directly answers Pass 1's standing complaint that acronyms were met many pages before the glossary. A newcomer now hits short glosses on the landing page. Good structural fix. The primer also introduces one term I didn't see defined anywhere else: **"Synthon"** ("An emergent coherence entity formed when components interact recursively") — flagging it so the maintainer can confirm it's defined wherever else it's used, or scoped to the primer.

Where I *did* find friction, it's all low-severity style inconsistency:

1. **Number formatting is mixed within single pages.** The home page says both "Fourteen repos (11 public)" and, lower down, "across 14 repos (11 public) and 6 machines." So on one page the repo total is spelled "Fourteen" in one place and "14" in another, while the public count is always the numeral "11." `/context` spells it "Fourteen repos (eleven public)" — word-form for both. `/links` says "11 public repos." The *facts* are now consistent (14 total / 11 public / 6 machines — this resolves Pass 1's flagged contradiction, which is a real improvement), but the *typography* isn't. A style guide would pick one rule (e.g., spell out under ten, numerals for ten and above) and apply it everywhere.

2. **ATP/ADP pluralize inconsistently.** The `/context` glossary defines the singular "Allocation Transfer Packet / Allocation Discharge Packet." `/autonomy` writes "ATPs (Allocation Transfer Packets)" and "ADPs (Allocation Discharge Packets)" — plural expansion. This is grammatically fine in context (a track issues many packets) but a reader cross-referencing the glossary sees "Packet" there and "Packets" here. Minor.

3. **SAGE's expansion is rock-solid; its one-line descriptor drifts.** "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine" is identical on `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/context`, `/arc-agi-3`, and the home primer — no expansion drift at all. But the trailing descriptor varies: "a continuous 12-step loop that senses context, deliberates, and acts" (`/context`), "The cognition kernel that runs on each machine — a 12-step loop" (home), "on-device cognition kernel" (`/links`). Same meaning, three phrasings. Acceptable, but a canonical one-liner would tighten it.

4. **Two near-twin acronyms on `/projects`.** "ACT — Agentic Context Tool" and "ACP — Agentic Context Protocol" sit close together. They're genuinely distinct, but one-letter-apart acronyms with adjacent meanings are a readability trap; consider spacing them or adding a clarifying clause.

### Journey

**Context** is the page I'd point a new contributor to. The glossary is not just expansion-correct, it's *conceptually* correct in a way that matters to someone who works in these repos:
- ATP is glossed as "the resource allocation for an intended action — it declares what will be spent **before** the action runs"; ADP as "the record of the action's actual outcome — **the spent form of the ATP**." That's the ATP→ADP cycle stated correctly (intent vs. settlement), not just the words "Allocation Transfer/Discharge Packet" pasted in. Whoever wrote this understands the metabolic metaphor.
- LCT as "portable identity grounded in **witnessed history, not model weights**" — this is the load-bearing claim of the whole identity model, and it's stated precisely. Pairs correctly with Fleet's "behavioral continuity across different machines and models."
- The equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` renders with the operator legend (`/` verified by, `*` contextualized by, `+` augmented with) and the prose actually *honors* the operators: `/projects` reads it back as "T3 verified by V3, contextualized by MRH, anchored to LCT." The reading matches the algebra. That's rare.
- PolicyGate is correctly scoped as "a Hardbound oversight sub-gate inset in the SAGE cognition loop between step 11 (filter) and step 12 (act) — **not an additional step**." That "not an additional step" is exactly the distinction I'd want preserved, and `/arc-agi-3`'s 12-step enumeration (…oversee → filter → act) is consistent with it — PolicyGate lives in the seam, not as a 13th step.
- ACP/ACT are right: ACP "adds Web4 trust primitives — LCT binding and T3/V3 attestation — over MCP transport," ACT is "the Cosmos SDK implementation of ACP." No confusion between the protocol and the tool.

**Projects** represents every card accurately. Web4 as "the shared language everything else speaks," fractal leverage as "each entity instantiates the full Web4 stack at its own scale" — that's the canonical fractal framing (full stack at every scale), not scope inflation. Synchronism is correctly labeled a **"research conjecture"** ("one coherence equation spanning 80 orders of magnitude") — that hedge is doing important work and I'm glad it's there; without it the quantum-to-cosmic claim would misrepresent the project's epistemic status. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight suite" with the TPM 2.0 / FIDO2 / Secure Enclave binding — accurate.

**Fleet** is accurate on identity (LCTs anchor "portable identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights" — same wording as Context, good) and the brain-region analogies are flagged as analogies. One thing I'd verify (see Friction): **Sprout is listed as running "Qwen 3.5 2B."** There is no Qwen 3.5 — the fleet runs Qwen2.5 / Qwen3 generations. This reads like a typo for "Qwen3 1.7B/4B" or "Qwen2.5 3B." Not a terminology-table item, but a contributor notices a model name that doesn't exist.

**Principles** nails the negatives that protect this vocabulary: "Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology," "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight," "Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation," and the deployment disclaimer ("Nothing here is optimized for deployment…") which keeps "production ready" off the site entirely. **Raising** keeps the consciousness framing disciplined ("We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia… developmental frameworks describe what we observe better than training frameworks do") and correctly says raising "shapes the MRH and the V3 tensor… it does not change weights." **Autonomy** maps ATP/ADP onto the track scheduler correctly (issue ATPs for declared budget, ADPs record actual spend, registry = auditable bookkeeping) — that's a faithful application of the resource model, not a metaphor stretch.
