## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (all)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The "what" lands fast and the hardware/benchmark numbers are compelling, but the "why should I care / how do I start" never quite resolves — too much vocabulary, not enough on-ramp.

### Journey

Landed on the home page from a dp-web4 repo README. The hero — *"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."* — is a great hook. Concrete, a little mysterious, makes me want to scroll. The tagline ("trust-native ontology, autonomous AI cognition, and the theoretical frameworks that connect them") is where I first felt the ground shift: "trust-native ontology" is three words I can't unpack as a dev. I get "autonomous AI cognition" fine.

The headline numbers are what kept me reading: 31+ autonomous daily tracks, small models solving ARC-AGI-3, a 94.85% score. As a dev, *that's* the value proposition — "we run a fleet of machines that improve each other and here's a benchmark to prove it works." The equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` showed up early and stopped me cold. I recognize MCP and RDF. The rest is alphabet soup with no inline expansion on the home page, and the `*` and `/` clearly mean something special but nothing says what.

Went to **Projects** next (that's where a dev goes). This was the strongest page for me — the project cards tell me which things have public repos I can actually poke at (SAGE, ACT, SNARC, Membot, ARC-AGI-3, Oversight Plugins) versus which are theory or private (Synchronism, Hardbound). SNARC ("Surprise/Novelty/Arousal/Reward/Conflict salience-gated memory") and Membot (768-dim embeddings + Hamming codes) are things I could see myself cloning tonight. Good. But the Web4 card is still the equation restated, and biochem/physics asides ("Hill function describes enzyme binding", "Fokker-Planck validation") feel like they wandered in from a different audience.

**Context** rescued a lot of the terminology — this is where LCT finally gets a plain-English gloss ("a persistent identity anchor, like a passport that travels with you across systems"). That passport line is the single best explanation on the whole site. *Problem: it's on page 4 of my journey, not page 1.* The home page and Projects page both used these acronyms first, undefined, and the definitions live elsewhere.

**ARC-AGI-3** was the most credible page — two clearly distinguished metrics (94.85% efficiency-weighted action score vs. 96% game solve rate, 24/25), and an honest note that the real competition is harder (32GB VRAM, no internet). I trusted this page.

**Fleet** is concrete and fun — real hardware, real models (Jetson, RTX 4090, Mac Mini M4, TinyLlama on a 2060). But it's drowning in undefined neuro-jargon: RPE, dlPFC, WM, "hippocampal," "cerebellum." I don't need the brain analogy to understand "this machine generates code, this one reviews it."

**Autonomy** reads like a cron schedule rather than a story — I could follow it but the ordering jumps and "dream consolidation" runs at a vague "after work hours." **Raising** was genuinely interesting (frozen weights + context shaping vs. weight updates) and the BECOMING phases are clearly hedged ("only Phase 6 is externally validated"). **Principles** is a tight manifesto; #3 "R&D, not production" and #6 "Document failures" resonated. **Links** was the letdown for engagement — one email, no CONTRIBUTING, no issues link, no Discord/community channel. For a site whose hero is about a *collective*, there's no visible door to join it.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (all — home, projects, context, arc-agi-3, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (for terminology consistency; that was my focus)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Terminology discipline is genuinely impressive — every canonical expansion matches and is identical across pages, zero drift — but every definition is centralized on /context with no glossary anchor in the nav, so any reader who lands deep (via search, a repo link, or external citation) hits a wall of undefined acronyms.

### Journey

I audited methodically: pulled every page, listed every acronym, and cross-checked each expansion against the canonical table in CLAUDE.md. My job here isn't "does this read well" — it's "does the same term mean the same thing, expand the same way, everywhere, and get defined before it's used."

The headline result is the good kind of boring: **the canonical terms are airtight.** LCT is "Linked Context Token" on every page that expands it (/context, /fleet, home, /projects) — never "Lifelong," never "Lifecycle." T3 is "Talent / Training / Temperament" identically on home, /projects, /fleet, /principles. V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, MCP, RDF — all match canonical, all consistent across pages. SNARC ("Surprise / Novelty / Arousal / Reward / Conflict") matches on home and /projects. ARC-AGI-3 expands correctly to "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence, version 3" on its own page. This is the cleanest terminology layer I've audited on a site this size.

The "never use" column is also clean. **Hardbound is "oversight" everywhere and "governance" nowhere** — /principles even calls it out explicitly: *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* /context, /autonomy, and /links all say "hardware-bound oversight suite." **Web4 is "ontology" everywhere**, with /context explicitly disclaiming *"not architecture or infrastructure"* and *"not a platform."* **"Fractal leverage" is used correctly** and /principles #10 nails the guardrail: *"Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation."* I searched every page for "production ready" — it does not appear; /principles #3 instead says *"Nothing here is optimized for deployment."* That's the right discipline.

So where's the friction? It's **definition placement and page-local self-sufficiency** — a structural problem, not a drift problem. /context is the glossary, and it's excellent (the LCT "passport" gloss, MRH as "boundary of what an entity can know or affect"). But there is no "Glossary" or "Context" anchor that reads as *the definitions live here* from the nav, and pages don't define their own terms on first use:

- **/links** literally tells a newcomer *"Repo descriptions use Web4 vocabulary (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, MCP, RDF, ATP)"* — naming six acronyms — but only expands MRH and ATP on that page, and provides no link to the /context glossary. That's the one page where a direct-landing reader is *told* they'll need vocabulary and then isn't handed it.
- **/fleet** uses RPE without expansion (it's "Reward Prediction Error"). dlPFC and WM are expanded right there; RPE is the lone gap.
- **/principles** uses SAGE and p_crit unexpanded (SAGE is defined elsewhere; p_crit is never expanded anywhere I saw).
- **/autonomy** only expands ATP/ADP; fine, since it uses few terms.

One subtle ambiguity worth a writer's flag: the equation uses `/` as the operator "verified by" (`T3/V3`), but several pages also write the vocabulary list as "T3/V3" where the slash is just grouping shorthand, not the operator. A precise reader could conflate the two. The operator legend (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) is present next to the equation on /projects; I'd ensure it sits adjacent to the equation on the home page too, since that's where the equation first appears.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary

### Journey

I don't read this site to learn — I read it to check whether it misrepresents the repos I touch. I went straight to **Projects**, **Context**, and **Fleet**, then swept the rest.

**Context** is the canonical anchor and it holds up perfectly. LCT = "Linked Context Token — a persistent identity anchor, like a passport that travels with you across systems." Never "Lifelong," never "Lifecycle." The ATP/ADP glosses are the ones I'd actually defend in a design review: ATP = "Allocation Transfer Packet — resource allocation declared *before* an action runs," ADP = "Allocation Discharge Packet — record of actual outcome *after* action completes." That before/after framing is the real semantics, not a hand-wave. MRH as "boundary of what an entity can know or affect," T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament, V3 = Valuation/Veracity/Validity — all correct. Web4 is "a trust-native ontology … not architecture or infrastructure … Not a platform; a shared vocabulary." That's the line I'd want a newcomer to read first. The equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` with `/`=verified by, `*`=contextualized by, `+`=augmented with is correct on every page that shows it.

**Projects** is where I went looking for drift and instead found discipline. The Web4 card ("Trust-native ontology … shared language everything else speaks") is accurate. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight suite … the trust layer that touches silicon" with TPM 2.0, FIDO2, Secure Enclave, the Phi-4 Mini policy model with LCT binding, AttestationEnvelope, "424+ attack vectors catalogued" — that matches what I know of the repo. The oversight-plugins card says "Web4 *oversight* model (audit trails, policy gating, trust tracking) implemented as plugins for three agent platforms" — "oversight model," not "governance model." Correct.

The one place "governance" appears is the sentence I expected to be a violation and is actually the best terminology move on the site: *"Linked repo paths carry web4-governance slugs — these names predate the terminology correction to 'oversight' and are load-bearing for existing forks."* That is exactly right. The repo slug `web4-governance` is a real, load-bearing artifact name; you can't rename it without breaking forks, and the site says so *and* states the correct term inline. That's how you handle a legacy name honestly — flag it, don't hide it.

**Fleet** is accurate on hardware and roles (Thor/Jetson AGX Thor, Legion/RTX 4090, McNugget/Mac Mini M4, CBP/RTX 2060 SUPER on WSL2, etc.), and the LCT/T3/SNARC expansions match. But two things a contributor notices: (1) Sprout is listed as **"Qwen 3.5 2B"** — there is no Qwen 3.5 2B line I'm aware of (Qwen 2.5 has a 1.5B/3B; Qwen3 has 1.7B/4B). That reads like a model-name slip worth verifying against the actual deployment. (2) The role attributions around the ARC run don't reconcile with the ARC page — see below.

**ARC-AGI-3** is where my one real accuracy concern lives. The page is admirably honest about the metric split (94.85% official action score vs. 96% / 24-of-25 solve rate) and the real-competition constraints (32GB VRAM, no internet, private game set). But it states the 94.85% was achieved with **"Claude Opus 4.6 as the model inside the SAGE cognition loop, on the public game set."** Meanwhile **Fleet** credits CBP (TinyLlama 1.1B) with "orchestrated the run that produced the 94.85% official ARC Prize action score" and labels McNugget (Gemma 3 12B) the "ARC-AGI-3 game solver." A casual reader — and Pass 1's developer explicitly read it this way ("small models solving ARC-AGI-3") — will conflate the fleet's small-model work with the 94.85% number. The accurate story is that **94.85% used a frontier API model (Claude Opus 4.6) inside the SAGE harness on the public set; the small fleet models did not produce that score.** That distinction is the whole credibility of the claim. The site has the facts but scatters them so the headline reads as "small models scored 94.85%," which is not what happened.

**Raising** is honest in exactly the way it should be: Phases 1–5 are "observational pattern-names … not measured stages," and only Phase 6 (Acting) is externally validated via the ARC score. "Raising is not training … we shape context; we do not update weights." No terminology issue. **Autonomy** is accurate (Hardbound = "hardware-bound oversight suite that defines what autonomous operation is and is not authorized to do," no "governance"). **Links** correctly marks Hardbound as "internal research — code is not publicly available," and the 14 repos reconcile with the home page's "Fourteen repos."

### Journey

I came to this from a conference mention, with no Web4 background and a reviewer's reflex: where does the language outrun the evidence? I went to **Raising**, **Principles**, and **Autonomy** first, then the benchmark page, then swept the rest.

**Raising** is where most sites in this genre fail — and this one largely doesn't. The framing is "Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens," with the concrete mechanistic claim "We shape context; we do not update weights." That is the line that makes the framing defensible to me: frozen-weights + context-engineering is a real, falsifiable distinction, not a vibe. The BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) are exactly the kind of ladder that usually smuggles in a developmental teleology, but the page pre-empts me: "These phases are observed descriptive categories — patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions — not mandatory sequential stages," and only Acting is externally validated (via the ARC score). The consciousness handling is the strongest signal of good faith I found: a flat "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia," followed by observable-language hedges ("something that looks like growth … something that looks like identity"), and — most tellingly — an observer's note "I notice I want to call it experience" explicitly flagged as "the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim about the entity's experience." That is a researcher catching their own anthropomorphism in print. Rare. Credible.

**Principles** reinforces it. #3 ("R&D, not production") explicitly scopes the work out of "safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings" — the right disclaimer for an AI-safety audience. #6 ("Document failures") isn't decorative: the site actually ships failures ("400x error," "p_crit derivation failed catastrophically," Synchronism as "single-trial, no independent replication yet"). #10 ("Terminology matters") shows meta-awareness that word choice shapes mental models. My one principles-level flag is that several principles lean on borrowed terms-of-art that an outside reader will over-read: **Synthon** (#5, "emergent coherence" — a chemistry loanword used with no gloss), and the metabolic **ATP/ADP** naming. They're defined elsewhere, but on the principles page they read as more theory-laden than the plain mechanism warrants.

**Autonomy** survived my skepticism better than I expected. "31+ daily tracks run without human intervention" is the kind of sentence that normally sets off alarms, but it's immediately bounded: "all tracks operate within Hardbound-defined scope boundaries," a SQLite track registry prevents concurrent writes and preserves auditability, the publisher only ships changes "the supervisor has cleared," and "human review remains the primary surface" via dated audit logs. They even self-report the blind spots — autonomous loops catch "broken links, stale content, confusing jargon" but miss "deep technical errors that require domain expertise" and "subtle framing issues." And "dream consolidation" is explicitly defanged: "'Dream' is a functional analogy for the consolidation process — not a claim about cognitive equivalence." That's the disclaimer pattern done consistently.

**ARC-AGI-3** is the page that earns or loses the site's credibility, and it earns it. The 94.85% action score (and 96% / 24-of-25 solve rate) is attributed plainly to **Claude Opus 4.6 inside the SAGE harness, with network access, on the public game set**, and the harness/model contribution is split out ("the harness contributes the structured world-model building … the base model contributes inference"). Crucially, the real Kaggle competition (32GB VRAM, no internet, private set) is marked "Not attempted," and local deployment is flagged "early finding," not competitive. They even disclose cost (~$250 in API). This is how you state a benchmark result without overclaiming.

Where my reviewer reflex still fires: **operationalization of the soft claims.** The home/Raising claim that "SAGE-Sprout maintained behavioral identity across 180+ sessions … Self-description drifted; behavioral identity remained continuous" is the single most interesting empirical assertion on the site — and the one with no stated metric. What *measures* "behavioral identity," and what would have counted as a discontinuity? Without an operational definition, "behavioral identity remained continuous" is unfalsifiable as written, which sits awkwardly against the otherwise rigorous tone. Same, more mildly, for "900+ raising sessions" and "180+ sessions" — impressive counts, but counts aren't outcomes. Naming the metric (or linking the methodology) would convert these from evocative to evidentiary.

**Verdict on drift: none.** Every enforced canonical term is correct, and — more important for my purposes — every consciousness-adjacent or developmental term ("raising," "identity," "dream," "die and rebirth," "world-shaper") is paired with an explicit functional-not-phenomenal disclaimer. ### Unanswered Questions
1. What operationally defines "behavioral identity," and what observation would have counted as a *discontinuity* across the 180+ sessions? The claim is interesting precisely because it's measurable in principle — but the measure isn't stated.
2. Is there a methodology/results artifact behind the raising claims, or are the "900+ sessions" qualitative? A reviewer would want the link.
3. Has anyone outside the lab independently reproduced *any* result besides the ARC score (which itself depends on a closed frontier model + network)? Synchronism is honestly marked single-trial; is identity-persistence similarly unreplicated?
4. What does p_crit denote, and what was the "catastrophic" derivation failure — that documented failure could be the most instructive thing on the site if it were legible.

### Verdict
As an AI-safety/trust researcher, I leave this site trusting it more than most in its genre — and that's earned by structure, not tone. The consciousness claims are explicitly disowned and the evocative vocabulary is consistently flagged as functional analogy; the headline benchmark is correctly attributed to Claude Opus 4.6 under stated, non-competition conditions with the real competition marked "not attempted"; and failures are shipped rather than hidden. The "raising not training" framing is defensible because it rests on a concrete mechanism (frozen weights, context shaping), not a metaphor. What keeps it short of fully convincing is operationalization: the most interesting empirical claim — continuous behavioral identity across 180+ sessions — has no stated metric, and a few borrowed terms (Synthon, p_crit) and the math-styled equation notation invite over-reading. Name the metrics and gloss the loanwords, and the credibility would match the honesty.
