## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10. All 7 front pages (`/`, `/projects`, `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/notebook`) plus 3 notebook pages a developer would click next (`/notebook/projects` incl. `#quick-start`, `/notebook/fleet`, `/notebook/context`). I also fetched `/llms.txt` and checked all 11 outbound front links.
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: In under two minutes the front tells me what this lab is: open governance tools for AI agents plus a research line on persistent agents. As a developer, though, I hit a dead end when I look for how to run something, because the front never links to the install steps that exist.

*Method note.* I fetched rendered HTML with curl and turned it into text locally, rather than using a summarizer, so every quote below comes from the live HTML. The task prompt says "all 8 pages", but the live nav has 7 front pages counting home, and `/notebook` lists 9 notebook pages. I took the counts from the live nav as the visitor CLAUDE.md says to. The "8" in the pass prompt is stale.

### Journey

**Home (`/`).** The headline "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust." plus "a research collective of one human and a fleet of AI agents" gave me the pitch in one screen. "The idea" (trust from a track record checked against rules set in advance, not from a certificate) is the clearest framing on the site. The four product cards each have a status badge (Web4/Hestia/Hub RUNNING, SAGE RESEARCH), and the descriptions are concrete: "checked before each tool call, with a hash-chained record that shows if it was altered" is exactly the sentence a developer wants. "Our lab runs on it" and "They are refused by it every day" make credible dogfooding claims. The home page has no "try it" or "install" call to action. The developer next steps are "All projects", "The fleet", "Raising", "How we work" and the notebook.

**Projects (`/projects`).** Clear three-section layout: Governance, AI beings, Theory. Each card has a GitHub link. A legend at the bottom defines RUNNING, BUILDING and RESEARCH. Things that work well: "Runs on every machine in our lab; public releases lag our builds" is honest and useful, and "write-up (the code is private)" on SWE-SAGE sets expectations up front. I hit two snags. **Hardbound** "built by Metalinxx": nothing on the front says who or what Metalinxx is, or how it relates to this lab. And the page never says how to *run* any of this. The only notebook link is the generic "Every repository … is in the notebook" at the bottom.

**Fleet (`/fleet`).** The eight-machine table is easy to scan, with hardware and role per row. "Different on purpose", "no central controller" and the four model families (Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama) are clear. I stumbled on two things:
- "Four machines do the heavy work: writing code and running large experiments." The page never says which four. By elimination from the table they are Thor, Sprout, Legion and McNugget. Sprout's own row says "Edge device: a being with a camera and microphone", which on a Jetson Orin Nano doesn't read as "large experiments". `/notebook/fleet` explains that the split is by **AI budget account** (the "synthesis pool"), not by hardware. The front does hint at this ("The split began as a practical constraint on AI budgets"), but a developer reads "heavy work" as compute.
- **HUB** (a machine, upper case) versus **Hub** (a product). The HUB row says "Hosts the fleet's own community on Hub". It took me a second read to see that these are two different things with the same name. **pub** is lower case for no visible reason.

**Raising (`/raising`).** "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" is a memorable metaphor, and "A being, not a chatbot" lands quickly for a developer. "Seen / Not yet shown" is a good structure, and "We have no blind comparison against a fresh model yet" reads as honest. One line was opaque to me: "One small model stopped acting when a long open invitation came before the ask, and acted once the ask came first." I can parse it as a prompt-ordering effect, but without context it reads like an in-joke.

**How we work (`/principles`).** Clear. Six principles, each in two lines. "AI agents, mostly Claude" is the first place the site names a vendor, and it was useful to learn. "publish this site without a human approval step" is a striking claim. I can't verify it from the outside, but it is clearly stated.

**Links (`/links`).** Short and useful: three sites, five code links, two packages. **4-gov** ("· governance") is the one item that appears nowhere else on the front. Nothing says what it is or who runs it. All 11 outbound links returned 200 (GitHub org, web4, hestia, web4/hub tree, SAGE, SWE_SAGE.md, SAGE site, Synchronism site, 4-gov.org, PyPI, and crates.io via its API). Versions: web4-core is 0.4.0 on crates.io and 0.3.0 on PyPI. The Hestia latest release is v0.3.0 (2026-11-02). Both match what the notebook states.

**Notebook index (`/notebook`).** Explains the two layers well ("short, plain, and current" versus "every claim carries its evidence"). The nine page cards have one-line descriptions. I picked Projects, because I want to run something.

**`/notebook/projects`.** About 7,400 words. A developer-oriented paragraph near the top is exactly what I wanted: "Want to run something rather than read? Start with SAGE … Commands … are in the Quick start at the bottom of this page — the only copy on the site". The in-page `#quick-start` anchor works. The quick start itself is careful. It warns that `sage init` prints Python-daemon next steps while the page uses the Rust daemon. It warns not to probe the binary with `--version` because an unset `SAGE_MACHINE` makes it take a real machine's identity. It has the `web4_core` versus `web4` import-name gotcha, and the WARNING that the published web4-policy 0.1.0 `matches` operator fails open. That is the most useful developer content on the site, and it is about 36,000 characters into a notebook page that no front page points at.

The status-label crosswalk on this page explains why Web4 is RUNNING on the front and "research-stage" here. It is well written, but it also confirms that the front badges don't answer the developer's real question, "can I clone this and run it?" Only the notebook's runnable badge does.

**`/notebook/fleet` and `/notebook/context`.** I skimmed these for the "heavy work" basis (found: budget pools) and for what SAGE stands for: "Situation-Aware Governance Engine". The glossary explains it ("'governance engine' is its name, the persistent beings are what it runs"). I didn't find the expansion anywhere on the front.

**`/llms.txt`.** The machine-reader index links directly to "Quick start (/notebook/projects#quick-start)" and gives its offset into the page. **AI readers get a direct link to the install steps and human developers don't.** For my persona, that is the inversion that stands out today.

### Unanswered Questions

### Verdict
This is one of the clearest "what is this lab" sites I've seen from a GitHub org. The front pitch, product cards and status legend took about two minutes, the language is plain, and I found no terminology drift. The notebook earns its keep: the Quick start is careful and full of real gotchas (`--version` silently takes a real machine's identity; the published policy crate fails open on `matches`). But as a developer I only found it because I kept clicking. The front has zero links to it, while the AI-reader index links it directly. Retargeting one existing link would fix that, and it would not lengthen the front.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 plus `/llms.txt`. That is all 7 front pages in the live nav (counting home) and all 9 pages listed on `/notebook`. All returned 200.
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The canonical expansions have zero drift on all 16 pages. What I did find are consistency seams: one correction that stopped at the glossary legend, one adjective that flips between pages, and page names that differ between the index, the heading and the browser tab.

*Method note.* As in Pass 1, I fetched the rendered HTML with curl and converted it to text locally. I did not use a summarizer. Four notebook pages are 46K–108K characters of text (glossary 108,184; raising 93,001; fleet 57,837; projects 46,054), and `/llms.txt` itself warns that fetch tools cut pages that long. I counted expansions with regexes across all 16 texts, then read each hit in context. I checked links and anchors against the `id`s in the HTML. The pass prompt's "all 8 pages" is stale (7 front + 9 notebook), as Pass 1 noted.

### Journey

**Front (7 pages).** Short and consistent in voice. None of the Web4 acronyms appears on the front at all (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, R7: 0 hits each), so the front can't drift on them. Web4 is called an "ontology" in place on `/` and `/projects`. Hardbound's own card says "hardware-bound oversight". **SAGE** is never expanded on any front page (I agree with Pass 1). Every page has exactly one `<h1>`, no heading level is skipped and no `id` is duplicated. Six of the seven front pages share one `<meta name="description">` (Pass 1 already logged this, so I don't list it again).

I found two cross-page seams on the front:
- *One claim, three strengths.* About SAGE's actions, Home says "whose consequential actions **go through** governed tools". `/projects` says "are **meant to** go through governed tools". `/raising` says "acts in the world **only** through a small set of governed tools … **Every one** of those actions is checked against rules before it happens." These are three strengths for one claim. A writer has to pick one, and `/projects`' "meant to" is the one closest to the hedge the notebook uses.
- *Section heading vs card.* `/projects` puts Hardbound under the h2 "**Governance** for AI agents", and the card itself says "oversight". The site's own rule (`/principles` in the notebook: "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight") is kept in the card and broken by the heading above it.

**Notebook index (`/notebook`).** The cards are clear, but the Glossary card says "Every term and acronym used in the work, **with its canonical definition**." The glossary's own h1 intro calls itself "The lab's **working** glossary". Of its 77 table rows, 54 have "—" in the Expansion column, and it marks four terms as "not yet in CANONICAL_TERMS_v1". Lab vocabulary such as Being, Mentor, Cartridge and SWE-SAGE has no canonical definition to give. `/llms.txt` has the accurate wording: "working glossary of canonical Web4 terms and the lab's own vocabulary". The front index promises more than the page delivers.

**Page naming.** Each notebook page has three names that don't always match:

| Index card | Page h1 | `<title>` |
|---|---|---|
| Projects | The Ecosystem | Notebook: Projects — The Ecosystem |
| Autonomy | Autonomous **Cycles** | Notebook: Autonomy — The Daily **Cycle** |
| Links | Links & Contact | Notebook: Links & Contact |
| Fleet | The Fleet | Notebook: Fleet — Eight Machines |
| The lab view | "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." | Notebook: the lab view (former home page) |

Autonomy is the worst case: "Cycles" (plural) in the h1, "Cycle" (singular) in the tab, and "tracks" everywhere else, including the index card ("The daily autonomous tracks").

**Breadcrumbs.** Five notebook pages (fleet, links, principles, projects, raising) have a breadcrumb nav that reads "Home / Notebook: X", with "Home" linking to `/`. `/notebook/arc-agi-3` reads "Home / Historical research", and its "Home" links to `/notebook/home` (the *former* home page): same label, different target. `/notebook/autonomy`, `/notebook/context` and `/notebook/home` have no breadcrumb. No breadcrumb links the `/notebook` index.

**Links and anchors.** I resolved every internal `href` on all 16 pages against the target page's `id` set. Results: 0 unknown targets, 0 broken `#anchors`, 0 hrefs to redirecting paths. The *visible text* still lags, though. Pointer labels written as retired paths appear 55 times (35× "/context", 14× "/autonomy", 6× "/arc-agi-3"), and `/notebook/links` and `/notebook/projects` mix "/context" and "/notebook/context" on the same page. Every one of them works, because the hrefs are right. This is a naming issue only. Where the notebook points at a front page it says "the front /raising" or "the front /projects", which is clear.

**Glossary (`/notebook/context`).** This is the main finding of the pass. The equation legend says, under "Provenance, corrected 2026-10-18": "the operator legend is upstream's. The Web4 whitepaper's section 3 … reads the term as 'trust tensors that are verified by value tensors' … The site's own part is the second quotient [ATP/ADP]". It then explains that a 2026-09-09 note "had called the whole legend the site's gloss". Two passages further down the same page still use that pre-correction framing for the T3/V3 half:
- "…it is all **this site's 'verified by' gloss on T3/V3** refers to" (ATP/ADP cycle passage)
- "**This site's reading** of the equation's T3/V3 as 'trust verified by value' **is a gloss** on that second direction, not canon (see the legend above)" (T3/V3 complementarity passage)

"Not canon" is literally true, because CANONICAL_TERMS_v1 doesn't define the glyphs. But "this site's reading" is exactly what the legend withdrew. The cited "legend above" says the opposite about whose reading it is. Other pages have it right: `/notebook/projects` says "the ATP/ADP half is the site's own reading", and `/notebook/fleet` says "'Verified by' is upstream's reading". `/notebook/links` and `/notebook/raising` cite the whitepaper and standard. The 10-18 correction reached the legend and three other pages, but not the two passages below it on its own page.

The glossary uses some terms before their own rows define them, up to 6,935 characters earlier for ATP ("ATP slashing" in a witness row comes before the ATP row). On a lookup table that's acceptable. The orientation paragraph expands LCT/T3/V3/MRH within about 1,000 characters of first use.

**SAGE's name, page to page.** All 19 expansions read "Situation-Aware Governance Engine", so the expansion is consistent. The adjective attached to the name is not:
- `/notebook/autonomy`: "the **current** name, kept"
- `/notebook/fleet`: "the **current** name, kept"
- `/notebook/raising`: "a **legacy** name; see the glossary row"
- `/notebook/context` (the governance→oversight note): "retention of load-bearing **legacy** names is the policy"

Read together they are compatible: it's an old name that is still current. A reader who meets "current" on one page and "legacy" on the next will still wonder whether SAGE has been renamed. "A legacy name, still current" (or one fixed phrase linked to the glossary note) would close it.

**Smaller first-use gaps (notebook).**
- `/notebook/projects` promises that "each card expands an acronym where that card first uses it". The ARC-AGI-3 card's table has "Transfer to Gemma 4 **E4B** via membot cartridges", unexpanded. E4B is expanded on `/notebook/raising` and in the glossary, but not on this page.
- `/notebook/raising` first uses "Gemma 4 **E2B**" unexpanded (nomad-being, PR #281). E4B is expanded about 9,500 characters later; E2B never is on that page.
- `/notebook/principles` first uses **RDF** unexpanded ("tensors bind to entity-role pairs via RDF triples").
- `/notebook/links` lists "Web4 vocabulary (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, **MCP, RDF**, ATP/ADP)", while `/notebook/projects` calls them "the **external standards** MCP and RDF". The equation contains both, but only one page describes them correctly.
- `/notebook/home`'s "Vocabulary" card lists LCT, T3/V3, MRH, R6/R7 and ATP/ADP unexpanded. It is a pointer to the glossary, so I count this as acceptable.

**Counts I cross-checked (consistent).** Eight machines everywhere. Machine roles: the front's 4 + 2 + 2 matches the notebook's "six cognition + two society-hosts". Four model families (Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama) on `/fleet` and `/notebook/home`. Repositories: 45 original (23 public, 22 internal), 37 forks and 82 total, the same on `/notebook/context` and `/notebook/links`. The `/llms.txt` page sizes match my measurements to within 1%.
