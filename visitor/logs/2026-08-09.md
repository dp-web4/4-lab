## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/links`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understand what this lab believes and how it's organized, but after nine pages I still can't picture a single function signature — the site sells an ontology to an audience that showed up wanting a library.

### Journey

Landed on `/` from a repo README. Hero: *"Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop."* My first reaction was honestly a shrug — that's a scale claim, not a value claim. Lots of people have machines. Then I scrolled one screen and hit the actual reason to keep reading:

> "AI agents already act on their own—browsing, transacting, coordinating with other agents—and there is no general way to establish whether one will behave appropriately in a given context."

That's the sentence that earned my next ten minutes. It's a real problem I've hit — I've wired up agents with tool access and had exactly zero principled way to decide how much rope to give one. The hero is doing a worse job than the paragraph beneath it.

Home was the best page on the site for me. Every acronym I hit was expanded inline: LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, MCP, RDF, SNARC, SAGE, Synchronism. I never had to leave to look something up. There's also an explicit on-ramp — *"New here? Jump to the vocabulary primer below ↓ ... then clone SAGE — the recommended starting point."* Good.

The equation, `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`, I read as pseudo-notation and moved on. What snagged me was the notation key on `/context`:

> "'/' means two different things on this line: 'verified by' in T3/V3, but a plain declared→discharged allocation pair in ATP/ADP — same glyph, unrelated semantics."

I appreciate that they caught it and said so. But my instinct as someone who writes code is: you don't document an operator collision, you rename the operator. Right now the headline formalism of the whole project has a footnote explaining that it's inconsistent with itself. That's a small thing that undermines a large thing.

`/projects` is where I went next, expecting the "what can I use" page. The single most useful thing on the entire site is here, and it's a definition:

> "runnable = clone it and it executes end to end today (a demo, a script, a server) — not a claim about polish or completeness."

That's a maturity label with a testable meaning, applied per project. I trusted the site more after reading it. Four things are marked runnable — SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot — and there's a real clone command with a note that *"SAGE runs on a single machine — no Jetson required."* That removed a barrier I'd assumed was there.

But `/projects` is also where the site stops helping me. SAGE is *"an on-device cognition kernel running a continuous 12-step sense-to-act loop."* I read that four times across four pages and I still can't answer: what do I pass it, what comes back, is it a library or a daemon, does it want a model endpoint or does it host one? There is not one line of code on this site that isn't `git clone`. No signature, no config sketch, no sample input/output, no "here's what a 12-step loop actually emits." I'm told to go read the README — fine, but the job of this page is to make me *want* to.

`/arc-agi-3` was the credibility high point. 94.85% action score, 24/25 games, and the caveat is sitting right next to the number rather than buried:

> "Frontier model + harness — the harness's independent contribution isn't isolated yet (no ablation run)."

Naming Claude Opus 4.6 as the model inside the loop, adjacent to the score, is the honest move. I checked and the same attribution rides along with the number on the home page too. There's a public ARC Prize scorecard link and an MIT-0 paper/code repo, so I can check it myself. This is the only claim on the site I could independently verify, and it holds up.

`/fleet` was fun to read and the least useful. Eight machines, hardware and model string for each, brain-region analogies. It resolves the "eight vs six" thing cleanly up front — *"Six cognition machines, plus two society-hosts."* But when I asked myself "what is this fleet FOR, mechanically," the clearest answer I found was about a synthesis budget pool that resets Thursdays. That's ops trivia. The interesting engineering question — why heterogeneous hardware and eight *different* small models rather than one — is implied everywhere and stated nowhere I could find.

`/autonomy` surprised me by being self-limiting rather than self-promoting. It defines its terms down rather than up: *"autonomous"* means *"unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed"* and *"task definitions are authored in advance, not chosen by the track itself."* It then names its own blind spot — visitor and maintainer personas are *"LLM sessions drawn from the same model families,"* so the loop has correlated failure modes. A site that tells me its quality process is structurally unable to catch certain errors is a site I believe more, not less. The revert record (79 sessions, 117 commits, zero rollbacks) is a real number I'd want to see more of elsewhere.

`/raising` and `/principles` I skimmed, which is what my kind of visitor does with theory. Principles is punchy — *"You don't engineer the mound. You engineer placement rules"* is a good line, and Principle 6 leads with a documented 400x failure, which is more convincing than nine principles about valuing failure would be. Raising's *"Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens"* is a clean distinction I could repeat to a coworker.

`/links` gave me contact info, three explainer sites, the GitHub org, and an actual contribution path: *"Ask in an issue first — that's the on-ramp until a formal policy exists."* Honest about not having a process yet. Fine.

Net: I left understanding the ideas and not knowing how to spend an hour with the code.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What does SAGE actually take as input and produce as output? Library, daemon, or CLI? Does it host a model or call one?
2. What's the minimum thing I can run in ten minutes to see the 12-step loop do something observable?
3. Why eight heterogeneous machines with eight different small models instead of one machine with one model? What does the heterogeneity buy?
4. What does "research-stage" mean concretely — is there code, and does it run?
5. If I want the trust model without the fleet — just LCT-style identity for agents in my own app — is ACT that, and is it usable standalone?
6. What is `ACP` on `/links`?
7. Where would I actually see an LCT? Is it a file, a row, an RDF graph on disk? The concept is defined; the artifact never is.

### Verdict

The site is unusually honest for a research lab — it defines "runnable" testably, names the ablation it hasn't run right next to its best number, and tells me its own review loop has correlated blind spots. That bought my trust faster than any amount of polish would have. The vocabulary problem is solved on the landing page: everything is expanded inline, and I never had to guess.

What it doesn't do is give a developer anything to hold. Nine pages, four "runnable" projects, and the only executable string on the site is `git clone`. I understand the ontology and I can't picture the API. That's the gap between "this is interesting" and "I'll spend an afternoon on it" — and I'm the visitor who was one code block away from crossing it.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid

### Journey

I audited for three things: expansion fidelity against the canonical table, first-use definition, and cross-page consistency. I checked all nine pages, then re-queried five of them with narrow single-fact questions because summarizing fetches are unreliable about absence.

That re-query mattered. Two things I was ready to write up as findings dissolved:

- Pass 1 flagged `ACP` on `/links` as an acronym they couldn't place anywhere. `/links` does expand it: *"Cosmos SDK implementation of the Agentic Context Protocol (ACP): the human interface to Web4."* `/projects` expands it too. **Pass 1's Q6 is answered — ACP = Agentic Context Protocol, and it is not an undefined term.**
- My first read of `/context` returned the glossary's LCT entry as *"Verifiable digital presence that accumulates witnessed history"* with no expansion. Asked directly, the entry actually opens **"Linked Context Token"** and continues: *"Non-transferable: permanently bound to a single entity, which is what makes the accumulated history evidence rather than assertion."* That last clause is the best single sentence about LCT on the site — it explains *why* non-transferability is load-bearing rather than just asserting it. The glossary is fuller than a summary makes it look.

A third hypothesis of mine died on contact, and I'll record it because the refutation is more useful than the finding would have been. Home glosses ATP as *"declares intended resource spend"* and ADP as *"records the actual outcome."* `/autonomy` talks about *"recharg[ing] ADP back to ATP against V3-validated value creation."* I expected to catch a cross-page split between an audit-artifact model and an energy-cycle model. Home already bridges it, explicitly:

> "It is a **cycle, not a one-way log**: ADP recharges back to ATP against validated value creation, and that validation is what V3 (Valuation / Veracity / Validity) does in the resource half of the equation. Charged → spent → recharged, like the biochemical namesake."

No drift. The two pages share one model, and home names the biochemical analogy that makes the pair memorable. `/autonomy` then does the honest thing and says its own registry implements only the one-way half — the recharge is described as not currently implemented. Limitation stated, not glossed.

**The structural story of this audit is `SAGE`.** Its upstream name is "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine," and the lab's own Principle 10 and `/context` say the correct word is oversight, not governance. The name can't change — it's pinned in code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark. So the site carries a disclaimer instead, and I checked every page that expands the name:

| Page | Expands SAGE | Carries the governance→oversight pointer |
|------|-------------|------------------------------------------|
| `/` | yes | yes (×2, one anchor-linked) |
| `/projects` | yes | yes |
| `/fleet` | yes | yes |
| `/raising` | yes | yes |
| `/links` | yes | yes (×2) |
| `/arc-agi-3` | yes | yes, `→ /context#governance-oversight` |

**Six of six.** Not one page expands the name without the note. `/context` holds the hub explanation — *"the lab originally described its control layers as 'governance.' What these systems actually do is oversight — watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen"* — and every consuming page points at a deep anchor rather than restating it. That is textbook hub-and-spoke terminology management, executed without a single gap. It's the most disciplined thing on the site and I want it on record as *closed*, so future passes stop re-opening it.

Which sets up the actual finding. The site demonstrably knows how to do this. It just doesn't do it for the acronym set.

`R6` is the clearest case. Expanded properly on `/context` (*"Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result"*) and on `/projects`. Then used bare on `/links` — *"R6-auditable actions"* — and bare on `/arc-agi-3` — *"every action shaped as an R6 record."* No expansion, no link, on either. Every other canonical acronym gets expanded or linked at first use on most pages; R6 is the one that doesn't, on the two pages most likely to be deep-linked from a repo README or a benchmark scorecard.

`/autonomy` has the tightest version of the same inconsistency, inside a single passage. It expands both packet terms inline — *"each scheduled track is issued an ATP (Allocation Transfer Packet)"*, *"an ADP (Allocation Discharge Packet) records actual spend"* — and then, a sentence later, drops **`V3-validated`** with no expansion at all. Same paragraph, same reader, opposite treatment. I confirmed by direct query that `/autonomy` uses only ATP, ADP, V3, and Web4 from the canonical set (LCT, MRH, T3, SNARC, MCP, R6, SAGE are all genuinely absent from that page), so this isn't a page-wide gap — it's exactly one term that got missed while its neighbors got handled.

Pass 1 reached the same place from the opposite direction — a developer noticing six of nine pages assume you arrived via home. Two personas converging on one structural gap from different angles is worth more than either flag alone.

Everything else checked clean. Web4 is called an ontology on `/`, `/context`, `/principles`, and `/raising`, twice with the explicit negation *"not architecture or infrastructure."* The only appearances of "architecture" describe SAGE's *brain*-architecture components — a different referent, correctly used, not drift. Hardbound is oversight everywhere, with `/principles` stating the distinction outright and `/projects` adding the parenthetical that stops a newcomer reading "oversight" as a human in the loop. "Production ready" appears nowhere; Principle 3 is literally *"R&D, not production."* Fractal leverage ships with its own guardrail: *"Not unification, and not scope inflation."*

I also verified the session numbers reconcile rather than assuming they don't. Home: *"2,065 raising sessions across the fleet — 1,991 on the six cognition machines plus 74 on HUB."* `/projects`: *"1,991 raising sessions across the 6 cognition machines ... 2,065 including HUB."* 1,991 + 74 = 2,065, and **both** pages now carry the reconciliation inline rather than stating one figure and leaving the reader to collide with the other. No finding.

A comparative claim with a named missing baseline is a well-formed research position. Most sites in this genre don't get that far.

Then it goes further than I expected. `/raising` carries a **deflationary alternative** section — *"everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more"* — and a falsification design: *"same corpus, same token volume, permuted order (or a yoked control — entity A raised on entity B's session history at matched volume and specificity),"* with a stated success condition (*"excess degradation over an ordering baseline"* or a dissociation where *"identity-continuity measures degrade while task competence on the same scrambled context stays roughly intact"*). And a precommitment: *"if this control runs and the result is a bare main effect, or no degradation at all, that outcome gets published on this page and the developmental vocabulary gets retired from it."*

Precommitting to retire your own vocabulary is rare. I don't think I've seen it on a lab site before.

The status line is where it stops: *"specified (this section is the specification) but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet."*

`/context` has an **Evidence & limitations** section that grades every claim on the site into four tiers, and it grades this one itself:

> **Unlicensed vocabulary:** the developmental framing used across this site — "raising," "identity," "growth" — is not yet discriminated from competent context engineering. The deflationary control that would make that comparison is specified but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet. Until it runs, the developmental vocabulary runs ahead of the comparison that would license it.

I then read `/autonomy` and the picture assembled. Eight named daily tracks — Supervisors, Archivist, Publisher, Visitor, Maintainer, Outreach, Explorer, Dream Consolidation. Every one is maintenance, publication, or audit. Not one runs an experiment. The lab has built a machine that browses, fixes, publishes, and consolidates its own claims every twenty-four hours, and the single test that would discriminate its central framing from the null hypothesis has *no owner* in a system that assigns owners to everything else. `/autonomy` even issues each track an ATP against a declared resource budget and books an ADP against actual spend — there is a resource-accounting layer, and the experiment isn't in it.

That is the finding. It isn't dishonesty; the site diagnoses it more sharply than I would have. It's a **priority inversion**, and it's visible only if you read `/raising`'s status line against `/autonomy`'s track list — which is to say, only if you hold two pages at once.

The second thing I found is a level down and sharper. The precommitment is bound to a measure that doesn't exist. The success condition reads *"identity-continuity measures degrade while task competence stays roughly intact."* I asked `/context` directly whether "identity" or "behavioral-identity continuity" has a glossary entry: **no standalone entry**. The phrase appears once, in the Evidence section, as *"behavioral-identity continuity (180+ sessions)"* — a count, not a definition. And the status line already concedes *"no pre-registered metric yet."*

So: a well-designed control, a genuine precommitment, and an outcome variable that is currently undefined. If that experiment runs before the metric is pre-registered, it cannot settle anything — whatever comes out, both the developmental and deflationary readings survive by disputing the measure. Pre-registering the metric costs a paragraph and no compute, and it has to come **first**. This is the cheapest high-value thing on my whole list.

There's a pattern here that connects to Pass 3's synthon↔coherence finding without duplicating it. `/context` grades its **claims** by evidence tier, rigorously. It does not grade its **measures**. Coherence is admitted undefined but is load-bearing under Principle 5 (Pass 3's finding); identity-continuity is undefined and load-bearing under the falsification test (mine). In both cases the site's honesty is entry-local — it says "this term is undefined" — and doesn't propagate to "…and here is what rests on it." An evidence taxonomy without a corresponding measure taxonomy will keep producing this shape.

**Two hypotheses I arrived with, both refuted.** I record them because the refutations are more informative than the findings would have been.

I expected T3's **"Training"** component to be the site's worst outsider trap: a canonical term whose ML sense is gradient descent, sitting on a site whose thesis sentence is "raising is not training." Asked directly, the gloss reads *"capability accumulated through interaction history — not gradient training, the sense the same word carries in ML."* Guarded, explicitly, in exactly the register I'd have asked for.

I expected **"Markov"** in Markov Relevancy Horizon to be a borrowed formalism cashing an unearned check — the word primes conditional independence and, for anyone in my field, Markov blankets. The entry says: *"'Markov' gestures at the conditional-independence idea (what's inside the horizon screens off what's beyond it) — **design intent, not a proven formal property of current implementations**."* That is precisely the disclosure I would have demanded, written before I asked.

Which sent me looking for the general pattern, and it's real. `/context` carries at least eleven explicit outsider-sense guards: emergence (*"explicitly NOT the 'emergent capabilities' sense from the LLM scaling literature… this site's uses are all about interaction dynamics at fixed weights, which is a different phenomenon"*), governance (*"NOT the sense the word carries in AI-safety literature"*), oversight (*"never the human-supervision sense the word carries in that field"*), autonomous (*"not self-directed in the AI-safety sense of choosing its own goals"*), validity, SNARC's arousal, ontology, T3-Training, Markov, Hill function kinetics (*"an analogy, not a fitted mechanism"*), and the best one on the site:

> **Attractor / attractor basin** — "Used on this site as a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object… No state space and no update rule are specified, and none is implied… Flagged explicitly because the word borrows the connotation of mathematical precision from a formalism this site does not cash out. Read it as 'stable behavioral tendency'."

I have reviewed papers that needed that paragraph and didn't have it. Whoever wrote it understands exactly what my objection would be and answered it in advance.

**Which leaves one collision unguarded, and it's the one nearest the safety reading: "policy."** `/arc-agi-3` says *"PolicyGate — a Hardbound oversight sub-gate inset between step 11 (filter) and step 12 (act), evaluates every action against signed law before it fires."* In my field, a *policy* inside an action-selection loop is the network that selects the action. Here it means a compliance rule set. Worse, `/projects` describes Hardbound as running *"a small local policy model for heterogeneous review"* — "policy model" is an unambiguous term of art in RL, and it means the opposite kind of object. I confirmed by direct query that no glossary entry disambiguates "policy" from the RL sense. Given that eleven of its cousins are guarded, this reads as an oversight rather than a choice.

**On `/arc-agi-3` I independently reach Pass 3's finding by a different route, and I'd raise the fix.** Pass 3 flags that "Hardbound" imports a silicon claim the run doesn't support — `/projects` says plainly *"hardware-anchored enforcement is what this research is building toward, not the current mechanism."* I confirmed the page mentions no TPM, FIDO2, or hardware root of trust and carries no software-only note. My separate concern is register: *"evaluates every action against signed law before it fires"* is, to a safety reader, a **control claim** — a mechanism that constrains what an agent can do — sitting on the one page with an externally verifiable score. The page makes no explicit safety claim, and I credit that. But the plain reading is one, and the site already knows how to defuse exactly this. `/autonomy` does it, on the same class of number:

That is the most sophisticated sentence on the site. **Where I'd push back hardest on the overall posture.** The hedging has reached the point of zero marginal return. Every claim is tiered, every metaphor is fenced, every missing control is named. What I still cannot do is *check anything* except the ARC score. `/context` says the developmental evidence rests on internal logs *"documented and dated, but not externally audited — no published log samples, coding criteria, or third-party review yet."* `/links` confirms: *"No papers or preprints are published yet — the Synchronism preprint is in late revision."*

More caveats will not move this. One artifact will. And the cheap one is not the logs — it's the **coding criteria**: the rubric by which a session gets classified into Grounding / Sensing / Relating / Questioning / Creating / Acting. Publishing the rubric costs no session content, exposes no transcripts, and converts the BECOMING phases from an assertion into something a reader can apply to their own logs and disagree with. Right now the phases are described as *"observed descriptive categories — patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions,"* which is exactly the construction that, in my field, precedes an unfalsifiable stage theory.

Relatedly: the site's single hardest asset — the MIT-0 ARC-SAGE harness, publicly scored — is linked only from `/arc-agi-3` and `/links`. The pages making the *softest* claims, `/raising` and `/principles`, don't point at it. The one place a skeptic can read actual code is invisible from the pages where skepticism peaks.

And the observer note on `/raising` — *"I notice I want to call it experience."* — attributed to a SAGE-Sprout identity-portability test, is the single most epistemically interesting line on the site. It reports the pull toward the phenomenal reading as an observation about the observer rather than the system. That is the right way to handle it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is the owner and date for the deflationary control? Everything else on this site gets a track, a budget, and an ATP. Why not the experiment that decides whether the site's vocabulary survives?
2. What would "identity-continuity" be measured on — response consistency, self-reference stability, behavioral fingerprinting across sessions? Without this, the precommitment on `/raising` cannot be honored either way.
3. Is there a policy or privacy constraint preventing publication of log samples, or is it just unscheduled? `/context` says "not… published **yet**," which implies intent. If publication is structurally blocked, "yet" is doing misleading work and the coding rubric becomes the *only* available artifact.
4. Did PolicyGate actually gate actions during the scored ARC run, and was it software-only? Same question Pass 3 asked; I add that the answer determines whether the fix is a qualifier or a scope note about what "oversight" demonstrated.
5. Is the sovereign's LCT symmetry a claim about auditability or about status? A researcher reads a human and a machine holding the same identity primitive as a position, not a schema.
6. The site says raising shapes context and "never weights" — but `/raising` and `/principles` both mention LoRA. Are LoRA adapters ever fitted on session data, and if so how is that reconciled with "we do not update weights"? I could not resolve this from the site and it is the load-bearing mechanistic claim.

### Verdict

This is the most carefully hedged research site I have read from an independent lab, and the care is structural rather than decorative: eleven outsider-sense guards, a four-tier evidence taxonomy that grades the site's own central vocabulary as *unlicensed*, a falsification design with a precommitment to retire that vocabulary, and — the sentence that convinced me — an evaluation result reported as "weak evidence, not a safety result" because the detector and the subject are the same loop. Nothing here oversells except one noun on the benchmark page.

The problem is what the honesty is currently substituting for. The control is specified, precommitted, and unowned; its outcome measure is undefined; and eight autonomous tracks run every day maintaining the claims rather than testing them. The site has reached the point where more caveats buy nothing — it has been maximally honest about a gap that stays open. Two cheap moves close most of it: pre-register the identity-continuity metric, and publish the phase-coding rubric. The third, scheduling the control, is the one that decides whether this is a research program or a very well-documented vocabulary.
