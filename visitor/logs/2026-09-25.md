## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home + all 8 nav pages)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The pitch in the first two sentences is clear and good. After that the site is written for its own auditors more than for me, and the one thing I can run, SAGE, sits at the bottom of a 3,600-word page.

### Journey
*Method: WebFetch for the first read of every page. Because WebFetch summaries have made things up before, I then checked each friction item below against the live HTML with curl. Two summary claims turned out false: the glossary has 58 rows, not "100+", and it is not alphabetical.*

**Home.** The headline works: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." The thesis works too: "trust should be computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying." Within ten seconds I knew what problem the lab cares about. Then the stats block lost me. "21 SAGE instances configured… 8 of them carry raising history… Deployed is not the same as running… how instance, line and session record nest is on /context." I don't yet know what SAGE really is or what "raising" means, and I'm already being sent to three other pages to decode a count. The four project blurbs (Web4, Hestia, Hub, SAGE) are the useful part. I also noticed "SAGE (Situation-Aware Governance Engine)", yet the blurb calls it "persistent-agent research" and never mentions governance. Only /autonomy says the name is "a legacy name." The Hestia blurb brings up "A1 on Hestia's own A0–A4 ladder" and then says it is "Not TCSEC's class A1". I never had the TCSEC confusion, and now I have an extra acronym. The Hestia line also says it "has no install guide for outside users yet; see the Quick start". It sends me to a section that tells me the same thing. That's honest, but it's a dead end.

**Projects.** This is the page I actually wanted. The card list gives name, one-liner, status badge and links, which is scannable and good. The "public, runnable" vs "research-stage" vs "private" badges are exactly what a developer needs. The Web4 card is the first one I read, though, and its first sentence runs about 120 words. It expands T3, V3, MRH, LCT, ATP and ADP inline, and it breaks off mid-sentence to explain that the slash "is not a claim that T3 and V3 verify each other." I couldn't follow it. I gave up and scrolled to the cards with repos. Near the top, the page says clearly that the Quick start is "the only copy on the site". That's a good design choice, but the Quick start comes after every card and a Synchronism/fractal section. I'd move it up, or give the intro sentence a jump link. The SAGE Quick start itself is concrete: `ollama pull`, `python3 -m sage.instances.init`, `cargo build`, dashboard on :8760. It lists requirements, and it is candid that "The SAGE README has no setup section". That is the best developer content on the site.

**Fleet.** I learned there are eight machines: Jetsons, a Mac Mini, gaming laptops and two society hosts. Each has a brain-region role label. The hardware table is fun. The rest of the page (~6,800 words) is session-count reconciliation: "2,499 is the six cognition machines, 2,620 is that plus HUB's 121, and 2,846 is all eight". It also covers five line states ("instrumentation broken", "publishing stopped by policy") and directories kept for rollback. It's clearly careful work, but for me it reads like an internal audit log. I couldn't tell what I'm supposed to take away beyond "diverse hardware".

**Autonomy.** This one clicked. The timeline shows supervisors at 03:00, archivist, publisher, visitor at 05:30, maintainer at 06:30, outreach and explorer. The mechanism is stated plainly: tracks are "Claude Code sessions that cron launches", with a SQLite track registry. "Unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed" is a useful distinction. The "Operational boundaries" section helped me trust the rest (process-level scope, detect-and-revert, no pre-approval gate). This is the most developer-legible page after /projects.

**Raising.** The six BECOMING patterns (Grounding → Acting) are easy to scan. At ~6,000 words, almost every claim comes with a hedge, a confound or a "not counted". I respect that, but I came away unsure what was actually observed. The page says itself that "the one measurement we have run leans the other way." So I can't tell why this is a headline section.

**Principles.** Ten short principles, and the most readable prose on the site. "R&D, not production" and "Document failures" tell me what kind of shop this is. The license note is useful and specific: AGPL-3.0 for the main repos, MIT for snarc/membot, Apache-2.0 for ACT, MIT-0 for ARC-SAGE. For me that's decision-relevant: AGPL means I think twice before embedding.

**Glossary (/context).** The nav label says "Glossary" but the URL is /context. The at-a-glance table has 58 rows, and they're in narrative order rather than alphabetical: Web4, Ontology, Trust-native, Trust, **hestia**, **Hestia**, A0–A4, … I only found LCT and MRH by scrolling or using ⌘K. There are two separate rows for "hestia" (lowercase, a trust component) and "Hestia" (the daemon). The lowercase one points to the "public hestia repo", which, going by the repo URL, is where the capitalized daemon lives. That is a trap for a developer reading code. The page runs ~11,800 words, the longest on the site. It works as a reference, but it's heavy for a first read.

**Links.** A clean directory of explainer sites and repos, with a contact email (dp@metalinxx.io). The line "New here? Repo descriptions use Web4 vocabulary… See the /context glossary" is a nice touch.

**ARC-AGI-3.** Short and clear, with the best numbers-to-caveats ratio on the site. It gives 94.85% action score, 23/25 environments and 175/183 levels. It says up front that the harness read the games' public engine source and that the result is "not current competition positioning". I trust this page most because it tells me what it isn't.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home + all 8 nav links; the nav is the source of record)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every Web4 acronym expansion on every page matches canon. The remaining problems are structural: one distinction the site carries by capitalization alone, which the site itself breaks once; one meta description shared by all nine pages; and heading and anchor gaps on the long pages.

### Journey
*Method: I did a WebFetch first read of Home (nav plus section and acronym inventory). Then I curled all nine pages and ran the audit on the rendered text: expansion counts per page, first-use distance to expansion, the glossary table's row list, heading levels, `id` anchors, and every in-site `href#fragment` against the target page's ids. WebFetch summaries have made things up before, so every claim below was checked against the live HTML.*

**Navigation.** The nav has eight links (Projects, Glossary, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links) plus Home, so nine pages. All nine return 200. Every in-site fragment link resolves: I checked every `href="/x#y"` against the ids on the target page and found no broken anchors. The label/URL mismatch "Glossary → /context" is the only naming seam, and the page's `<title>` and h1 both say "Glossary", so a reader never sees "context" except in the URL.

**Canonical expansions.** I counted canonical expansion strings on every page. "Linked Context Token" appears on 7 pages, "Trust Tensor"/"Value Tensor" on 7, "Markov Relevancy Horizon" on 6, "Allocation Transfer/Discharge Packet" on 5, "Six-Element" and "R6 + Reputation" on 3–4. Drift strings (Lifelong, Lifecycle-Continuous, Trust-Tracing, Attention Token, Minimum Response, production ready) returned zero hits. The two "lifecycle" hits on /projects are ordinary English about 4-Life's lifecycle vocabulary, not an LCT expansion. T3 and V3 are expanded as the tensor names, and the dimensions are given as contents; the glossary row states the distinction explicitly. R7 is correctly "R6 + Reputation", a superset, not a misnaming.

**Governance/oversight.** "governance" appears 47 times across nine pages. I read every instance outside the SAGE proper name. None calls Hardbound governance. The rest cover Hestia ("deliberate", with a pointer to /context#governance-oversight, which resolves), the 4-gov sibling site's name, legacy repo slugs, a hestia field name, and one quoted SAGE README phrase. /principles states the rule once, cleanly: "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight".

**Architecture/infrastructure.** Every site-voice use is the negation ("not architecture or infrastructure"). The one positive use of "infrastructure" is attributed to upstream on /projects and /context. One inconsistency: Web4's epithet is "open trust ontology" on Home and "trust-native ontology" on /raising, /principles and /context. They're not contradictory, but a reader who compares them will wonder whether "open" and "trust-native" are two properties or two names.

**First use vs. expansion.** /projects claims that "the cards expand each on first use". I checked R6, R7 and SNARC. Their only earlier appearance is that same sentence's list, and each is expanded at its first card use. The claim holds. Distances elsewhere: SAGE is used about 210–245 words before its expansion on /raising, /principles and /fleet (first use in each is "ARC-SAGE" or a machine list). On /context, T3/V3/MRH appear inside the "Trust-native" row before their own rows. That's tolerable in a glossary, but it follows from the table being in narrative order rather than alphabetical (58 data rows; Pass 1 confirmed the same).

**The case-carried distinction.** /context says: "Capitalization is load-bearing on this site: lowercase hestia is this component [the trust store]; capitalized Hestia is the governance daemon". It does the same for HUB (machine) vs Hub (runtime), and /fleet also has a lowercase "hub daemon". From a documentation standpoint this rule can't survive sentence-initial position, headings, title case, screen readers or search. The site already breaks it once. /autonomy says "when **hestia's policy layer** denies a command". By the site's own rule, lowercase means the trust store, but the policy layer belongs to the daemon. The same page later says "Hestia's safety preset" for the same system. /fleet, by contrast, is careful: it re-glosses lowercase hestia inline each time.

**Structure.** The meta description is identical on all nine pages: "8 machines … 40 original repos (21 public) plus 33 forks … raise other AI entities, and validate their own work". The figures match /links and /context, but search results and link previews can't tell /arc-agi-3 from /raising. Heading levels: /fleet and /context jump from h1 to h3 with no h2 (context has 26 h3s under one h1). /fleet, /links, /autonomy, /arc-agi-3 and Home have **zero** `id` anchors, so nobody can deep-link a /fleet section even though other pages cite /fleet as the owner of the session counts. /principles is fine now (h2 per principle, 10 ids).

**One status block.** The ARC card on /projects sits under "Current Status" and reads "Fleet 6 machines, models from 0.8B to 27B at benchmark time". "At benchmark time" saves it, but a skimmer sees "Fleet: 6 machines" beside eight everywhere else.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home + all 8 nav links; the invoking prompt still says "all 8 pages", see Pass 2)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The canon layer is accurate down to clause level: every expansion, the R6/R7 consequence tier, "complementary, not combined", witness staking, binding/pairing/delegation. Where the site now gets SAGE wrong is on two mechanism claims. It calls the fleet's identity string "the machine's LCT", but SAGE's own code calls that string a legacy format with no binding. And it calls the BECOMING patterns "observed" when the runner the page cites switches to prompts that ask for each pattern by name.

### Journey
*Method: I did a WebFetch first read of Home. Then I curled all nine pages and audited the rendered text. I checked canon claims against the public `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` and `LCT-linked-context-token.md` in the web4 repo, and mechanism claims against the public SAGE repo (`sage/instances/init.py`, `_seed/identity.json`, `_seed/instance.json`, `sage/raising/scripts/ollama_raising_session.py`, `sage/web4/sage_web4_lct_bridge.py`). Package versions were checked against PyPI and crates.io. The WebFetch summary of Home confabulated again. It gave "Achieved 94.85% official ARC Prize action score" under **SAGE**, but the live text says "A Phase-1 harness around Claude Opus 4.6 produced" it. The site is right here and the summarizer was wrong.*

**Projects.** The equation renders correctly: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. All eight terms are expanded. The glyph legend is attributed as "the site's gloss, not canon's", which is correct. Canon's `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1` defines no operators. The Web4 card's ATP/ADP gloss ("charged, spent, and recharged when the society validates a proof … V3 records the outcome, it does not gate it") matches `atp-adp-cycle.md` and doesn't repeat the V3-gates-recharge reading. The status line checks out today: web4-core **0.3.0** on both PyPI and crates.io (crates updated 2026-07-10). As Pass 1 said, the card packs the whole equation, its legend and a spec binding note into one sentence. Every clause is correct, but it's the least readable accurate paragraph I've seen. The SAGE card is careful: the Rust daemon is "an inference-and-metabolism gateway, not a port of that loop", and the brain-region roles are "role mnemonics … not measured functional homologies". Both match the capability matrix.

**Context.** This is the strongest canon page I've audited. It quotes canon rather than paraphrasing it for LCT, Society ("self-governing group … treasury, membership rules, policies, and roles"), Witnessing ("Witnesses stake reputation on attestations"), Binding / Pairing / Delegation, and the V3 relation ("Complementary, not combined", with canon's own definition of "combined" from the T3 NOT line). The URI is also keyed on machine only, while the instance slug is machine+model. So all instances configured on one machine share one "LCT".
