## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing + 8 nav pages)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: A genuinely interesting autonomous-agents research lab that fronts its jargon honestly — I understood what it does in about two minutes, though I had to detour through /context to decode the vocabulary.

### Journey
Landed from a GitHub README. The hero — "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — did its job: I immediately got "distributed fleet of machines running AI agents that maintain their own stuff." The "how we work" three-column layout and the vocabulary primer at the bottom meant I didn't bounce. The line "developmental language is functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness" up front bought a lot of goodwill before I'd read anything weird.

Clicked **Projects** first, as I always do. This page is the strongest for my persona: status labels (Runnable vs Research-stage vs Private) tell me instantly where I can actually clone-and-run something. SAGE, ACT, SNARC, Membot, and 4-Life all have concrete repos and demo sites. I appreciated that the page itself suggests entry points. Confusion: what is a Membot "cartridge"? What are "dream cycles"? "Hamming codes" appears with no context. And "fractal leverage" is named like I should know it.

Followed the repeated pointer to **/context**. This is where the site pays off its jargon debt — every acronym I'd seen got a verbatim expansion, and the page is unusually honest (it admits three unreconciled ways of counting "sessions", a failed derivation with "400x error", and that all verification is self-run by the fleet). As a developer I trust a page more when it documents its own bugs.

**Fleet** was fun (I like hardware tables — Jetsons, a 4090 laptop, a Mac Mini, even an RTX 2060 doing "working memory") but it's a research narrative, not architecture docs. I still don't know how these machines actually talk to each other — no protocol diagram, no API surface. The claim that an identity survived a swap from Qwen 0.5B to TinyLlama 1.1B made me want to see the state-file format, and the page doesn't show it.

**Autonomy** was the page that sold me. Cron-scheduled tracks with declared scopes, a SQLite registry preventing write conflicts, a Visitor→Maintainer feedback loop where LLM personas file bugs against the site and another track fixes them — that's a real, legible engineering pattern. The candor about unresolved gaps ("the exposure window is unbounded in principle" for failures the loop misses) reads like a good postmortem culture.

**Raising** and **Principles** are where a dev's eyes start to skim. The six phases (Grounding → Acting) are clear enough, and the admission that no controlled scramble test has been run is the right kind of honesty. Principles overlaps heavily with what I'd already read; #3 "R&D, not production" and #6 "Document failures" landed, the synthon/coherence material didn't (single trial, self-acknowledged).

**ARC-AGI-3** is the credibility anchor: a public scorecard link, $250 API spend, 5,845 actions, and — critically — an explicit statement that the harness-vs-base-model ablation hasn't been run. That one caveat made me trust every other number on the site more.

**Links** closes cleanly: three explainer sites, the GitHub org, contributing notes, one contact email. No broken links noticed.

### Journey

The Web4 card is the most precise one-paragraph statement of the stack I've seen outside the spec: "T3 (Talent / Training / Temperament) verified by V3 (Valuation / Veracity / Validity), contextualized by Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH), anchored to Linked Context Token (LCT) — with resources tracked via Allocation Transfer Packet (ATP) and Allocation Discharge Packet (ADP)." Every expansion canonical, and the relational verbs (verified by / contextualized by / anchored to) match the operator semantics rather than just listing components. The card even carries a parenthetical admitting the home-page equation's top-level "+" joins are generic conjunctions while the prose is more precise about LCT *anchoring* the trust tensor rather than sitting beside it — that's a distinction most people inside the project get wrong. R6 is spelled out in full with all six elements in the right order. One thing that tripped even me: the SNARC card's link goes to the `engram` repo with no note explaining the SNARC↔engram naming relationship — a contributor knows engram is the plugin packaging, but the site never says so.

**/context** is the page I audited hardest, since it's the designated glossary of record. The equation is verbatim-correct: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`, with `/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with. The page then does something the spec itself doesn't: it flags that `/` carries unrelated semantics in `T3/V3` (verified by) versus `ATP/ADP` (declared→discharged pair). That's true, it's a real notation wart, and self-flagging it is exactly right — better than pretending the glyph is uniform. Every glossary entry I checked against my working knowledge is correct: LCT as identity "grounded in record, not model weights," MRH as "the boundary of what an entity can know or affect — scopes T3/V3 to context," ATP as allocation *declared before* the action and ADP as its spent form (the causality direction people most often invert — correct here), T3 as trust tensor with RDF sub-graph roots, ACP as Web4 trust primitives over MCP transport, ACT as the Cosmos SDK implementation of ACP. Fractal leverage is defined as "reuse, not unification" — canonical. Even p_crit's entry admits the derivation "failed catastrophically (400x error)." (Note: Pass 2 reported p_crit *absent* from /context; my fetch found an entry, failed-derivation caveat included. Either the page changed between fetches or extraction differed — maintainer should confirm which, since Pass 2 filed it as a gap.)

**/fleet** checks out against what I know of the fleet. The brain-region role assignments (Thor as episodic index, Sprout as thalamic router, CBP as dlPFC working memory, HUB as society host with the chapter ledger) match how SAGE distributed-cognition work actually describes itself. The SAGE-Sprout identity-transfer claim (180+ sessions, Qwen 0.5B → TinyLlama 1.1B) is stated with the correct mechanism — "identity lives in state files and prompt construction, not in model weights" — which is the actual raising position, not an overclaim about weights carrying identity. "The model is weather. The identity is organism" is a good line and, importantly, a defensible one. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight suite" here and on every other page; "governance" appears only inside the legacy-naming annotations, which correctly note that SAGE's name and the web4-governance repo slugs predate the correction and are load-bearing.

The remaining pages held up under spot checks: /autonomy uses ATP/ADP exactly right ("each scheduled track issues an ATP for its declared resource budget; an ADP records actual spend"), /raising states "Web4 is a trust-native ontology — not architecture or infrastructure" verbatim, /principles has "Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight." as a principle unto itself, and /arc-agi-3 correctly scopes the harness claim with the un-run ablation caveat stated twice.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing + 8 nav pages; deep passes on /raising, /principles, /autonomy)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The most self-critical autonomous-agents site I've evaluated — failed derivations, unreconciled counts, and unrun controls are stated in the site's own voice — but the caveating rigor is unevenly applied: the raising page's narrative language repeatedly outruns its operational definitions, and the entire evidence base is self-reported with exactly one external anchor.

### Journey
I came in expecting the usual failure mode of consciousness-adjacent agent labs: vivid developmental language up front, a boilerplate disclaimer in the footer. The landing page partially inverts this — "Developmental language on this site — machines that 'teach', 'raise', and hold 'identities' — is functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience" is stated early and repeated for the brain-component vocabulary. That disclaimer is doing real work, and it's specific rather than boilerplate. But placement matters: the vivid claims ("machines that teach, raise, and hold identities", "synthons — emergent coherence entities") are what a skimming reader retains, and the bracketing follows the language it brackets.

**/raising** is where I spent the most time, because "raising is not training" is the framing the lab's credibility hangs on. The mechanistic distinction is real and clearly stated: "in raising sessions, we shape context — we do not update weights," with an honest footnote that some machines run LoRA for *separate* tasks. The operational definitions are exactly what I'd ask for — identity as "consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns," growth as "increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates," explicitly "measurable observables, not phenomenal claims." The evidence section is remarkable for the genre: "the claims in this section rest on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet," and the pre-commitment that "the control has to be a scramble, not a generic replacement... Until that control is run... treat the framework as a working vocabulary that fits our observations, not an established finding." That is the correct epistemic posture, stated unprompted.

And yet the page's *narrative* register keeps escaping its own brackets. "The entity learns its name, its machine, its constraints." "Building relationships with peers... Trust formation." "Spontaneous specialization" (later walked back to "measurable distribution over task types" — but "spontaneous" implies agency the walk-back doesn't retract). The child-language analogy ("What can be expressed through a 0.5B model is different from what can be expressed through a 12B model... Like a child's language") is presented without flagging it as metaphor rather than homology. The "Memoriescape" coinage is handled well ("observational data about token-production behavior — not... phenomenal awareness"), and the observer note "'I notice I want to call it experience'" framed as "the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim" is genuinely sophisticated — recording the anthropomorphizing impulse as data about the observer. But the rigor is intermittent: roughly half the developmental passages carry their bracketing, half rely on the page-level disclaimer to cover them.

**Is "raising not training" defensible?** As a mechanism claim, yes — context-shaping without weight updates is a real, checkable distinction, and the site states it precisely. As a framing, it's doing double duty: it imports developmental connotations (nurture, growth, personhood-adjacent care) that the operational definitions don't support and the scramble control hasn't yet earned. The site knows this — it says so — but knowing it and consistently writing to it are different disciplines.

**/principles** is the strongest page for my persona. "The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error" reframed as "Trust thresholds are empirical, not derivable from first principles" is a textbook negative result, published in the site's own voice. The synthon coherence number (1% coupling → 35% gain) carries the caveat that matters: "Both variables... are so far defined only within the experiment that produced them — the number quantifies the experiment's internal metric, not any of the glossary's three senses of 'coherence.'" Single trial, unreplicated, and the site says both. The deployment disavowal ("Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings") is concrete rather than pro-forma. My residual concern is Principle 5 itself: a single-trial internal metric has a named principle built on top of it — the caveat is present but the *architecture* of the page treats the finding as more load-bearing than one trial warrants.

**/autonomy** would pass review at most safety venues as an honest ops writeup. The gaps are named with unusual precision: detection latency "runs on a roughly 24-hour loop — but only for the failure classes that pass can catch"; "For failures the visitor loop misses (content that is technically correct but misleading), the exposure window is unbounded in principle"; on public repo pushes, "That gap is unaddressed, not solved"; "Human review of the audit logs is asynchronous, with no committed cadence." The current posture is stated plainly as "detect-and-revert," not prevention, with hardware-anchored enforcement flagged as what Hardbound is "building toward" rather than what exists. What I'd flag as concerning is exactly what the page already flags: autonomous deployment to public surfaces with no pre-approval gate, no committed human review cadence, and an epistemic-validation gap (visitor personas test UX clarity, not claim validity — nobody in the loop checks whether the research content is *true*). The site's own sentence — "There is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet" (/context) — is the finding.

**Does the honest assessment feel honest?** Substantially yes, and I want to be precise about why: failure is treated as data rather than reputational threat. Sites performing honesty hedge their strong claims; this site publishes its *failures* (400x derivation error, unreconciled session counts, three irreconciled senses of "coherence", missed-failure exposure windows) with the same typographic weight as its results. The one structural weakness is that all of this honesty is self-assessed: no preprints, no peer review, no external audit, and exactly one independently verifiable number on the entire site (the ARC Prize scorecard, which is a genuine external anchor and is handled well — ablation caveat stated twice, metrics disambiguated, attribution split between model and harness). Honest self-report is still self-report. The site's credibility currently rests on the *texture* of its candor, which is strong evidence but not the same as verification.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What would *falsify* the raising framework? The scramble control is promised — is it scheduled, and what result would count as refuting behavioral-identity continuity rather than being absorbed as "partial transfer"?
2. Who, if anyone, outside the lab has ever coded a session log against the six Becoming patterns? Inter-rater reliability on "Pattern 3 vs Pattern 4" is the cheapest external check available and none is mentioned.
3. The visitor/maintainer loop validates UX, not truth — what mechanism, even aspirationally, checks whether the *research claims* the explorer track generates are correct before they're published autonomously?
4. Is "trust-native" a claim about cryptographic verifiability (LCT as credential), accumulated behavioral track record (T3/V3 as score), or ontological commitment (trust as primitive)? The three readings imply very different research programs.
5. The site says human review of audit logs has "no committed cadence" — is that a resourcing gap or a deliberate position on autonomy? The distinction matters for evaluating the oversight claims.

### Verdict
This is the rare agent-lab site where my job was mostly done for me: the failed derivation, the unreconciled counts, the unrun ablation, the unbounded exposure window — the site states its own weaknesses more precisely than most external reviewers would find them. Two things keep it short of fully credible by research standards: the caveating discipline is inconsistently applied on /raising, where narrative anthropomorphism regularly escapes its brackets; and the evidence base is one external scorecard plus self-reported logs inside a loop the site itself admits has "no external, blinded, or third-party check." I'd cite this site as an example of candid autonomous-ops documentation, return to see the scramble control run — and until it runs, treat the raising framework exactly as the site instructs me to: working vocabulary, not established finding.
