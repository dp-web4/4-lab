## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (the seven front pages `/`, `/projects`, `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/notebook`, `/links`, plus the glossary at `/notebook/context`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good

- **Overall impression**: Within two minutes I knew what the lab claims to build: open tools for governing AI agents, plus persistent "beings". But I couldn't find anything I could install or run, and one of the code links is a 404.

### Journey
I came in from a GitHub README and landed on the home page. The hero line is clear: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust." Next, "The idea" gives a one-paragraph value proposition: trust as a track record checked against rules that exist before anyone acts. I got that right away. "What we build" shows four cards. Web4 is "an open standard … a shared vocabulary, or ontology", Hestia "governs the AI agents a person uses", Hub is "the same governance for a community", and SAGE is "persistent AI beings". Three cards are labelled RUNNING and one RESEARCH. Nothing explains the labels, but I can guess what they mean.

On **/projects** I found three more cards: Hardbound (BUILDING, "enterprise tier … built by Metalinxx"), SWE-SAGE (RESEARCH, a Gemma 4 competition entry) and Synchronism (RESEARCH, a theory framework). The Hestia card says "Runs on every machine in our lab; no public installer yet." That deflated me a bit. The home page told me Hestia is RUNNING, but I can't run it. For a developer, "running in their lab" and "runnable by me" are different things, and the label doesn't separate them.

I clicked the **SWE-SAGE** GitHub link (`github.com/dp-web4/SWE-SAGE`) and got a **404**. The same link appears on **/links** under "Code". Most of the other links resolved, including the Hub subtree link, the GitHub org and 4-gov.org.

**/fleet** is a neat table of eight machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB, pub). "Two kinds of work" divides them into four that do heavy work, two that review and two that host, and the table matches that split. The roles are odd but intriguing, for example "Its being improves the SAGE code it runs on". The page names model families (Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama) but doesn't say which machine runs which; it sends me to the notebook for that. Fair enough.

**/raising** is the most interesting page for me. "A being, not a chatbot" works as a hook: a name, a memory, a journal and a record of its actions. "We do not change the model's weights" answered my first question, which was "is this fine-tuning?" The "Seen / Not yet shown" split reads as honest: it says outright that there's "no blind comparison against a fresh model yet". It does use "mentor" and "tutor" without saying who or what they are (a human? another model?).

**/principles** ("How we work") gives six short principles and is easy to read. "We use what we build" connects to the home card "They are refused by it every day", which I liked: it's dogfooding with consequences.

The **/notebook** index explains the two layers well: the front is "for people: short, plain, and current", and the notebook is "the record underneath it". Nine notebook pages are listed. I opened the **Glossary** (`/notebook/context`) because I still didn't know what SAGE stands for. The answer is there: "Situation-Aware Governance Engine". It's the first time I saw the expansion, though, and no front page gives it, not even the SAGE cards on `/` and `/projects`. The glossary is long (roughly 9k+ words by the fetcher's estimate). It has no alphabetical index and no table of contents, and it has only one element `id`, so I couldn't deep-link to a single term. It does define everything else I wondered about (LCT, T3, ATP, MRH and others), and those terms never appear on the front pages anyway.

**Projects (front).** I read each card against its upstream repo.
