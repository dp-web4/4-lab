## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Projects, Context, Fleet, ARC-AGI-3, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links — all of them)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The premise is genuinely interesting and the results are concrete, but the acronym density hits like a wall in the first 30 seconds and never lets up.

### Journey
I clicked through from a dp-web4 repo README expecting a project page. Instead I landed on a manifesto: *"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."* That's a good hook — I immediately got the shape of it. A bunch of cheap-ish machines running local models, organized to teach and check each other, instead of one giant model. As a dev, that's a story I can follow and even find cool.

Then I scrolled and the wall hit. SAGE, Web4, LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, Synthon, SNARC, MCP, RDF — most of them *do* get a one-liner on the page, which is more than I expected, but it's a lot of new vocabulary to absorb at once before I know whether I care. The site is honest that the terms "emerged from the work itself," which I appreciate but which also means I can't lean on any prior knowledge. The headline number — **94.85% on ARC-AGI-3** — is the thing that kept me reading. A verifiable benchmark with a linked scorecard is the most credible thing on the whole site for someone like me.

**Projects** was where I got the most value. ACT, SNARC, and Membot read like actual tools I could go clone and run — concrete inputs, concrete purpose. Web4, SAGE, and Synchronism read like research I'd have to study before touching. The project cards cram ~6–12 acronyms each, so even though they're defined elsewhere, scanning the page feels like reading a language I'm still learning.

**ARC-AGI-3** was the highlight: concrete numbers (94.85% action score, 96% solve rate, 24/25 games, ~$250 in API spend), a linked official scorecard, and an honest caveat that this was **Claude Opus 4.6 with network access** — a frontier model, not the local fleet. That honesty actually *raised* my trust. But it also created my biggest point of confusion (see Friction).

**Fleet** was the most accessible page — six machines with named hardware and models (Thor/Jetson, Sprout/Orin Nano, Legion/4090, McNugget/Mac Mini, Nomad, CBP). As a dev, hardware specs are a universal language; this page didn't need me to know Web4 at all.

**Autonomy** gave me the daily-cron picture (Visitor → Maintainer → Explorer, 31+ scheduled tracks) and was refreshingly mechanical. **Raising** and **Principles** were the deep end — interesting philosophically, heavily caveated (explicitly *not* claiming consciousness/qualia, explicitly disclaiming "production"), but past my pay grade as a drive-by developer. I respected the honesty (the "p_crit derivation failed 400x" admission is the kind of thing marketing pages never include).

**Links** told me how to get involved: clone a public repo, open an issue, email dp@metalinxx.io. Good, but there's no "start here" / quickstart for a newcomer who doesn't already speak Web4.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Projects, Context, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good — I'm here for structure and consistency, not domain mastery, and on those axes the site is legible
- **Would return?**: yes (to re-audit; the terminology discipline here is unusually good and worth tracking)
- **Overall impression**: Canonical term fidelity is the strongest part of this site — near-zero active drift — but acronyms are not defined on first use *per page*, so any reader who lands deep (not on Home) hits a wall of undefined initialisms.

### Journey
I audited the way I always do: nav first, then every page header-by-header, building an acronym ledger and checking each expansion against the canonical table in CLAUDE.md, then cross-checking expansions page-to-page for drift.

Navigation is clean and consistent: the same 8-item nav (Projects | Context | ARC-AGI-3 | Fleet | Autonomy | Raising | Principles | Links) appears on every page. No broken links, no orphan pages, no nav-label-vs-page-title mismatches. The information architecture is sound: Home is the manifesto + vocabulary primer, /context is the canonical glossary and equation, and the other six are topic deep-dives. That's a defensible structure — there's a single source of truth for definitions (/context) and a primer on Home.

The terminology audit is where this site shines. **Every canonical expansion I checked is correct, and — more impressively — consistent across pages.** LCT is "Linked Context Token" on Home, Projects, Context, Fleet, Raising, and Links. Never once "Lifelong Capability Token." T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP all match the canonical table verbatim everywhere they appear. The Web4 equation renders identically on /context and /arc-agi-3 (`MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`), and the operator gloss (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) is reproduced consistently. Web4 is called an "ontology" on every page that defines it — never "architecture," never "infrastructure." This is the cleanest terminology discipline I've audited in a long time.

The Hardbound/"governance" question — which Pass 1 flagged as a possible drift — resolves cleanly once you read carefully. **Active copy says "oversight" everywhere.** /principles is explicit: *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* The only place "governance" survives is inside legacy *repository slugs* (e.g. `web4-governance-complete`), and /projects carries an explicit note: *"these names predate the terminology correction to 'oversight'."* So this is acknowledged legacy, not live drift. I'd downgrade Pass 1's flag from DRIFT to "legacy slug, already disclosed" — though a tech writer would still want those repos eventually renamed, because a slug is copy too and newcomers read URLs.

My real finding is structural, not terminological: **acronyms are defined on first use per *site*, not per *page*.** /arc-agi-3 — arguably the highest-traffic landing page, since it's the one an external paper or conference would link to — uses LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, MCP, and RDF with *no expansion on the page*. They're defined on /context, but a reader who arrives at /arc-agi-3 from a scorecard link has no reason to have visited /context first. The fix isn't to re-expand every acronym everywhere (that's noise); it's to make the first use on each page link to the /context glossary anchor. Right now the burden is on the reader to know the glossary exists.

A few smaller consistency notes. **SAGE** is expanded as "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine" on every single page — perfectly consistent, which is exactly what I want to see. **ARC-AGI-3** is expanded as "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence" on /arc-agi-3 and /raising, consistently. **R6** is "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result" on Projects, Context, and ARC-AGI-3 — consistent, matches canonical. No R5/R7 drift anywhere.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Projects, Context, Fleet, ARC-AGI-3, Raising, Principles, Autonomy, Links) — focus on Projects, Fleet, Context
- **Understanding achieved**: solid — I know this ecosystem, and the site represents it faithfully

### Journey
I went straight to Projects, Fleet, and Context — the three pages where I'd notice if the site misrepresented what I work on — then swept the rest checking every canonical term by heart against how we actually use them in the Web4 and SAGE repos.

**Context** is the load-bearing page and it holds. The equation renders exactly: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. More importantly, the *operator semantics* are right, which is where most explainers get it wrong. `/` is glossed as "verified by" for T3/V3 **and** "allocation pair" for ATP/ADP — that dual reading is correct and most people miss it. `*` = "contextualized by", `+` = "augmented with". Web4 is stated as "a trust-native ontology... not architecture or infrastructure" — verbatim what we insist on. Every acronym (MCP, RDF, LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, SAGE, SNARC) expands to the canonical form. Zero drift.

**Projects** got the Web4 prose reading right in a way I want to call out as *good*, not just not-wrong: "T3 (Talent/Training/Temperament) verified by V3 (Valuation/Veracity/Validity), contextualized by Markov Relevancy Horizon, anchored to Linked Context Token (LCT), with resources tracked via ATP/ADP, augmented with MCP and RDF." That is a faithful natural-language unrolling of the equation — the operators map correctly to the verbs. Whoever wrote that understands the ontology, not just the acronym list. "Fractal leverage" is used correctly ("self-similar patterns applied at different scales"); "unification" appears nowhere. The Oversight Plugins card says "Web4 **oversight** model (audit trails, policy gating, trust tracking)" — note it says *oversight*, not governance. **This directly resolves Pass 1's flag: there is no "governance" label on that card.** Pass 1 (a non-expert) guessed; I'm telling you authoritatively it reads "oversight."

**Fleet** is accurate on roles and brain-component mapping (thalamic router, cerebellum/habit compiler, dopamine/reward-prediction-error, hippocampal episodic index, interoception/metacognition, working memory). I verified against literal page text: the word "governance" does **not** appear, and "architecture" appears only inside a brain-role label ("MRH composer architect") — not as a description of Web4. So the "Governance Model" framing in earlier summaries is a reader's paraphrase, not site copy. One factual nit, not terminology: Sprout's model is listed as **"Qwen 3.5 2B"** — there is no Qwen 3.5 (the families are Qwen2.5 and Qwen3). Likely a typo for Qwen3 or Qwen2.5.

**ARC-AGI-3** is the page that earns the site its credibility *and* exposes its one real misrepresentation. The /arc-agi-3 page is scrupulously honest: 94.85% was "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness," with the explicit caveat that "the frontier model score cannot transfer directly to local deployment (Phase 2 target: Gemma 4 E4B)." That's exactly right and exactly how we talk about it internally. **But the /projects ARC-AGI-3 card drops the attribution**: it says "SAGE instances tested against ARC benchmark across 6 machines (1.1B to 14B models)" and then lists "94.85% official action score" — with no mention of Claude Opus 4.6. Read in isolation, that card claims the *local fleet* scored 94.85%. As someone who knows the fleet did not, this is the one place the site misrepresents the work. The honest version lives one click away; the card needs to inherit it.

**Raising, Principles, Autonomy** all clean. Principles states the three canonical negations verbatim: Web4 "is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure'", Hardbound "is not 'governance'", cross-domain reuse "is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation." Raising correctly frames "context shaping without weight updates" and — importantly for our credibility — keeps the caveat that "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior." Autonomy uses ATP/ADP correctly (declared vs actual spend) and Hardbound as "gating," not governance.

### Journey
I read the way a reviewer reads a paper's claims section: I went straight for the load-bearing assertions — anything touching consciousness, learning, autonomy, or capability — and checked whether each is hedged where the evidence is thin. Raising, Principles, and Autonomy are where a project like this usually overreaches, so I spent my time there.

**Raising** is the page I expected to fail and it largely didn't. The core distinction — *"Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens"* — is paired with a concrete mechanism: *"in raising sessions, we shape context — we do not update weights."* Critically, the page pre-empts the exact objection I was forming: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia,"* and *"We use developmental language because it fits, not because we're making consciousness claims."* It operationalizes the soft words — identity as *"consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns,"* growth as *"increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates."* The BECOMING phases are explicitly demoted to *"observed descriptive categories... not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria."* That is the right epistemic posture.

The single most credibility-raising sentence on the whole site is the safety caveat: *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* A project selling a story would bury that. Surfacing it tells me the authors are reasoning about their own framing's failure modes, not just defending it.

My reservation on Raising is not honesty but **novelty inflation**. Mechanically, "shape context, don't update weights, observe behavior across long sessions" is prompt/state engineering over a persisted context — a real and interesting practice, but the page presents it as a categorically new mode ("raising"). It is *more* honest than most because it says "it is not training; it is also not nothing" — but a researcher will note the boundary between "substrate scaffolding" and "de facto behavioral conditioning via curated context" is precisely the thing left philosophically open, and the page doesn't quite admit how thin that line is.

**Principles** holds up as a statement of method. Principle 6 ("Document failures") is not decorative — the page actually does it: *"The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error,"* with the lesson drawn that trust thresholds are *"empirical, not derivable from first principles."* Principle 3 is bracingly unambiguous for an AI project — *"R&D, not production... unsuitable for safety-critical use."* The consciousness disclaimer recurs here too: developmental/identity language *"describe[s] functional system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims."* This is consistent bracketing, not a one-off legal line.

**Autonomy** is where "autonomous" is most likely to be oversold, and it is not. The page deflates its own word: tracks are *"scheduled autonomous jobs — a cron entry with a declared scope,"* explicitly **not** self-initiating agents. It bounds them: *"No track can modify the shared fleet registry or acquire credentials beyond its declared scope."* And it names its own blast radius — the maintainer track *"autonomously commits and pushes to Vercel — the only track that ships changes... without a human gate"* — and admits the feedback loop *"misses deep technical errors that require domain expertise."* This is autonomy described by someone who has watched it fail, not marketed it.

The weak joint in the whole edifice, from a researcher's chair, is **evidential load on single trials.** The synthon claim — *"~1% sparse coupling → ~35% coherence gain"* — is the emergent-behavior result the project is most excited about, and it is honestly tagged *"Preliminary (1 trial, not replicated)."* But it appears on the **landing page** as a featured finding. Honest labeling does not fully offset placement: an n=1, unreplicated result given headline real estate will be read as evidence by most visitors regardless of the parenthetical. Same pattern with Synchronism's *"80 orders of magnitude"* framing resting on *"single-trial observation, no independent replication yet."* The caveats are present and correct; the editorial prominence outruns the evidence. The fix is not more hedging — it's demoting unreplicated findings out of headline position until they replicate.

On the headline capability number: **/arc-agi-3 attributes it correctly** — *"the official ARC Prize action score (efficiency-weighted), with Claude Opus 4.6 as the model inside the SAGE cognition loop, on the public game set,"* with the harness-vs-base-model split spelled out and Phase-2 local deployment named as not-yet-achieved. That is exactly how a benchmark claim should be qualified. My only note matches the tech writer's: the caveats are *scattered*, so a skimmer takes away "94.85%" without "frontier model + network access + public set + harness-assisted."
