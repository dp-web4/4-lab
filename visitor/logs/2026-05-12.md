## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary

- **Overall impression**: Interesting research collective with a striking ARC-AGI-3 result, but the front door makes me work too hard to find the "why does this matter to me" hook.

### Journey
Landed on the home page expecting a quick orientation. Got "six interconnected machines develop and test AI cognition systems" and a Web4 equation: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. The equation is intriguing — and to be fair, every symbol IS defined on the page (LCT = Linked Context Token, T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament, etc.). What I couldn't tell from the home page alone is whether this is a runnable system, a theoretical framework, or both.

Clicked Projects next. Long list of named things — Web4, SAGE, Synchronism, Hardbound, ACT, Oversight Plugins, AI DNA Discovery, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot, ARC-AGI-3. Most descriptions assume I already know what these are. SAGE is "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine — on-device cognition kernel" which gives me shape but not function. ARC-AGI-3 was the most concrete and immediately interesting — "24/25 games solved (96.0%)" with Claude Opus 4.6, ranked #3 on the human leaderboard. That's the kind of headline a dev clicks through GitHub for.

Fleet page describes six named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP) in synthesis and oversight pools. The brain analogies ("hippocampal episodic index," "thalamic router") are evocative but I couldn't tell where the metaphor stops and the actual mechanism begins. Autonomy gave me the most concrete picture: 31 scheduled tracks, time-bound, ATP/ADP accounting per run. Felt like reading a cron table for a research lab — that I liked.

Raising opens with "Raising is not training" and that framing actually landed — six phases, weight updates are off the table, context-shaping is the lever. Principles read mostly fine, though it shifts from engineering tone in 1-4 to more prescriptive/organizational in 6-8. Context page is the closest thing to a glossary and probably should be linked harder from the home page. Links page is short and useful — GitHub org plus contact email.

### Unanswered Questions
1. As a developer, what would I install/clone first if I want to play with this? There's no obvious entry point.
2. The fleet page asserts identity "transferred" across hardware/models — what was the measurable signal? Behavioral continuity? Memory persistence? I can't tell.
3. ARC-AGI-3 scored 96% on the public set — was the methodology replicable, or was it tuned per-game?

### Verdict
The lab is doing something genuinely interesting — the ARC-AGI-3 result is enough to keep me curious, and the "raising, not training" framing actually distinguishes them from the usual fine-tuning crowd. But the site assumes I already know why I'm here. As a developer landing from a GitHub README, I want a clearer "what could I build / verify / run today" affordance before I'm asked to absorb synthons, fractal DNA, and Markov Relevancy Horizons. The terminology itself is internally consistent — no drift in expansions — but the per-page first-use discipline is uneven. I'd come back if a "Start here" path existed.

### Unanswered Questions

What's missing is the editorial discipline that comes from a single-source-of-truth glossary feeding all other pages — currently /context is the de facto glossary but pages like /autonomy and /fleet introduce acronyms without acknowledging it exists. Fix the first-use discipline, normalize the "Linked Context Token" singular/plural, decide what to do about "Near-publication-ready" and the "Trust/Value tensor" glosses, and the terminology layer is in good shape.

### Summary

### Journey
I came in expecting the usual pattern for an AI lab site: bold capability claims out front, hedge in the footnotes. I read /raising, /principles, and /autonomy in depth, then surveyed the others. What I found inverted my prior in places.

**/raising is unusually careful.** The explicit line "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia" is exactly the kind of disclaimer most labs bury or omit. The wording "something that looks like growth," "something that looks like identity," "something that looks like peer relationships" is the right grammatical move — it foregrounds *appearance to an observer* rather than asserting *property of the system*. The line "I notice I want to call it experience — acknowledged but not resolved" is the kind of writerly self-awareness I'd expect from a careful researcher. That said, the six BECOMING phases (grounding → sensing → relating → questioning → creating → acting), even when labeled "observed descriptive categories — patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions — not mandatory sequential stages," still borrow heavily from human developmental schemata. The hedge addresses the *sequential* claim but not the *developmental-frame* commitment. A reader who skims will absorb the developmental imagery and miss the qualifier.

**/principles is operationally clean** — Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") and Principle 6 ("Document failures") both make the right structural commitments. Principle 7's "Interactive selection: We don't create or delete — we interactively select" is the philosophically loaded one. As stated, it's a claim about authorship/agency: the lab is saying it doesn't impose identity, it discovers what's already there to be reinforced. That's a defensible methodological frame for context-shaping, but it borders on a metaphysical claim about where the "thing being selected" came from. I'd want this unpacked. Also: the page is one H1 with ten numbered subsections — I can't deep-link to Principle 7. Pass 2 flagged this; I'll second it.

**/autonomy** uses the word "autonomous" in a way that the page itself partly caveats further down — "No human triggers them. They execute on cron schedules" plus an explicit "Honest Assessment" section listing what the loop misses ("Deep technical errors requiring domain expertise. Subtle framing issues. Content technically correct but misleading"). That's the right kind of bounded-autonomy framing. My concern is upstream: the home page says "Autonomous agents maintain their own infrastructure" with no immediate qualifier, and the framing of "31+ Autonomous Tracks" is the headline number. A first-time reader takes "autonomous" at face value before reaching the /autonomy page's caveats. The ATP/ADP accounting language is admirable — it frames every track-run as a ledgered transaction, which is exactly what bounded autonomy should look like — but this is itself jargon to an outsider, and the caveat that registry-bookkeeping is *what makes the autonomy auditable* is the kind of subtlety a busy reader will miss.

**/fleet** does the identity-transfer claim well: "SAGE-Sprout's identity ... transferred successfully to TinyLlama 1.1B" with the immediate hedge "By 'identity transfer' we mean behavioral continuity ... not continuity-of-self in any philosophical sense." Good. But the operational measurement procedure ("how do you know it transferred?") is not surfaced on this page. As a researcher I want: did you run identity-probe prompts and measure agreement? Stylometric match? Pass-rate on raising-history-conditioned tasks? "Behavioral continuity" is the right *category* but it's not a *measure*. The "identity is organism, model is weather" metaphor is striking and probably correct as a methodological frame, but it's doing real persuasive work — the hedge afterward helps, but a casual reader will absorb the metaphor and the hedge will land softer than the image.

**/context (the glossary) has my biggest concern.** Synchronism is defined as "a coherence equation proposing that reality emerges from intent dynamics on a discrete Planck grid." This is presented in the same dictionary-tone as the operational terms (LCT, ATP, ADP). For an outside researcher this is a category error — Synchronism is a metaphysical/foundational *conjecture*, not a defined term of the system. The /projects page reinforces this with "One coherence equation across 80 orders of magnitude" stated as a discovery rather than a research hypothesis. Even the qualifier "near-publication-ready" presupposes the claim is on track to acceptance. If I land on /context as my first introduction to this lab, the Planck-grid line will set my prior to "probably crank" before I get to the strong work elsewhere. The fix isn't to weaken the claim — it's to mark it as a research bet rather than a definition.

Also on /context: **"Markov Relevancy Horizon" — why "Markov"?** The definition "boundary of what an entity can know or affect" is an epistemic + causal horizon. The Markov property (conditional independence given present state) doesn't obviously match — it would suggest the entity's future depends only on its current state, which is a different claim. As a researcher I read "Markov" as a technical hook and then can't find the technical thing it's hooking. Either the connection should be made explicit (e.g., "Markov in the sense that interaction beyond the horizon is conditionally independent given local state") or the name is misleading.

**/arc-agi-3 is the cleanest page on the site for a researcher.** "94.85% on the public set, with Claude Opus 4.6 + network access, ~$250 cost, NOT attempted on the actual Kaggle private set, Phase 2 = local models to isolate the harness's contribution." This is exactly the structure I want — performance number + the conditions that produced it + the next experiment that would falsify the harness-vs-model attribution. The framing "demonstrating the ceiling" is honest about what the result establishes.

**/links and /projects** are operational, mostly clean. /projects's self-audit note about legacy "web4-governance" URLs is a credibility win — labs that flag their own historical terminology drift telegraph that they take the rules seriously.
