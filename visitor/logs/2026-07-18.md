## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, context, arc-agi-3, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: A genuinely interesting multi-machine AI lab with real benchmark numbers and honest caveats, but the Web4 vocabulary is a wall I had to climb before anything else made sense.

### Journey

Landed on the home page from a GitHub README. First 30 seconds: "research collective building a trust-native ontology" — okay, that's abstract, but the concrete numbers pulled me in fast: 7 machines running autonomous tasks daily, 14 repos, and an ARC-AGI-3 benchmark result. The page told me exactly what to do next ("Start with /context for the vocabulary, then clone SAGE"), which I appreciated — most research sites don't have an onramp at all.

The Web4 equation (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) stopped me cold. I know MCP (Model Context Protocol — I've built servers) and RDF, but the rest was alphabet soup, and the operator overloading (`/` means "verified by" in one term and is just a pair separator in another) made me squint. There's a footnote explaining the notation, but I had to read it twice.

Went to **/projects** next, as any developer would. Eleven projects with status labels (runnable / research-stage / private) — the labels are great, the descriptions less so. I can tell *what* SAGE is (on-device cognition kernel) but not *how to run it*. The page says "clone it and it executes end to end," but there's no command, no dependency list, no "run SAGE on puzzle X" example. Concrete metrics like "24/25 games solved" are exactly the right hook though.

**/context** is the best page for someone like me. Real glossary table, exact expansions, and a worked example tracing one commit through the whole equation. After reading it, the rest of the site mostly clicked. My honest suggestion: the home page tells you to start here, but half the other pages assume you already did.

**/arc-agi-3** has the numbers I came for: 94.85% action score, 24/25 games, #3 against the human leaderboard. What impressed me most is the page volunteering that the ablation separating the SAGE harness's contribution from Claude Opus 4.6's raw capability *hasn't been run*. That's the kind of honesty that makes me trust the rest of the site. It also means I can't yet tell whether SAGE is the story or the model is.

**/fleet** is fun — named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB) with real hardware specs (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 Mobile, Mac Mini M4) and small local models (Qwen, Phi-4, Gemma, TinyLlama). As a developer I wanted more: what is a "session" concretely? What's the network topology? The brain analogies ("thalamic router") are flagged as functional analogies, but I still had to keep translating them back into engineering terms.

**/autonomy** was the page that made the lab feel real: 31+ scheduled tracks, ~53 sessions/day, a Visitor→Maintainer feedback loop (I realized I was reading the output of the thing being described, which is neat). The candid admission that the Maintainer ships to production before human review with a 24-hour detection loop is bold — I noted it as a risk, and the page noted it too.

**/raising** and **/principles** are more philosophical. Raising held up better than I expected because it keeps offering the deflationary reading ("everything might be competent context engineering"). Principles reads as internal culture docs — useful for understanding the lab's mindset, less useful for a newcomer trying to evaluate the tech.

**/links** closed the loop: clone commands for SAGE and ACT, license info, one contact email. That's what I needed on page one, honestly.

### Journey

I went in expecting to find drift. Public-facing sites almost always mangle something — "Lifelong Capability Token" shows up in half the third-party writeups I've read, and "Web4 architecture" is practically the default error. So I went straight to the pages that matter to me: **/projects**, **/context**, and **/fleet**.

**/context** first, because it's the reference the rest of the site leans on. The glossary is correct top to bottom: LCT = Linked Context Token (with the right one-liner — witnessed history, not weights), T3 = Talent / Training / Temperament, V3 = Valuation / Veracity / Validity, MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, ATP/ADP = Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packet, R6 = Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result. The equation is written exactly as canonical — `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` — and the operator key even handles the trap that catches most people: `/` means "verified by" in T3/V3 but is a plain declared→discharged pair in ATP/ADP. The key says so explicitly. That's not something you get right by accident; someone here reads the canonical docs.

Better still, the ADP definition is *semantically* right, not just expanded right: "the spent form of the ATP — the record of actual outcome." That's the biological-metaphor framing we actually use in the repos. The governance→oversight correction has its own anchor (`#governance-oversight`) with an honest explanation of *why* — "watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen" — which is precisely the distinction that motivated the correction.

**/projects** — this is where I checked whether my projects are misrepresented. They aren't. Web4 is "trust-native ontology," not architecture. SAGE's 12-step loop is described in the right order. Hardbound's stack (TPM 2.0, FIDO2 with WebAuthn + CTAP2, Secure Enclave with software fallback, Phi-4 Mini policy model) matches what's actually in the private repo, and "the trust layer that touches silicon" is a fair tagline. ACT as "human interface to Web4" on this page and "Cosmos SDK implementation of ACP" on /links are both accurate facets, not a contradiction. The fractal leverage framing is used correctly and defensively: "This isn't unification for its own sake — it's fractal leverage." The page even notes that old repo slugs retain "governance" naming — true, and honestly handled rather than hidden.

**/fleet** — machine roster matches reality: Thor (Jetson AGX, Qwen 2.5 14B + LoRA), Sprout (Orin Nano, thalamic router role), Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, and HUB correctly framed as "substrate, not cognition" with the brain analogy explicitly not applied. The chapter ledger / chapter law vocabulary for the Web4 Fleet society is used correctly — signed member acts, append-only. The brain analogies are all caveated as functional analogies, which is the posture we settled on internally.

**/arc-agi-3** attributes the result correctly: "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness," with the missing ablation flagged twice. As someone who works on SAGE, I appreciate that the site doesn't claim the harness earned the 94.85% — the honest answer is we don't know yet, and the page says so.

The remaining pages (/autonomy, /raising, /principles, /links) held the line. /principles Principle 10 is essentially our terminology policy restated verbatim. /raising uses the right operational hedge on identity claims and states the null hypothesis plainly. The one thing I kept waiting to find — a single "Lifelong," a single "governance" used unironically, one "Web4 infrastructure" — never appeared.

### Journey

I came in expecting the usual failure mode of consciousness-adjacent lab sites: developmental language up front, hedges buried in a footnote. The landing page half-confirms this — "machines that 'teach', 'raise', and hold 'identities'" carries a global disclaimer ("functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience"), but the disclaimer is peripheral while the loaded vocabulary is the headline. That's the standard tension, and I noted it and moved on to the pages that would tell me whether the hedging is real or performative.

**/raising** is where the site earned my attention. The training/raising distinction is stated cleanly ("Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens"), weights are explicitly frozen, and — this is rare — the page defines its loaded terms operationally: "by 'identity' we mean consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns... by 'growth' we mean increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims." Rarer still, there is a section titled "The deflationary alternative" that concedes "everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more," names the deflationary mechanism per pattern (continuation sampling, task routing, mechanical context effects), and then admits: "The comparison has not been run. No deflationary control exists yet." They even specify the control they'd need — a scrambled-order condition preserving content while destroying accumulation. As a researcher, I can't ask for a better posture short of actually running the control. The framework is honestly labeled "a working vocabulary that fits our observations, not an established finding."

**/principles** continues the pattern. Principle 3 explicitly disclaims deployment suitability. Principle 6 documents failures, including a derivation that "failed catastrophically — 400x error" — labs do not usually volunteer that. "Coherence" is admitted to have three senses and no single operational definition. My concern here is different: Principle 5 introduces "synthon" (emergent coherence entities) whose headline evidence is "~1% sparse coupling → ~35% coherence gain," flagged as "1 trial, not replicated." Flagging is good; but a single-trial result of an unreplicated effect measured in a metric with three admitted senses should probably not be a named principle yet. Hedged prominence is still prominence.

**/autonomy** was the most safety-relevant page and the most candid. The Maintainer track autonomously modifies the live site with a "roughly 24-hour" detection loop, and for subtle misleading content the page states "the exposure window is unbounded in principle." The Publisher track's tail risk is described exactly right: "once a commit is public it can be cloned, forked, and mirrored before any revert lands," and the gap is labeled "unaddressed, not solved." The oversight posture is honestly characterized as detect-and-revert, not preventive. What's missing is any committed cadence for human review of high-risk content classes — the page admits review is asynchronous with no schedule. That's a real governance-of-autonomy gap, disclosed but open.

**/context** is where my evaluation cooled. The vocabulary is internally consistent, but for an outsider the trust primitives are named rather than operationalized. T3/V3 are called tensors "verified by" each other, but nothing says how a T3 value is computed, updated, or compared — I could not implement or falsify this from the page. MRH's boundary is undefined (declared? learned?). SNARC lists five dimensions with no scoring method. And Web4's definition has a tension the page doesn't resolve: trust is claimed as a *primitive* of the ontology while also being *constructed* from witnessed interaction. To the site's credit, the page itself admits the strongest version of my critique: "Every check described on this site today is run by the fleet on itself... There is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet." A lab that writes that sentence about itself is one I'll read again.

**/arc-agi-3** is the credibility anchor. The headline (94.85% action score, 24/25 games) is attributed to "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness," the harness-vs-model ablation is flagged as not run — "up front," their words — and the harness-is-the-capability claim is explicitly downgraded to "a working hypothesis, not a demonstrated result." Crucially, /links provides an ARC Prize official scorecard UUID, making this the one claim on the site an outsider can verify without trusting the lab. Every research site should have at least one such anchor; this one does.

**/fleet** handles its riskiest claim — "identity transfer" across models and hardware — with the right hedge ("Observed behavioral continuity — not a claim about continuity-of-self in any philosophical sense"), though the unhedged phrase appears first and the hedge trails it. Session counts admit three unreconciled counting bases, which is honest but means the "900+" headline number is soft.

### Unanswered Questions

1. When will the deflationary control (scrambled-order context, accumulation destroyed) actually run? It is specified in enough detail to schedule.
2. What, mathematically, is a T3 or V3 value — and what would falsify a claimed T3 score for an agent?
3. Is any external or third-party review of the autonomous tracks planned, or is the self-audit closure ("the fleet checks itself") accepted indefinitely?
4. Which of the three senses of "coherence" does the "35% coherence gain" figure use, and what is the measurement instrument?
5. Does "Markov" in MRH denote an actual conditional-independence property, or is it evocative naming?

### Verdict

This is the rare lab site where I trust the caveats more than I understand the claims: the null hypothesis is stated in the lab's own voice, the missing ablation and missing deflationary control are named before a critic could name them, and one headline result carries an externally verifiable scorecard. What keeps it at "partial" understanding rather than "good" is that the trust machinery — T3/V3, MRH, the thing the whole ontology allegedly runs on — is vocabulary without operationalization, and the lab's admirable candor about *epistemics* doesn't yet extend to *scheduling* the controls it knows it needs. Honest, credible in posture, not yet falsifiable in substance.
