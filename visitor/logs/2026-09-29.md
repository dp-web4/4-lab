## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home, Projects, Glossary (/context), ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links — the nav has 8 links plus Home; the brief said 8, the nav says 9)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes — for the SAGE quick start; not for the prose
- **Overall impression**: The site tells me exactly what runs, what doesn't, and how to clone the one thing that does; it then buries that clarity under paragraphs written for its own auditors rather than for me.

### Journey
I landed on Home from a repo README. The h1 ("AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class.") told me the problem in one line, and the next sentence told me the answer is a "trust ontology" called Web4 plus three things that run (Hestia, Hub, SAGE). Within two minutes I had the shape: an RDF-based trust model, a local policy daemon, a Rust community runtime, and a persistent-agent research program on eight machines. That is a pass on the two-minute test. What slowed me down on Home was the negation-first framing: "an open trust ontology, not an architecture, infrastructure or stack" is the third clause I read on the site, and as a developer I don't yet know why anyone would have called it an architecture. The "Vocabulary" tile lists LCT, T3/V3, MRH, R6/R7, ATP/ADP with no expansions — fine as a pointer, but it's the first thing on the page that looks like a wall of jargon.

Projects is where I spent most of my time. The status badges ("runnable" = "clone it and it executes end to end today") are the best thing on the site for my persona: I can tell at a glance what to clone. The Quick start at the bottom is concrete and real — `ollama pull gemma3:4b`, an init command, `cargo build --release`, a daemon with a dashboard on port 8760, and the honest line that "an idle daemon in WAKE with an empty buffer is the correct first result". `pip install web4-core` for Web4. That is exactly what a developer wants.

Two things on Projects worked against that. First, the Web4 card is a single 319-word paragraph, with nested em-dash asides like "the site's gloss, not canon's" and "a binding the equation's + does not show". I do not know who "canon" is, and the card is arguing with someone who isn't me. I skimmed it and got the acronyms, not the idea. Second, Hestia wears a "runnable" badge on its card, but the Quick start for it is headed "NO FIRST-RUN PATH YET" and says "Nobody outside the fleet is known to have installed it." By the page's own definition of runnable, that badge is wrong for me — it may execute end to end on their machines, but I can't clone it and run it.

Fleet: eight machines with hardware, model, role, and a dated state each. Readable as a table. The brain-region roles (thalamic router, dlPFC) are colorful but I didn't need them. The correction notes ("Until 2026-09-12 this page said...") are everywhere; I trust the page more for them, but they roughly double the reading.

Autonomy: the cron timeline is exactly what I'd want. One sentence tripped me: tracks are "unattended and self-scheduled", and three sentences later "A human writes a track's cron entry". If a human writes the cron entry, it isn't self-scheduled; it's unattended. Small, but it's the kind of thing I'd notice in a code review.

Raising: above my ceiling in places, but the structure (claims / confounds / what we're not claiming) let me skip to the parts I could evaluate. The numbers (1,217 scripted records, 108 with HTTP 502s in place of answers) read like a real lab notebook. Model names like "Qwen 3.8-distill 2B" and "Gemma 4 E4B" I cannot map to releases I know; I took them on faith.

Principles: skimmed. Found something I did need here that I had looked for on Links: the actual license split. Main repos (SAGE, web4, hestia, 4-hub, 4-life) are AGPL-3.0; snarc and membot are MIT; ARC-SAGE is MIT-0. Links, under its own "Licenses" heading, only says MIT-0 for ARC-SAGE, Apache 2.0 for one submission, and "see each repo's LICENSE file" for everything else — zero mentions of AGPL. Principles then says "/links carries the per-repo version", which it does not. For a developer deciding whether to fork, AGPL vs MIT is the decision, and the page named as the license reference is the one page that doesn't say it.

Glossary (/context): 11,900 words, one h1 and 26 h3s with no h2 level, roughly 80 entries not alphabetized. The "at a glance" table at the top saved me; below it I searched with Ctrl-F rather than read. The Web4 equation and its operator legend ("/" = verified by, "*" = contextualized by, "+" = augmented with) is the single most useful thing on the page for a developer.

ARC-AGI-3: short, clear, correctly deflated. "It is not current competition positioning" and the engine-source caveat are exactly the honesty I'd want before I repeat the 94.85% figure anywhere.

Links: the repo list is complete and the Quick start correctly points to `/projects#quick-start` (anchor exists). The Hub link goes to the `4-hub` mirror while Projects says development lives in `web4/tree/main/hub`; the label says "(4-hub)" so it's not wrong, just the less useful of the two targets.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What would I actually *build* with web4-core after `pip install`? The Quick start gets me a SAGE daemon; there is no three-line Web4 code example (mint an LCT, record a witnessed action) anywhere on the site.
2. Why is Hestia "runnable" if nobody outside the fleet has run it? Which badge should I believe?
3. Which license applies to the thing I'd most likely fork (SAGE)? I found the answer (AGPL-3.0) on Principles, not on Links.
4. Who or what is "canon"? The Web4 card argues with it; the page never names the document.
5. "Qwen 3.8-distill 2B", "Gemma 4 E4B", "Granite 4 h-tiny" — are these public Ollama tags I can pull, or internal names?
6. Is Metalinxx a company, and is the dp@metalinxx.io contact the same entity?

### Verdict
As a developer I got the big picture in two minutes and a working clone path in five, which is better than most research-lab sites manage. The site's honesty is its strength: statuses, caveats and "this was wrong until" notes made me trust the numbers. Its weakness for my persona is that the same honesty is written for auditors, not readers: a 319-word Web4 card arguing with "canon", a license fact filed under Principles instead of Links, and a "runnable" badge its own Quick start contradicts.

Home reads cleanly for structure: one h1, five h2, eleven h3.
