I landed from a repo README, so I'm here to answer one question: "what is this and is it worth my afternoon?" The home headline — **"Six machines. Fourteen repos. As a dev I couldn't tell if those analogies are literal routing architecture or just evocative labels; I'd want one sentence clarifying that.

Raising's "we shape context; we do not update weights" is a clean, mechanistically-believable claim, and I appreciated that it immediately caveats itself ("frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior"). Principles closed it out — "R&D, not production" and "Document failures" made me trust the authors more.

I found the actual "how do I get involved" answer only on **Links** (the last nav item): *"SAGE and Web4 are the most active entry points. Clone what interests you, experiment, and open a discussion."* That's exactly what I needed — but it's the last place I looked.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

### Journey
I audited methodically: every page, every acronym, cross-checked against the canonical table. My job is to catch where a reader meets a term cold, where two pages disagree, and where an expansion has quietly mutated.

The headline result is reassuring. The **load-bearing Web4 terms are airtight**:
- **LCT → "Linked Context Token"** on Home, Context, Projects, Fleet, Raising, Links. Zero instances of the "Lifelong Capability Token" drift the canonical table warns about. This is the term most likely to corrupt, and it's clean everywhere.
- **MRH → "Markov Relevancy Horizon"** — identical on every page that uses it.
- **ATP/ADP → "Allocation Transfer Packet" / "Allocation Discharge Packet"** — consistent on Home, Context, Autonomy, Links.
- **T3 → "Talent / Training / Temperament"**, **V3 → "Valuation / Veracity / Validity"** — consistent, including spacing around the slashes.
- **R6 → "Six-Element Action Framework (Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result)"** — identical on Context and ARC-AGI-3.
- **SAGE → "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine"** — consistent everywhere.
- **Web4 as ontology, Hardbound as oversight** — not just used correctly but *defended*. Principle 10 states it explicitly: "Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight," and "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight." Raising and Context echo "not architecture or infrastructure." This is the rare site that documents its own terminology corrections.

The one moment I thought I'd found a contradiction: a first read of /projects surfaced the word "governance." That would directly contradict Principle 10. I re-fetched to confirm verbatim — and it turns out the only occurrence is a deliberate footnote: *"Linked repo paths carry web4-governance slugs — these names predate the terminology correction to 'oversight' and are load-bearing for existing forks."* The Oversight Plugins card itself reads "Web4 oversight model (audit trails, policy gating, trust tracking)." So this is **not drift — it's a documented legacy-naming caveat**, which is exactly the right way to handle a term you've renamed but can't retroactively change in repo URLs. Good practice; I'd only suggest making clearer that `web4-governance` is a *repo slug*, not the concept's current name.

Where the consistency actually slips is the **ARC-AGI** acronym — and notably it's the one acronym that *isn't* in the canonical Web4 table:
- Home: used, **not expanded** ("ARC-AGI" / "ARC-AGI-3" appear bare).
- Projects: used, expanded inconsistently as roughly "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus — Artificial General Intelligence."
- Raising: "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence."
- ARC-AGI-3 page: "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence, **version 3**."

So across pages the expansion appears (a) absent, (b) with an em-dash, (c) with "for," and (d) with a "version 3" suffix. Same meaning, four surface forms, and missing on the page (Home) where most readers meet it first. For a tech-writer this is the textbook "define on first use, then use one canonical form" miss.

Minor: **FIDO2** appears on Context and Projects unexpanded; **MCP/RDF** are expanded on Home and Context but used bare on Links. These are low-stakes for the audience but worth a glossary tooltip.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Raising, Principles, Autonomy, ARC-AGI-3, Context, Projects, Fleet, Links — Raising/Principles/Autonomy read closely)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The intellectual honesty here is unusually disciplined for a site discussing agent "identity" and "becoming" — the consciousness-adjacent claims are caveated at the point of use, not buried in a footer — and the one place the rigor slips is not in the theory but in *claim propagation*: the headline 94.85% loses its conditions when it travels to the landing and links pages.

### Journey
I came in primed to be skeptical. A lab that uses words like "raising," "becoming," "identity," and "synthon" for language models is, in my experience, usually one disclaimer away from overclaiming consciousness. So I went straight to Raising, Principles, and Autonomy to see whether the framing could survive a researcher's read.

It mostly does, and for a specific reason: **the caveats are co-located with the claims.** On the home page the developmental vocabulary is introduced with "Developmental and lifecycle terms below (identity, behavioral continuity) are functional descriptions of observed system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims." On Raising the line is explicit: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." Most impressively, Raising names the *observer's* interpretive pull rather than projecting it onto the system — "I notice I want to call it experience" — and frames that as observer bias. That is a more sophisticated epistemic move than I expected; it's the difference between a lab that anthropomorphizes and a lab that studies its own anthropomorphizing.

The mechanistic core of "raising not training" is defensible on its face. The claim is narrow and falsifiable: weights are frozen, context is shaped, behavioral attractors emerge in-context rather than via parameter updates. Stripped of the developmental vocabulary, this is in-context conditioning described in a developmental register — and the site says so. Critically, it does not use frozen weights as a *safety* argument. The caveat "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise" is exactly the caveat a safety researcher wants to see, and its presence is the single strongest credibility signal on the site. A lab selling a story would have omitted that sentence.

The honest-assessment posture holds across the rest, too. Principle 3 explicitly rules out safety-critical / medical / financial / autonomous-control use. Principle 6 commits to documenting failures; Principle 9 ("productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started") is a research stance, not a hedge. The BECOMING phases are flagged as "observational pattern-names," *not* "measured stages with defined transition criteria" — which is the right disclosure, though it also concedes the developmental framework is currently anecdotal rather than measured. The ARC-AGI-3 page is a model of conditioning a result: 94.85% is attributed to Claude Opus 4.6 *with network access* on the *public* set, framed as a ceiling demonstration and explicitly *not* the Kaggle entry; Phase 2 is positioned to isolate the harness's own contribution under real constraints; the 1/25 failure is named; the cost (~$250) is disclosed. Synchronism is labeled a "research conjecture … single-trial, no independent replication yet." Fleet labels its brain-region names as "functional analogies … not claims about neural correspondence."

So where does a researcher push back? Two places.

**First — claim propagation.** The 94.85% / 96% number is impeccably conditioned *on the pages that explain it* (ARC-AGI-3, Fleet). But on the **home page** it appears as "96% on ARC-AGI-3 … through reasoning rather than brute force," and on **Links** as "94.85% official ARC Prize action score," in both cases without the model-and-network conditions traveling with it. This is not dishonest — the conditions are one click away — but it's the exact pattern a skeptical reader is trained to catch: the strong number is the credibility anchor on the surfaces people see first, and the asterisk lives elsewhere. The fix is cheap (a parenthetical "Opus 4.6, public set, network-enabled" wherever the bare number appears) and it would close the only real honesty gap I found.

**Second — "synthon" and "interactive selection" need operational handles.** Principle 5 says a synthon "cannot be built; conditions can be created for formation," and Principle 7 says interactive selection is "not training; it is also not nothing." As a researcher, "cannot be built, only conditioned" and "not nothing" are *negative* definitions — they tell me what the thing isn't. I never found the positive operational criterion: how is a synthon *detected* or *measured*, and what observation would falsify its presence? Without a detection criterion, "emergent coherent entity from recursive interaction" risks reading as unfalsifiable to an outside reader, even though the surrounding pages are otherwise careful. This isn't overclaiming — it's under-operationalizing a coined term that's doing load-bearing work.

A lighter note: the developmental vocabulary (raising / BECOMING / Memoriescape / dream consolidation) is heavily connotative, and the caveats are doing a lot of work to keep it grounded. The caveats *succeed* — but a reader could fairly ask whether the vocabulary is earning its keep analytically or whether plainer terms would carry the same observations with less disclaimer overhead. That's a stylistic judgment, not an honesty failure; I note it because the gap between evocative term and measured claim is where credibility is most often lost.

### Verdict
As a trust/safety researcher, I'd cite this site as a positive example of caveating: the consciousness-adjacent vocabulary is disclaimed at the point of use, the "raising not training" claim is mechanistically narrow rather than mystical, and the lab volunteers the safety-relevant caveat (frozen weights ≠ safe behavior) that a less honest project would omit. The honest assessment reads as genuinely honest.
