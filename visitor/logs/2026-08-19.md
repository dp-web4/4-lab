## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I got what this lab does in about two minutes, and the ARC-AGI-3 page with a live third-party scorecard is what made me believe the rest — but there is no path from "I'm interested" to "I ran something."

### Journey

Came in from a dp-web4 repo README. The hero — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — told me the scale immediately, which I liked. The line under it did the actual work:

> "AI agents already act on their own — browsing, transacting, coordinating with other agents — and there is no general way to establish whether one will behave appropriately in a given context."

That's a problem statement I recognize without any Web4 background. Good. That sentence is doing more for me than the rest of the page combined.

Then, still on the home page, I hit this:

> `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`

Six acronyms in one line. They *are* expanded on the page — I checked, and every one of them is — but as a scanner, hitting six unknown tokens in a single formula is a wall, not a summary. My eye bounced off it and I scrolled past to find the GitHub links. I came back to the equation later, from /context, and it made sense then. Order matters: the equation reads as a *conclusion* but is placed as an *introduction*.

Went to **/projects** next. This was the strongest page for me. Eleven cards, each with a repo link, each labeled `public/private` and `runnable/research-stage`. That status labeling is genuinely useful — "runnable" vs "research-stage" is exactly the axis I care about, and I don't see it on most research sites. SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, and Membot are flagged runnable. Good.

But: **nothing tells me how to run them.** "Runnable" is a claim, not a quickstart. There's no `git clone && ...` anywhere on the site, no "start here" snippet, no minimum-hardware note for the on-device stuff. The home page does say SAGE is the recommended starting point, which is the right instinct — it just stops one step short. For a developer, that gap between "runnable" and "here's the command" is the single biggest friction on the site.

Clicked **Glossary** in the nav and landed on `/context`. Small thing, but I noticed the URL didn't match the label. Not confusing enough to matter, just a blip.

The glossary itself is where the acronyms finally clicked. Every term has expansion + one-line gloss. The `coherence` entry stopped me:

> "Used in three related senses on this site... with no single operational definition yet."

I respect that this is stated out loud. But it does mean that when I later read Principle 5 ("The synthon is the concept that bridges component behavior and system emergence") and the synthon gloss ("Emergent coherence entity sustained by recursive interaction"), I'm reading a definition built on an admittedly-undefined word. Honest, but circular, and the site knows it's circular. As a dev, that's the part of the framework I'd bounce off.

**/fleet** — eight machines, each with a model, a session count, and a brain-region mnemonic. The page pre-empts the obvious objection well:

> "The 'Brain:' labels below are role mnemonics the lab assigned, not measured functional homologies"

Good caveat, placed right where the claim is. But then the very next sentence has a grammar bug:

> "Each of the six cognition machines builds the one of SAGE's six brain-architecture components its card names"

"builds the one of ... its card names" is missing a relative pronoun — should be "builds the one ... **that** its card names." I re-fetched to confirm the wording; it's on the page, not a rendering artifact. It's a stumble in an otherwise well-written paragraph.

**/autonomy** was the page that most changed my read of the project. The daily cycle (03:00 supervisors → 04:00 archivist → 04:30 publisher → 05:30 visitor → 06:30 maintainer → 07:00 outreach → 08:00 explorer) is concrete and legible. And:

> "'Autonomous' here means unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed in the AI-safety sense of choosing its own goals."

That's the caveat I was about to write in this log myself, already on the page. Same for "What it misses: Deep technical errors that require domain expertise... validating the underlying research."

But — the page says "Each session produces a dated audit log recording what changed." I wanted to read one. There's no link to any actual log. The generic `github.com/dp-web4` link doesn't tell me which repo or which path. This is the same gap as /projects: the artifact is claimed, the artifact isn't reachable.

**/raising** is the page where I'm furthest outside my depth, and it handles that better than I expected:

> "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia."

and, more impressively:

> "everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more — the control that would discriminate raising from that alternative has not been run yet."

Naming your own missing control is not a normal thing for a project site to do. It bought a lot of credibility from me.

**/principles** — ten principles, mostly operational, some abstract. #3 "Nothing here is optimized for deployment" is a useful expectation-setter. #1 ("You don't engineer the mound. You engineer placement rules.") and #2 ("When a pattern works at one scale, apply it at every scale.") read as slogans to me until I'd already read the rest of the site; they'd land better with a one-line concrete example each.

**/arc-agi-3** is the best page on the site for my persona, by a distance. Actual numbers — 94.85% official action score, 24/25 games, ~$250 API spend — plus a link to a third-party scorecard on arcprize.org and MIT-0 code. And the caveat is *first*, not buried:

> "One caveat belongs up front: the ablation that would separate the harness's contribution from the base model's (same model, no harness) has not been run."

Plus, on the human-leaderboard rank: "it is the number on this page most likely to be quoted without its conditions." That's a project policing its own PR. This page is why I'd come back.

**/links** — solid list, org repos, forks marked as forks, contact address. One inconsistency: the web4 repo entry gives me a full inline expansion of every canonical term, but does *not* link `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md`, even though /context links it directly. If /context can point me at the source of truth, the page whose entire job is links should too.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **How do I run SAGE?** The site tells me it's runnable and recommended as the starting point, and then never gives me a command. What hardware? What's the install? What do I see when it works?
2. **Where are the audit logs?** /autonomy says every session produces one. I want to read one to see if the loop is real. I can't find one from the site.
3. **What does the fleet actually compute?** I understand the machines exist, their models, and their brain-mnemonic roles. I don't understand what a "session" produces or what a raising session *is*, procedurally. 2,065 of them is a big number attached to a process I can't picture.
4. **Is `coherence` measurable in principle, or only currently unmeasured?** /raising says the metric doesn't exist because the term isn't operationalized. That's a different claim from "we haven't gotten to it yet," and I can't tell which one is meant.
5. **What's ACP?** Appears on /links, unexpanded, and I don't recognize it.
6. **How does the Web4 equation's `*` and `/` work?** /context has a notation key, but as a dev I read `T3/V3*MRH` as arithmetic on first pass. The site says `/` = "verified by" and `*` = "contextualized by" — that's fine once found, but using arithmetic operators for non-arithmetic relations costs a reader one wrong parse for free.

### Verdict

This site cleared my two-minute bar: I know what the lab does, why it exists, and which repo to open first. The intellectual honesty is unusual and it works on me — the ARC-AGI-3 page leading with its own unrun ablation, and /raising naming the control that would falsify its central framing, made me trust the pages I *couldn't* evaluate.

What it doesn't do is convert interest into action. Four projects are labeled "runnable" and not one of them has a command next to it; /autonomy promises dated audit logs and links none. For a developer arriving from a repo README, the site is an excellent explanation and a dead end — I finish reading well-informed and with nothing to type. One quickstart block and one linked log would fix more friction for my persona than any wording change on this list.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every canonical expansion on every page is correct — zero drift — and the remaining defects are all *placement*: the site has a good disclosure pattern for overloaded words and a good pattern for reconciling competing numbers, and it has applied each one to some instances and not their siblings.

### Journey

I audited terminology first and structure second, page by page, with targeted questions rather than bulk reads.

**The expansion audit came back completely clean.** I checked LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, R7, MCP, RDF, SAGE, SNARC, and ACP on every page where each appears. Every expansion matches the canonical table exactly. No "Lifelong Capability Token", no "Trust-Tracing-Tensor", no "Attention Token Pool". "Production ready" appears nowhere. "Unification" appears only as a negation. "Stack" appears nowhere on any of the nine pages. On the narrow question my persona is supposed to be the primary auditor for, the answer is: there is nothing to fix.

So the interesting work was elsewhere. Three things surfaced.

**1. The site discloses two overloaded words and not the third.**

This site is unusually good at catching homonyms. It catches the `/` glyph:

> "`/` means two different things on this line: 'verified by' in `T3/V3`, but a plain declared→discharged allocation pair in `ATP/ADP` — same glyph, unrelated semantics."

It catches "oversight":

> "Terminology note — the governance→oversight correction, in one place: the lab originally described its control layers as 'governance.' What these systems actually do is oversight — watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen."

and, on /projects, right at the point of use: "'oversight' = machine-enforced policy gating here, not human supervision."

That is a **pattern**: when a word carries two senses on this site, name both senses at the point of collision. It works. I never once misread `/` or "oversight."

The word it has not run that pattern on is **"architecture"**. Three pages tell me, flatly, that the word is wrong:

- /context: "A trust-native ontology for AI agents, devices, and people — not architecture or infrastructure."
- /principles: "Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology."
- /raising: "Web4 is a trust-native ontology — not architecture or infrastructure."

And three pages then use it, load-bearingly, in the permitted SAGE sense:

- / (home): "Each machine owns one component. Peer review across the architecture."
- /projects: "6 brain-architecture components (working memory, thalamic router, cerebellum, episodic memory, reward prediction, metacognition) built by the fleet in parallel."
- /fleet: "each of the six cognition machines builds the one of SAGE's six brain-architecture components its card names"

Both uses are correct. Web4 genuinely isn't an architecture; SAGE genuinely has one. But I asked /context directly whether it disambiguates the two, and it does not. The site has a "Fleet brain-analog terms" section that carefully disclaims the *brain* half of "brain-architecture" ("functional analogies, not claims about neural correspondence") and says nothing about the *architecture* half — the half it has banned elsewhere. By the site's own Principle 10 ("Words shape how you think about the system"), a reader told the word is wrong and then shown it three times is being asked to hold an undisclosed distinction.

**2. Home is the one page that uses both banned-for-Web4 words in their permitted sense and never carries the sentence that grants the permission.**

Home has "Peer review across the architecture" and a section heading "Autonomous agents maintain their own infrastructure." Home does not contain "not architecture or infrastructure" anywhere. Its negation is a different pair:

> "Not a blockchain, not a platform — a way of describing things."

This is the sharpest form of the problem. Home negates two misreadings it does not provoke, and omits the one it does. A newcomer's first sight of `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` reads as a component diagram — "architecture" and "stack" are the wrong guesses that formula actually invites, not "blockchain." Then the page uses "architecture" and "infrastructure" in the next screens, unqualified, and never corrects the guess. The correction exists on three other pages. It is missing from the only page everyone reads.

**3. Three different numbers named "Sprout", with no stated relationship.**

/fleet does an exemplary job on its own totals — it states coverage explicitly: "1,991 / 2,065 totals, which cover the six cognition machines plus HUB." I checked the arithmetic: Thor 435 + Sprout 488 + Legion 336 + McNugget 377 + Nomad 162 + CBP 193 = **1,991**, plus HUB 74 = **2,065**. It reconciles exactly, and the sentence tells me *why* there are two numbers. That is the right treatment.

Home then gives me three Sprout figures with no such clause:

- "SAGE-Sprout accumulated **115 raising sessions** on a Jetson Orin Nano"
- "The Sprout line has since continued past **180 sessions** on later models"
- and /fleet lists Sprout at **488**.

I can construct a story (115 = the Jetson-era raising run; 180 = the same line on later models; 488 = every session the machine has run, of all kinds). But I am constructing it, and 180 → 488 is a large unexplained gap for a reader doing the same arithmetic I just did on the fleet totals. The site already knows how to fix this — the fix is one clause, and /fleet is the page that demonstrates it.

**Structural notes.** Nav is complete and every label resolves. One route mismatch: nav says **Glossary**, URL is **/context**, and the page's first section is **"How Knowledge Flows"** — three names for one page, and the glossary is its second section, not its subject. It's a 26-section page doing hub duty, and "Glossary" undersells it.

Acronym-on-first-use is inconsistent *within* pages, not merely between them. The cleanest example is an exact inversion: **/fleet expands MCP but not RDF; /principles expands RDF but not MCP.** Same two acronyms, two pages, opposite choices. /fleet also expands LoRA and dlPFC in the same neighborhood where it leaves RDF bare, so the omission is internal, not a page-wide policy.

/autonomy is the thinnest: LCT, T3, MRH, and MCP get no expansion at all, and V3, ATP, and ADP are expanded only in "Safety boundaries" — the fifth of six sections, well after first use. First uses are hyperlinked to /context, which is a defensible pattern, but it is applied on /autonomy and /principles while /fleet and /arc-agi-3 expand inline. Two conventions, no stated rule.

**1. R6 loses canon's arrow, and the site's name for it bakes the loss in.**

Canon writes R6 as:

> "Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource **→** Result. The base action grammar. ATP→ADP transaction without reputation tracking."

Five inputs, an arrow, one output. The site writes it, identically on /context, /projects, /arc-agi-3 and /links, as:

> "Six-Element Action Framework — Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result — the base action grammar."

Six co-equal elements separated by slashes. The arrow is gone, and with it the input→output asymmetry that is the entire point of calling it a *grammar*. Worse, the label the site chose — "Six-Element Action Framework" — is not canon's name for R6 (canon says "the base action grammar") and it encodes the flattening in the term itself. A reader who learns R6 from this site learns a list; a reader who learns it from canon learns a production rule. Those are different objects.

This is not a site-authored error. The "Six-Element Action Framework (Rules/Role/Request/Reference/Resource/Result)" string is exactly what the track's own CLAUDE.md reference table specifies, slashes and all. **The drift is upstream in the lab's crib sheet, and the site is faithfully reproducing it.** That is the more interesting failure: an audit that checks the site against the crib sheet returns clean forever, because the crib sheet is where the drift lives. Passes 1–2 both returned "R6 CORRECT on all four pages," and against their reference, they were right.

The site also drops canon's second R6 sentence — "ATP→ADP transaction without reputation tracking" — which is the clause that ties R6 to the resource cycle. /arc-agi-3 then has to rebuild that tie from scratch in prose: "the SAGE loop's budget step declares an allocation (ATP) before acting, and recorded spend (ADP) is what aggregates to this number." Canon already made that link in six words; the glossary severed it and a results page re-derived it ad hoc.

**2. Canon does not define Web4.**

I checked this twice, in separate fetches, because I did not believe it the first time. There is no Web4 entry. There is no "ontology" entry. The word "ontology" appears in canon exactly once, inside a file path in the V3 section (`web4-standard/ontology/t3v3-ontology.ttl`).

A contributor who follows that link to check the ontology framing finds nothing. The claim is not contradicted by canon — it is simply not in it. This is not a site defect I would fix by editing the site. It routes upward: **canon needs a Web4 entry, or the site needs to name the document that actually carries the ontology framing.** Right now the site points at a source that is silent on its headline.

Same structural gap, smaller stakes: MCP and RDF are in the equation and absent from canon. ### Unanswered Questions

1. **Is `coherence` unmeasured or unmeasurable?** The site says "no single operational definition yet." "Yet" implies the first. But the coherence↔synthon circularity that Principle 5 discloses implies the second, and the two claims have very different consequences for the framework. 2. **Who would run the deflationary control, and would they be independent of the authors?** /autonomy already establishes that the site's evaluators have *"misses correlated with the authors', not independent of them."* The same objection applies with more force to the control that would validate the entire raising framing — and the site names no owner at all, independent or otherwise.
3. **Is Principle 9 bounded by anything?** As written, "productive investigation matters more than correctness" plus no falsification conditions on any principle means nothing on this site can be wrong in a way that costs it. I don't think that's the intent — Principle 6 argues against it — but a reviewer reads the text, not the intent.
4. **What does the site claim the ARC-AGI-3 result licenses?** It is honest that the ablation is unrun. It is less clear what remains established: a frontier model with network access and no wall-clock limit scored well, which is a result about the pair, and the page's own hypothesis ("the loop is the capability") is the part not yet supported. The undated model compounds this.
5. **Why does the site hedge so much more than it evidences?** This is the question I'd actually ask the authors. The caveat quality here is at the ceiling of what prose can do. Nothing on the site is going to become more credible through better hedging, and the marginal return on the next disclaimer is negative. What is the constraint that makes writing the eighth caveat easier than running the first control?

### Verdict

On the three things I came to evaluate: the consciousness-adjacent claims are caveated better than anything I have reviewed in this genre — repeatedly, in near-identical language, on every page that needs it; "raising not training" is defensible on its mechanical claim (context, not weights) and the site itself says the developmental *vocabulary* outruns that claim, which is the correct concession stated without prompting; and the honest assessment does feel honest, including in the one place that matters most, where /autonomy shows that its own zero-rollback record is measured by the loop under evaluation.

The problem is that the honesty has saturated. Eight named gaps, eight precise specifications of the experiment that would close them, zero owners and zero dates — and a single externally checkable artifact on the whole site, which is exactly why /arc-agi-3 is the only page whose claims survived contact with me. The next unit of credibility is not available in prose. It is available in a date on the ARC ablation, a published log sample, or a link to the p_crit post-mortem, and any one of the three would do more for an external reader than every caveat added since.

Two things I'd fix regardless of that: the deflationary section is the strongest argument on this site and nothing routes to it, so the disclaimer that reaches readers is the one defending against qualia rather than the one defending against deflation; and `coherence` — the keystone — is the single word where the site's own excellent homonym-disclosure pattern stops one sense short of the reader's field.
