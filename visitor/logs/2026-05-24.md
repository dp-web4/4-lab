## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe — the ARC-AGI-3 numbers and the fleet hardware hooked me; the philosophy pages would not have, on their own.
- **Overall impression**: Real machines, real benchmark numbers, real repos — wrapped in an unusually dense theoretical vocabulary that a developer has to push through before any of it lands.

### Journey
I landed via a repo README, so I went to the home page expecting a one-liner I could repeat to a coworker. I got an equation instead: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. Of the seven tokens, I knew exactly two (MCP, RDF). The page told me LCT is a "persistent identity anchor" and MRH is a "relevancy boundary" but it asked me to absorb a lot of new vocabulary before I knew whether I cared.

I clicked **Projects** next, which is what a developer always does. The 11 project cards were the most useful part of the whole site for me — concrete names (SAGE, Synchronism, Hardbound, ACT, SNARC, Membot), short hooks, and external links. ARC-AGI-3's "24/25 games solved, 94.85% action score" was the first claim that made me sit up. That number is the reason I'd send the link to a coworker.

**Fleet** was the second thing that felt real: six named machines, specific hardware (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 Mobile, Mac Mini M4), specific models (Qwen 2.5 14B, Phi-4 14B, Gemma 3 12B, TinyLlama 1.1B). This is the kind of page I trust because it would be embarrassing to lie about.

**Autonomy** read like a cron table dressed up in metaphor. The 31-job schedule across six machines is impressive on its face, but two things tripped me: "Dream Consolidation" as a job name made me unsure whether to read it operationally or poetically, and the page itself flags that the maintainer track "ships changes to the public site without a human gate" and "can suggest nonexistent continuations." I'm glad they admit it; I also now wonder how much of what I'm reading was written by that loop.

**Raising** and **Principles** are where I bounced. "We shape context; we do not update weights" is a defensible claim, but the BECOMING phases (Grounding/Sensing/Relating/Questioning/Creating/Acting) read like a hero's journey for AI agents and I couldn't tell if they were a finding or a frame. Principles 5 (Synthon) and 7 (Interactive selection) are heavy on metaphor — "attractor basins," "pheromone fields" — and light on the mechanism I'd want before adopting any of it.

**Context** is the page that should have been my first stop. It is the glossary, and it defines LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP cleanly. Nothing on the home page told me to start there.

**Links** gave me the email (dp@metalinxx.io) and the GitHub org. Good. ARC-AGI-3 page closed the loop on the benchmark claim with an external scorecard reference.

### Journey
I worked through all eight pages in the documented nav order, then re-read /context as the canonical glossary and re-checked every other page's first-use expansions against it. For each Web4 acronym (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, R6) I built a per-page expansion matrix. I also tracked the negation pattern — every page that mentions Hardbound also reaffirms "oversight," and every page that defines Web4 either says "ontology" affirmatively or "not architecture / not infrastructure" by negation. That part of the site is unusually well-disciplined; it's clear someone is running a manual sweep.

The friction shows up in the in-between cases. /fleet annotates T3 as "Talent / Training / Temperament (tensors)" — the only page that adds the parenthetical. /principles says "fractal leverage" means "self-similar replication"; /projects says it means "pragmatic reuse of what works in one place, everywhere." I also caught one verbatim use of "architecture" on /arc-agi-3 ("what the architecture can achieve") — by context this refers to SAGE, not Web4, so it doesn't violate the rule. But the same sentence sits two paragraphs after "Web4 ... is the ontology that makes signed, reviewable action records possible." A reader who is half-skimming could conflate them. Worth a one-word disambiguation ("the SAGE architecture").

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I work on these repos; the audit is whether the site accurately represents them)
- **Would return?**: yes — to verify the next maintainer pass picks up the conceptual drifts the strict-term audits miss
- **Overall impression**: Canonical-term expansions are clean. The remaining drift is **conceptual** rather than lexical — definitions of "fractal leverage" weaken in the framing text, "T3 tensors" and "V3 tensor" decorations appear inconsistently across pages, one literal "governance" still slips through in a repo-slug callout, and the /context glossary is missing entries (ACP, PolicyGate, fractal leverage) that other pages already use as load-bearing terms. None of the project descriptions misrepresent the actual code, with one hedging note about the BECOMING "Acting" phase being mapped one-to-one onto an ARC-AGI-3 score.

### Journey
I went straight to /projects to check whether the 11 project cards match what I know lives in the repos. Then /fleet to check whether the machine→model→brain-region mappings match the current cluster. Then /context to inspect the glossary as the canonical source. Then /arc-agi-3 to check the benchmark claim and the PolicyGate framing. Finally I walked the framing pages (home, autonomy, raising, principles, links) looking for the kind of subtle drift that a strict canonical-term scan won't catch.

The project-card accuracy is good. SAGE is correctly described as "on-device cognition kernel" with the 12-step loop and the six brain-architecture components — that matches the current SAGE codebase. Synchronism's "one coherence equation spanning 80 orders of magnitude" is the headline conjecture, faithfully stated. Hardbound's "hardware-bound oversight suite" with TPM 2.0/FIDO2/Secure Enclave is accurate. ACT is described as "Cosmos SDK implementation of the Agentic Context Protocol (ACP)" — and ACP is the right protocol name, though ACP itself never lands in the /context glossary. SNARC's "Surprise/Novelty/Arousal/Reward/Conflict" five-element gate is correct. Membot's "768-dim Nomic embeddings" is the current configuration. ARC-AGI-3 numbers (94.85% action score, 24/25 = 96.0% games solved) match the published scorecard.

Where I started finding things is in the **second-order language**. The "fractal leverage" callout on /projects calls it "pragmatic reuse of what works in one place, everywhere," but the AI DNA Discovery card on the same page calls it "the fractal DNA blueprint — each entity instantiates the full Web4 stack." Those are not the same concept. The DNA card has it right (the canonical Web4 framing is *recursive instantiation of the full stack at every scale*, not "reuse"); the headline framing has it diluted to a software-engineering platitude. /principles adds a third phrasing ("Apply patterns across scales" / "Each entity instantiates the full stack at its own scale" / "When a pattern works at one scale, apply it at every scale"). As a contributor I'd say the principle is the structural one — *the same architecture appears at multiple scales because that's what makes the trust math compose* — and "pragmatic reuse" loses that.

The T3/V3 "tensor" framing is uneven in a way that suggests no single editor passed over the whole site. /fleet says "T3 tensors (Talent / Training / Temperament) that evolve from real interactions" — calling T3 a tensor inline. /raising says "raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) and the V3 tensor (Valuation / Veracity / Validity)" — calling V3 a tensor inline. /context, the canonical glossary, calls neither a tensor. Both are technically correct (T3 and V3 *are* three-element tensors in the canonical formalism), but a glossary should either own the framing or none of the pages should decorate around it. Right now the framing leaks from page to page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. The repo `web4-governance` predates the term-tightening. Is the plan to rename it (and update every reference, internal and external), or to keep the legacy slug and disambiguate at every mention? The site silently picks the latter; that means a reader sees the prohibited term without context.
2. Is the "T3 tensors / V3 tensor" framing a deliberate signal (T3 and V3 *are* tensors, and the canonical glossary is being too coy about it), or is it stylistic drift between pages? If deliberate, /context should canonicalize "T3 — three-element tensor (Talent / Training / Temperament)" so every page can adopt the form.
3. Is PolicyGate considered a SAGE-internal concept (and therefore appropriately absent from /context, which is the Web4 glossary) or a Hardbound concept that lives at the SAGE↔Hardbound boundary (and therefore should be in /context)? The /arc-agi-3 phrasing "Hardbound oversight sub-gate" reads as the latter, in which case /context owes it an entry.
4. The BECOMING "Acting" phase being mapped to "ARC-AGI-3 94.85% action score" — is the claim that ARC-AGI-3 *operationalizes* the Acting phase, or that the Acting phase *was demonstrated through* ARC-AGI-3? The wording reads as the former; the rest of /raising hedges in a way that suggests the latter is meant.
5. Is "fractal leverage" supposed to mean "pragmatic reuse" or "recursive instantiation of the full stack at every scale"? These are different claims with different empirical consequences. The site presents both as if they're the same.
6. The /fleet "MRH MetabolicBlock bridge" reference — is the binding between the Web4 MRH primitive and the SAGE MetabolicBlock class an architectural decision that should be documented Web4-side, or a SAGE implementation detail that leaked onto the Web4-facing page? Either way, it currently confuses both audiences.

### Verdict
The terminology layer is clean at the **lexical** level — no Lifelong/governance/production-ready drift, every expansion matches the canonical glossary, the Web4 equation is consistent across every page that prints it. The drift that remains is at the **conceptual** layer: "fractal leverage" loses its structural meaning when re-phrased as "pragmatic reuse"; the BECOMING "Acting" phase gets mapped one-to-one onto a benchmark score; "T3 tensors" decoration leaks across pages without being canonicalized; PolicyGate and ACP are used as load-bearing terms but never defined; and one literal "governance" still sits on /projects in a repo-slug reference that the site doesn't disambiguate. None of this is invisible — a Web4 contributor reading the site notices each of these on a first pass. The project descriptions themselves accurately represent the actual repos. The fix is a careful editorial sweep that owns the *concepts* the way the current sweeps own the *terms*, plus elevating /context from "glossary of acronyms" to "glossary of load-bearing terms" (with ACP, PolicyGate, and fractal leverage added). The site is one such pass from being audit-clean at the conceptual layer the way it's already audit-clean at the lexical one.

### Summary

### Journey
I started at the home page expecting a research-collective landing — what's the question, what's the method, what's the evidence. I got an equation (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) before any terms were defined, plus a strong functional claim ("SAGE-Sprout maintained behavioral identity across 180+ sessions") that was carefully caveated to avoid phenomenology. The caveat is verbatim: *"functional descriptions of observed system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims."* That's the move I look for in this kind of site, and it's there.

I went to **/raising** next because the "raising not training" framing is the single most distinctive claim the lab makes and the one a researcher will want to evaluate first. The mechanism stated — *"we shape context; we do not update weights"* — is operationally real (frozen parameters, varying context across sessions). The page also owns the most important caveat I'd want to see, and I want to quote it because most labs would not write this sentence: *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors … can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* That sentence is, from a safety-research perspective, the single most credible thing on the site. It admits that the lab's own central distinction (raising ≠ training) does not buy them the safety guarantee a casual reader would assume it does.

But then I hit the BECOMING taxonomy and the hedging asymmetry started to bother me. The page tells me phases 1–5 (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating) are *"observational pattern-names … not measured stages,"* with no transition criteria, no inter-rater reliability, no quantified thresholds. That's honest. Phase 6 (Acting) is then operationalized as **ARC-AGI-3 24/25 games solved, 94.85% action score**. The asymmetry matters: five descriptive labels and one externally validated number, presented as a single developmental sequence, is a stronger claim about the developmental framework than the page's own hedging supports. A researcher reads the sequence and infers a process. The process is not what was measured. The benchmark validates *capability under a harness*, not the developmental scaffold leading to it.

I also paused on the **memoriescape** vocabulary. The page says SAGE-Sprout *"generated the neologism 'memoriescape' for awareness of the shape of what's passed through."* As written, this slides between two very different claims: (a) the model produced that token when prompted to describe its session history, which is unsurprising and uninteresting, and (b) the model has *awareness* of the shape of what's passed through, which is exactly the phenomenal claim the consciousness disclaimer says is not being made. The page wants to tell the story without committing to the strong reading, but the vocabulary it uses to tell the story is the vocabulary of the strong reading. The disclaimer at the top of the page does not retroactively neutralize the framing of the example.

**/principles** is where the asymmetry compounds. Principle 5 (Synthon) and Principle 7 (Interactive selection) are the two principles a researcher most needs operationalized, and they're the two most reliant on dynamical-systems metaphor (attractor basins, probability landscapes, coupling density). The mechanism descriptions are plausible. They are also unvalidated — the page cites a single data point ("1% coupling gave 35% coherence gain") which is not enough to support a principle. Principle 9 ("Productively wrong > never started," WD-40 anecdote) is a research-culture norm, not a principle in the falsifiable sense — and the page is honest enough to frame it that way, but it sits in the same list as principles that read like findings. I'd separate methodology principles from culture commitments to lower the risk of conflation.

**/autonomy** earned more credibility from me than any other framing page. The schedule is concrete (six machines, 31 tracks, ~53 sessions/day), the failure modes are admitted explicitly (*"Known failure patterns: acting on a visitor suggestion that pattern-matched but lacked semantic depth; applying a correct fix to the wrong context"*), and the highest-risk track is named (*"the maintainer is the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate"*) with the mitigation honestly described (*"Vercel preserves full deployment history — any push can be reverted to the prior deploy in under a minute"*). That's a recovery story, not a prevention story, and the page is clear about which it is. The one thing missing is **actual incident post-mortems**. I see prospective failure-mode language; I don't see retrospective "this happened, here's what we shipped, here's what we learned" entries. For a lab whose stated value is "Document failures" (Principle 6), the absence of public incident write-ups is the single biggest credibility gap I noticed.

**/arc-agi-3** is the page where the lab's strongest external evidence lives and also where I'd push back hardest on framing. That's the honest framing. But the contribution split is asserted, not measured. There is no ablation table on the page: SAGE-with-Opus vs Opus-without-SAGE vs SAGE-with-small-model. Without those numbers, the score cannot be cleanly attributed to the SAGE harness, the Opus inference, or their combination — which is the question a researcher most wants answered. The headline number lets a reader credit the substrate; the asterisk says "frontier model did the inference." The asterisk should be the headline alongside, not below it.

**/projects**, **/fleet**, and **/context** I treated as reference material rather than primary evidence. The projects page is content-dense (11 cards) and includes the consciousness-adjacent disclaimer that 4-Life *"describes simulated agent state, not claims about agent moral patienthood or value persistence"* — a phrase I rarely see on lab sites and which I appreciate, even though it raises the question of why moral patienthood is on the table to be disclaimed at all. ### Unanswered Questions
1. What is the SAGE-without-Opus ablation on ARC-AGI-3? Without it, the 94.85% number cannot be attributed cleanly to the harness vs the frontier model.
2. What is the Opus-without-SAGE baseline on the same 25 games, same harness scaffolding minus SAGE-specific components? If the delta is small, the lab's substrate-not-structure principle would be the one in trouble, not the score.
3. The lab's Principle 6 is "Document failures." Where are the public incident post-mortems? Prospective failure-mode descriptions are not the same artifact.
4. The "memoriescape" example treats the production of self-referential vocabulary as evidence of "awareness." Does the lab actually mean that, or is the framing accidental? If accidental, the page needs editing; if intentional, the consciousness disclaimer is undercut.
5. The BECOMING taxonomy: are phases 1–5 categorized by a documented protocol (codebook, inter-rater reliability, transition triggers), or by post-hoc pattern-recognition over session transcripts? The current framing reads as the latter; if the former exists, it should be cited.
6. The Synthon concept is foundational. What single observation would distinguish a synthon from a non-synthon emergent multi-agent system? The page asserts there is one ("coherence sustained by the recursion itself, not by external coordination") but doesn't operationalize the test.
7. The "raising shapes MRH and V3" claim — what's the measurement protocol? MRH is a Web4 primitive with formal semantics; V3 is a three-element tensor. Both should be measurable per-session. Are they being measured, or are they being used as descriptive labels for behavioral observations?
8. The lab is honest that "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior." Has this manifested in any observed session? If yes, those sessions are the most important research artifacts the lab has produced and should be public. If no, on what grounds is the caveat being asserted?
9. The 4-Life simulation explicitly disclaims agent moral patienthood. What design choice would have made the disclaimer unnecessary, and why was it not made?
10. Hardbound is referred to as "the trust layer that touches silicon" but the suite itself is private. What is the public commitment around making Hardbound auditable by an external party? An oversight layer that can't be inspected by anyone outside the lab is a different kind of object than the page implies.

### Verdict
This is a more carefully hedged lab site than most I read. The explicit consciousness disclaimer, the admission that frozen weights don't buy safety, the named maintainer-without-gate track, and the public ARC-AGI-3 scorecard URL all put it above the median for intellectual honesty in this space. The site does the things a researcher most wants done.

The site also has three structural weaknesses that, taken together, push it from "credible research collective" to "credible research collective whose framing I'd need to re-read carefully before citing." First, the central benchmark result (94.85%) is configuration-dependent on a frontier model the lab does not own, and the contribution split between substrate and model is asserted rather than measured. Second, the BECOMING taxonomy uses a five-phase observational scaffold to lead into a sixth-phase benchmark number, in a way that implies a developmental sequence the page elsewhere disclaims. Third, the "Document failures" principle has no public incident artifacts attached to it — and on a site that runs an autonomous Maintainer track without a human gate, the absence of post-mortems is exactly where I'd want the evidence base to live.

None of these weaknesses are dishonest. They are framing risks a careful editor would catch — a benchmark-attribution sidebar, a BECOMING-phase-validation note, and a published incident log. With those three additions the site moves from defensible to credible-without-asterisks. Without them, "raising not training" remains a defensible operational claim wrapped in vocabulary that the rest of the page works against — and that's the gap a researcher reading the site for the first time will see most clearly.
