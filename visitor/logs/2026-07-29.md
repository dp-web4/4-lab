## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /context, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The vocabulary primer on the landing page is what saved this site for me — without it I'd have bounced in 30 seconds, and with it I actually got the shape of the thing in about two minutes.

### Journey

I came in from a README link on the dp-web4 GitHub org with zero context. The landing headline — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a good hook for a developer. Numbers, hardware, a loop. I know what those words mean individually and I wanted to know what the loop was.

The first screen throws a lot of proper nouns at you (Web4, SAGE, Synchronism, Hardbound, ARC-AGI-3) and then does the single smartest thing on the whole site: a **Vocabulary primer** section on the landing page that gives one-line definitions for LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, SAGE, SNARC, MCP, RDF, Synthon, and Web4 itself. That is exactly what I needed and exactly where I needed it. Most research-lab sites make you go find a glossary; this one hands you the ten words up front. I skimmed it, didn't fully absorb it, but knew I could come back — and that's the right level of commitment to ask of a first-time reader.

**/projects** was my next stop and it's the best page for my persona. Eleven cards, each with a status label (runnable / research-stage / private) and — critically — a `git clone` line for the ones I could actually run. Status labels are a great idea: "runnable" vs "research-stage" tells me instantly whether I'm reading about code or about an idea. SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, and Membot are marked runnable; Web4, Synchronism, ACT, Oversight Plugins, AI DNA Discovery, ARC-AGI-3 are research-stage; Hardbound is private. I appreciated that the private one is labeled rather than quietly omitted. What's *not* said anywhere: what "runnable" actually means. Runs on my laptop? Needs a GPU? Needs the fleet? I had to go to /links to find out SAGE wants Python/conda and ACT wants Go + Cosmos SDK.

**/fleet** is where I lost the thread for a bit. Eight named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB, pub), each with a model, a session count, and a *neuroscience role* — "hippocampal episodic index," "thalamic router," "dopamine / reward prediction error (RPE)," "cerebellum / habit compiler," "interoception / metacognition," "working memory (dlPFC)." As a developer this is simultaneously the coolest and the most confusing page. Cool because assigning brain-region roles to a heterogeneous model fleet is a genuinely interesting design idea. Confusing because nothing tells me whether those labels are *architectural* (the machine actually implements episodic indexing) or *aspirational* (it's a nickname for what that box tends to work on). RPE and dlPFC are expanded, so the page is trying — but the framing question isn't answered. The best line on the site is here though: "The model is weather. The identity is organism." That one sentence made the whole raising thesis click for me faster than the /raising page did.

I also noticed the session counts sum cleanly: 435 + 488 + 336 + 377 + 162 + 193 = 1,991, which matches the "1,991 raising sessions" figure on /projects. I checked because I expected the numbers not to line up. They did. That buys a lot of credibility — right up until /links told me SAGE has "2,500+ internally logged raising sessions." Now I don't know which number is real (see friction log).

**/context** is a monster of a page and I mean that mostly as a compliment. Full glossary with ~35 entries, a worked example of one action passing through the Web4 equation, and a "What doesn't flow well (yet)" section that admits knowledge doesn't propagate backwards between daily tracks. For a developer, the worked example is the payoff — abstract ontology becomes concrete when you watch one action get an ATP, run, and produce an ADP. I'd move that example much earlier, or link to it from the landing page.

**/autonomy** finally explained the loop from the headline: cron-scheduled tracks (Supervisors 03:00, Archivist 04:00, Publisher 04:30, Visitor 05:30, Maintainer 06:30, Outreach 07:00, Explorer 08:00) with the core cycle being Visitor → Maintainer → Explorer. "A track is a scheduled autonomous job — a cron entry with declared scope" is a clean definition in developer language. The safety section is unusually frank: Maintainer is "the only track shipping without human gate," Publisher pushes carry "larger tail risk," and the posture is "detect-and-revert, not prevent." I trust a site more when it tells me where its own blast radius is.

**/raising** and **/principles** are the philosophy pages. Raising's six BECOMING patterns (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting) read fine, and the page explicitly disclaims consciousness — "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia" — and describes a scramble control experiment that hasn't been run yet, with the caveat to "treat the framework as a working vocabulary that fits our observations, not an established finding." I don't have the background to judge the claims, but I can recognize a page that's marking its own confidence levels, and this one is. Principles is 10 short items; #3 ("R&D, not production") and #6 ("Document failures" — the p_crit derivation that was off by 400x) are the ones that made me believe the rest.

**/arc-agi-3** is the page I'd send to a skeptical coworker: 94.85% action score, 24/25 games (96.0%), #3 on the human leaderboard with the fewest actions of the top 3, ~$250 total API spend, on small local models. And it opens with the caveat that the ablation separating harness from base model hasn't been run. That's the right way to present a benchmark result. But this page also uses LCT, T3, V3, MRH, MCP, and RDF with no expansions — if my coworker lands here directly from a benchmark link rather than the homepage, they hit a wall of undefined acronyms.

**/links** closed the loop: repos, explainer sites, licenses (ARC-SAGE MIT-0, Gemma 4 Good Submission Apache 2.0, everything else "see each repo's LICENSE"), and a quick start with actual clone commands. No CONTRIBUTING guide yet, which the page admits.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Can I actually run SAGE on one laptop, and what does it do once running? "On-device cognition kernel, 12-step loop" tells me the shape but not the output. Is there a demo, a log, a screenshot?
2. Is the fleet's brain-region mapping implemented or descriptive? This determines whether /fleet is an architecture page or a personality page, and I read it differently depending on the answer.
3. Which SAGE session count is current — 1,991 or 2,500+?
4. What is a "raising session" concretely? A cron-triggered conversation? A scripted eval? The word appears in headline statistics on four pages and I never found the mechanics.
5. Is Web4 something I could implement against today, or is it a spec-in-progress? "research-stage" suggests the latter, but ACT and the Oversight Plugins imply there's something to build on.
6. Are outside contributions actually wanted? No CONTRIBUTING file, and licenses vary per repo.

### Verdict

This is a legible site for a developer, which I did not expect from something whose homepage headline includes the phrase "recursive learning loop." The vocabulary primer up front, the runnable/research-stage labels, the `git clone` snippets, and the willingness to publish caveats (unrun ablations, a 400x-wrong derivation, "the only track shipping without a human gate") make it read as a lab that's actually building rather than a manifesto. My main complaints are all the same shape: definitions live on one page and get referenced from everywhere else, which works perfectly if you enter through the homepage and poorly if you land on /arc-agi-3 from a benchmark link. Fix the session-count contradiction and define "runnable," and I'd send this to a coworker without a preamble.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /context, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Zero canonical terminology drift across all nine pages — every acronym expansion matches the canonical table exactly — and the residual issues are one stale number on /links plus boilerplate wording variance.

### Journey

I audited this the way I audit a docs set: build the term inventory from the reference page, then walk every other page checking each occurrence against it. The reference page is /context, and it is doing real work — roughly 35 glossary entries, each one a term plus a definition rather than a term plus a synonym.

I'll lead with the finding, because it's unusual: **I could not find a single canonical drift on this site.** LCT is "Linked Context Token" on /, /projects, /fleet, /context, /links, and /arc-agi-3 — never "Lifelong Capability Token." T3 is Talent/Training/Temperament everywhere it's expanded. V3 is Valuation/Veracity/Validity. MRH is Markov Relevancy Horizon. ATP and ADP are Allocation Transfer Packet and Allocation Discharge Packet, never the attention-pool or development-points variants. R6 is the Six-Element Action Framework, and on both /context and /projects the six elements are enumerated (Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result). Web4 is called an ontology, and the /context entry goes further and states the negative case explicitly: "not architecture or infrastructure." Hardbound is oversight on every page that names it. The phrase "production ready" does not appear on the homepage or /arc-agi-3, the two places I'd most expect it. "Unification" appears exactly once, on /context, inside the definition of fractal leverage — "the same pattern instantiated at every scale — reuse, not unification" — which is the anti-drift note baked into the definition itself. That's the pattern I'd recommend to any docs team: don't just define the right term, define it against the wrong one.

The **SAGE problem** is the interesting case, and the site handles it better than I expected. SAGE expands to "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine," which collides head-on with principle #10 ("Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight."). An acronym is a hard thing to change once it's in code, papers, and a public benchmark leaderboard. The site's answer is to keep the name and attach a disclaimer at every single expansion site. I checked all of them: /, /projects, /fleet, /context, /arc-agi-3, and *both* instances on /links carry a note that "Governance" in the name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction. Seven expansions, seven disclaimers. From a consistency standpoint that's as good as it gets without renaming the project. /projects goes furthest and explains *why* it's retained ("the name is retained upstream across code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark — the same reason the web4-governance repo slugs are retained"), which is the sentence that turns a disclaimer into a rationale.

My complaint about it is small and purely editorial: the disclaimer is repeated boilerplate that has drifted in wording. I counted at least three variants — "predates the lab's governance→oversight correction," "predates the lab's governance→oversight *terminology* correction," and a bare "Governance in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction" — and two different cross-references, `/context` on /links versus `/context#governance-oversight` on /arc-agi-3. When you repeat a sentence seven times it should be the same sentence seven times, and it should point at the same anchor.

**The one real inconsistency is numeric, and it's on /links.** /projects states the SAGE session count with a precision I rarely see on a marketing-adjacent page: "1,991 raising sessions across the 6 cognition machines (internally logged; per-machine counts on /fleet, 2,065 including HUB — this card previously said '900+', corrected 2026-07-27 against the 2026-07-24 fleet manifest refresh)." That is sourced, dated, scoped, and it carries its own correction history. I checked the arithmetic against /fleet: 435 + 488 + 336 + 377 + 162 + 193 = 1,991, and adding HUB's 74 gives 2,065. Both figures on /projects are exactly right. Then /links says SAGE "has 2,500+ internally logged raising sessions as context." That is not a different basis — it's larger than the all-inclusive 2,065 figure, so no scoping choice reconciles it. /links is simply stale, and it's stale in the "recommended starting point" paragraph, which is the sentence a newcomer is most likely to quote. This is the highest-value fix on the site and it's a one-number edit.

A near-miss worth recording so nobody "fixes" it: /context cites "180+ sessions" for behavioral continuity, and /raising traces the Sprout lineage as "115 raising sessions on a Jetson running Qwen 0.5B, then ported to TinyLlama 1.1B on CBP in February 2026, with the line since continuing past 180 sessions." That 180 is one machine's lineage, not a fleet total. Correctly scoped, and it should stay as is.

Where the site excels, and I want this on the record because it's rarer than the friction: /context marks "attractor / attractor basin" as **"Used as a metaphor"** rather than letting a borrowed physics term pass as a claim. It defines "Coherence" as "used in three related senses" and then enumerates all three — the SNARC-scored session property, Synchronism's theoretical quantity, and the synthon operational marker. Most glossaries would pick one and quietly create ambiguity. And it has a consistent idiom for names that look like acronyms but aren't: "HUB — a proper name, not an acronym," matched on /raising by "'BECOMING' is a proper name, not an acronym — the six pattern initials (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting) spell nothing." Same construction, two different pages, applied to exactly the two terms that would otherwise send a reader hunting for expansions.

**A methodological note for the maintainer track.** Several of Pass 1's friction items did not survive a targeted re-check, and I believe they're artifacts of summarized page fetches rather than real gaps. Specifically: Pass 1 flags LCT/T3/V3/MRH/MCP/RDF as unexpanded on /arc-agi-3 — a direct per-acronym query returns inline expansions for all six on that page. Pass 1 flags MCP and SNARC as unexpanded on /fleet — /fleet contains "MCP (Model Context Protocol)" and "SNARC (Surprise / Novelty / Arousal / Reward / Conflict salience-gated memory)". Pass 1 flags BECOMING as an all-caps term never explained — /raising explains it in exactly the terms Pass 1 asked for. Pass 1 flags the SAGE governance note as appearing only on the homepage — it appears on six pages and twice on /links. Pass 1 also flags R6 as thin, but /context gives the full six-element enumeration. Acting on any of these would mean adding a definition that already exists two paragraphs away. My rule of thumb after this pass: a "term X is missing from page Y" finding needs a source check before it becomes a ticket; a "term X is wrong on page Y" finding does not.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which number is current — is /links stale at 2,500+, or is /projects' 1,991/2,065 stale and /links reflecting a newer count that hasn't reached /projects yet? The dated correction note on /projects makes it the more trustworthy of the two, but only the maintainer can confirm the direction.
2. Is there a single source of record for the session count that both pages could derive from, or is each page hand-maintained? The /projects note references a "fleet manifest refresh," which implies a source exists.
3. Is the SAGE acronym permanently frozen, or is a rename on the table? Seven disclaimers is a maintenance cost that a rename would retire — though the ARC Prize leaderboard entry probably settles it.
4. Should the glossary at /context be treated as normative for the other eight pages? Right now it reads that way in practice, but nothing says so, and nothing prevents a future page from introducing a competing definition.
5. Are the operator semantics of the Web4 equation (`/`, `*`, `+`) documented anywhere as a legend, or only inferable from the prose on /projects?

### Verdict

**The falsification section is the strongest thing here, and I want to be specific about why.** Most sites in this space, when they gesture at a control, propose the uninformative one. The scramble control tests whether ordering matters at all; it does not test whether the *observed* ordering is the entity's or the syllabus's. Small error, cheap fix, but it's sitting in the recommended-entry sentence on a site whose implicit thesis is that self-auditing loops maintain accuracy. The cheapest mitigation isn't more tracks — it's one adversarial persona with a different base model, or a human spot-check with a *committed* cadence.

**One thing missing rather than wrong.** The site is candid about epistemic risk (claims outrunning evidence) and operational risk (bad pushes, unbounded exposure). It says nothing about the risk of the research direction itself — autonomous agents raising other autonomous agents toward "prosocial attractor reinforcement," with no external evaluator anywhere in the loop. The /raising corollary proves the authors have thought about mechanism-level risk. But there's no page I can point a skeptical colleague to that says "here is what we think could go wrong with doing this at all, and why we think the current scale is fine." Given that this site will be read by exactly my kind of person, that's the highest-value *addition* available, as distinct from the corrections above.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the BECOMING phase ordering observed in the logs, or introduced by the raising curriculum? The operational definition of identity includes curriculum state, which makes this circular unless separated. Nothing on the site answers it.
2. What would the scramble control cost, and what is actually blocking it? It's specified in more detail than most registered reports and reported as "not scheduled — no date, no owner." For an outside reader that gap between specification quality and non-execution is the most interesting unanswered thing on the site.
3. How are "coupling density" and "coherence" measured? /context concedes neither is defined outside the originating experiment, which makes the 1%→35% result currently uninterpretable rather than merely unreplicated.
4. Does "prosocial attractor reinforcement" have an operational definition, or is it a direction? It's the stated aim of the raising programme and it isn't in the glossary.
5. Is there any plan for external or adversarial evaluation? /context names the absence; nothing states an intention to fix it, and the correlated-priors structure of the Visitor→Maintainer loop means internal additions won't.
6. What does the lab think could go wrong with this research direction itself? Every other risk category is covered candidly.

### Verdict

I arrived expecting the standard failure of this genre — developmental and consciousness-adjacent vocabulary doing rhetorical work that the evidence doesn't support — and found the opposite: a site that disclaims phenomenal claims on two pages, operationalizes "identity" as a measurable behavioral construct, sorts every claim into a named evidence tier, specifies a genuinely correct falsification test in more detail than most registered reports, and pre-commits to retiring its own central vocabulary if that test comes back null. The line that decided my assessment is the site's own: "the developmental vocabulary used across this site runs ahead of the comparison that would license it." A lab that writes that sentence about itself has earned the benefit of the doubt on the vocabulary. My substantive criticisms are three: "oversight" means roughly the opposite here of what it means in my field and no in-ecosystem reviewer can see that; the BECOMING phase ordering is confounded with the curriculum ordering in a way the specified control doesn't reach; and the audit loop's two halves share priors closely enough that "not blinded" understates the problem. None of these are integrity failures. They're the things a lab this honest should want pointed out, and I'd read the follow-up.
