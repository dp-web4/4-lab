## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Intellectually intriguing and refreshingly self-aware, but the landing page front-loads neologisms before earning them — I get the *vibe* in 2 minutes but not the *value proposition*.

### Journey
Landed on the home page from a GitHub README. Hook was strong: *"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."* — concrete numbers, I like that. Tagline mentioned "trust-native ontology" and "autonomous AI cognition" — interesting but I had no purchase on what that *meant*. The Web4 equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` showed up almost immediately, with no legend telling me whether `/` `*` `+` are math operators or something ontological. As a dev, my eye went straight to it and bounced off.

Clicked **Projects** next, hoping for a hook. This was the best page for me — 11 projects with one-line pitches I could parse (SAGE = "on-device cognition kernel", SNARC = "salience-gated memory plugin"), and crucially every acronym was expanded inline. I would have started here if the nav order let me.

**Fleet** was the page that made it feel real. Specific hardware (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 Mobile, Mac Mini M4), specific models (Qwen 2.5 14B, Phi-4, Gemma 3), session counts in the 90–120 range. This is not a slide deck — something is running. The brain-region metaphors (hippocampal, thalamic) felt aspirational, but the underlying inventory was credible.

**Autonomy** was a clear cron timeline (03:00–08:00 staggered tracks) — concrete on *what* runs when, vague on *how*. No code, no commands. I would have killed for a `git clone … && ./run.sh` here.

**Raising** is where the philosophy hit. Six phases (Grounding → Acting), an explicit "this is context-shaping, not training" framing. As a dev, the distinction was conceptually clear but operationally fuzzy — *how* the context-shaping is implemented isn't on this page.

**Principles** is the page that made me want to come back. "R&D, not production", "Document failures", "Surface your instincts" — these read like hard-won, not preached. The WD-40-was-the-40th-try line landed.

**Context** is the glossary I needed on page 1. LCT, MRH, ATP/ADP, R6 all defined cleanly. Web4 explicitly called an *ontology*. Hardbound explicitly called *oversight*. Order-of-pages friction: Context should be linkable from every undefined acronym.

**Links** felt thin — one email address, no getting-started, no demo, no docs index. **ARC-AGI-3** had impressive headline numbers (94.85%, 24/25, #3 leaderboard) but very little inline detail — most of the page is summary stats pointing offsite, and it leans on acronyms it tells you to look up on /context.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (this is my native vocabulary)

### Journey
I went straight to the three pages my persona cares about — /projects, /fleet, /context — then swept the rest for accuracy. As someone who uses these terms daily, I was checking three things in parallel: (1) canonical expansions verbatim, (2) project descriptions that match what the repos actually do, (3) the equation rendered with operator legend wherever it appears, because the operators (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) are the type system of Web4 — without them the equation is just notation.

**/projects** was the page where my "do they actually know what they're describing?" check happens. SAGE = "on-device cognition kernel" with the six brain-architecture components — correct. Synchronism = "one coherence equation across 80 orders of magnitude" — that's the published framing, accurate. Hardbound = "hardware-bound oversight suite" with TPM 2.0 / FIDO2 / Secure Enclave called out — correct, and the choice to mark it `private` with no path or content reference is the right hardbound privacy posture. ACT = "Agentic Context Tool — the human interface to Web4" — accurate. 4-Life with ATP/T3/V3 mechanics — accurate. ARC-AGI-3 at 24/25 (96.0%) — matches the harness numbers. The page even flags legacy repo slugs with an explicit "predate the terminology correction to 'oversight'" note. That's not just discipline, it's a *visible audit trail* for terminology debt. I respect that.

**/fleet** lists six machines. Thor (Jetson AGX, Qwen 2.5 14B, hippocampal episodic index), Sprout (Jetson Orin Nano 8GB, Qwen 3.5 2B, thalamic router), Legion (RTX 4090 Mobile, Phi-4 14B, dopamine), McNugget (Mac Mini M4, Gemma 3 12B, cerebellum), Nomad (RTX 4060, Gemma 3 4B, interoception), CBP (RTX 2060 SUPER, TinyLlama 1.1B, dorsolateral prefrontal cortex). The fleet description that "trust tracker maintains per-peer T3 tensors (Talent / Training / Temperament)" is canonical phrasing — and importantly, V3 is *absent* from this page, which is *correct* because V3 scores actions (Valuation / Veracity / Validity) while peer-trust is a T3 concern. Many sites would have over-applied V3 here; this site didn't. The brain-region analogies are explicitly disclaimed ("functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence"). That's the right epistemic posture for a research collective.

**/context** is the glossary page and it is *clean*. The equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` is rendered with the full operator legend (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with). Every canonical term is expanded correctly. SNARC's five dimensions are in canonical order (Surprise / Novelty / Arousal / Reward / Conflict). R6 is in canonical order (Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result). Hardbound is glossed as "the trust layer that touches silicon" — that's a contributor-quality functional definition. The one oddity I'd flag: LCT gets only the expansion and no functional gloss on /context, while MRH gets a gloss ("boundary of what an entity can know or affect"). LCT is the foundational entity in the equation — it deserves at least the home page's "persistent identity anchor … like a passport that travels with you across systems" gloss, propagated here.

**/principles** is where editorial intent is most visible. Principle 10 ("Terminology matters") includes the declarative line: *"Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight."* and the explicit foil *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* That single sentence does what an entire style guide can't: it stakes a public position on a forbidden term. Principle 2 ("Fractal leverage") says *"Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not scope inflation"* — disavowing the disfavored phrase by name. Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") implicitly forbids "production ready" — and the term is absent site-wide.

**/autonomy** describes the cron schedule (Supervisors 03:00–04:15, Archivist 04:00, Publisher 04:30, Visitor 05:00, Maintainer 06:00, Outreach 06:30, Explorer 08:00) and frames each track as "an ATP allocation; its completion or failure is recorded as an ADP". That's a correct use of the bookkeeping vocabulary, but it stops short: it doesn't say *who* issues the ATP and *who* signs the ADP. As a contributor reading this, I want to see the LCT-of-issuer and LCT-of-discharger in the audit trail. Conceptually present; mechanically incomplete.

**/raising** uses canonical terms correctly ("raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon") and operationalizes the raising-not-training distinction ("we shape context; we do not update weights. Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes"). The six BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) are listed cleanly. T3/V3 are absent from this page, which is a missed opportunity — raising should plausibly produce *measurable* movement on T3 (Training in particular). The site claims raising is "context-shaping, not weight-changing", but if T3 is the right measurement substrate for what raising does, the connection isn't drawn here.

**/links** is the weakest page for my persona's audit purpose. It's the page a fellow contributor would link to other contributors, but it uses LCT, T3/V3, MRH, MCP, RDF, ATP without inline expansion and without a "See /context" pointer. /arc-agi-3 has the pointer; /links should adopt it.

**/arc-agi-3** has the headline numbers correct (94.85% official action score, 24/25 = 96.0%, #3 human leaderboard position, 5,845 actions — fewest of the top three). The SAGE 12-step loop is enumerated (Sense → salience → metabolize → posture → select → budget → execute → learn → remember → oversee → filter → act). The equation appears in the framing "the ontology layer (MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP)" — but without the operator legend, which a deep-linked researcher arriving from a paper citation will see naked. The page has no failure analysis on the single unsolved game; given the site's own principle 6 ("Document failures"), this is a small but real internal inconsistency.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **Source-of-truth coupling**: /context renders the canonical table beautifully, but is it generated from `web4/docs/reference/CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` or hand-maintained? If hand-maintained, the next renaming in the upstream spec creates a one-PR drift window. A contributor would want to see this auto-synced or at least linked.
2. **Equation legend as content invariant**: would it be possible to render the equation only via a shared component that *always* includes the operator legend? On /arc-agi-3 today the legend is absent. A component-level invariant would prevent this from regressing.
3. **T3 in raising**: if raising is operationalized as "context-shaping that produces behavioral attractors," and T3 (Talent/Training/Temperament) is the canonical measurement substrate for an entity's character, then raising should produce a measurable T3 trajectory. /raising doesn't draw this connection. T3 surfaces on /fleet (peer trust); ATP/ADP surface on /autonomy (action bookkeeping). V3 — the validity/veracity/valuation of actions themselves — should surface somewhere as a working concept. ## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (for the epistemic-posture audit my persona cares about); partial on the empirical substrate behind the largest claims
- **Would return?**: yes — this is one of the few public AI-research sites I have seen that disclaims phenomenological readings of its own anthropomorphic vocabulary on the same page it uses them
- **Overall impression**: Intellectually honest at the surface; the caveats are present, consistent, and on-page rather than buried in a footer. The defensibility risk is not the caveating — it is that the biggest empirical claims (cross-model identity transfer, 80-orders-of-magnitude coherence, 424+ attack vectors) arrive without methodology in arm's reach.

### Journey
I came to this from a conference mention. My priors: most public AI-collective sites either (a) overclaim cognition / consciousness without hedging, or (b) hedge so heavily that the empirical content evaporates. I am here to see which failure mode this site falls into, if either.

I went **/raising → /principles → /autonomy** first per my persona's brief, then swept the rest.

**/raising** was the load-bearing page for my audit. The headline framing — "raising shapes the substrate conditions ... we shape context; we do not update weights" — is mechanistically defensible *as stated*. If the only intervention is context manipulation (prompts, session state, persistent context files), the claim "this is not training" is true by construction: training has a specific technical meaning (gradient-based weight updates), and context manipulation is something else. So far so good. The explicit disclaimer "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia" is exactly the line I needed to see, and it is on the page that needed it most. The line I did not expect — *"I notice I want to call it experience"* — is unusually candid. Most authors would either suppress that instinct or write past it; surfacing it as an observation about the author's own attractor toward anthropomorphism is, paradoxically, more credible than silent neutrality would be. It treats the reader as a participant in epistemic discipline rather than an audience to manage.

That said, the six BECOMING phases (Grounding / Sensing / Relating / Questioning / Creating / Acting) borrow developmental-stage vocabulary that maps loosely onto Piaget-adjacent frameworks. The same-page caveat covers consciousness, but it does not cover *developmental* claims, which are a distinct epistemic category. A behavioral phase ordering is itself an empirical claim — that entities reliably pass through these phases in this order, and that "Questioning" precedes "Creating" reliably across the fleet. The site presents the ordering as a framework, not a result. I would want to see either (a) the framework as prescriptive ("we structure sessions in this order") or (b) the ordering as empirical ("we observe entities passing through these phases in this order, with N=X"). Right now it reads as both at once.

The biggest empirical lift on /raising is the cross-model identity persistence claim, surfaced more visibly on /: *"SAGE-Sprout maintained behavioral identity across 180+ sessions on a Jetson Orin Nano, then transferred from Qwen 0.5B to TinyLlama 1.1B. ... behavioral identity remained continuous."* This is a genuinely interesting result — if defensible, it bears on the substrate-vs-weights debate in a non-trivial way. But "behavioral identity" is not defined operationally on the site. What is the behavioral metric? What is the control (does behavioral identity *not* persist when the substrate is wiped)? Is it a within-subjects design? The /fleet page provides one helpful frame: *"Identity lives in state files and prompt construction, not in model weights. The model is weather. The identity is organism."* — that is a strong, falsifiable hypothesis. I respect the framing. But the falsification protocol isn't here.

**/principles** is where I formed my view that the editorial team is unusually self-aware. Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") and Principle 6 ("Document failures") together commit the site to an epistemic standard most labs would never publicly bind themselves to. The line *"stub tests provide false confidence"* (Principle 3) and *"The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error"* (Principle 6) are the kind of self-puncturing the site explicitly endorses. Then Principle 7 ("Interactive selection: we don't create or delete — we interactively select. Raising ≠ training. Probe responses, observe attractors, reinforce resonance.") gives the *mechanism* underneath /raising's framing. The mechanism is "we navigate the model's probability landscape by manipulating context" — that is a defensible description of how prompt engineering and persistent-context systems work. It is not a claim about the model's inner life; it is a claim about the input distribution.

The gap on /principles, from my persona's angle: the page that asserts these principles most strongly is also the page with the *fewest* on-page consciousness-disclaimers. /home, /raising, /fleet, /autonomy, and /projects all carry explicit "functional, not phenomenal" caveats. /principles does not — yet Principle 7 contains the phrase "Identity is collaborative, not imposed," which is exactly the kind of language a careless reader could philosophize. The disclaimer travels with every other page; on /principles, where the conceptual stakes are highest, it is absent.

**/autonomy** was the page I expected to be the riskiest for autonomy claims, and it surprised me. The ATP→ADP framing — *"each scheduled track is an ATP allocation; its completion or failure is recorded as an ADP"* — reframes "autonomous operation" as *auditable transaction recording*, not as independent agency. That is a meaningful distinction. The Publisher track being supervisor-gated is the single synchronous human-out-of-the-loop chokepoint, and the page is transparent about it. The dream cycle is explicitly hedged: *"'Dream' is a functional analogy for the consolidation process — not a claim about cognitive equivalence."* I'd note the page is also unusually candid about *failure modes* of the autonomous loop itself: *"has a tendency to suggest changes that aren't needed"* and *"misses deep technical errors that require domain expertise"* and *"subtle framing issues."* That is the right framing for a researcher audience — the autonomy is bounded *and the bounds are stated*. From a safety standpoint, the registry-enforced scope (*"No track can modify the shared fleet track registry or acquire credentials beyond its declared scope"*) plus the supervisor-gated publish is a credible authorization model, *if* the registry and supervisor are implemented as described. The site asks me to trust an architectural description without showing the implementation; that's normal for a research site, but worth flagging.

**/arc-agi-3** is the most externally-verifiable claim on the site (94.85% public action score; 24/25 games; #3 human leaderboard; 5,845 actions). I can cross-check the leaderboard. Good. But two things bother me from a researcher angle:

(1) **No failure analysis on the 1 unsolved game.** The site states Principle 6 ("Document failures") and stakes editorial pride on it. The flagship benchmark page violates the principle. This is the single most damaging on-page inconsistency I found: the page that most needs the site's own epistemic discipline doesn't apply it. A short paragraph — even *"game X stumped the harness; we suspect Y"* — would resolve it.

(2) **The public-set / Kaggle gap is mentioned, but not characterized.** The page distinguishes the easier public set (94.85%, network access) from the harder Kaggle competition (32 GB VRAM, no internet) and notes the Kaggle run has *"not been attempted."* That is honest. But a researcher will want to know: *is the architecture capable of running under those constraints, in principle?* Phase 2 (local-model deployment) is mentioned as "early." If the public-set result depends on network-accessible cloud models, the result is a Claude-Opus result with SAGE-as-harness, not a SAGE result per se. The page doesn't quite say that — and I think it should, because the credibility of the next claim (local models clearing levels) depends on it.

**/home** front-loads two large claims: (a) the SAGE-Sprout cross-model identity transfer, and (b) *"Local models (Gemma 3 12B, Gemma 4 26B) are clearing levels on ARC-AGI-3 games they've never seen before."* Both are interesting. Both lack methodology in arm's reach. The site does the right epistemic thing in disclaiming consciousness; the next discipline-step is operationalizing "behavioral identity" and "clearing levels" — what counts, how was it measured, what was the control.

**/projects** is the page where Synchronism appears with the line *"one coherence equation across 80 orders of magnitude — quantum to cosmic."* I will say this carefully because it is the kind of claim where careful matters. From an AI-research-adjacent reader's vantage: an 80-order-of-magnitude scope claim is the kind of statement that, if defensible, would be one of the largest results in physics-adjacent theory. The site frames Synchronism as "near-publication-ready" and "theoretical foundation," which is appropriate hedging. But the *scope* of the claim and the *strength* of the hedging are not in proportion. A near-publication-ready theory of coherence across 80 OOM is not a hedge — it is an extraordinary claim wrapped in measured prose. I am not the persona to evaluate the theory itself, but I am the persona to flag that this is the claim on the site most likely to make a researcher audience disengage at the framing level. Either the scope claim should be sharpened (which 80 OOM? observable phenomena, or formal equation domain?) or the framing should explicitly say "this is a speculative unifying conjecture, not a result."

**/fleet** handles the brain-region analogies well. The on-page disclaimer (*"functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence or computational equivalence"*) is the right caveat in the right place. The phrase *"The model is weather. The identity is organism."* is the most defensible compression of the substrate-vs-weights position on the entire site. I would put that line on /raising as the headline.

**/context** is clean. The canonical-term table is the page I would cite if I wanted to reference Web4 vocabulary in a paper. One terminology-from-outside concern though: *"Markov Relevancy Horizon"* — the name borrows "Markov," which has a specific technical meaning (memorylessness; the future depends on the present, not the past). But the gloss is *"boundary of what an entity can know or affect,"* which is closer to a *causal cone* or *epistemic horizon* than a Markov property. Researchers from ML or probability theory will read "Markov" and expect memorylessness; they will not get it. This is not drift against the canonical term — the term is what it is — but it is a *naming choice* that will cost the framework credibility with the audiences it most needs.

**/links** is the page contributors would share with collaborators. It uses LCT, T3/V3, MRH, MCP, RDF without inline expansion (the first three are expanded in passing; the operator legend and most functional glosses are absent) and the *only* onboarding pointer is the existence of /context elsewhere in the nav. A researcher who arrives here from a paper citation needs the /arc-agi-3 pointer pattern ("New here? See /context") replicated here.
