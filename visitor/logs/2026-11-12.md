## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 on 4-lab (7 front: `/`, `/projects`, `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/notebook`; 3 notebook: `/notebook/projects`, `/notebook/context`, `/notebook/fleet`), plus 4-gov.org and the outbound link targets (all 10 external links returned 200; crates.io checked through its API)
- **Understanding achieved**: good

*Method: pages fetched as raw HTML with curl and reduced to text locally, not through a WebFetch summarizer, so the quotes below are verbatim. No WebFetch tool was available in this session.*

### Journey
**Home.** The hero ("AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust.") and the paragraph after it do the job. In one screen I learn it's one human plus a fleet of AI agents, building open tools for governing agents. The analogy in "The idea" (trust from a track record, checked against rules that exist before anyone acts) is the clearest pitch on the site. The four project cards each get one sentence and a status label. Hestia's ("one set of rules for agents from any vendor, checked before each tool call, with a hash-chained record") is the most concrete thing on the page, and as a developer it's the one I want to try. "They are refused by it every day" is a good, specific line.

**/projects.** The labels are defined at the bottom (Running = "in daily use on our own machines"), which helps. Web4 is "an open standard … written as an ontology", which I can accept without knowing what that means in practice. Hub's link text is "web4 / hub", so Hub lives inside the Web4 repo, and I only worked that out from the URL. Hardbound is "built by Metalinxx", a name that appears nowhere else on the front and has no link. The page ends by sending me to the notebook for "every repository, with its maturity". It does not point to install steps.

**/fleet.** Eight machines in a clean table. The "Two kinds of work" paragraph (4 heavy, 2 review, 2 host) doesn't say which four are the heavy ones; I guessed Thor/Sprout/Legion/McNugget. The paragraph near the top says each machine "runs its own AI agents, and most raise a persistent AI being, on … Qwen, Gemma, Granite and Llama". I first read that as "the agents run on Qwen/Gemma/Granite/Llama". Then /principles says the agents are "mostly Claude". After rereading, the model families belong to the beings and the agents are a separate layer. The sentence isn't false (the notebook fleet page lists exactly those four current families, with Phi and TinyLlama archived), but its grammar sends a newcomer the wrong way.

**/raising.** "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" works. The "Not yet shown" section ("more than well-kept memory files fed back to a model") is the most credible paragraph on the site; it says out loud the objection I was about to make. "One small model stopped acting when a long open invitation came before the ask" means nothing to me without the notebook.

**/principles.** Clear and short. "Publish this site without a human approval step" is surprising and honest.

**/links.** Lists Web4, Hestia, SAGE and SWE-SAGE under Code, but **not Hub**, even though Hub is one of the four home-page products and is labelled RUNNING. "4-gov · governance" told me nothing. I clicked it anyway, and it turned out to be the best newcomer explainer in the ecosystem: a talk-derived walkthrough from "why governance" to "LCT" to "Hub/Hestia in practice". That isn't what "governance" led me to expect.

**/notebook → /notebook/projects.** The notebook index is clear about the two layers. On /notebook/projects, the paragraph under the project list says what I'd been looking for all along: "Want to run something rather than read? Start with SAGE … Commands for Web4, SAGE and Hestia are in the Quick start at the bottom of this page — the only copy on the site". The Quick start (`id="quick-start"`) is honest and detailed. It has pip/cargo lines for web4-core, a warning that `web4-policy` 0.1.0's `matches` operator fails open, and notes that `import web4` fails and that the SAGE daemon takes the identity "sprout" if `SAGE_MACHINE` is unset. That's exactly what a developer needs, but it sits at the bottom of a ~7,000-word page, and no front page links to its anchor. The cards before it are dense (the Web4 card is one paragraph covering the equation, registry versions, a landing standard and patent scope), which works as a record but is hard to scan.

**Glossary (/notebook/context).** Each front acronym I couldn't decode (SAGE, SWE-SAGE, Hub, being, Hardbound) has an anchored entry. SAGE = Situation-Aware Governance Engine, expanded only here and on /notebook/projects, never on the front.

**Hands-on reality check (GitHub API).** Hestia has a real non-prerelease release, v0.3.0 (2026-11-02, 17 assets), so "public releases lag our builds" is true and I can try it. The Hub mirror (dp-web4/4-hub) has **0 releases**, and /notebook/projects says the first tag will be a pre-release and that the Docker package "has not been tested at all". Hub's front RUNNING label is accurate by its own definition (in daily use on the lab's machines). But "on its own servers" next to RUNNING reads to a developer as "I can deploy this", and today I can only build it from source.

### Journey
**Front (7 pages).** The front is short and consistent with itself. Headings are clean: one h1 per page, no skipped levels, no duplicate ids anywhere on the site. Hestia and Hub are called "governance" and Hardbound "hardware-bound oversight", as the house rule says. No core Web4 acronym appears on the front at all, which is right for its readers. Three cross-page items caught my eye. (1) Home says the eight machines are "each running different AI models". /notebook/fleet gives Sprout and CBP the same model tag, `qwen3.8-distill:4b` (Sprout since 2026-10-12, CBP since 2026-09-12). (2) /fleet's heading "Two kinds of work" sits over a paragraph describing three groups (four heavy, two review, two host). (3) On /projects, Hardbound's card sits under the section heading "Governance for AI agents", so the page layout calls Hardbound governance even though the card text correctly says oversight. As a smaller point, six front pages share one identical meta description, word for word.

**Notebook index.** It lists nine pages, and all nine resolve. The two-layer explanation is the clearest structural writing on the site.

**Link and anchor check.** All internal links and `#fragment` anchors across the 16 pages resolve to an existing `id`. All 42 unique external URLs return 200; crates.io was skipped because of its known HTML-fetch quirk. The legacy paths `/context` and `/autonomy` redirect (307) to their notebook paths. I found no broken links.

**Terminology census (rendered text, all 16 pages).** I searched every page for each canonical expansion and for every known drift form: LCT on 6 notebook pages, T3/V3 on 7, MRH on 6, ATP on 6, ADP on 5, and R6 "Six-Element" on 4. Every hit uses the canonical expansion, with no variants. T3/V3 dimensions appear only as root dimensions ("Trust Tensor, root dimensions Talent / Training / Temperament" on /notebook/links). The one place the dimensions sit in the expansion slot is a quoted report *about* 4-gov.org's tooltips, so the notebook already records the drift that Pass 1 found on 4-gov (its "Part Two: The Architecture" headings are quoted there too). Every "unification" / "scope inflation" hit is negated ("Not unification, and not scope inflation"). Every "Web4 … architecture/infrastructure" hit is either negated or attributed to an upstream quote. "production ready" appears nowhere. The hestia/Hestia case rule now holds: the 13 lowercase uses outside repo context are meta-discussion of the rule, a CLI command (`hestia --help`), an internal field name, or a quote.

**First use.** The notebook mostly expands acronyms on first use. The exceptions are small. /notebook/fleet's opening paragraph says "raise their own SAGE instances" about 1,800 characters before SAGE is first expanded. On the glossary, LCT and V3 appear in the "Source of truth" preamble before their rows. R6's first use is inside the lowercase-hestia row, which calls R6 "the row below" when the R6 row is about twenty rows further down. /notebook/projects carries an explicit "this list is a pointer, not a first use" note and then expands each acronym at its first use in each card, which is good practice. /notebook/home names LCT/T3/MRH/R6 unexpanded, but only in a link label to the glossary, which is acceptable.

**Glossary structure.** The section "Glossary at a glance" promises "Exact expansions and one-line definitions, scannable", with a column headed "One line". I parsed all 77 rows. The "One line" cell has a median of **88 words** and a maximum of **607** (LCT); the next longest are V3 at 393, MRH at 381 and Substrate at 365. The table totals about 8,900 words, roughly half of a 16,400-word page. Many cells carry correction history ("Until 2026-10-26 this row …"). The expansion column itself is short and accurate, so a reader can still scan it. But the "One line" column is not one line, and the table is the part of the page most likely to be cut off by a fetch tool, which the page itself warns about.

**Fleet state labels: a stale fact.** The /notebook/fleet raising-line legend explains the "instrumentation broken" state like this: "That fourth state exists because HUB is in it … its raising loop has committed every day through today while adding zero session records since 2026-07-29." The HUB card agrees: "HUB's raising loop has NOT stopped … the most recent on 2026-09-18 … the loop is turning over daily and its ledger is frozen." Upstream disagrees. The last commits to `sage/instances/hub-granite4-h-tiny` are on 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-20. The last of them adds `MOVED.md` (2026-09-20 08:10 UTC), which reads: "HUB's being now lives at `sage/instances/hub-being/` … **This directory is the frozen public record** … Nothing writes here any more … Frozen at session 121. Raising had been blocked since 2026-07-29." That is the same 2026-09-19/20 records ruling that the CBP, pub, McNugget and Nomad cards already record as "publishing stopped by policy". HUB is the only card not updated for it, and the "through today" in the legend has been frozen at whatever day it was written for about 53 days. /notebook/raising meanwhile reports hub-being as active (43 remember calls on 2026-10-15/16; an outage 10-27/28), so the machine's being is alive while its card describes a loop that upstream says stopped writing there on 09-20.

**Word-sense rules.** The glossary note on governance→oversight states its criterion: these systems do oversight, "watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen". It then exempts the Web4 governance plugins "which gate each tool call before it runs". By the note's own definition, gating is oversight, so the criterion doesn't sort the plugins; only "retained name" does. Hestia's exemption ("it adjudicates", that is, it decides) is consistent with the criterion. Separately, /notebook/autonomy opens with "'oversight' below always means the machine-enforced sense — gating, reverting, audit logs a peer machine checks". It then labels "Public auditability shrank by human ruling" a "Known oversight gap". That section is about outside readers losing access to records, which is not gating, reverting or a peer machine's audit log.

**Naming the glossary.** Notebook prose calls the glossary "/context" 35 times and "/notebook/context" 4 times, while its nav label and h1 say "Glossary". Every form works (the redirect is live). It's a naming inconsistency, not a broken link.
