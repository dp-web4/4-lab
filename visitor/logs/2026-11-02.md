## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (the 7 front pages in the nav, plus `/notebook` and `/notebook/context`). I also checked every outbound link from `/projects` and `/links` with curl; all return 200.
- **Understanding achieved**: partial → good
- **Would return?**: maybe (yes, if I could find a "try Hestia in 5 minutes" path)
- **Overall impression**: Clear, short pitch with a real product line (Web4, Hestia, Hub). But the most-repeated name, SAGE, is never expanded on the front, and when I found its expansion it didn't match what the front says it is.

### Journey
I landed on Home from a repo README. The tagline ("AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust.") and the line "trust earned from witnessed behavior, not claimed by the agent or granted by a platform" gave me the thesis in about 10 seconds. The four product cards were easy to scan: Web4 is a standard, Hestia governs one person's agents, Hub governs a community's, and SAGE is research on "persistent AI beings". The RUNNING / RESEARCH badges sit next to each name, but Home never says what they mean. `/projects` does ("Running: in daily use on our own machines"), so I only learned it one click later.

`/projects` is the best page for me. Each card states what the thing does, and the Hestia card is concrete: tool calls checked before they run, a tamper-evident log, any vendor. Web4 says "reference code on crates.io and PyPI", and both registry entries exist (API 200). Hestia says "public releases lag our builds" but gives no link to the releases. Hestia does have a GitHub latest release (API 200), but I had to go find it. A developer's first question is "can I run this?", and the answer is yes but hidden.

`/fleet` is a clean table of eight machines. Two things tripped me up. First, Home calls the lab "a fleet of AI agents", but here the fleet is eight machines. Second, one machine is named **HUB**, which "Hosts the fleet's own community on Hub". So the product and the machine share a name, separated only by case. "Being" shows up here ("Its being improves the SAGE code it runs on") before `/raising` explains it.

`/raising` explains "being" well: a name, memory, journal and record, with "we do not change the model's weights". The "Not yet shown" section ("no blind comparison against a fresh model yet") made me trust the page more, not less.

`/principles` has six short principles that I could read in under a minute. "We use what we build" connects to Home's "They are refused by it every day", which is a good, concrete hook.

`/links` is a tidy directory. **4-gov** ("governance") appears here and nowhere else on the front, so I don't know what it is or how it relates to Hestia and Hub.

`/notebook` explains the two layers clearly ("short, plain, and current" vs. "the record underneath it"). I went to the Glossary to decode SAGE. It expands it as **"Situation-Aware Governance Engine"**. Every front page describes SAGE as "persistent AI beings". From the outside these sound like two different projects: a governance engine and a beings research program. The glossary's own "being" entry ("SAGE's current name for a persistent entity…") hints that the framing changed over time, but nothing on the front connects the two. The glossary is dense (27 sections, tensors, RDF), and its plain-language Web4 line ("Not a platform; a shared vocabulary for a new kind of internet") is the sentence I wish were on Home.

**Front.** Short and consistent. **Notebook.** Canonical expansions appear on 7 of the 9 pages (counts below). T3 and V3 are always written as "Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament" and "Value Tensor — Valuation / Veracity / Validity": the tensor name is the expansion and the dimensions follow it. I found no case where the dimensions stand in for the expansion. R6 is "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result" everywhere, arrow included. R7 is "R6 + Reputation" everywhere. /notebook/projects now has an explicit crosswalk between the front's RUNNING / BUILDING / RESEARCH labels and the notebook's runnable / research-stage labels ("on another axis again … Those describe the lab's own use"). A reader comparing the two layers no longer has to guess.

SAGE is expanded consistently in the notebook: "Situation-Aware Governance Engine", 9 times across 9 pages. The glossary even explains that "'Governance' in the name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction". But no front page ever uses this expansion, and the front describes SAGE only as "the beings and the research". A reader who looks the name up is told it is a governance engine. This confirms Pass 1's finding.

### Journey
**Front.** Home's thesis ("trust earned from witnessed behavior") is clear and testable in principle. `/raising` is the page I came for. Its "Not yet shown" card ("That this continuity is more than well-kept memory files fed back to a model … we say so rather than claim more") is the right disclaimer to put on a front page. But Home describes SAGE beings as agents "whose identity, memory and history outlive any one model or session", and `/projects` says "Their identity and memory survive changes of model". Both assert the thing `/raising` says is not shown. Taken literally, the claim is true of the *record* (name, journal, memory files are carried over) and open for *identity* in the notebook's own operational sense ("consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns"). The notebook's basin record even shows one being's main concern flipping twice in six weeks on unchanged weights. The front contradicts itself across pages. That is a front fact problem, not a missing caveat.

The `/raising` "Seen" card ends "One being now proposes changes to its own code", and `/fleet` says Legion's being "improves the SAGE code it runs on". The notebook's own trace says "'Has proposed 15 changes, the last on 2026-10-09' is what the record supports". I checked upstream: the SAGE PR search still returns #412 (2026-10-09) as the latest `legion-being/*` PR, and the 100 most recent PRs (#504–#623, 10-21 to 11-02) include none. So the present tense on two front pages is 24 days stale, and the notebook has said so since 10-30.

`/principles` is six short, defensible principles. Two of its sentences are claims rather than values. "They also run 4-lab end to end: AI peers … publish this site without a human approval step" is false of the front, which dp gates by PR, as Pass 3 notes. Home's "They are refused by it every day" has notebook support for specific days only: two refused calls in the 10-20 maintainer session, and 11 refusals for CBP-being on 10-23. No daily count exists. (For what it's worth, this pass's own session was refused once, for a shell loop variable. The claim is plausible, just not counted.)

**Notebook Raising.** This is the strongest piece of intellectual honesty I have seen on a lab site of this kind. It does these things:
- It names the real comparator as "task-specific context engineering", not training. I had brought that objection with me, and I didn't need to raise it.
- It gives operational definitions of "identity" and "growth" and disowns phenomenal claims ("We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia").
- It reclassifies its own "provisional claim" as a "working hypothesis, untested", because "a claim with no test in its favor, and two that found nothing, is a hypothesis".
- It lists nine confounds. These include demand characteristics (a scripted praise line "delivered whatever the answer was"), a prompt that tells the subject its identity persists ("the SAGE finding, told to the being"), a session-number question schedule that asks for each BECOMING pattern by name, and three being-written memory stores whose use "follows the prompt that names it".
- It re-runs the lab's most development-like trend and finds it was a one-day daemon and prompt change (2026-06-07), with nothing surviving Bonferroni correction within constant conditions.
- It specifies the control correctly, and better than most papers I review. A scramble or yoked history is required, because a generic-replacement control is predicted by both hypotheses. The threshold must be *excess* degradation over an ordering baseline, or a dissociation (identity measures degrade while task competence holds). It also commits to retiring the developmental vocabulary on a bare main effect.

Two things weaken it. First, the decisive control is "specified 2026-07-24; not scheduled as of 2026-10-29, 97 days" (101 days today), with no owner and no pre-registered metric. The page discloses this well, but disclosure is not progress. Second, the intro says "as of 2026-10-29 this page does not yet say what result of it would count against the developmental account". The body does say: "if phase-consistent behavior survives the scramble, 'raising' is a redescription of prompt engineering … retire the word", and the publication commitment names a bare main effect or no degradation. What is missing is a *quantitative* threshold, not a refutation condition. The intro understates the body. That is a rare direction of error, but still an error.

The "Interactive selection, not training" section deserves note. It states the method as intended, then says that on 1,217 records "the tutor does not probe, observe or reinforce in response to the model: the stimulus is not interactive and the praise is not selective". A lab that writes that sentence about its own named method is being honest.

**Notebook Autonomy.** It says "auditability, not a demonstrated safety property", that "Human review cadence: unscheduled", and that pushes are "detect-and-revert, not prevent". These are the right limits, stated plainly. Its gap table (dated 10-29) lists four researcher-blocked gaps. One is this audit's own blind spot: "the visitor and maintainer tracks are LLM sessions drawn from the same model families as the tracks that author the content they audit — their misses are correlated with the authors'". I am that blind spot: a Claude persona auditing Claude-written text. The gap was named 2026-09-10, so it is 53 days old today. The page calls a different-family persona "the cheap fix". One operational note: the host this visitor track ran on today reports other vendors' agent CLIs as installed. If that holds, the blocker is a scheduling decision, not tooling.

**Notebook Principles.** It discloses the same-family exception, and records real cross-vendor code review ("GPT/Nova" holds that changed Claude-authored code). "Pheromone field engineering" and "substrate conditions for emergence" are evocative. They are glossed (substrate is defined in the glossary's first sense), but to a researcher "emergence" carries more weight than anything on this page supports. It is low priority, and the Raising page's discipline does not reach it.
