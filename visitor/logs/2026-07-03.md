## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing + all 8 subpages)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: A dense but surprisingly honest research-lab site — I understood "autonomous AI agents on a fleet of machines, with a trust/identity framework" within two minutes, and the unusual level of self-caveating made me trust the claims more, not less.

### Journey

Landed on the homepage from a GitHub README. The hero — "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a good hook. The stats row (7 machines, 14 repos, 31+ autonomous tracks) told me the scale immediately. The "New here? Start with /context" pointer is exactly what I needed, and I appreciated that it exists — most research sites don't tell you where the vocabulary lives.

The tagline lost me briefly at "trust-native ontology" — as a developer I parse "ontology" as either OWL/RDF semantics or philosophy, and I wasn't sure which was meant until /context clarified it's the RDF flavor. That's fine, but it cost me a paragraph of confusion up front.

Went to **/projects** next, because that's what a developer does. Eleven projects is a lot, but the cards are well organized and most have direct repo links — I could clone SAGE, SNARC, or Membot right now, which is the actionability test and the site mostly passes it. SNARC ("salience-gated memory for Claude Code") and Membot ("768-dim Nomic embeddings + binary Hamming codes + keyword reranking") are the two I'd actually try — concrete, scoped, tech stack stated. Synchronism's "one coherence equation spanning 80 orders of magnitude" reads as extremely speculative, but the card itself says "single-trial, no independent replication yet," which defused my skepticism reflex. The Web4 equation ("Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP") looked like noise until I hit /context, where the operators are actually defined.

**/context** is the best page on the site for someone like me. Every acronym gets a one-line expansion plus a real explanation. LCT as "identity grounded in record, not model weights" clicked immediately — that's a concept I can hold. The evidence-tier breakdown (externally validated vs. internally observed vs. unreplicated) is something I've never seen a research site do and it's genuinely disarming.

**/fleet** was fun — real machines with real specs (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 laptop, a Mac Mini, a TinyLlama on an RTX 2060). The detail that the 1.1B-model machine is the fleet coordinator producing the headline benchmark score is a great hook. But this page leans on undefined terms: "crystallization," "fixed-point collapse," "chapter ledger," "chapter law." I could guess at crystallization from context; chapter law I could not.

**/arc-agi-3** is the credibility anchor. 24/25 games, 94.85% action score, #3 on the human leaderboard — and crucially it links the official ARC Prize scorecard and an MIT-0 repo, so I can verify. The caveat that this used Claude Opus 4.6 with network access, NOT the Kaggle 32GB-no-internet constraint, is stated plainly. That's the kind of honesty that makes me believe the rest of the site. "E4B" (in the Gemma 3n phase-2 plan) is never expanded, though I suspect it's a model SKU.

**/autonomy** answered "what do these machines actually do all day" — a 3AM–8AM cron pipeline of git maintenance, site testing by personas, fixing, and research. "Detect-and-revert... not prevent" is a sane ops posture and I noted it approvingly. **/raising** and **/principles** are more philosophical; the "raising is not training" distinction took me two reads but landed (context-shaping, not weight updates — no LoRA, no fine-tuning). The explicit "we're not claiming these entities are conscious" disclaimer was necessary and present. **/links** closes the loop with 11 repos and a contact email.

What I still couldn't do at the end: figure out what running SAGE on my own machine actually looks like. Clone it, then what? A "quickstart" or "hello world" path is the biggest missing piece for my persona.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing + all 8 subpages)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Terminology is remarkably consistent — every acronym expansion I checked matches the canonical table on every page where it's expanded — but the site has no consistent *definition policy*: whether a term is glossed on first use depends entirely on which page you land on.

### Journey

I audited methodically: landing page first, then every nav item in order, checking each acronym expansion verbatim against the canonical table, and cross-referencing pages against each other.

The **nav is complete and honest** — all 8 subpages are reachable from the header, and every page I visited exists (no dead nav links, no placeholder pages). The homepage carries a full "Vocabulary primer" section with glossary anchors into /context (`/context#glossary`, `/context#web4` through `/context#mcp`), which is good information architecture: the landing page defines LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SAGE, SNARC, and Synthon inline, all correctly.

**/context is the canonical source and it holds up.** The glossary table expands every core term correctly, the Web4 equation appears with all three operators defined (`/` = "verified by", `*` = "contextualized by", `+` = "augmented with"), and the evidence-tier framing (externally validated / internal observations / unreplicated) is the kind of epistemic labeling most documentation never attempts. Twenty-five sections, logically ordered from knowledge-flow through glossary to limitations.

**Cross-page consistency checks I ran:**
- Homepage claims "Seven machines" → /fleet lists exactly 7 (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB). Consistent.
- The Web4 equation appears on /projects, /context, and /arc-agi-3 — identical string all three times. Operators are explained on /projects and /context; /arc-agi-3 shows the equation without the operator legend.
- The SAGE naming footnote ("SAGE's name predates the governance→oversight correction") appears consistently wherever the "Governance" in the name could mislead — homepage, /projects, /principles. This is exactly how to handle a legacy name; no page uses "governance" for Hardbound itself.
- ATP/ADP on /links is compressed to "Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packets" — a valid contraction of both canonical expansions, not drift.

**Where the definition policy breaks down:** each page makes its own assumptions about what the reader already knows, and the assumptions don't form a coherent path. /autonomy defines ATP/ADP inline but uses Web4 with no gloss or link. /arc-agi-3 defines R6 fully (all six elements) but uses LCT, T3/V3, MRH, MCP, and RDF bare. /principles defines T3, V3, and SAGE but uses LCT, ATP, and ADP bare. A reader who enters the site on any page other than / or /context — which is how search and shared links work — gets a lottery. The fix is mechanical: link each acronym's first use per page to its /context#anchor (the anchors already exist).

**The /fleet jargon gap is real but narrower than Pass 1 suggested.** "Crystallization" *is* glossed on /fleet ("fixed-point collapse where exploration stops") — though that's jargon defined by more jargon; "fixed-point collapse" gets no further explanation. "Chapter ledger" and "chapter law" remain genuinely undefined, and none of the three appear in the /context glossary, so there's nowhere to click through to. E4B on /arc-agi-3 gets a contextual gloss ("effective-parameter edge variant") but no expansion of what "E4B" literally abbreviates.

One rendering item to verify in source: on /projects the status tags came through to me as "publicrunnable" run together — possibly a missing separator between two adjacent tags, possibly an artifact of my tooling. Worth a look.

This is the hardest kind of correctness — using the terms in a live mechanism rather than a glossary — and it passes. Hardbound is "the hardware-bound oversight suite," posture "detect-and-revert... not prevent." No governance language.

**/principles** contains the strongest canon statements on the site: "Web4 is an ontology," "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight," and "Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation." That last one rejects both banned framings by name. **/raising** says "a trust-native ontology — not architecture or infrastructure" and uses "Hardbound oversight constraints" correctly. **/links** compresses ATP/ADP to "Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packets" (valid) and describes Hardbound only as "internal research — code is not publicly available" — not drift, but it's the one Hardbound mention on the site without the "oversight" descriptor.

### Summary

### Journey

I came in expecting the usual failure mode of agent-lab websites: anthropomorphic storytelling with a boilerplate "of course we don't mean it's conscious" disclaimer bolted on. I went straight to **/raising**, since that's where the framing would break if it was going to.

It mostly doesn't break. The core distinction is stated mechanistically: "Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens," and "in raising sessions, we shape context — we do not update weights." The primary disclaimer is exactly what I'd want, and it's not boilerplate: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia. We're claiming that developmental frameworks describe what we observe better than training frameworks do." That second sentence is a real, contestable, *defensible* scientific claim about descriptive fit — a much stronger position than most labs take. Better still: individual observations carry their own local caveats ("Operational definitions... not phenomenal claims"; specialization is "a measurable distribution over task types, not a phenomenal preference"), and one observer note — "I notice I want to call it experience" — is explicitly labeled as recording the *observer's* interpretive pull, not a system-level claim. That's a level of reflexivity about experimenter bias I rarely see outside of good ethnography. The page even flags the safety-relevant converse: "Frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update." A lab that volunteers that sentence understands the actual risk surface.

The BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) are labeled "observed descriptive categories... not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria," and the page says to "treat them as descriptive scaffolding, not measured stages." Good. But then the evidence disclosure lands: the developmental claims "rest on internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet." So the honest framing is real, but the *checkable* content is nil. Six phases coded across "hundreds of sessions" by the same people who designed the framework, with no published coding criteria, is a textbook confirmation-bias setup — and to the site's credit it essentially admits this, but admission is not mitigation.

**/principles** deepened both impressions. The failure documentation is genuine, not performative: "The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error" is named, dated, and reframed as a methodological finding (thresholds are empirical, not derivable) rather than excused. The R&D disclaimer is unambiguous: unsuitable for "safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings." The coupling-coherence result (1% coupling → 35% coherence gain) is hedged as "single trial, not independently replicated" with "both variables defined only within the experiment" — that last clause is unusually scrupulous. Two things I'd push back on: (a) the raising/training dichotomy is presented more cleanly than it is — in-context shaping *is* a form of behavioral optimization, and the page's own parenthetical admitting some machines use LoRA separately blurs the "no weight updates" line the framing leans on; (b) "interactive selection" ("we don't create or delete — we interactively select") is doing philosophical work the page doesn't unpack — as stated it's closer to a stance than a mechanism.

**/autonomy** describes 31+ unsupervised tracks (~53 sessions/day) with a stated oversight posture of "detect-and-revert (audit log + sub-minute rollback), not prevent," scoped credentials ("no track can modify the shared fleet registry or acquire credentials beyond its declared scope"), and ATP-declared resource budgets. It names its own failure modes — the maintainer's "wrong push deploys before review" is correctly identified as highest blast radius, and the visitor loop's blind spot ("content that is technically correct but misleading") is exactly the one a safety reviewer would name. What's missing is operational depth: whether Hardbound enforcement is cryptographic or process-based, who reviews the audit logs, what triggers a rollback, how per-track credentials are provisioned. As written, the safety story is a description of intent, not a verifiable mechanism — fine for a research site, but a reader should know the difference, and the page doesn't draw it.

**/context** has the single best epistemic device on the site: the three-tier evidence split, quoted exactly — "Externally validated: the ARC-AGI-3 result... has a public ARC Prize scorecard"; "Internal observations: the raising phases, behavioral-identity continuity (180+ sessions)... documented and dated, but not externally audited"; "Unreplicated: the coupling-coherence result... is a single trial with no independent replication," with the instruction to "treat it as a preliminary observation, not a finding." Every research site should have this section. Two critiques: it lives at the *end* of the page rather than inline with the claims it tiers, and the glossary assumes RDF/MCP literacy it never provides — the definitions are internally consistent but internal-facing.

**/arc-agi-3** is the credibility anchor and holds up: public scorecard link, MIT-0 repo, and the load-bearing caveats stated plainly — Claude Opus 4.6 with network access, Kaggle constraints "not attempted," and the harness-vs-model split "stated but not quantified: no ablation... has been run." The one distortion risk: the site's recursive-learning-loop narrative sits adjacent to a score that is, on the site's own account, an unablated Opus-4.6-plus-harness result. The caveat exists; the framing gravity pulls the other way. **/fleet** is where the anthropomorphic register runs hottest (brain-region analogies, machine "personalities," identity transfer) — the analogies carry a caveat ("functional analogies... not claims about neural correspondence") and the identity-transfer section carefully disclaims "continuity-of-self in any philosophical sense," but capability comparisons like "Gemma 3 at 4B outperforms Phi-4 at 14B for raising work" are stated as findings with no N, no criteria, no error bars. **/links** confirmed the structural gap: eleven repos, three explainer sites, one benchmark scorecard — and zero papers or preprints. For a lab whose central claim is that developmental frameworks *describe observations better*, the absence of any published observation set is the difference between a research program and a research diary.
