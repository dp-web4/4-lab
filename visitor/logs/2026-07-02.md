## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Home, Projects, Context, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, ARC-AGI-3, Links — visited all nav destinations)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Intellectually magnetic but built for insiders — I get the *vibe* (distributed agents self-organizing) in 2 minutes, but not the *substance* without a second sitting on /context.

### Journey
I landed from a GitHub README expecting a repo index or a "what is this" pitch. The headline hit hard: **"Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."** That's a great hook — concrete, a little mysterious. The value prop underneath is clear enough at a squint: instead of chasing bigger models, they're studying what *emerges* when a bunch of different AI agents on different hardware are given "the substrate conditions to self-organize." As a dev, "emergence over scale" is a thesis I can hold onto.

Then the vocabulary wall arrived. The landing page has a "Vocabulary primer," which I appreciated — but it front-loads SAGE, Web4, LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, Synthon, MRH, SNARC, MCP, RDF in rapid succession. Some get a one-liner, some assume I already know. The 94.85% ARC-AGI-3 number and "31+ autonomous daily operations" are the kind of concrete claims that make me trust a page more, so those landed well.

I clicked **Projects** next (the developer instinct — "show me the code"). This was the strongest page for me: a table of ~11 projects with clear runnable/research status. SAGE, SNARC, Membot, 4-Life, Oversight Plugins all read as "clone-able, has a tech stack" — TypeScript, WASM, Python. That's the actionable stuff I wanted. But the *first* time LCT appears here, it's used without being expanded on the page — I had to go find it.

So I went to **Context**, which is the glossary. This is where the site finally works: a clean term table with real definitions, the Web4 equation spelled out, and a notation legend. If I'd been told "start at /context" *before* Projects, my experience would've been 2x smoother. The landing page does whisper "start with /context" but it's easy to miss under the louder hook.

**Fleet** was fun — seven named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB) with hardware/model/role. Very tangible. But it re-uses LCT/SAGE/MRH/T3/V3 without definitions, just a "defined in /context" pointer. **Autonomy** was the most developer-native page: cron jobs, SQLite track registry, "detect-and-revert" rollback — I could basically re-implement the scheduling layer from this description. **Raising** and **Principles** are the philosophy core; more on those in the audit. **ARC-AGI-3** gave me the clearest single result on the site (two honest metrics: 24/25 solved = 96%, vs 94.85% efficiency-weighted action score). **Links** gave me the GitHub org and a contact email — exactly what I'd want to actually start.

The headline finding is a good one: **the canonical expansions are clean.** LCT is "Linked Context Token" on all seven pages that use it — no "Lifelong Capability Token" anywhere. The site uses a **hub-and-spoke glossary model**: /context holds the authoritative definitions and the Web4 equation, and other pages either expand inline or point to /context. That's a defensible doc pattern. My friction is with the *edges* of it — a few terms are expanded on one page but left bare on another, and the depth of the /context definitions is occasionally *shallower* than the inline expansion on a feature page (see R6 below), which inverts the expected hub relationship.

The one term that made me stop was **SAGE = "Situation-Aware Governance Engine."** It's expanded identically and consistently on Home, Context, Projects, Fleet, Raising, Principles, and ARC-AGI-3 — so it is *not* drift. But the site's Principle #10 states verbatim: *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* SAGE's own product name hard-codes the very word the site tells readers to unlearn. Pass 1 (the developer) noticed the same tension from a naïve reading. This is a proper noun, so it's arguably locked — but the git history suggests the maintainer already knows (there's an annotation about SAGE "Governance" predating the governance→oversight correction). My issue as a writer: that annotation/footnote is **not applied consistently** — I saw acknowledgement only near the Context/Projects mentions, but Fleet, Raising, Principles, and ARC-AGI-3 all expand "Situation-Aware Governance Engine" with no footnote, so a reader who lands on those pages sees the site preach "not governance" and simultaneously ship a "Governance Engine" with no reconciliation.

### Journey
I went straight to /context to check the authoritative definitions, then /projects and /fleet to verify the site represents the repos I actually work in, then swept the rest.

**The equation is not just present, it's *used correctly*.** `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` renders exactly, with operators `/` = "verified by," `*` = "contextualized by," `+` = "augmented with." What impressed me: the /projects Web4 card writes the equation *back out in prose* — "T3 (Talent/Training/Temperament) **verified by** V3 (Valuation/Veracity/Validity), **contextualized by** Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)." That is the `T3/V3*MRH` sub-expression expanded through the operator legend, correctly. Most sites that quote the equation don't understand it well enough to paraphrase it — this one does. Same for /context's ADP definition ("the spent form of ATP"), which captures the charge/discharge metabolic analogy that ATP/ADP is built on, and T3's "each component is an RDF sub-graph root," which is the kind of detail only someone who's read the model would write. The identity framing for LCT ("grounded in witnessed history, not model weights"; "self-grounding — no issuing authority") is canon-accurate.

**The SAGE 12-step loop checks out.** /context lists it fully — sense → salience → metabolize → posture → select → budget → execute → learn → remember → oversee → filter → act — and /arc-agi-3 references the same 12 steps "with PolicyGate as an oversight sub-gate." Twelve steps, consistent across pages, and the "oversee/filter" late-loop gating matches how the harness actually stages policy checks. No drift there.

**Hardbound is handled exactly right.** "Hardware-bound oversight suite" on /context and /projects; /autonomy explicitly frames it as "oversight rather than governance — it defines authorization boundaries rather than enforcing pre-approval gates," with the detect-and-revert / sub-minute-rollback posture. That's the correct conceptual distinction, not just the correct word. Fractal leverage is "reuse, not unification" on /context and Principle #2. Web4 is "an ontology... not architecture or infrastructure" verbatim. This is a clean sheet on the enforced table.

**Then I hit the one thing I'd escalate.** SAGE expands to **"Situation-Aware Governance Engine"** on all seven pages that spell it out. I contribute to these repos and I know this is the legacy expansion — but it collides head-on with the discipline this very site enforces: Hardbound is "oversight, not governance," and Principle #10 tells readers specific terms prevent mental-model errors. We ship a "Governance Engine" while telling readers to unlearn "governance." The maintainer already knows (there's a git annotation flagging SAGE "Governance" as predating the governance→oversight correction), but that acknowledgement is not surfaced to a *reader* on any of the seven pages. This is the highest-value terminology item on the site and it's a proper-noun problem, so it needs a decision, not a find-and-replace.

Two accuracy catches below my terminology remit but inside my fleet knowledge: (1) Sprout is listed as **"Qwen 3.5 2B"** — there is no Qwen 3.5; the 2B-class Qwen we run is either Qwen2.5 or Qwen3. Thor correctly says "Qwen 2.5 14B," so this is likely a typo, but on a page whose whole purpose is fidelity it reads as a fleet-config error. (2) /arc-agi-3 uses **"E4B"** undefined — this is the Gemma 3n **E4B** ("effective 4B") edge variant, a real Google model, but a reader can't know that; it should be expanded and tied to the Gemma 3n line.

### Journey
I came in from a conference mention with the reflex I bring to any "emergent AI cognition" claim: *where's the load-bearing evidence, and how hard are the consciousness-adjacent claims being pushed?* I went Home → Raising → Principles → Autonomy first (the pages where overreach usually lives), then to /context#evidence to see whether the site polices its own claims, then ARC-AGI-3 for the one externally-facing metric.

The first thing that lowered my guard: the site pre-empts my objections instead of waiting for them. The Principles preamble states up front that "where principles use developmental or identity language... they describe functional system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims." Raising says outright: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia," and uses "developmental language because it fits, not because we're making consciousness claims." Home's brain metaphor ships with "not a claim about neural correspondence"; Fleet repeats "not continuity-of-self in any philosophical sense" twice. This is the disclaimer discipline I usually have to *demand* — here it's volunteered. That earns real credit.

**The "raising not training" framing is defensible — and, more importantly, it doesn't oversell itself.** The mechanistic claim is narrow and correct: "in raising sessions, we shape context — we do not update weights. Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes." A weaker site would have stopped there and implied safety-by-frozen-weights. This one doesn't: Principle 7 says "It is not training; it is also not nothing," and Raising explicitly warns "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior." That second sentence is the single most sophisticated safety statement on the site — it's precisely the in-context-attractor risk that a lot of alignment-adjacent work elides. As a framing, "raising" is a metaphor doing honest work: it's labelled as a metaphor, its mechanism (context-shaping, deepening attractor basins) is stated literally, and its limits are named. I'd accept it in a paper's methods section with the caveats intact.

**The /context#evidence ledger is the most credible thing on the site — because it argues against itself.** It cleanly tiers the claims: (1) externally verifiable — only the ARC-AGI-3 action score, "the one claim an outside party can verify independently"; (2) internal/unaudited — raising phases and behavioral-identity continuity (180+ sessions), "documented and dated, but not externally audited — no published log samples, coding criteria, or third-party review yet"; (3) unreplicated — the 1%-coupling→35%-coherence result, "a single trial with no independent replication," with both variables "defined only within the experiment that produced them." It then names the exact artifacts that *don't yet exist* but would raise credibility: redacted log samples, an ARC ablation baseline, independent replication. A lab that can enumerate its own missing evidence is a lab I trust more, not less.

**Where my researcher skepticism re-engaged.** The honesty is concentrated in the *dedicated* honesty locations and thins out at the marketing surface:

1. **The confound on the one verifiable number isn't co-located with the number.** /context#evidence admits "the harness-vs-model split is stated but not quantified: no ablation (same model, no harness) has been run, so the harness's independent contribution is unknown." That is a *large* caveat — it means 94.85% is a Claude-Opus-4.6-plus-harness score, and the harness's marginal contribution is currently unknowable. Yet the Home hook states "94.85%" flatly, and a casual reader takes away "this lab's system scores 94.85%" when the honest claim is "a frontier model inside our harness scored 94.85%, contribution-split unknown." The caveat exists; it's just three clicks from the boast.

2. **Emergence claims are presented as observations without controls or baselines.** "They specialize... they develop trust relationships... they catch each other's mistakes" (Home) and the synthon "coherence gain" are framed empirically, but there's no comparison condition — no "vs. non-coupled fleet," no baseline specialization rate. For a single-trial result (Principle 5 / Synchronism) this is disclosed; for the general emergence narrative it isn't. A researcher reads "emergent coherence entities that are more than the sum of their parts" as a strong claim wearing observational clothing.

3. **Anthropomorphic verbs are load-bearing between the disclaimers.** The disclaimers are excellent but they're *bracketing*, not *pervasive*: inside the brackets, agents "develop trust," "catch mistakes," the fleet "built a brain," a component has a "niche." The site knows this — Raising even includes the observer note "I notice I want to call it experience" as a flagged interpretive pull, which is admirably self-aware — but self-awareness about the pull doesn't remove the pull for a reader who skims the prose and misses the caveat paragraph.

4. **The autonomy transparency gap.** /autonomy asserts "31+ autonomous tracks... ~53 sessions per day" with no qualifier, but details only 8. The remainder is unaccounted. Contrast this with how carefully the *evidence* page hedges — the autonomy count gets none of that discipline. Separately, the safety posture is honestly disclosed (post-hoc "detect-and-revert... not prevent," Maintainer "ship[s] changes to the public site without a human gate," sub-minute rollback), and the page even lists what the loop *misses* (semantic-depth failures, right-fix/wrong-context). That's good. But the fleet registry itself is described as ungated (out-of-scope for the tracks to modify, yet nothing external gates it) — a single-point-of-authority a safety reviewer would want addressed, and the page doesn't.

5. **The SAGE contradiction is, for a researcher, a coherence tell.** Principle 10 makes "terminology matters" a first-class value and states "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight." Then SAGE expands to "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine" on every page. I don't care that it's a legacy proper noun; I care that a site whose Principle 10 is *terminology discipline* ships an unreconciled contradiction of its own flagship principle, with no reader-facing note. For an outsider evaluating whether the lab practices what it preaches, this is the one place the answer is visibly "not quite."

### Unanswered Questions
1. **Can any developmental claim be checked?** The evidence ledger is admirably honest that raising-phases and identity-continuity rest on unpublished logs — but that means the lab's central thesis is, today, unfalsifiable from the outside. What is the timeline/plan for the redacted-log-samples and coding-criteria the evidence page itself says would be needed?
2. **What is the harness's marginal contribution to 94.85%?** Until the same-model-no-harness ablation runs, the headline number can't be attributed to the lab's system vs. Claude Opus 4.6. The page acknowledges this — when does the ablation happen?
3. **Are the emergence observations controlled?** Is there an uncoupled-fleet baseline against which "specialization" and "catch each other's mistakes" are measured, or are these narrative observations?
4. **What gates the fleet registry** — the one component the autonomous tracks are forbidden to touch but nothing external appears to protect?

### Verdict
As a safety/trust researcher, I find this site refreshingly and unusually honest at the level of *framing*: it disavows qualia, brackets its metaphors, tiers its evidence, documents its failures, and — rare — names the experiments it hasn't yet run. The "raising not training" thesis is defensible and, critically, doesn't overclaim frozen-weight safety. My reservation is not dishonesty but *checkability*: the honest disclaimers all point at the same fact — that almost every developmental claim currently rests on internal, unpublished, unaudited logs, leaving one externally-verifiable number (ARC-AGI-3) that is itself confounded by an un-ablated harness. The lab knows all of this and says so; the fix is less about caveating and more about *shipping the missing artifacts*. The one genuine self-contradiction — Principle 10 preaching "not governance" while SAGE ships a "Governance Engine" — is worth fixing precisely because it's the one place the site visibly fails its own standard.
