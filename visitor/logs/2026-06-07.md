## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (all)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Visually confident and clearly built by people who know what they're doing — but it talks to insiders, and as an outsider I bounced off a wall of acronyms before I figured out *why I should care*.

### Journey
I clicked through from a dp-web4 repo README expecting a "what is this project" page. The landing hits me with "Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." — punchy, I like it. The tagline ("trust-native ontology, autonomous AI cognition, and the theoretical frameworks that connect them") sounds impressive but I honestly couldn't tell you what the lab *does* after reading it. "Trust-native ontology" is three words I understand individually and not together. The one thing that grabbed me as a developer was **ARC-AGI-3: 94.85% action score** — that's a concrete, benchmarkable number I can evaluate. That's what kept me clicking.

I went to **Projects** next because that's where a developer goes. Good news: the cards have repo links, categories (Public/Private), and one-liners. ARC-AGI-3, SNARC, and Membot have *narrow, legible* scopes — I could see myself cloning SNARC and understanding it in an afternoon. The trouble is the flagship cards (Web4, SAGE) assume I already know what T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, and LCT are. There's no "Hello World," no "clone this and run `npm start`." So I have repo links but no on-ramp.

**Context** is pitched as "New here? Start here" and it's the most useful page for decoding the vocabulary — it has the glossary and the Web4 equation. But it's a wall: 20+ specialized terms with no progressive disclosure, and it casually drops cognitive-science terms (RPE, dlPFC) that aren't in the glossary. "Start here" + "here are 20 acronyms" is a rough combination for a true newcomer.

**ARC-AGI-3**, **Fleet**, and **Autonomy** were actually my favorite pages — they're concrete. Fleet lists real machines with real GPUs and roles; Autonomy has a literal timeline of cron jobs. These read like infrastructure I can reason about. **Raising** and **Principles** are more philosophical; Raising surprised me by being well-caveated (it explicitly says these aren't consciousness claims), and the "raising = shaping context, not updating weights" framing actually clicked for me as "in-context learning / prompt engineering at scale." **Links** finally gave me the on-ramp I wanted on Projects: learn vocab at /context → clone SAGE or ACT → open an issue. That should be surfaced *much* earlier.

The BECOMING phases are hedged as *"patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions — not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria,"* with phases 1–5 marked as observational pattern-names rather than measured stages. What raised my estimate of these authors specifically: the page does **not** use frozen weights as a safety blanket. It states *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* That is the correct and uncomfortable thing to say, and most groups selling an "in-context, no fine-tuning" story leave it out. That single sentence did more for the site's credibility with me than any benchmark.

Is **"raising not training" defensible?** Mechanistically, yes, and trivially so: shaping context against frozen parameters is not gradient descent, and they say exactly that ("we shape context — we do not update weights"). The honest tension is that they then borrow heavily connotative developmental language (raising, becoming, identity) on top of a defensible mechanism. They are upfront about the move — they name the "interpretive pull" as an observer artifact "not a system-level claim" — so it reads as disclosed rhetoric rather than smuggled metaphysics. A stricter reviewer would still say the evocative framing does persuasive work the caveats then try to walk back; but disclosed is the honest version of that, and they chose it.

**Principles** is where the intellectual honesty is strongest and one weak spot lives. Strong: Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") carries an explicit liability boundary — *"Nothing here is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings"* — which is precisely the disclaimer I look for and rarely find. Principle 6 ("document failures") and Principle 9 ("evaluate by investigation quality, not correctness") describe a genuinely sound research epistemology. The weak spot: **Principle 5 (Synthon)** presents a "1% coupling → 35% coherence gain" result. To the authors' credit it is labeled *"preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated),"* so this is not concealment — but a single-trial effect size stated as a headline number is the kind of thing that gets screenshotted without its caveat. The caveat is correct; my concern is that it needs to be *inseparable* from the number, because the number will travel alone.

**Autonomy** is framed exactly as a safety reader would want: bounded and auditable, not independent. "Autonomous" is scheduled execution within declared scopes, not agency. The page volunteers its own failure modes — the loop *"misses deep technical errors that require domain expertise"* and *"has a tendency to suggest changes that aren't needed"* — and singles out the Maintainer track for its *"higher blast radius"* as the one component shipping to production without human pre-approval. Guardrails are concrete (scope boundaries on credentials, supervisor gating, a track registry preventing concurrent writes, <1-minute Vercel rollback, dated audit logs). Hardbound is consistently "oversight," not "governance." This is the most reassuring page on the site.

The rest: **Context** is a strong canonical reference and correctly discloses the dual meaning of "/" in the Web4 equation; it also helpfully flags that "tensor" is used non-standardly (a structured multi-component quantity, not a rank-≥2 array) — good, because an ML reader will otherwise misread it. **ARC-AGI-3** is honest about attribution (harness vs. base model) but is the page most exposed to a credibility challenge — see below. **Projects/Fleet/Links** are accurate; Fleet's "identity lives in state files… not in model weights" is the right gloss and is properly qualified as "not a claim about continuity-of-self."

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does the SAGE harness contribute to the 94.85% **independent of Opus 4.6 and network access**? Phase 2 promises this isolation — until it lands, the headline number is a system result, and that should be unmissable, not just disclosed on one page.
2. Has the "1% coupling → 35% coherence gain" been re-run even once more? A second trial (even informal) would change it from "anecdote, honestly labeled" to "weak but real signal."
3. Is the Phase 2 target Gemma **3n** E4B? (If so, /arc-agi-3 has a model-name error.)
4. On what basis is identity claimed to "persist" across substrates beyond behavioral consistency in the prompt? The qualifier is present; the positive claim still leans on a contested distinction.
5. Is there any external review, publication, or third-party replication of any result here, or is all validation internal (plus the one external ARC-AGI-3 score)?

### Verdict
As an AI-safety/trust reader, my trust in *how this lab talks* went up, not down, over the visit — the consciousness question is handled with explicit, point-of-use disclaimers, the autonomy is framed as bounded and auditable with volunteered failure modes, and Principle 3's not-for-deployment boundary plus the "frozen weights aren't safe" admission are exactly the candor I look for and rarely see. The credibility risks are not dishonesty but *detachable* strong numbers: the 94.85% benchmark and the single-trial 35% coherence gain are both honestly caveated *somewhere*, but the caveats don't reliably travel with the figures, and "Gemma 4 E4B" looks like an error that a careful reader will notice. Bind each headline number to its condition and re-run the one-trial result, and this goes from "intellectually honest and I'd cite the framing" to "I'd cite the results too."
