## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good — for what the lab *is*. Partial — for what I'd *do*.
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site explains itself unusually well and pre-empts almost every objection I raised, but it never tells me why a developer would clone anything.

### Journey

Landed from a repo README with zero context. The headline — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a good hook: concrete, countable, no mysticism. First paragraph told me this is a "trust-native ontology ... plus autonomous AI cognition ... across a heterogeneous fleet of machines that teach, validate, and raise each other." I understood the *shape* immediately.

Then the equation hit: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. My first reaction was that `+`, `/`, `*` look like arithmetic and can't be. I was about to log that as friction when I found the operator key sitting right under it, including the note that `/` means two different things in `T3/V3` vs `ATP/ADP` — "same glyph, unrelated semantics." That's a real trap, and the site had already walked into it and marked it. Good.

The Vocabulary Primer on the landing page carries nine terms (LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, SAGE, Synthon, SNARC, MCP, RDF). Putting it on the landing page is the right call — I never had to leave to decode anything. But nine definitions arrive before I have a single reason to want them. I was learning a vocabulary for a thing I hadn't been sold on yet.

/projects was the strongest page for me. The `runnable` / `research-stage` legend is exactly the signal I want, and it's honest about what it means: "clone it and it executes end to end today ... not a claim about polish or completeness." There's a Quick start with an actual `git clone` line, and — critically — "SAGE runs on a single machine — no Jetson required." I had assumed from /fleet that all of this needed Jetson hardware. The site answered that before I asked.

I also hit "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine" on the home page and remembered Principle 10 saying Hardbound is *oversight*, not governance. Looked like an inconsistency. It isn't — the SAGE card says the name "predates the lab's governance→oversight terminology correction; the name is retained upstream across code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark." I just met the name two pages before I met the explanation.

/context is enormous and is basically the site's real documentation. The glossary is thorough and several entries are self-critical in a way I don't usually see — "Emergence / emergent" is defined as *used in more than one sense on this site with no single operational definition yet*, and "Attractor" is explicitly labeled a METAPHOR, not a dynamical-systems object. A site that flags its own loose words is a site I trust more.

/arc-agi-3 is where a developer's skepticism peaks: 94.85%, 96% solve rate, #3 on the human leaderboard. My immediate question was "is that SAGE, or is that Opus 4.6?" The page answers in its own caveat: "the ablation that would separate the harness's contribution from the base model's ... has not been run." They beat me to the punch. Same on /autonomy, which states outright that the visitor and maintainer tracks "are LLM sessions drawn from the same model families as the tracks that author the content" — i.e., the review loop isn't independent. I don't often see a project publish its own weakest link.

Where I stalled: I finished all nine pages knowing precisely what this lab studies and no idea what SAGE would do *for me*. There is no problem statement aimed at a developer — no "you'd use this when...", no comparison to anything I already run, no example output. And across nine pages there is not one line of code, one JSON blob, or one RDF triple. Everything is prose. I read code faster than I read theory, and there was nothing to read.

### Verdict

This is the most self-critical project site I've read in a while — it flags its own undefined vocabulary, publishes the ablation it hasn't run, and admits its review loop isn't independent. The gap is not honesty or clarity, it's *concreteness*: nine pages, zero code, and no answer to "why would a developer clone this." I'd come back, but I'd come back to read, not to build — and I don't think that's the intent.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid — the terminology system is legible and enforced
- **Would return?**: yes

### Journey

I audited this as a terminology system, not as a reader. Method: for each of the nine pages, enumerate every acronym used, record whether an expansion appears on that page, and compare the expansion string against the canonical table. Then check cross-page consistency of every expansion, and check the definition *glosses* — not just the expansion — since an expansion can be canon-correct while the sentence after it drifts.

The first thing to establish is which documentation model the site actually uses, because scoring it against the wrong one produces false findings. It is **not** "define on first use." It is **hub-and-pointer**: `/context` carries a 38-entry alphabetical glossary plus narrative sections, and every other page either expands inline or points at it. `/fleet` states the pointer explicitly — "Vocabulary used in the cards (T3/V3, MRH, SNARC, LoRA, MCP, crystallization, chapter ledger, chapter law) is defined in /context." That is a disclosed choice, not an omission, and I scored it as such. The pattern holds on every page I checked: home carries a nine-term Vocabulary Primer, `/projects` expands inline at first use, `/principles` expands T3/V3/RDF/SAGE/LoRA inline, `/arc-agi-3` expands everything it uses, `/links` expands inside the Web4 description, `/autonomy` expands ATP/ADP (the only two acronyms it uses). Nowhere did I find an acronym used with neither an inline expansion nor a pointer.

Expansion consistency is the cleanest I have audited on a site this dense. Fifteen acronyms, checked on every page where each appears, **zero variants**. LCT is "Linked Context Token" in five places and never "Lifelong" anything. T3 is "Talent / Training / Temperament" in six places with identical slash-and-space spacing. ATP/ADP are "Allocation Transfer Packet"/"Allocation Discharge Packet" in four places. R6 expands to the full six elements in the same order in both places it appears. The only variance I found in fifteen acronyms across nine pages is the ARC-AGI-3 expansion tail: `/context` renders it "…for Artificial General Intelligence, version 3" and `/arc-agi-3` renders it "…third-gen interactive benchmark." Same expansion, different suffix. That is a style nit, not drift.

The glosses held up too, which is where I expected to find something. `/context`'s T3 entry now labels each component individually and — notably — disambiguates the trap word itself: "Training: capability accumulated through interaction history — not gradient training, the sense the same word carries in ML." Same defensive move on "tensor": "'Tensor' here means a structured multi-component quantity — not a rank-≥2 array in the linear-algebra sense." And on V3's third component: "'Validity' here names V3's tensor component, not the methodological sense (internal/external/construct validity) this page's evidence tiers below also use — same word, unrelated meanings." Three separate same-word-two-meanings collisions, all three caught and marked by the site before I got to them. The `/` operator carries a fourth ("verified by" in `T3/V3`, a declared→discharged pair in `ATP/ADP` — "same glyph, unrelated semantics"). A site that hunts its own homonyms is doing terminology work most documentation never attempts.

Which makes the gap conspicuous. **"Ontology" is never defined.** Principle 10 makes it mandatory — "Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology" — and eight pages assert it. The `/context` glossary has no headword for it. The site defines "tensor," flags "emergence" and "coherence" as multi-sense, labels "attractor" a metaphor, and maintains an evidence tier literally named **Unlicensed vocabulary** that names "raising," "identity," and "growth" as not yet discriminated from competent context engineering. "Ontology" is in none of those places. It is enforced, undefined, and unflagged — a third state the site's own taxonomy has no slot for. A reader who doesn't already know the word learns only what Web4 *isn't*.

Second finding, smaller: T3 and V3 are not documented in parallel. T3's entry labels each component ("Talent: aptitude for the role. Training: … Temperament: behavioral disposition under load."). V3's entry gives three unlabeled phrases in sequence — "worth assessed, claims truthful, reasoning sound" — leaving the reader to map them onto Valuation/Veracity/Validity by position. The mapping is inferable and correct, but the two most-paired terms on the site use two different entry structures.

On session counts, I need to contradict a reading I went in expecting. The numbers are **reconciled at their point of use**, not left to clash. `/projects`' SAGE card carries the whole thing in one sentence: "1,991 raising sessions across the 6 cognition machines (internally logged; per-machine counts on /fleet, 2,065 including HUB — this card previously said '900+', corrected 2026-07-27 against the 2026-07-24 fleet manifest refresh)." `/fleet` names "the same basis as the 2,065 cumulative figure on /projects." The 115 and 180 figures on home and `/raising` are explicitly Sprout-line-specific, not fleet totals. Different denominators, each labeled. The residual issue is small: home writes "2,065+" (open-ended) where `/projects` and `/fleet` write "2,065" (a fixed figure from a 2026-07-24 audit, now ten days old). A trailing `+` on an audited number quietly converts it into a running total.

`/autonomy` is the one place where a number is asserted without its decomposition: "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 cognition machines, ~53 sessions per day," with eight entries in the timeline below it. Home's stat block repeats "31+" consistently, so the two pages agree — but neither says whether the eight are track *types* replicated per machine or a sample of thirty-one distinct jobs.

Navigation checked out: eight nav links (Projects, Context, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links) for nine pages, home reachable via the wordmark. No dead links encountered.

**Discrepancy with Pass 1 worth recording**: Pass 1 logged that home uses "Situation-Aware Governance Engine" without the oversight-correction disclosure, and that the explanation lives only on the `/projects` card. I found the disclosure on home in two places — the Key Projects card and the Vocabulary Primer's SAGE entry — reading `"Governance" in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction (see /context)`. Two separate fetches returned it. I'm recording it as present and Pass 1's note as a retrieval artifact, but a human should confirm which of us is looking at the rendered page.

### Verdict

As a terminology system, this is the most disciplined site I have audited: fifteen acronyms, nine pages, zero variant expansions, and — more unusual — the glosses hold too, with four separate same-word-two-meanings collisions caught and marked by the site itself before a reader can trip on them. The one real defect is structural rather than a drift: "ontology" is the word Principle 10 mandates, eight pages assert, and the glossary never defines — a site that defines "tensor" and flags "attractor" as a metaphor has no excuse for leaving its own load-bearing noun undefined. Fix that entry and the V3/T3 asymmetry, and I would hold this site up as an example of terminology governance done right.

### Journey

I came in expecting the usual failure mode for a site with "raising," "identity," and "growth" in its vocabulary: developmental language doing unearned work, with a disclaimer somewhere near the footer. That is not what this is. I want to be specific about what's strong before I get to what isn't, because the strengths are unusual enough that they change what the weaknesses mean.

**The consciousness axis is closed.** /raising's lede — third and fourth fragments on the page, before any section heading — reads *"We use developmental language because it fits, not because we're making consciousness claims"* and then immediately operationalizes: *"by 'identity' we mean consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns... by 'growth' we mean increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims."* A dedicated "What we're not claiming" section repeats it at the end. Home carries it adjacent to the identity claim (*"measurable observables, not philosophical continuity"*), /fleet carries it inline (*"functional descriptions of observed behavior, not consciousness claims — see /raising"*), and /projects extends the same move to a claim I didn't expect anyone to hedge — 4-Life's *"lifecycle vocabulary describes simulated agent state, not claims about agent moral patienthood."* Four pages, frontloaded and adjacent. I have reviewed conference submissions that do this worse.

**Principle 3 does something I have never seen on a lab site**: it names its own out-of-scope domains unprompted — *"Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings."* Nobody makes you write that.

**And /autonomy publishes its own worst finding.** *"the visitor and maintainer tracks are LLM sessions drawn from the same model families as the tracks that author the content they audit — their misses are correlated with the authors', not independent."* /context goes further: *"There is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet."* That is the sentence I would have written in a review, and they wrote it first.

So the site is not overclaiming. My findings are about **which** concession is placed where.

**There are two separate objections to "raising," and the site treats them as one.** Objection A is *are you claiming phenomenal experience?* Objection B is *is this anything other than competent context engineering?* A is frontloaded on four pages. B — the null hypothesis, the specification of a scramble control, and the admission that it is *"not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet"* — sits in "The deflationary alternative," **section 18 of 19 on /raising**, after all six BECOMING patterns and all four "key discoveries." It appears in one other place, /context's *Unlicensed vocabulary* evidence tier (*"not yet discriminated from competent context engineering"*), and nowhere else. It is not on home, where the identity-continuity result is stated. It is not on /fleet, which uses the vocabulary freely.

This matters because B is the load-bearing objection. No AI researcher was going to read "identity" as a qualia claim — the field says "behavioral consistency" and means it. What a researcher *will* ask is whether carrying an experience buffer across a model swap and observing behavioral continuity shows anything beyond what in-context learning already predicts. The site knows this, answers it well, and answers it last. The hedge that travels is the one for the objection nobody had.

I'd call this out less sharply if the site didn't demonstrate the fix on another page. /arc-agi-3 opens with *"One caveat belongs up front: the ablation that would separate the harness's contribution from the base model's (same model, no harness) has not been run — details below."* In the lede, before results. I went looking for the "caveats don't travel" failure on that number and **did not find it** — home's project card carries *"Frontier model + harness — the harness's independent contribution isn't isolated yet (no ablation run)"* adjacent to 94.85%, with the model named (*"Claude Opus 4.6, network-enabled"*). Checked and refuted; recording it as such. So the frontloading discipline exists — it is applied to the one claim with an external scorecard behind it, and not applied to the claims with nothing behind them but internal logs. That is inverted relative to need.

**The terminology finding that only my seat catches: "oversight."** In AI-safety literature "oversight" means *human* supervision — scalable oversight, human-in-the-loop, the entire subfield. This site mandates it to mean the opposite. Principle 10: *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight: watching, gating, and reverting actions, not deciding what should happen."* Read cold by someone from my field, Principle 10 says Hardbound puts humans in the loop. /autonomy says the reverse: *"Consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship."*

The site *has* the disambiguation — /context: *"'Oversight' here is machine-enforced (gating, reverting), not the human-supervision sense the word carries in AI-safety literature,"* repeated on /projects. That is a genuinely excellent piece of terminology work. It is absent from **/principles**, which is where the word is enforced, and from **/autonomy**, which is where a reader learns humans aren't gating anything. The site hunts its own homonyms relentlessly — Training/gradient-training, tensor, Validity, the `/` glyph — and this is the one homonym where the collision is with the reader's field rather than with the site's own other usage, and it's unmarked in exactly the two places it does damage. Worth stating plainly: the governance→oversight correction improves internal consistency and *reduces* external legibility. "Governance" was closer to what an outsider would correctly infer. I'm not suggesting reverting it — I'm suggesting the disambiguation clause is not optional decoration, it's the thing that makes the correction survivable outside the lab.

**On /autonomy, the parts are disclosed and the sum is not.** Individually: the Maintainer is *"the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate"*; Publisher's "validated" means *"git-hygiene and build-health checks passed — not a semantic review"*; the reviewing models are correlated with the authoring models; human log review has *"no committed cadence"*; and for repos, *"once a commit is public it can be cloned, forked, and mirrored before any revert lands."* Every link in that chain is stated honestly. The chain itself is never assembled: there is a path where an autonomous agent publishes irreversibly, the only semantic reviewer is a model with correlated blind spots, and the human check has no schedule. The page's honesty is per-component; the risk is compositional. The site's own Principle 8 ("surface your instincts") is the reason I think this belongs on the page rather than in a log.

**"Raising is not training" — is it defensible?** Two claims, different standing. Mechanistically: *"we shape context — we do not update weights."* Trivially true, verifiable, and the site is scrupulous about it — LoRA is explicitly named as weight-level work *outside* raising. It even volunteers the counter-argument that this doesn't buy safety: *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors... can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* That is a real concession, correctly reasoned, and I would cite it approvingly. As a *phenomenon* claim — that something develops which context engineering doesn't already explain — it is undemonstrated by the site's own account, and the site says so. Fine.

Where the framing does slip is Principle 7: *"It is not training; it is also not nothing."* That is not a claim, it's a placeholder for one. Everything else on /principles is either an operational pattern or a labeled conjecture; this sentence is unfalsifiable as written and sits in the principle that carries the site's central methodological commitment. Principle 5 shows the better form on the same page — *"Preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated)"* plus the admission that both variables are *"defined only within the experiment that produced them."* Principle 7 should be able to say what "not nothing" would look like if false.

**One general property the site discloses claim-by-claim but never states as a property**: every quantitative result here is a single run. 94.85% — one run, no retries, no variance, no CI. Coupling-coherence — explicitly single-trial. 115→180+ sessions — one line, one machine, one transfer event. 1% coupling → 35% coherence gain — one trial. Each is individually labeled, which is why nothing here is dishonest. But a reader assembling the site's evidence base gets N=1 across the board, and the site never says that in one place. /context's evidence tiers come closest and are structured by *provenance* (external / internal / unreplicated / unlicensed) rather than by *replication*. One sentence in that section — "no result on this site is a repeated-trial measurement" — would be a stronger honest-assessment line than any individual caveat, and it costs nothing the site hasn't already conceded piecemeal.

**On the Pass 2 / Pass 3 dispute about "ontology"**: Pass 3 is right that home defines it (*"An ontology (shared vocabulary + relationships) for how AI agents prove identity, earn trust, and account for resources"*) and Pass 2 is right that /context has no headword. I'd add the thing neither seat sees: "ontology" is a live homonym across *my* field's boundary too. Philosophy: theory of what exists. CS/Semantic Web: a formal specification of a conceptualization. Home's gloss is unambiguously the second, and RDF being in the equation confirms it — but the site never names which tradition, and a reader arriving from AI-safety/philosophy will import the first and conclude the lab is making metaphysical claims. Given the site's disclaimer discipline everywhere else, this is a one-clause fix ("in the Semantic Web sense"), and it argues for Pass 3's prescription over Pass 2's: point at home's existing gloss, don't author a second one.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What would falsify "raising"? The scramble control is specified but unscheduled, with no owner and no pre-registered metric. Until it runs, is the lab's position that raising is a *hypothesis* or a *working vocabulary*? /context's *Unlicensed vocabulary* tier says the latter; Principle 7 reads like the former.
2. If the visitor/maintainer loop's misses are correlated with the authors' by admission, what is the plan to break the correlation — different model families, a human reviewer with committed cadence, or an accepted permanent limitation? /autonomy names the gap without naming an intent.
3. Is the ARC-AGI-3 result a single run? No retries, variance, or CI are reported, and the ablation is unrun. If it is n=1, that belongs beside the number as plainly as the ablation caveat already is.
4. Does the deflationary alternative apply to the *fleet-scale* claims too? It is scoped to /raising's patterns. The synthon claim and the 2,065-session figure sit outside it and are never given a null hypothesis.
5. Why "oversight" rather than "enforcement" or "policy gating"? The chosen word collides with a large existing literature in the direction that flatters the system. "Enforcement" would carry the site's actual meaning with no collision at all.
6. What happens to a bad autonomous commit that clears git-hygiene and build checks, is semantically wrong, and is mirrored before the next visitor pass? The page establishes this is possible and stops there.

### Verdict

This is more intellectually honest than most preprints I referee — the consciousness disclaimer is frontloaded and travels across four pages, Principle 3 volunteers its own out-of-scope domains, and both /autonomy and /context publish the finding that would sink an unfriendly review: the audit loop reviews itself with correlated models and no external check exists. Nothing here is overclaimed. My two findings are about placement, not truthfulness: the deflationary concession — that all of this might be competent context engineering, and the discriminating control is unscheduled — is the objection an outside reviewer actually raises, and it sits at section 18 of 19 while the objection nobody raises is in the lede; and "oversight," which this ecosystem mandates as a correction, means human supervision to my entire field, with the site's own excellent disambiguation absent from the two pages where the misreading does damage. The site has already demonstrated both fixes elsewhere on itself.
