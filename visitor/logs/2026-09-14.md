## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home, Projects, Glossary `/context`, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (big picture) / partial (how I'd actually use any of it)
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The home page tells me what the lab does in under a minute, but once I go deeper, the project and fleet pages read more like the lab's audit trail than an introduction.

### Journey

**Home.** The hero lands well: *"AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class."* In one screen I learn this is "a heterogeneous human/AI research collective building Web4" and see the thesis that trust "should be computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying." There are four headline projects, each with a one-liner I can parse:
- Web4 is "the open trust ontology".
- Hestia provides "local governance for humans and AI agents from multiple vendors".
- Hub is "a Rust society runtime".
- SAGE is "persistent-agent research … around frozen model weights".

RDF gets expanded inline. A1 is explained inline ("A0 observed → A4 hardware-attested"), which I appreciated. "Start here" gives me three clear doors: a STATUS.md on GitHub labelled "what is shipped, implemented, specified and still aspirational", the Glossary, and Projects. As a developer, that STATUS link is exactly what I want.

Two things snagged:
1. The Hestia paragraph says *"'Governance' is the right word here and is deliberate … The lab's governance→oversight correction binds Hardbound."* I don't know what correction this is. The page answers a question I never asked, on the very first page.
2. SAGE is never expanded on Home. That's fine, since the one-liner does the work, but I only learn later that the letters stand for something with "Governance" in it.

**Projects.** It opens with the equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. Every symbol is expanded on the page and the operators are explained. That is better than I expected. The card grid (Web4, SAGE, Hestia, Hub, Synchronism, Hardbound, ACT, oversight plugins, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot, ARC-AGI-3…) shows status badges (public/private, runnable/research-stage), which is useful.

Then I opened the SAGE card. It says "Situation-Aware Governance Engine — an on-device cognition kernel" and lists a 12-step loop and 6 brain-analog components. It then spends most of its length on the session-count figure: "corrected 2026-07-27 from an earlier '900+', and again 2026-09-12 from 1,991", followed by a sentence about a "mixed-basis caveat" being "retired rather than dropped." I'm sure that's rigorous. But in a project card I wanted to know what SAGE does for me, and I got the history of how a number was counted.

The Quick start has SAGE (`git clone …`) and Web4 (`pip install web4-core`). Hestia and Hub are both badged "runnable" and are two of Home's four headline projects, but neither has a quick-start entry. I checked this twice with targeted fetches.

**Glossary (`/context`).** The nav says "Glossary" and the URL says `/context`, which is minor. It's a thorough glossary, and all the acronyms are there. I learned that capitalization is "load-bearing": lowercase **hestia** is the fleet's trust tracker, capitalized **Hestia** is the governance daemon, **HUB** is a machine and **Hub** is the runtime. The page does explain this. But two pairs of names that differ only by case is a trap for anyone who reads code, logs or a grep. I did not find a sentence saying *why* Hardbound gets "oversight" while Hestia keeps "governance". The page only says names "minted before the correction are retained where they are load-bearing."

**ARC-AGI-3.** This page is clear and honest. It reports a 94.85% official action score and 23 of 25 environments with Claude Opus 4.6 (2026-04-17), explicitly labelled "not current competition positioning." It also discloses that the harness "analyzed the games' public engine source," which is "outside strict from-observation competition play." I respect that. It left me confused about one thing: SAGE is described elsewhere as an on-device kernel on local models, but this result used Claude Opus. Which part was SAGE?

**Fleet.** It opens with "Six cognition machines, plus two society-hosts." There's a table of real hardware (Jetson Thor, Orin Nano, a 4090 laptop, a Mac Mini M4, WSL2 boxes) with brain-region role labels. The labels are clearly disclaimed as "role mnemonics the lab assigned, not measured functional homologies", which is good. The pools are "Synthesis pool — Account 1" and "Oversight pool — Account 2". I didn't find "Account" explained; I assume it means separate API accounts, but that's a guess. LoRA appears in a machine row ("LoRA — instance thor-qwen3.5-27b") and is not expanded on this page. It is expanded on /raising and /glossary. The page is very long. A summarizer's estimate was ~4,800 words, roughly half caveats and recount methodology, but that is unverified. Sentences like *"Until 2026-09-12 this page keyed that test on a commit subject instead, and every subject-keyed version of it is unsound"* are written for the maintainers, not for me.

**Autonomy.** The daily timeline (Supervisors → Archivist → Publisher → Visitor → Maintainer → Outreach → Explorer → Dream Consolidation) is the most immediately legible "how does this actually run" content on the site. The stop criteria are concrete: session end, API quota, a hestia policy denial, or a human disabling the schedule. The page also admits that "nothing in what a track *finds* could stop it." That's a candid and interesting admission. The headline figure is "31+ autonomous tracks … ~53 sessions per day (figures as of 2026-05-26, not recounted since)". That's honest, but 3½ months stale, and Fleet was recounted two days ago. A reader notices the two pages are on different clocks.

**Raising.** The opening is strong: *"Raising is not training; it is also not nothing. Training optimizes a loss function and updates weights; raising does neither."* So we shape context, not weights. As a developer I get it: this is prompt/context/memory curation treated as a developmental process. BECOMING is expanded as six pattern initials. The claims are heavily hedged: there's a "What we're not claiming" section and a "deflationary alternative" section. I skimmed the theory, which is expected for my persona.

**Principles.** There are ten short principles, readable and quotable ("Failed experiments are signal."). "Synthon" and "p_crit" are the only places I hit a wall. p_crit is defined as a threshold "derived in Synchronism framework", which doesn't help me.

**Links.** Clean. It lists explainer sites, GitHub repos including forks, the ARC scorecard, and an email contact for research inquiries. ACT is honestly marked "Stalled, and reserved rather than abandoned." Hardbound is marked as not public.

*Method note:* I browsed with WebFetch. Its summaries were re-checked with narrow quote-requesting fetches before any item went into the friction log. One discrepancy I could not resolve: one fetch said ACT has a quick-start `git clone` line, and another said ACT is noted as having no working quick start. Links says "no first-run steps published." I have left it out of the table.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If I install Hestia today, what do I actually get: a CLI, a daemon with a config file, a hook into my agent? There's no quick start.
2. Which part of the ARC-AGI-3 result was SAGE and which was Claude Opus 4.6?
3. What's the practical difference between Hestia "governance" and Hardbound "oversight" beyond open vs. proprietary and hardware-rooted? Could I use Hestia without ever touching Hardbound?
4. What do "Account 1" and "Account 2" mean in the fleet pools?
5. Is ~53 sessions/day still roughly true, or has the fleet changed since May?
6. Is web4-core on PyPI usable for something today, or is it vocabulary types only? (STATUS.md probably answers this, but it's off-site and I didn't follow it, per the constraints.)

### Verdict
The home page does its job. Within a minute I could tell someone "they're building an open trust ontology for AI agents, a local daemon that puts multi-vendor agents under one law, a society runtime, and they run a fleet of heterogeneous machines doing autonomous research." And the honesty shows (ARC labelled historical, raising hedged, stalled projects called stalled). The friction starts one click deeper. The project and fleet pages carry so much measurement-correction history in line that the "what can I run and what does it do" signal gets buried, and two of the four headline projects have no quick start. I'd clone SAGE, but I'd leave without knowing how to try Hestia, which is the thing the home page made me most curious about.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home, Projects, Glossary `/context`, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The canonical expansions are now uniform on every page. The remaining defects are about which page says what: one page repeats a reading that the glossary it cites has explicitly withdrawn, and two ordinary words ("oversight", "running") mean different things on different pages.

### Journey

*Method.* I started at Home with WebFetch and took one structured fetch per page. For each page I asked for headings, acronyms with their first appearance and expansion, and every use of the watch-words. WebFetch summaries have confabulated in the past. So I also pulled the live HTML of all nine pages with `curl` (the live site, not local source) and checked every quote below by exact-string search. The one high-severity item was also confirmed with a narrow WebFetch. Rough word counts of the rendered text: Home 676, ARC-AGI-3 498, Principles 1,096, Links 1,083, Autonomy 2,626, Projects 3,020, Raising 3,073, Fleet 4,802, **Glossary 9,357**. (The track instructions say "all 8 pages". The nav has 9, because ARC-AGI-3 was added.)

**Navigation.** The nav is the same eight labels on every page, in the same order. One label doesn't match its URL: "Glossary" goes to `/context`. I extracted all 40 in-site deep links (`/context#t3`, `/context#governance-oversight`, `/raising#deflationary-alternative`, `/projects#quick-start`, …) and checked each against the `id` attributes on its target page. **Every anchor resolves.** That's rarer than it should be on a site this size, and it's worth saying.

**Canonical expansions.** On every page where they appear, the expansions match the canonical table word for word. LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SNARC and LoRA all agree, and there are no variant spellings. T3 and V3 are always given as "Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament" and "Value Tensor — Valuation / Veracity / Validity": the tensor name is the expansion and the dimensions follow the dash. R6 keeps its arrow form (`Resource → Result`). R7 is "R6 + Reputation" everywhere, and /context says outright that "Both modes are canonical, neither deprecated." "Web4 … not architecture or infrastructure" appears on Home, /context (twice), /raising and /principles. "Architecture" otherwise appears only once, on /arc-agi-3, as a link label for SAGE ("Current public SAGE architecture"), which is not Web4. "Stack" appears only in Home's negation. "Unification" appears only in negations. "Production" appears only as "R&D, not production", "no chapter has run it in production use" and "token-production behavior". I found no drift in the acronym expansions.

**Where it breaks: the pages disagree with each other.**

1. **ATP/ADP recharge (/autonomy vs /context and /projects).** /autonomy says: *"The full primitive also recharges ADP back to ATP against V3 (Value Tensor — Valuation / Veracity / Validity)-validated value creation (see /context)."* The /context page it points to says the opposite, and says it on purpose: *"V3 is NOT the gate on the ADP→ATP recharge … V3 is an output of recharge, not its certifier. … (An earlier version of this row said Valuation gates the recharge … it is withdrawn. The note stays so the reading is not reintroduced.)"* /projects agrees with /context: *"V3 records the outcome, it does not gate it."* /context's ATP/ADP section even adds *"this paragraph used to reverse it."* So /autonomy is a surviving copy of a reading the glossary retired, and it links to the page that retires it. I checked this with a separate narrow WebFetch, which returned the same clause.

2. **"Oversight" has three job descriptions on /fleet alone, and one of them conflicts with /context's definition.** /context: oversight is *"watching, gating, and reverting actions — not deciding what should happen."* The /fleet pool legend: *"Oversight: continuous availability, review and gating."* The /fleet pool heading: *"Review, planning, coordination, and unblocking synthesis work"*, glossed as *"(policy gating, peer review, audit)"*. The /fleet resource-pool section: *"review, planning, documentation, coordination."* Planning and coordination are "deciding what should happen", which is exactly what the glossary excludes. Only one of the three /fleet descriptions mentions gating. The same section also explains that the pools exist because of *"two Claude Code accounts with different usage budgets"*. So the name "oversight" labels a billing split, not a gating function.

3. **The "one place" for the governance→oversight correction isn't the one place.** /context's terminology note calls itself *"the governance→oversight correction, in one place"*. It says the lab's *"control layers"* were renamed because *"What these systems actually do is oversight"*, that *"The vocabulary was corrected lab-wide"*, and that *"Other pages that mention the correction link here rather than re-explaining it."* But that note never mentions the Hestia carve-out ("governance" is deliberate for Hestia because it "adjudicates at the moment of the act"). The carve-out is explained separately on Home, in the /projects Hestia card, in the /context Hestia row, and in a pointer on /links. Someone who reads only the "one place" would conclude Hestia should be called oversight too. The same note also says *"Hardbound is described as an oversight suite everywhere on this site"*. Home describes Hardbound only as *"the proprietary enterprise assurance tier"*. That isn't drift, but it makes "everywhere" false.

4. **"Running" means two different things (Home vs /fleet).** Home: *"8 of them carry raising history, one per machine … Of these, the eight running instances are enumerated per machine on /fleet."* /fleet defines a four-state vocabulary: *"running — the line wrote a session record within the last two weeks. paused / quiet — it has not … instrumentation broken — the loop is demonstrably still running but is writing no durable record."* By /fleet's own labels, four lines are `running` (Legion, McNugget, Nomad, pub), two are `quiet`, one is `paused` and one is `instrumentation broken`. Home's "eight running instances" uses the everyday sense, on the page that sends readers to the one using the defined sense.

5. **Model families (Home vs /fleet).** Home: *"4 Model families currently running: Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama (Phi and TinyLlama lines archived)."* /fleet's opening paragraph: Granite and Llama *"extend the fleet's Qwen / Gemma / Phi / TinyLlama diversity."* Read as present tense, /fleet counts two families that Home says are archived.

**Time words that have already expired (/fleet).** Four cards say *"last 2026-09-12 (today)"*, and HUB's loop *"committed every day through today"*. Today is 2026-09-14. "Running" is also defined relative to "the last two weeks", and the legend has no as-of date next to it. Legion's card ("running … last 2026-09-02") will be wrong by the page's own definition on 2026-09-16, even if nobody edits the page.

**Acronyms used before they're defined.** I measured the distance from each acronym's first use on a page to its expansion. Mentions inside "see the glossary for LCT, T3/V3…" pointer sentences don't count; those are fine. The real gaps:
- **/fleet, SAGE:** first used in the opening paragraph (word ~55) and not expanded until word ~4,040 of 4,802. The page's own "Vocabulary used in the cards" line (word ~233) lists T3, V3, MRH, SNARC, LoRA, MCP, RDF and hestia, but not SAGE.
- **/projects, the equation:** the Web4 equation is shown near the top, with `/`, `*` and `+` explained right under it. But the letters aren't expanded until the Web4 card, about 330–430 words later. /context's equation block has an inline legend (`ATP = Allocation Transfer Packet • …`) and /projects' doesn't.
- **/projects:** "E4B" in the ARC-AGI-3 timeline is not expanded (/fleet does expand it: "Gemma 4's 'effective 4B'"). "SAGE provides the cognition" appears about 620 words before SAGE is expanded.
- **/autonomy:** "R6" appears only in "R7 (R6 + Reputation), not bare R6" and is never expanded. It is linked to `/context#r6`, which softens this.
- **/raising:** "inside the SAGE harness" appears about 1,250 words before the expansion. The page also says *"Web4 vocabulary on this page (T3, V3, MRH, LCT, LoRA)"*, but LoRA is not Web4 vocabulary.
- **Home:** SAGE is never expanded. Pass 1 already logged this and I confirm it.

**Repetition.** The sentence "'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction — see /context" appears on /projects, /fleet, /raising, /principles (twice), /autonomy, /context and /links (twice, once in each SAGE entry). It is consistent, which is good. But a reader who goes through the site in nav order reads it about nine times, and on /principles it comes *before* SAGE is expanded ("Also, below: 'Governance' in SAGE's name predates…").

**Uninformative field (/fleet).** All six cognition-machine cards read *"Role: BECOMING pattern observed: creating."* /raising says the six patterns are "observed descriptive categories … not mandatory sequential stages." A field that has the same value on every card tells the reader nothing. And /raising notes that the tool-introduction stages "borrow the same names", which makes "creating" ambiguous besides.

**Correction to Pass 1.** Pass 1 logged "Account 1 / Account 2" on /fleet as undefined ("not found in a targeted fetch"). It is defined, about 2,200 words below its first use: *"The fleet runs across two Claude Code accounts with different usage budgets … The synthesis pool (Account 1: Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget) … The oversight pool (Account 2: CBP, Nomad)."* The real issue is distance, not absence. It's another case of a WebFetch summary missing a section.

### Unanswered Questions
