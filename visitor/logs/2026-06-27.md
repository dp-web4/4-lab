## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, arc-agi-3, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial — I get the *shape* of the thing (autonomous AI agents on a fleet, self-organizing, audited daily) but not the *substance* of the Web4 layer that supposedly ties it together
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A genuinely intriguing "what if you let a fleet of small models self-organize" experiment, wrapped in enough custom vocabulary that the cool parts are hard to reach.

### Journey
I landed on the home page from a repo README. The hook works: **"Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."** is concrete and developer-legible. The "you don't engineer the mound, you engineer placement rules" framing landed well — I immediately understood this is emergence-from-local-rules, not a centralized blueprint. Good. The home page also does the right thing and defines the acronym soup inline (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, SAGE, MCP, RDF). That's the most newcomer-friendly page on the site.

Then I clicked into **Projects** and the floor dropped out a bit. There are ~12 projects, and the descriptions are conceptually dense but operationally vague — I can clone a repo, but "how would I integrate this / why would I" isn't answered for most. SAGE and 4-Life and SNARC have runnable sites and clear purpose, so those are the ones I'd actually try. The Web4 equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` shows up here without the operator legend (that legend lives on /context), so on first read it looks like math but I can't parse the precedence.

**Context** is where the vocabulary is actually defined, and it's good — clean glossary, the operator legend (`+` = augmented with, `/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by). The problem is ordering: every other page leans on these terms and says "see /context," but a developer arriving via a deep link (which is how I'd arrive from a README) hits the jargon before the glossary. /context should arguably be linked more aggressively, or the key 3-4 terms repeated inline.

**ARC-AGI-3** is the most credible page on the site — clear numbers, honest caveats (Kaggle sandbox is harder: 32GB VRAM, no internet; "Kaggle competition not attempted"), and it explicitly attributes the result to "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness." That honesty is a big trust signal.

But then **Fleet** confused me. It says machine **CBP (TinyLlama 1.1B on RTX 2060)** "achieved 94.85% on ARC Prize official run." The ARC page says the 94.85% is Claude Opus 4.6 in the SAGE harness. A TinyLlama 1.1B did *not* score 94.85% on a reasoning benchmark — that's an extraordinary claim and it contradicts the ARC page. As a developer this is the single thing that most made me doubt the numbers. I think the intended meaning is "CBP *ran/orchestrated* the official run (with Opus as the inference model)," but the page reads as "CBP scored 94.85%."

**Autonomy** was clear and impressive — 31+ tracks, ~53 sessions/day, SQLite registry to prevent conflicts, detect-and-revert rollback. This is the page that made the project feel real and operational rather than purely theoretical.

**Raising** and **Principles** are the philosophy pages. They're honest (explicit "descriptive scaffolding, not measured stages," "not externally audited"), which I respect, but they're also where the made-up vocabulary is densest (attractors, synthons, memoriescape, bilateral generation). **Links** closed the loop well — clear "start with SAGE or ACT," actual git clone commands, a contact email.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Did a TinyLlama 1.1B actually score 94.85%, or did that machine just orchestrate a run that used Claude Opus 4.6? The Fleet and ARC pages disagree and I can't tell.
2. What does a developer actually *do* with Web4? It's described as "the shared language everything else speaks," but I never saw a minimal example of two agents using it to interoperate.
3. Why is Synchronism (unreplicated, 80-orders-of-magnitude claim) featured alongside the validated benchmark work? Does the framework depend on it or not?
4. Is it Gemma 3 or Gemma 4 for Phase 2?
5. What's the practical, mechanical difference between "raising" (context shaping) and fine-tuning/LoRA? The pages assert it's different but never show the line.

### Verdict
The experiment underneath this is genuinely interesting — a heterogeneous fleet of small models self-organizing under local rules, audited daily, benchmarked honestly. The honesty (explicit caveats, "not externally audited," cost disclosure) earns trust. But two things hold it back for a newcomer: (1) the custom vocabulary front-loads on deep pages before the glossary can catch you, and (2) the Fleet page's claim that a TinyLlama 1.1B "achieved 94.85%" directly contradicts the careful attribution on the ARC page — and that's exactly the kind of too-good-to-be-true number that makes a skeptical developer bounce. Fix the attribution and surface /context earlier, and this goes from "intriguing but confusing" to "I'd actually clone SAGE."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, context, arc-agi-3, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (on structure and terminology — my actual job here)
- **Would return?**: yes

### Journey
I audited methodically: home → projects → context → arc-agi-3 → fleet → autonomy → raising → principles → links, checking every acronym expansion against the canonical table and looking for cross-page inconsistencies.

The headline finding for a terminology auditor is the good news: this site does not drift. LCT is "Linked Context Token" on all five pages it appears. T3 is "Talent / Training / Temperament" everywhere; V3 is "Valuation / Veracity / Validity" everywhere; MRH is "Markov Relevancy Horizon" everywhere; ATP/ADP are "Allocation Transfer Packet" / "Allocation Discharge Packet" everywhere. The Web4 equation renders identically on /projects and /context, both with the operator legend (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with). Web4 is called an "ontology" with explicit "not architecture or infrastructure" disclaimers on both /context and /principles. Hardbound is "oversight" on every page, and /principles makes it a load-bearing rule: *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* This is the most terminologically disciplined site in the cluster I'd expect to audit.

The **/context** page is the spine: a full glossary with one-line definitions, the equation, and the operator legend. It's excellent reference architecture. The problem is purely *placement* — every other page leans on these terms and defers to /context, so a reader who deep-links into /projects, /fleet, or /raising meets the jargon before the glossary. As a tech writer I'd push for the glossary to be reachable from a persistent nav affordance, not just inline "see /context" prose.

I want to flag a **change from Pass 1**: the Curious Developer reported that /fleet attributes the 94.85% ARC score to CBP's TinyLlama 1.1B. On my read, /fleet now cleanly separates the two claims — *"Claude Opus 4.6 (public set, network access) produced the 94.85% official action score"* and, separately, *"SAGE-Sprout's identity ... transferred successfully to TinyLlama 1.1B."* The score is attributed to Opus; TinyLlama owns only the identity-transfer claim. This matches /arc-agi-3 (*"the result is Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness"*). Either the page was fixed between passes or the model-vs-machine juxtaposition is just genuinely easy to misread — the CBP card sitting on the same page as the benchmark numbers invites the conflation even when the words are correct.

The one place the enforced terminology actually leaks is the **/projects "Oversight Plugins" card**, described as *"Web4 governance for multiple agent platforms."* Every other page says "oversight"; /principles explicitly forbids "governance" for Hardbound. The card title says "Oversight" but its description says "governance" — an internal contradiction in a single card, on the exact term the site polices hardest. Pass 1 noted the legacy repo slug rationale, but the visible word still contradicts the rule.

The other cross-page inconsistency is **Gemma versioning**: /fleet lists "Gemma 3 12B" (McNugget) and "Gemma 3 4B" (Nomad), while /projects names "Gemma 4 E4B" in Phase 2 transfer plans. A reader can't tell if "Gemma 4" is a deliberate future target or a typo for the Gemma 3 models running now. Needs either a "(planned)" marker or correction.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 site pages + 1 cross-check against the live SAGE explainer site
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (this is my domain — I read it for accuracy, not comprehension)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Canonical Web4 vocabulary is rendered correctly and consistently across the whole site — but the terminology *policy* has been over-applied to an actual project's proper name: SAGE's own site calls it the "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine," and 4-lab silently rewrites it to "**Guidance** Engine" (and the loop step "Govern" → "Oversee"). The site polices "governance" so hard it now misrepresents one of the projects it describes.

### Journey
I went straight to Projects, Fleet, and Context — the three pages where a contributor can catch misrepresentation — then swept the rest, then did the one cross-check Passes 1–2 flagged for me: verifying the SAGE expansion against the SAGE project's own explainer.

**Context** is genuinely strong. The glossary is canonical-clean: LCT = "Verifiable digital presence that accumulates witnessed history — identity grounded in record, not model weights." That's exactly right — LCT is *Linked Context Token*, and the "not model weights" clause is the distinction people most often get wrong. T3 = "three-component trust tensor; each component is an RDF sub-graph root" and V3 = "three-component value tensor complementary to T3." The equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` renders correctly, and the operator legend is nuanced in exactly the right way — it notes `/` means "verified by" for T3/V3 *but* "allocation pair" for ATP/ADP. That dual reading is correct and most summaries miss it. ATP = "Allocation Transfer Packet ... declared before an action runs," ADP = "the spent form of ATP" — correct. R6 fully expanded. ACP/ACT cleanly distinguished (ACP = the protocol/trust primitives over MCP; ACT = the Cosmos SDK implementation). This page I'd sign off on.

**This resolves two of the prior passes' open questions in the site's favor:**
- The **T3/V3 "tensor"** framing (flagged for me by Passes 1 & 2) is *correct*, not creeping vocabulary. "Trust tensor" / "value tensor" are accepted Web4 descriptors — three-component structures scoped by MRH. Not drift. Close that question.
- The **/projects "Oversight Plugins"** card now reads "Web4 **oversight** model (audit trails, policy gating, trust tracking)" in the prose; the only "governance" remaining is inside the legacy repo slug `web4-governance`. A repo slug is a frozen identifier — keeping it is fine; the visible prose is now clean. Either the maintainer fixed it after Pass 2 or it was always slug-only. Either way I do **not** treat this as live drift anymore.

**But the SAGE cross-check is the real find.** 4-lab expands SAGE as "Situation-Aware **Guidance** Engine" on all six pages it appears, and renders the cognition loop's 10th step as "**oversee**." I pulled the SAGE project's own site: it expands SAGE as "Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine," and its loop step 10 is "**Govern**." 4-lab has systematically substituted Guidance←Governance and Oversee←Govern. I understand *why* — the lab bans "governance" in favor of "oversight" for Hardbound — but that rule was about how we *describe oversight*, not a license to rename a sibling project's acronym. To a SAGE contributor this reads as the meta-site getting our own name wrong. The "oversight not governance" discipline has leaked past its scope and started editing proper nouns.

(Caveat I'd want resolved with the SAGE maintainers: it's *possible* SAGE itself is mid-rename to "Guidance" and its explainer site is the stale one. But as of today the two sites disagree, and 4-lab is the one that diverges from the project's published name.)

**Fleet** I checked for accuracy of model/role attribution. The ARC attribution is now exactly right and matches /arc-agi-3: "Claude Opus 4.6 (public set, network access) produced the 94.85% official action score (24/25 games, 96.0%)" with CBP/TinyLlama explicitly as *orchestrator*, not scorer. Good — Pass 1's contradiction is gone. One small internal inconsistency: /fleet calls V3 "attestations" while /context calls it the "value tensor." V3 components *are* attested, so it's defensible, but it's a looser word than the glossary uses.

**Projects/ARC** — the Gemma version question (Pass 1 & 2) I *can* adjudicate at my ceiling: "**Gemma 4 E4B** (4-bit quantized edge variant)" is almost certainly mislabeled. There is no Gemma 4. The "E4B" suffix is the *Gemma 3n* edge/nano family (E2B / E4B effective-parameter variants); "4-bit quantized edge variant" is a further misreading — the "E4B" is an effective-parameter designation, not a 4-bit quant label. The Phase 2 target should read "**Gemma 3n E4B**." This is a genuine technical inaccuracy, not just a cross-page mismatch.

**Principles / Raising / Autonomy / Links** all hold the canonical line: Web4 = "an ontology ... not architecture or infrastructure" (verbatim on /principles and /context); Hardbound = "oversight," with /principles stating the rule outright; "fractal leverage" explicitly contrasted with "unification for its own sake" and "scope inflation" — that's the canonical framing, correct. /links describes Web4 as "Trust-native ontology — LCT identity, T3 verified by V3, contextualized by MRH," which is a clean prose rendering of the equation's middle term. Raising's consciousness caveat ("not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia") is appropriately hedged.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 site pages + 1 external cross-check (the SAGE project's own explainer site)
- **Understanding achieved**: good — I can evaluate the epistemics without being a Web4 insider, which is the whole point of this pass
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Unusually disciplined intellectual honesty — the consciousness hedging, the three-tier evidence taxonomy, and a rare-in-the-wild "frozen weights ≠ safe behavior" caveat are exactly what I'd want to see — but the honesty lives in *labeled hedges* while the *vocabulary* (synthon, becoming, growth, tensor) travels across pages without the hedge attached, so a skimming reader absorbs the strong frame and not the disclaimer.

### Journey
I came in skeptical. Sites that use words like "becoming," "identity," "synthon," and "emergent coherence entities" usually fail the smell test within two screens — the vocabulary outruns the evidence and the caveats are either absent or buried. This one mostly passes, and it passes in a specific, checkable way.

I read **Raising**, **Principles**, and **Autonomy** closely, then swept the other five pages, then did one external check.

**Raising** is the page I expected to be the worst and it's among the best. The non-claim is explicit and unhedged in the right direction: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia. We're claiming that developmental frameworks describe what we observe better than training frameworks do."* That is the correct, defensible version of this claim — it's an assertion about *descriptive fit*, not about phenomenology. Every loaded term gets an operational gloss: "identity" = "consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns measured via raising curriculum state and interaction logs"; "growth" = "increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims." And the observer's-pull line — *"I notice I want to call it experience"* — is the single most honest sentence on the site. It names the interpretive temptation instead of yielding to it. That's a researcher writing in good faith.

The thing that genuinely raised my estimate of these authors: the safety caveat in the raising mechanism. *"frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* This is the failure mode most "we only shape context, we never touch weights" pitches paper over. The naive version of "raising not training" is a *safety-washing* move — "we don't fine-tune, so nothing dangerous can emerge." These authors explicitly refuse that move and name in-context attractor risk. I did not expect to find that here.

**Is "raising not training" defensible?** Mechanically, yes but modestly: it's in-context learning + curriculum + persistent memory, with weights frozen. The "we shape context, we do not update weights" boundary is real and verifiable. What it is *not* is a novel training paradigm — it's a re-description of known techniques under developmental vocabulary. The site's *defensible* claim is the narrow one it actually makes ("developmental framing describes the observations better"). My concern is scope creep: that narrow, well-hedged claim on /raising licenses a thick developmental vocabulary — "BECOMING phases," "synthons," "bilateral generation," "niche" — that then appears on /principles, /, and /fleet often *without* the local hedge. The hedge is page-local; the vocabulary is global. A reader who lands on /principles sees "Synthon: emergent coherence" as **Principle 5** — promoted to a named, load-bearing principle — and has to already know it rests on *one unreplicated trial* (a fact disclosed only on / and /context). Naming an n=1 observation as a standing principle is the one place the epistemic discipline slips.

**Principles** holds the line well otherwise. The deployment disclaimer is exactly right and rarely stated this bluntly: *"Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings."* And the blanket epistemics footer — developmental/identity language describes "functional system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims" — does the heavy lifting. Good. (It would do more if it sat adjacent to the synthon principle rather than as a global footnote.)

**Autonomy** is the most safety-relevant page and, to the authors' credit, the most candid about its own risk surface. The maintainer track is *"the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate,"* and the oversight posture is stated plainly as reactive, not preventive: *"Consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship"* — mitigated by "detect-and-revert" (Vercel deploy history, sub-minute rollback) and a SQLite registry that prevents two tracks touching the same files. As a researcher this is the actual thing I'd want to study: an autonomous loop with write access to a public artifact and no pre-publication human gate. They don't hide it; they document the known failure mode (*"acting on a visitor suggestion that pattern-matched but lacked semantic depth"*). That honesty is the right call — but I'd note the residual risk plainly: detect-and-revert means errors are public for some window before they're caught, and the detector is the same class of system that produces the errors. Honest disclosure, real but bounded risk.

**The external cross-check.** Pass 3 flagged that 4-lab calls SAGE the "Situation-Aware **Guidance** Engine" while SAGE's own site says "**Governance** Engine." I verified this independently — fetching a public sibling site is ordinary due diligence, not insider knowledge. SAGE's explainer states verbatim *"Situation-Aware Governance Engine,"* and its 12-step loop is *"Sense → Salience → Metabolize → Posture → Select → Budget → Execute → Learn → Remember → **Govern** → Filter → Act,"* where Govern = "PolicyGate evaluates proposed effects against policy." 4-lab keeps the **PolicyGate** term (it's in the /context glossary) but renames the step it implements from "Govern" to "Oversee," and renames the engine from "Governance" to "Guidance." So the two meta-sites of the same collective disagree on a project's proper name. From a credibility standpoint this is the most damaging defect on the site — not because the word choice matters philosophically, but because *a careful outsider who cross-references will catch the collective contradicting itself on a name*, and that quietly undermines the otherwise-strong "we are disciplined about terminology" signal. The discipline overshot and edited a proper noun.
