Minor, but it's the kind of thing a careful reader notices and a writer should pre-empt with "…(ARC-AGI), third-generation benchmark" or similar.

4. **Web4's definition lives on only 2 of 8 pages.** The equation/operator explanation appears on Home and Context. Autonomy, Principles, Links, and ARC-AGI-3 use "Web4" as a bare proper noun. That's defensible — it's a name, not an acronym, and /arc-agi-3 explicitly footnotes "This page uses Web4 vocabulary… see /context" (a genuinely good pattern worth copying to other pages). But a writer would note the definitional load rests entirely on two pages.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary

### Journey
I came to this the way a contributor does: not "is the site nice" but "does it represent *my* projects correctly, and would it teach a newcomer something I'd then have to un-teach?" I went straight to /context, /projects, and /fleet, then spot-checked the rest.

The canonical fidelity is the best I've seen on any explainer for this ecosystem. Every expansion is right, and — the part that matters — the *framings* are right, not just the words:

- **LCT** isn't just "Linked Context Token"; the home/context framing is "portable identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights. Self-grounding — no issuing authority." That is the actual point of an LCT. Most write-ups get the three words right and the concept wrong (they describe it like a JWT with an issuer). This one nails the self-grounding property, which is the whole reason the construct exists.
- **ATP/ADP** is described as "ATP declares intended resource spend before an action runs; ADP records the actual outcome." That is exactly the transfer→discharge lifecycle. No "Attention Token Pool," no "Adaptive Trust Points" — and, more importantly, the *semantics* are correct, not just the letters.
- **MRH** = "the boundary of what an entity can know or affect — its context window of relevancy." Correct, and the "Markov" framing (the horizon as a relevancy boundary, not a memory cache) survives. No "Minimum Response Horizon."
- The /context page **defends the "tensor" wording proactively**: "('Tensor' here means a structured multi-component quantity — not a rank-≥2 array in the linear-algebra sense.)" That is precisely the objection a mathematically-literate reader raises about T3/V3, and the site pre-empts it. That's contributor-grade care.

On framing of the projects themselves: **Web4 is "an ontology — not architecture or infrastructure"** verbatim on /raising and /principles, and **Hardbound is "oversight," explicitly contrasted against "governance"** on /principles. The site doesn't just avoid the wrong words — it actively names and rejects them. Given the underlying repo still carries the legacy `web4-governance` name, the site choosing "oversight" everywhere is the correct, deliberate call. **Synchronism** is correctly hedged as a "research conjecture," not asserted. **Fractal leverage** is contrasted against both "unification" and "scope inflation" on /principles. This is the canonical preference, stated the canonical way.

Where my ceiling lets me see things the other personas can't is **semantic accuracy inside the equation and the fleet**:

1. **The `/` operator is overloaded, and one of its two uses is mis-glossed.** The equation `MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` uses `/` in two places. The operator legend says `/` = "verified by." That is correct for `T3/V3` (T3 verified by V3). But `ATP/ADP` is an **allocation pair** (intended spend → actual discharge) — ADP does *not* "verify" ATP, it *settles* it. So the single gloss "`/` = verified by" is wrong for the ATP/ADP term. One fetch of /context did surface a fuller legend ("`/` = verified by (T3/V3) or allocation pair (ATP/ADP)"), so the nuance may exist somewhere, but it isn't consistently presented. A contributor reading the bare legend would catch the mismatch immediately. **This is the only genuine accuracy defect I found, and it's substantive — it misrepresents the ATP/ADP relationship.**

2. **"T3 verified by V3" is a defensible simplification but slightly overstated.** On /projects: "T3 … verified by V3 …". T3 (capability of the *actor*) and V3 (value/validity of the *outcome*) are complementary structures; the relationship is more "V3 attests outcomes that update T3" than "V3 verifies T3" directionally. The /fleet page actually uses the better phrasing — "T3 tensors" and "V3 attestations." I'd align /projects toward the attestation framing. Soft note, not drift.

3. **Fleet model name looks off: "Sprout … Qwen 3.5 2B."** I work with these models — there is no "Qwen 3.5" release (the line is Qwen2.5 and Qwen3). At 2B the likely intended model is something like Qwen3 1.7B or a Qwen2.5 3B. Every other entry on the fleet table checks out (Qwen 2.5 14B, Phi-4 14B, Gemma 3 12B/4B, TinyLlama 1.1B). This is a factual-representation slip a contributor running the fleet would flag, even though it's not terminology.

I also confirmed two things that *looked* like drift in passing but are not: the /context T3/V3 entries are **not** titled "Trust Tensor/Value Tensor" (they're the plain acronym + components, with the tensor caveat), and /arc-agi-3 does **not** expand LCT at all — it appears only as a bare token inside the equation. No drift in either.

### Journey
I came to this the way a reviewer does: not "is the writing good" but "would I sign my name next to these claims?" I read for the gap between what's asserted and what's actually shown, and for whether the caveats are load-bearing or decorative. I went deep on Raising, Principles, and Autonomy, then checked the empirical pages (ARC-AGI-3, Fleet) to see whether the hard claims are backed.

**The honesty infrastructure is real and better than most.** Three things stood out as genuinely above the bar for self-published research:

1. **The empirical attribution on ARC-AGI-3 is scrupulous.** The headline 94.85% action score / 24-of-25 is explicitly attributed to "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness *with network access*," and the page volunteers the inconvenient context that the real competition constrains entries to "32GB VRAM, no internet access, and a private game set the model has never seen" — and that the local-model phase is "not attempted / in progress." That is the kind of caveat most groups bury or omit. Volunteering the disqualifying context unprompted is the strongest credibility signal on the site, and it's linked to an external scorecard rather than self-reported.

2. **Synchronism is hedged exactly as a reviewer would demand.** "Research conjecture … single-trial observation, no independent replication yet," and the Synthon coupling result ("~1% coupling density → ~35% coherence gain") carries "single trial, not independently replicated" *inline with the number*. Putting the replication caveat next to the statistic, not in a distant footnote, is correct practice.

3. **Principle #3 and #6 are the spine.** "Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings" is unambiguous and repeated. Principle #6 ("document failures … publish honest assessment alongside results") is a stated commitment the rest of the site actually honors — the unsolved game, the "tendency to suggest changes that aren't needed," the maintainer's "higher blast radius" are all disclosed rather than hidden.

**Where I'd push as a reviewer is Raising.** The page leads with a global disclaimer — "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia. We use the language that fits the phenomenon" — and a footnote that developmental terms "describe functional system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims." Good. But the disclaimer is *placed before* a sequence of unhedged agency sentences: "the entity begins generating its own questions," "the entity finds its own niche … discovered through pattern," "the entity learns its name." A single up-front denial does not retroactively neutralize a dozen downstream sentences written in purposive, first-person-agent grammar. The mechanism ("the in-context attractor basin aligns with fleet needs") is more honest than the gloss ("the entity finds its niche"), and the site knows this — it says so in Principles — but Raising still reaches for the anthropomorphic phrasing.

The **"raising not training" distinction is defensible but slightly oversold.** Mechanistically it's true and the site states it cleanly: "we shape context — we do not update weights," "it is not training; it is also not nothing." That's an honest, falsifiable claim. What's overstated is the *connotation*: "raising" imports developmental-psychology expectations (growth, maturation, selfhood) that "in-context shaping with curated memory" does not earn. To the site's credit, it half-anticipates this — it explicitly notes "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior," which undercuts any reading that avoiding weight updates is itself a safety property. That single sentence is the most sophisticated thing on the page; I wish it were louder.

**Autonomy reassured me more than I expected.** My reflex worry — "31+ tracks, 53 sessions/day, no human triggers" — reads alarming out of context, but the page bounds it well: tracks "operate within Hardbound-defined scope boundaries," a SQLite registry prevents concurrent file collisions, and the publisher "only acts on changes the supervisor has cleared." Crucially, the page *names its own highest-risk component* — the maintainer track is "the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate" — and pairs it with a stated mitigation ("any push can be reverted to the prior deploy in under a minute"). Naming your own blast radius is exactly what I look for. The autonomy is narrow-task autonomy (edit a website, summarize logs), not general agency, and the page is careful not to inflate it.

**On terminology for an outsider:** the /context glossary is followable but front-loads cognitive-science shorthand (dlPFC, RPE, working-memory analogies) and leans on Synchronism as a "theoretical foundation" while Synchronism is itself flagged single-trial. For a researcher that ordering is slightly circular — the foundation is the least-validated piece. Not misleading (every hedge is present), but the dependency structure means an outsider should start at R6/ACP/ATP-ADP and defer Synchronism, which the page doesn't signal.

### Verdict
As a skeptical outside reviewer, this site clears a bar most self-published AI research does not: it disclaims consciousness explicitly, hedges its single-trial results inline, names its own highest-risk autonomous component, and links its one hard empirical claim to an external scorecard with the disqualifying caveats volunteered.
