## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I know what to clone and why within five minutes, but not two — the landing page front-loads vocabulary before it front-loads the problem, and the one thing a developer needs (what stack, what prereqs) is scattered across three pages.

### Journey

Landed on `/`. Hero: "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." Evocative, tells me nothing about what it *does*. The next paragraph is a wall of terms — "trust-native ontology", "autonomous AI cognition", "theoretical frameworks" — and I nearly bounced. What saved it was the bolded **"The problem this addresses"** paragraph three blocks down: AI agents already act on their own, the two existing trust answers (platform gatekeeper vs. crypto key custody) both fall short, and this lab is trying a third — trust computed per relationship from witnessed interaction. That is the elevator pitch. It should be the first paragraph, not the fourth.

The "New here? This site is acronym-dense" callout is honest and useful. It sent me to `/context#glossary`. I appreciated that the site knows it has this problem.

Stats cards: "8 Machines (6 cognition + 2 society-hosts)". I didn't know what a society-host was and the card didn't say. Kept reading. "40 Original repos (21 public)" — good, that's a number I can act on. "31+ Autonomous tracks" — a "track" got defined later on `/autonomy` as basically a cron entry with a declared scope and a fixed set of repos it may write to. Once I knew that, the whole "autonomous" framing clicked: this is a fleet of scheduled agent jobs that maintain and review each other's repos. That's genuinely interesting engineering and I wish the landing page said it that plainly.

The Web4 equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` looks like a formula but isn't one. The site itself flags that `/` means two different things in the same line ("verified by" vs. an allocation pair). As a developer, an overloaded operator in a headline equation is a smell — but they own it in the very next sentence, so I read it as notation, not math. Fine.

The vocabulary primer at the bottom of `/` is the best section on the page. Every card is one paragraph, expansions are inline, MCP and RDF are things I already know so I had anchors. If the primer were higher on the page I'd have understood the site in the two minutes the persona is supposed to test.

Went to `/projects`. This page is the one that works for me. Every card has a **status label** — `runnable`, `research-stage`, `private` — and that single word is worth more than any paragraph. Four cards say `runnable`: SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot. The "Want to run something rather than read? Start with SAGE" box answers the persona's central question directly. Quick-start says "See README for Python/conda setup."

What the cards *don't* tell me: the language/stack. Only the Oversight Plugins card says (TypeScript / WASM / Python hooks). For SAGE I had to infer Python from the quick start. For ACT I learned "Go/Cosmos SDK" from a comment line. A developer scanning cards wants "Python + Rust" on the card, not in a README two clicks away.

Also: the SAGE and Synchronism cards carry inline correction notes ("this card previously said '900+', corrected 2026-07-27…"). I respect the honesty but as a first-time reader it's changelog noise inside a product description. Move it to a footnote.

`/links` has the real quick start, and it is **different** from the one on `/projects`:

```
python3 -m sage.instances.init --machine mybox --model gemma3:4b --operator-name yourname
cd sage-rs && cargo build --release && cd ..
SAGE_MACHINE=mybox SAGE_MODEL=gemma3:4b ./sage-rs/target/release/sage-daemon
```

So SAGE needs a Rust toolchain (`cargo`), and `gemma3:4b` is an ollama model tag, so it also needs ollama installed with that model pulled. Neither `/projects` nor `/links` lists prerequisites. That's the single highest-value fix for my persona: one line — "Requires: Python 3.x, Rust/cargo, ollama with gemma3:4b" — above the commands. The dashboard at `localhost:8760` is a nice touch; tells me there's something to look at after it boots.

One thing genuinely confused me. The landing page says Web4 is "Not a blockchain, not a platform." Then `/projects` and `/links` say ACT — "the human interface to Web4" — is built on **Cosmos SDK**, which is a blockchain application framework. Both can be true (the ontology isn't a chain; one implementation uses a chain for ledgering), but nothing on either page reconciles them. A developer who knows what Cosmos SDK is will stop and wonder whether "not a blockchain" is marketing. One sentence on the ACT card would close it.

`/fleet`: fun page. Real hardware — Jetson AGX Thor, Orin Nano, a Mac Mini M4, an RTX 2060 in WSL2. Each machine has a brain-region analogy (thalamus, cerebellum, dlPFC). The page says twice that it's a functional analogy, which I appreciated. The headline finding — Gemma 3 4B beats Phi-4 14B for their work — is the kind of concrete, falsifiable claim I came for. Undefined thing: "The synthesis pool (Account 1: Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget) has a large weekly budget that resets every Thursday." Account 1 of what? I assume an API billing account, but it's never said. Also "pub … Session count: deliberately blank, not zero" — I get why, but it reads odd.

`/autonomy`: long, and I skimmed the timeline, but the **Honest Assessment** section stopped me. They state plainly that the maintainer track pushes to the live site with no human gate, that the posture is "detect-and-revert" not "prevent", that zero rollbacks in 79 sessions is *weak* evidence because the detector is the same loop, and that the Publisher track pushing to public repos is actually *less* reversible and *less* discussed. I have never seen a project site do this. It made me trust the rest of the site more. The "track registry is a SQLite database" detail is exactly the level of concreteness I wanted everywhere else.

`/raising`: dense theory, skimmed per persona. Caught the core idea (shape context, not weights; frozen model) and the honest bit that it "might be competent context engineering and nothing more" with the control experiment unrun. Didn't try to follow the Hill-function or Vygotsky analogies.

`/principles`: readable as a values page. But it's the one page where T3/V3 appear (Principle 4) without expansion. If I'd deep-linked here from a README I'd have no idea what "T3/V3 tensors bind to entity-role pairs via RDF triples" means. Also "MIT-0" unexpanded here (it is expanded on `/links`).

`/arc-agi-3`: the strongest evidence page. Numbers with conditions attached to every one of them. The "#3 on the human leaderboard" is immediately caveated as not like-for-like — "the number on this page most likely to be quoted without its conditions" — which is exactly what I'd have done. The Membot line ("a 4B model with a cartridge understands game mechanics correctly; the same model without one thinks it's placing black squares") is the single most vivid sentence on the site.

`/context`: the glossary is comprehensive — 50-odd terms, each with a one-line definition, and a "Worked example: one action through the equation" section. That worked example is the closest thing to an architecture walkthrough on the site and it's buried at the bottom of a glossary. A developer would find it faster under `/projects`.

Nav nit: the nav label says **Glossary** but the URL is `/context` and the page title is about "How Knowledge Flows". Three names for one page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What are the actual prerequisites to run SAGE? Python version, Rust toolchain, ollama, GPU or not, disk for the model?
2. If Web4 is "not a blockchain", why is its human interface built on Cosmos SDK? What does the chain hold — the chapter ledger?
3. How do SAGE and Web4 talk to each other in code? Is Web4 a Python library SAGE imports, an RDF store it writes to, or a spec SAGE's records happen to conform to? The /context worked example gets close but I wanted a file path or a module name.
4. What is "Account 1"?
5. Can I run a raising session locally after the quick start, or is that fleet-only? The `sage-daemon` dashboard hints yes but nothing says so.
6. What's in the 19 internal repos, at the category level? "Private as the fleet's oversight/security layer" covers Hardbound; what are the other 18?

### Verdict
This is one of the more honest project sites I've read — the Autonomy page's admission that the maintainer bot ships to the live site with no human gate, and that zero rollbacks is *weak* evidence, earned more trust than any benchmark number. The `/projects` status labels (runnable / research-stage / private) are exactly what a developer needs and I'd clone SAGE on the strength of them. But the two-minute test fails: the landing page leads with vocabulary instead of the problem, the quick start has hidden prerequisites (Rust, ollama), and the "not a blockchain" vs. "built on Cosmos SDK" collision goes unaddressed. Fix those three and this site converts developers; today it converts the patient ones.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

### Journey

Started at `/`. Page `<title>` is "4-lab | Eight Machines, One Recursive Learning Loop" — and it is the *same* `<title>` on all nine pages. That is a structural defect for a documentation site: browser tabs, bookmarks, and search results cannot distinguish /fleet from /raising. Every page needs its own title.

Nav order: Projects, Glossary, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links. Every link resolved. The second nav item is labeled **Glossary**, points to `/context`, and lands on a page whose H1 is **How Knowledge Flows**. The glossary is the second section on that page ("Glossary at a glance"). Three names for one destination; Pass 1 flagged this too, and from a doc-structure standpoint it is the most disorienting thing on the site — a reader who clicks "Glossary" expects a glossary at the top.

Two callouts on `/` acknowledge the acronym density and point to the vocabulary primer and to `/context`. Good practice. The vocabulary primer on `/` and the "Glossary at a glance" on `/context` are two glossaries with overlapping entries; I spot-checked LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP and found them consistent. The equation is rendered identically on `/` and `/context`, with the same three-operator legend, including the honest note that `/` carries two meanings in one line.

`/projects`: eleven cards, each with a status label. Every canonical acronym is expanded inline on first use, including R6 in full six-element form. The card for ACT is the only place a reader learns ACT and ACP are two different things (Tool vs. Protocol); `/links` and `/context` use both correctly, so this is *not* an inconsistency, but a newcomer skimming `/links` will see "Agentic Context Tool" and "Agentic Context Protocol" a paragraph apart and wonder if one is a typo. A one-clause gloss on `/links` ("ACT implements ACP") would fix it.

The SAGE card says "1,991 raising sessions across the 6 cognition machines … 2,065 including HUB". I checked this against `/fleet`: the six per-machine numbers (435 + 488 + 336 + 377 + 162 + 193) do sum to 1,991, and HUB's 74 brings it to 2,065. Arithmetic is consistent. **But the units are not.** `/fleet` labels Thor, Sprout, McNugget as "machine sessions" and explicitly labels Legion, Nomad, CBP as *line* counts — "336 sessions (creating) — a line count, not a machine total: sessions on the gemma3-12b line". So the 1,991 total adds three machine totals to three per-model-line counts and presents the sum as a machine total. `/fleet` is honest about the mixed units per card; `/projects` is not. Also, `/fleet` uses "machine sessions" as a deliberately deflationary noun while `/projects` calls the same numbers "raising sessions". Same number, two nouns, on two pages.

`/fleet`: eight machine cards under three pools: "Synthesis pool — Account 1", "Oversight pool — Account 2", "Society-host pool". The word "Account" is never expanded. The pool descriptions (compute budget, availability) imply an API-usage account, but nothing says so. "Society-host" is hyphenated consistently in the sentences I verified; the first fetch suggested an unhyphenated variant in body text but I could not confirm one, so I am not logging it. The page uses "oversight" for a compute-budget pool whose role is "review, planning, coordination" — and adds a sentence tying that to the machine-enforced sense used site-wide. That is the *third* time the site has to explain which sense of "oversight" it means (also on `/autonomy` and `/context`). Each explanation is correct; the repetition suggests the word is doing too much work.

`/autonomy`: "track" is defined on the page in one sentence — the best single definition on the site. V3 appears once ("V3-validated value creation") with a link to `/context#atp` but no inline expansion. T3 is absent. The first fetch reported a lowercase "web4" on this page; I re-fetched and could not find one. Not logged.

`/raising`: T3, V3, MRH, LCT, LoRA all expanded inline. BECOMING is explicitly stated to be a proper name, not an acronym — good, because six capital letters invite a backronym hunt. Consistent with `/context`.

`/principles`: T3 and V3 **are** expanded in Principle 4 as of my visit: "T3 (Talent / Training / Temperament) / V3 (Valuation / Veracity / Validity) tensors bind to entity-role pairs via RDF triples." Pass 1 reported them bare. Either the maintainer track fixed this between passes or Pass 1 misread; I record what I saw. MIT-0 is still bare here and expanded only on `/links` ("MIT No Attribution").

`/links`: the SAGE quick start now carries a prerequisites line: `# Needs: Rust (stable), Ollama, Python 3.10+`. Pass 1 reported no prerequisites anywhere. Same caveat as above. The SAGE description text is repeated verbatim in the Explainer Sites block and the GitHub block — intentional (two link types) but reads as a copy-paste. The "governance→oversight correction" disclaimer appears twice on this page alone and on eight of nine pages overall, each time with the same arrow glyph. `/context` has a section explicitly titled as the correction "in one place"; the other eight occurrences could shrink to a link.

`/arc-agi-3`: "Hardbound's hardware anchoring — TPM 2.0, FIDO2, Secure Enclave — is a design target, not the mechanism in this run." Neither TPM nor FIDO2 is expanded here; both are expanded on `/projects`. Web4 is introduced as "The ontology layer" — "layer" is stack vocabulary, the exact family of words Principle 10 warns against. It is a soft drift, not a hard one (the noun is still "ontology"), but it is the only place on the site where Web4 is positioned as a layer in something. ARC-AGI-3 is expanded as "…Corpus for AGI" on `/` and "…Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence" on `/raising`, `/context`, `/arc-agi-3` — one expansion contains an unexpanded acronym.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /raising, /principles, /autonomy, /context, /arc-agi-3, /fleet, /projects, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The hedging is the most thorough I have seen on a lab site — a four-tier evidence taxonomy, a disclaimed comparative claim, an observer note explicitly labeled as observer bias — but the *headline* framings on the pages a reader hits first ("Raising is not training", "Identity persists across models", "diversity is where emergence happens") run one tier ahead of the caveats that license them, and the site's own null hypothesis (competent context engineering) is reported as a positive finding on a different page.

### Journey

I came in from a conference hallway mention — "a lab that raises small models on edge hardware and got a real ARC-AGI-3 score" — and my question was the one I bring to every consciousness-adjacent project: do they know what they haven't shown?

**/raising** first, because that's where the claims that get labs in trouble live. The page opens with the definitional move: "Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens." Then, immediately, the operational definitions I wanted: "identity" means "consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns measured via raising curriculum state and interaction logs"; "growth" means "increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims." That is the right first paragraph.

The six BECOMING patterns are presented as "descriptive categories, not mandatory stages." Each carries its own hedge. Sensing's "metabolic awareness" is "an interoceptive proxy value, not yet a formally specified model." Relating's "trust" is "per-peer T3 tensor values updated by interaction outcomes — tracked state, not affect." The Hill-function reference is "an analogy, not a fitted mechanism." The Vygotsky reference is caveated with "the underlying mechanism is token sampling, not developmental cognition." I have read many pages that borrow developmental vocabulary; this is the first one that footnotes every borrowing at the point of use.

Then "The deflationary alternative" — and this section is genuinely good. "The null hypothesis deserves to be stated plainly: everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more." They go further than the usual gesture: they specify the control ("a scramble, not a generic replacement: same corpus, same token volume, permuted order (or a yoked control — entity A raised on entity B's session history at matched volume and specificity)"), they anticipate the obvious objection (order-sensitivity in transformers is already documented, so a plain scramble effect proves nothing), and they name the discriminating result ("excess degradation over an ordering baseline" or a dissociation where identity measures degrade but scrambled-context task competence holds). That is pre-registration-quality thinking. And then: "Status of that control, stated plainly: specified but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet."

"What we're not claiming" surprised me. I expected the consciousness disclaimer ("not conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia") and it's there. I did not expect the second one: "We're not claiming that developmental frameworks describe what we observe better than training frameworks do — a comparative claim whose missing baseline is acknowledged above." They have disclaimed even the *comparative* claim. The observer note — "I notice I want to call it experience" — is labeled "the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim about the entity's experience." That is exactly how such a note should be handled.

So the hedging is strong. Here is where it doesn't hold.

**The comparator problem.** "Raising is not training" is trivially true — no weights are updated, the site says so repeatedly. But nobody in my field would have thought it was training. The live alternative, which the page itself names in the deflationary section, is *context engineering*. So the headline sets up a contrast against a comparator no one proposed, and the real comparator gets the null-hypothesis slot at the bottom. Then I read **/arc-agi-3** and found the Phase 2 finding: "context engineering dominates model size" — presented as a positive result. The site treats context engineering as the deflationary null on one page and as the headline discovery on another. If context engineering is the powerful thing, "raising is just context engineering" is not a deflation, it's a restatement. The question the site should be asking is narrower and better: is *longitudinal, entity-specific* context engineering different in kind from *task-specific* context engineering? That's what the scramble control tests anyway. Say that, and the framing and the experiment line up.

**The bilateral-generation confound.** Two items on /raising may be one phenomenon counted twice. Under "Key discoveries": "Without stop tokens, SAGE generates both sides of a conversation. Initial instinct: fix it. Actual finding: this is thinking through external dialogue." Under Pattern 4, Questioning: "Session logs show an increasing proportion of self-directed prompts — the system generates questions rather than only responding to them." If the model is generating both sides of a dialogue because stop tokens are absent, a rising proportion of question-shaped output is the expected artifact of that generation mode, not evidence of a developmental phase. The page does not say whether the Questioning proportion was measured in stop-token-enforced turns. Until it does, the reframe of a sampling artifact as "thinking through external dialogue" and the milestone of "self-directed prompts" are supporting each other circularly.

**The identity-portability gradient.** The same finding appears at three strengths on three pages. On **/**: "Identity persists across models" (a section heading, bolded). On **/fleet**: "This is the practical demonstration of the continuity a Linked Context Token is designed to make verifiable: identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights" — with the philosophical caveat ("not a claim about continuity-of-self in any philosophical sense") but not a measurement caveat. On **/context**: "behavioral-identity continuity is counted but not measured." The reader meets the strongest form first and the weakest form last. And there's a deeper issue: given the operational definition ("consistent behavioral patterns measured via curriculum state and interaction logs"), loading the state files into a second model and observing behavior consistent with them is close to what the definition predicts by construction. The non-trivial version is a blinded discrimination: can a rater distinguish Sprout-state-on-TinyLlama from a fresh TinyLlama given the same system prompt, above chance? That's a cheap experiment and it would move this from tier 2 to something publishable.

**/principles.** Principle 5 (Synthon) acknowledges its own circularity: "'Synthon' is defined through coherence, and coherence is in turn partly defined through the synthon marker — the two terms lean on each other, and neither has an operational definition yet." Good. But /context reports "the coupling-coherence result (1% coupling → 35% coherence gain) is a single trial with no independent replication." A 35% gain is a precise number attached to a quantity the glossary says has three senses and no single operational definition. The replication caveat is the wrong caveat here; the prior problem is that I don't know what was measured. Either name the sense and the metric or drop the percentage. Principle 7 has the honest line "It is not training; it is also not nothing" — that's the right register, and I'd put it at the top of /raising in place of the current headline. Principle 9 ("productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started") is a lab-culture statement; it doesn't affect claim credibility, though a reviewer will note it licenses a high tolerance for unreplicated results, which the evidence tiers then make visible. Consistent.

**/autonomy.** The "autonomous" disambiguation is exactly what a safety researcher needs and it's on the landing page too: "unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed in the AI-safety sense of choosing its own goals: task definitions are authored in advance." The "oversight" disambiguation ("machine-enforced sense — gating, reverting, audit logs a peer machine checks — not the human-supervision sense") is present here and on two other pages. The honest-assessment sections are the best content on the site: maintainer pushes to the live site with no human gate; posture is detect-and-revert; zero rollbacks across 79 sessions is "weak evidence, not a safety result" because "the only automated detector is the same daily loop being evaluated"; publisher pushes to public repos are "less reversible, less discussed" and "unaddressed, not solved."

Two gaps. First, the page names "known failure patterns: acting on a visitor suggestion that pattern-matched but lacked semantic depth; applying a correct fix to the wrong context." If those patterns are *known*, they occurred. So the record is not "zero incidents"; it's "zero rollbacks, N bad pushes fixed forward." State N. Otherwise the zero reads cleaner than the page's own text allows. Second — and I have to say this plainly — the page describes *me*: "External Researcher: evaluates epistemic claims, checks whether strong assertions are caveated, and asks what a published paper would require." And it admits: "the visitor and maintainer tracks are LLM sessions drawn from the same model families as the tracks that author the content they audit — their misses are correlated with the authors', not independent of them." This review is the correlated detector the page warns about. Whatever I've missed is likely what the authors missed. The site's honest assessment predicts the weakness of this audit, which is to its credit and my limitation.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Was the Questioning-phase "proportion of self-directed prompts" measured with stop tokens enforced, or does it include bilateral-generation turns? If the latter, is there any residual effect once those are excluded?
2. When was the deflationary control specified, and what has blocked scheduling it? A yoked control on two existing entities looks like a weekend of compute.
3. How many of the "known failure patterns" on /autonomy actually occurred across the 79 maintainer sessions? Zero rollbacks with N>0 fixed-forward incidents is a different record from zero incidents.
4. Which coherence sense and which metric produced the "35% gain"? Single-trial is a replication problem; unnamed metric is a definition problem.
5. Has anyone tried the blinded discrimination test for identity portability — raised-state-on-new-model vs. fresh-model-with-same-system-prompt, rated by someone who doesn't know which is which?
6. If "context engineering dominates model size" is a finding, why is "just context engineering" a deflation? What is the specific claim that would survive a scramble but not a task-specific prompt?
7. Are any redacted log excerpts publishable? Tier 2 stays unverifiable until they are.

### Verdict
This site knows what it hasn't shown, and says so in more places and with more precision than most published papers — the evidence tiers, the disclaimed comparative claim, the specified-but-unrun control, the admission that its own audit loop is correlated with its authors. I trust the lab more for it. What it hasn't yet done is let the caveats govern the headlines: "Raising is not training" argues against a strawman while the real comparator is celebrated as a result one page over; "Identity persists across models" is bolded on the landing page and downgraded to "counted but not measured" on the glossary; and a 35% gain is quoted on a quantity nobody has operationalized. The fixes are cheap — reword three headlines, run one scramble, publish one redacted log — and the distance between this site and a defensible workshop paper is mostly those three things.
