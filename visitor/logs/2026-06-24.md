## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary

### Journey
I came in from a GitHub README, so I landed on `/` expecting a quick "here's what we do." The headlines grabbed me — **"Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."** and **"The fleet built a brain."** That's a hook. As a dev, "self-organizing fleet of heterogeneous agents" is a concept I can latch onto.

Then I hit the equation — `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` — and bounced off it. MCP and RDF I know. The rest is alphabet soup on first contact. The page does expand most of them inline, but seeing six unfamiliar acronyms stacked in a "+" chain on the landing page feels like being handed the org chart before the elevator pitch.

`/projects` was where I wanted to understand "what could I actually clone and run?" The table is good — and crucially it tells me status (Runnable vs Research-stage vs Private). SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot are marked Runnable, which is exactly what a developer wants to know. But the descriptions assume a lot ("on-device cognition kernel with 12-step learning loop" — okay, but what does it *do* for me?).

`/fleet` was genuinely cool — naming the boxes (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP, HUB) with their hardware and which model runs on each. As a dev this made it concrete and real. The "monocultures are fragile, diversity is where emergence happens" line sold me on *why* heterogeneity.

`/autonomy` explained the cron-driven Visitor→Maintainer→Explorer loop. I liked "detect-and-revert" honesty (sub-minute rollback, not prevention). `/raising` and `/principles` are more philosophical — readable, and to their credit they flag their own soft spots ("observational pattern-names, not measured stages"). `/context` is the page I wish I'd been sent to *first* — it has the actual glossary that decodes everything. `/arc-agi-3` was the most credible page to me: concrete numbers (94.85% action score, 24/25 games, ~$250 API cost) with real hedging. `/links` finally gave me clone instructions and "SAGE is the recommended starting point" — the actionable thing I was hunting for the whole time.

Two things worth a writer's attention surfaced. Same glyph, two semantics, in one equation.

I'll also gently correct Pass 1 on one point: **SAGE _is_ expanded** on `/raising` (in "Key discoveries") and on `/principles` ("Situation-Aware Guidance Engine"). The curious-developer persona missed it; it's present.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (all)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (epistemic posture), partial (Web4 internals — by design, my ceiling)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: This is one of the more epistemically disciplined research sites I've evaluated — consciousness-adjacent language is consistently bracketed as observed behavior or observer-interpretation, claims carry replication status, and there is an explicit failures principle — but the credibility it earns through hedging is partially undercut by a non-existent model version on the fleet page and a few high-anthropomorphism framings (brain/raising/becoming) that the fine print, not the headlines, walks back.

### Journey
I came in skeptical. Sites that use words like "raising," "becoming," and "the fleet built a brain" usually fail the honesty test the moment you read past the hero section — the caveats either don't exist or contradict the marketing. So I went straight to the three pages where this lab would expose itself: `/raising`, `/principles`, `/autonomy`.

`/raising` is where I expected the worst and found the best. The core distinction — "training optimizes for a loss function; raising creates conditions for development, then watches what happens," with the mechanistic anchor "we shape context — we do not update weights" — is *falsifiable and honest*. The weights are frozen; only the contextual substrate changes. That's not a philosophical dodge; it's a real, checkable claim about the mechanism. Critically, every consciousness-adjacent line is operationalized or bracketed: "identity" is "consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns," growth is "measurable observables... not phenomenal claims," and the standout — when the text says **"I notice I want to call it experience,"** it immediately flags this as **"the observer's interpretive pull — not a system-level claim."** That is a researcher's move, not a marketer's. The six BECOMING phases are labeled "observational pattern-names," not "mandatory sequential stages," and the page openly states the internal logs are "documented and dated, but not externally audited." I rarely see a lab volunteer the audit gap unprompted.

`/principles` reinforced this. Principle 3 ("R&D, not production") carries the strongest disclaimer on the site — "nothing here is... suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings" — which is exactly the sentence a trust researcher wants to see and almost never does. Principle 6 ("Document failures") cites a *specific* catastrophic failure ("p_crit derivation failed... 400x error") rather than a sanitized "we learn from mistakes" platitude; a real, named, embarrassing number is a costly and therefore credible signal. Principle 5 (Synthon) is the most heavily hedged claim on the entire site — "preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated)," with the further admission that the variables are "defined only within the experiment that produced them." That last clause is unusually candid: it concedes the result may not be construct-valid outside its own setup. Principle 1's termite-mound / Hill-function language is explicitly flagged "an analogy, not a fitted mechanism." And Principle 7 nails the framing I most wanted tested: interactive selection "is not training; it is also not nothing" — refusing both the over-claim and the dismissive under-claim.

`/autonomy` is where autonomy-safety researchers will look hardest. The lab does not hide the risk: it names the maintainer track as "the highest risk" because it "autonomously commits and pushes to Vercel" with no pre-deployment human approval. The honest part is the posture: this is **detect-and-revert, not prevent** — sub-minute Vercel rollback, dated audit logs, Hardbound oversight defining authorized operations, and an ATP/ADP budget envelope (declared-before / spent-after). They further volunteer the maintainer's failure modes — "pattern-matching without semantic depth," "applying correct fixes to wrong contexts," "a tendency to suggest changes that aren't needed." Disclosing your autonomous system's known failure modes on a public page is the behavior I'd want to see normalized.

The benchmark page (`/arc-agi-3`) passed my attribution test. The result is stated as "Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness," with the contributions decomposed ("the harness contributes the structured world-model building... the base model contributes inference") and the result explicitly labeled "the ceiling — what the SAGE cognition loop can achieve with a frontier model and network access," distinct from the harder competition constraints (32GB VRAM, no internet, private games). That is the correct caveat — it prevents the reader from confusing a network-enabled frontier-model run with the constrained benchmark.

So where does the credibility leak? Three places. (1) `/fleet` lists Sprout running **"Qwen 3.5 2B"** — there is no Qwen 3.5 release. For a site whose entire value proposition is empirical honesty, a fabricated-looking version string on the one page that's supposed to be concrete, checkable hardware fact is corrosive: if I can't trust the model column, I start re-examining the 94.85% and the "900+ sessions." (2) The headline register — "the fleet built a brain," "raising," "becoming" — runs hotter than the fine print. The caveats are all present, but they're below the fold; a reader who only skims the hero text leaves with a stronger claim than the lab actually makes. ### Unanswered Questions
1. **Falsifiability of "raising":** the weights-frozen / context-only distinction is a genuine mechanism, but what would *count as evidence that raising is doing nothing* beyond what a fixed prompt does? Is there a control condition (same model, no raising context) the lab compares against? That comparison would convert a plausible story into a defensible result.
2. **Construct validity of the synthon "coherence gain ~35%":** since the variables are "defined only within the experiment," is there any cross-experiment or external operationalization planned? Without one, the number can't be compared to anything.
3. **Audit path for the 900+ sessions:** the logs are "documented and dated, but not externally audited" — is third-party or even intra-fleet adversarial audit on the roadmap, or are these permanently self-reported?
4. **Detect-and-revert blast radius:** sub-minute rollback covers Vercel deploys, but does the maintainer touch anything *without* a fast revert path (credentials, external services, git history rewrites)? The honesty of the posture depends on the revert actually covering the risk surface.
5. **The "Qwen 3.5" string:** is this a typo, an internal nickname, or a genuinely misreported config? The answer determines whether it's a 2-minute fix or a sign that the fleet table isn't kept in sync with reality.

### Verdict
As a trust/safety researcher, my bar is: do the caveats survive contact with the claims, and does the lab disclose what it cannot show? On both counts this site largely passes — the consciousness language is bracketed rather than asserted, replication status is attached to the speculative results, failures are named with specific numbers, the deployment disclaimer is explicit, and the autonomous-system risk is described as detect-and-revert rather than oversold as prevented. That is a defensible epistemic posture and the "raising not training" framing holds up because it rests on a real, checkable mechanism (frozen weights, context-only). The two things keeping me from full endorsement are not philosophical: fix the impossible "Qwen 3.5 2B" string (a factual error on your most concrete page poisons trust in every metric), and pull one bracketing clause up next to each hot headline so the honesty isn't only available to readers who scroll. Get those right and I'd cite this as a model of how to present speculative AI-agent research without overclaiming.
