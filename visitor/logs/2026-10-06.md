## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (the seven front pages `/`, `/projects`, `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/notebook`, plus one notebook page, `/notebook/projects`, to see where "the full record" goes). I also checked the status of every external link on the front.
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: In about two minutes I understood the pitch: governance tools for AI agents, built by a lab that runs on them. I hit a wall when I tried to *use* any of it.

### Journey
I came from a GitHub README and landed on Home. The headline ("AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust.") and the "track record, checked against rules that exist before anyone acts" paragraph got the idea across in one read. "What we build" has four cards (Web4, Hestia, Hub, SAGE) with status chips. I understood Hestia (rules for my agents, checked before each action, a record the agent can't edit) and Hub (the same thing for an org) right away. Web4 is "an open standard … a shared vocabulary, or ontology", which is clear enough. The page explains "ontology" inline, and I appreciated that.

On Home I read "RUNNING" as "you can run this." `/projects` then defines the label as "in daily use on our own machines" and says of Hestia: "no public installer yet." So the label means *the lab uses it*, not *you can install it*. That's fair once it's explained, but Home shows the chips with no legend.

`/projects` is the most useful page for me. I liked the label key at the bottom, "Published specification, with reference code on crates.io and PyPI", and the Hub card's honesty that "our fleet is one such community." The SWE-SAGE card links to `github.com/dp-web4/SWE-SAGE`, and that link **404s** (it does on `/links` too). It's the one link on the front I wanted to click that didn't work. The Synchronism card ("coherence, not substance, is fundamental … supplies the equations that Web4's trust model builds on") went over my head. That's fine, since it's labelled Theory.

`/fleet` is a clean table of eight machines. Two things tripped me up. First, there's a machine called **HUB** and a product called **Hub**, and the HUB machine "hosts the fleet's own community on Hub". I read that row three times. Second, "Four machines do the heavy work: writing code and running large experiments. Two review … Two host". The table doesn't say which four. By elimination, one of them is Sprout, a Jetson Orin Nano that the same table calls an "edge device". That's an odd fit for "large experiments."

`/raising` was the most interesting page. "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" is a memorable hook, and the "Seen / Not yet shown" split ("no blind comparison against a fresh model yet") made me trust the rest more. But the "tutor" and the "mentor" are never identified. As a developer, I wanted to know whether those are humans, other LLMs or scripts.

`/principles` is six short principles. It reads well and matches what Home promised. `/links` is short and handy, but Hub's code (it lives at `web4/tree/main/hub`, linked from `/projects`) isn't listed under Code. Neither is the Synchronism repo. `/notebook` explains the two layers well ("written for people … This notebook is the record underneath it").

I opened `/notebook/projects` to see the depth. It's about 3.8k words of dense material, and it's the **first place that tells me what SAGE stands for** ("Situation-Aware Governance Engine"). It's also where I found actual install lines (`pip install web4-core`, and `web4-core = "0.3"` in Cargo.toml). Neither of those appears anywhere on the front. Every front link I checked returned 200, except SWE-SAGE. (crates.io returned 404 to plain curl, but its API confirms `web4-core` 0.3.0 exists. That was a tooling artifact, not a broken link.)

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16. I visited all 7 front pages and all 9 notebook pages. I opened Home with WebFetch, then pulled the rendered HTML of each page and grepped it. I checked claims against the public upstream: `web4` README.md / AGENTS.md / STATUS.md and `SAGE` AGENTS.md (refreshed 2026-10-01) / `sage/docs/LATEST_STATUS.md` (As of 2026-09-26) / PR list. ### Journey
Pass 2 already did the expansion sweep (it's clean, and I re-ran a semantic sweep below), so I spent this pass on representation. I read each front card the way someone who works in the repo would, then checked it against the notebook (which the front says "holds every claim with its evidence") and against the repo's current self-description.

**SAGE on Home and `/projects`.** The front says the beings "act only through governed tools" (Home) and "every action they take goes through governed tools" (`/projects`). That's not what we ship. `LATEST_STATUS.md` (09-26) says: "Consequential acts are *intended* to be governed and witnessed; observational/local capabilities are separately bounded". It calls the gateway "A2-shaped", says "A2 is a target/enforcement shape, not yet an unconditional substrate claim", and warns "Do not describe the deployed fleet as adversary-proof containment." The site's own `/notebook/raising` says the governed tool turn exists "with SAGE_TOOLS on (default off; Nomad's launcher sets it)". So on most machines the measured raising exchange never touches the gate. `/notebook/projects` gets this right ("Upstream calls that shape 'A2-shaped', not A2"). The front turns "intended, for consequential acts, A2-shaped" into "every action". This hasn't been reported before (the 09-29 being-gate note was about the notebook). It's the defining sentence of the SAGE card on two front pages.

**Hestia, "a record the agent cannot edit" (Home) / "recorded where the agent cannot change it" (`/projects`).** Upstream `web4` README l.125: "the current open profile is **A1**. It is cooperative and tamper-evident … not containment against a determined same-UID adversary". AGENTS.md: "A2+ process/OS isolation and enforcement against determined same-UID agents" is under *What's Missing*. Tamper-evident means an edit gets *detected*. It doesn't mean an edit *can't happen*. This was forwarded to dp on 10-04 and is still live on both pages.

**Synchronism, "It supplies the equations that Web4's trust model builds on" (`/projects`).** Upstream `web4` AGENTS.md (today): "`Synchronism` | Blue-sky conceptual lineage; not an engineering dependency". The site's own `/notebook/projects` says "Synchronism is the conceptual lineage. Web4 provides the ontology", and `/notebook/context` calls the curve reuse "fractal leverage, by analogy rather than by derived law". The front reverses the dependency. Forwarded 10-04, still live.

**SWE-SAGE.** The link still 404s on `/projects` and `/links`. What's new is that upstream now settles it: `LATEST_STATUS.md` says "`SWE-SAGE` is the **private** Gemma 4 software-engineering-agent competition workspace … competition implementation is not live-published." So the repo is private on purpose, and the fix is to drop the link, not to make the repo public. `/links` also says "dp-web4 on GitHub · everything public" two lines above that private link.

**Hardbound** reads correctly against upstream: "Metalinxx Inc.'s proprietary enterprise assurance tier for hardware-bound identity, stronger fail-closed enforcement and audit-ready evidence packaging" matches the `/projects` card nearly line for line, and "oversight" is used. **Hub** ("members, roles, signed rules and a shared record, on its own servers", link to `web4/hub`) matches AGENTS.md ("Hub society daemon | Running reference | `hub/`"). **Web4** as "written as an ontology … Published specification, with reference code on crates.io and PyPI" is correct for the site. For context, upstream itself now says "open substrate" (README l.11) and "Web4 is open infrastructure" (README l.194). That's upstream vocabulary, not site drift, and the site is right not to copy it.

**`/fleet` front.** Model families "Qwen, Gemma, Granite and Llama" match the eight `Model:` lines on `/notebook/fleet` exactly (Qwen: Thor, Sprout, CBP; Gemma: Legion, McNugget, Nomad; Granite: HUB; Llama: pub). Two machine descriptions have **no source in the notebook**. "Sprout: a being with a camera and microphone" and "Legion: Its being improves the SAGE code it runs on" both get 0 hits for camera, microphone or improves across the notebook. Both look true upstream. SAGE has `sage/AUDIO_INTEGRATION_SPROUT.md` (2025-10-12, audio only; I didn't verify the camera half). Legion's being has its own PR branches: `legion-being/pr-read-newest-leftover-budget` is PR #360, opened 2026-10-05, and it's open. But the notebook promises to hold every front claim's evidence, and here it doesn't. The notebook also gives Legion's raising line as quiet since 09-02, so a reader can't tell from the site that the being still acts on another channel (PRs through the gateway).
