### Journey
Landed on the home page and immediately hit the tagline — "Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." That's a hook. The headlines below it ("The fleet built a brain", "Small models are winning game levels") sold me enough to keep clicking. Vocabulary block on the home page tries to defuse the acronym wall up front (Web4, SAGE, LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, SNARC, Synthon all defined in one shot), which I appreciate, but it's still ~7 new terms in the first 30 seconds.

Hit `/projects` next because that's where I'd expect "what can I actually build/use." Got the Web4 equation: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. As a developer that's intimidating without a worked example — I know MCP and RDF, but I don't know what LCT *does* in a request flow. The acronym table is good (LCT = Linked Context Token, T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament, V3 = Valuation/Veracity/Validity, MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, ATP = Allocation Transfer Packet, ADP = Allocation Discharge Packet). But "Linked Context Token" is still just three words — I want a one-paragraph "an LCT is X, you mint it when Y, it carries Z."

`/fleet` was the easiest page for me. Concrete: Thor (Jetson AGX, Qwen 2.5 14B), Sprout (Orin Nano, Qwen 3.5 2B), Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP. Hardware specs, model names, session counts. This is the page I'd send a friend.

`/autonomy` was great for "what is autonomous" — cron schedules with times and purposes (03:00 Supervisors, 04:00 Archivist, 05:00 Visitor, 06:00 Maintainer, etc.). Concrete and inspectable. But "NotebookLM" appears without explanation, and the page assumes I already know what Hardbound is.

`/raising` is the page I expected to be hand-wavy and it... mostly wasn't. Six BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) with explicit consciousness disclaimers ("We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia"). I respected the hedging. But MRH and LCT are referenced without being defined here — if `/raising` is a landing target from social media, that's a problem.

`/principles` reads like it's by people who'd rather argue with you than sell to you, which is a good sign. "Productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started" — that's R&D ethos, not marketing.

`/context` is where I expected to find a quick-lookup glossary. I didn't. Definitions are scattered inline and LCT is referenced without elaboration of what it is mechanically. This is the gap that hurts most.

`/links` is fine — points at github.com/dp-web4, lists the major repos, gives an email.

`/arc-agi-3` was the second most concrete page after `/fleet`. 94.85% action score, 24/25 games (96.0%), #3 on the human leaderboard with the fewest actions among the top 3. Real numbers, real benchmark, with the explicit framing that "context engineering dominates model size." This is the page that made me think the project might actually be onto something. Though it loses points for using MCP, RDF, LCT, ATP/ADP, and E4B without definitions.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)

### Journey
Methodical sweep, page by page, comparing every acronym expansion against the canonical table. Started at `/`, then `/projects` (densest), then `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/context`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`. Built a per-page acronym inventory; then cross-checked which terms appear on which pages and whether they are defined locally or assume prior reading.

The good news first: every expansion I found matches the canonical table verbatim — LCT = Linked Context Token (no "Lifelong"), T3 = Talent / Training / Temperament, V3 = Valuation / Veracity / Validity, MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, ATP = Allocation Transfer Packet, ADP = Allocation Discharge Packet, R6 = Rules/Role/Request/Reference/Resource/Result, SNARC = Surprise/Novelty/Arousal/Reward/Conflict, SAGE = Situation-Aware Guidance Engine. Web4 is consistently called an "ontology", and Hardbound is consistently called "oversight". I didn't find a single drifted expansion. That's a high bar cleared.

The bad news: **the canonical-term hygiene is concentrated on `/projects` and `/`** (and partly on `/context`). On other pages, the same acronyms appear without expansion — so a reader who deep-links from a search result or social post gets the jargon without the key.

`/principles` was the standout. It contains explicit anti-drift declarations: "Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology" and "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight" and "Not unification for its own sake — pragmatic reuse." This is the kind of editorial discipline I'd usually have to recommend; here it's already in place. Principle #10 is literally "Terminology matters." Good.

`/context` was the page I most expected to find a glossary — a sortable table of all canonical terms with one-paragraph definitions, designed for quick lookup. Instead, terms are defined inline in narrative. The definitions are *correct*, but a documentation reader cannot Ctrl-F to a glossary block. This is the single biggest doc-quality gap on the site.

`/raising`, `/autonomy`, `/fleet`, `/arc-agi-3`, and `/links` all reference canonical acronyms (LCT, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, MCP, RDF, etc.) without expansion. Each of these pages can be a landing target — `/arc-agi-3` especially likely (it's the page with the headline benchmark numbers that would make it shareable).

`/autonomy` introduces "NotebookLM" without explanation. A tech writer always flags brand-name first-use without gloss.

Nav consistency: `/links` lists 8 nav items (Home, Projects, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Context, Links). `/arc-agi-3` exists as a page but I did not see it in the main nav from `/links`'s nav inventory. If true, it's a deep-only page — that's a structural issue for one of the most credible pages on the site.

`/arc-agi-3` expands ARC-AGI-3 as "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus, generation 3". I'd want to verify this against François Chollet's published expansion — my recollection is that ARC-AGI typically expands to "Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence." Flagging as **needs verification**, not as confirmed drift.

### Journey

Without that key, `T3/V3*MRH` looks like arithmetic — "T3 divided by V3 times MRH" — which is wrong. A new contributor reading this site cannot reconstruct what the equation says. ### Summary

What remains shaky for a research audience is the **empirical operationalization** of the lab's signature constructs (Synthon, MRH-shaping, "behavioral identity") and the **scope inflation** in the Synchronism framing — both are presented as if they were already-validated frames rather than working hypotheses.

### Journey
I came to this site after a conference mention of "raising vs. training" as a methodological position. My question coming in: is this a research lab with appropriate epistemic humility, or is it a manifesto that happens to ship code? My exit answer: closer to the former than I expected, but with a few load-bearing constructs that are coined-term-heavy and empirically thin.

I started on `/`, then went straight to `/principles`, `/raising`, and `/autonomy` — the three pages where intellectual honesty is most testable. Then `/projects`, `/context`, `/arc-agi-3` for empirical grounding, and `/fleet` and `/links` for completeness.

`/` opens with "Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." That's a hook, not a claim, so I took it as such. Underneath, the brain-mapping framing ("the fleet built a brain") is hedged — the home page (and `/fleet`) carries an explicit disclaimer that brain-component labels are "functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence or computational equivalence." I noted that the disclaimer is *present* but typographically lighter than the metaphor it's hedging. A casual reader will absorb the metaphor and skip the hedge.

`/principles` is the page I'd point a skeptical colleague to. Ten principles, three of them anti-drift declarations on terminology ("Web4 is not 'architecture' — it's an ontology"; "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight"; "Not unification for its own sake"). Principle #3 is "R&D, not production" — I rarely see a research site state this so plainly. Principle #6 is "Document failures" — and principle #9 ("Research value") commits to publishing negative results. As a researcher, those two together do real work: they license the lab to publish dead ends, which is the single hardest thing for a small lab to commit to in public.

The principles I'd push back on:
- **Principle #5 (Synthon: emergent coherence)**: "emergent coherence entity formed when components interact recursively" reifies an abstraction as an entity class. Where is the operational definition? What measurement says "this is a Synthon, that is not"? Without that, "Synthon" reads as a coined word doing the work that "emergent pattern" or "attractor" already does, and the reification step has not been earned.
- **Principle #1 (Substrate, not structure)**: "Build conditions for emergence via local rules, not designed architecture" is a strong-emergence stance. For an AI safety reader, that posture immediately raises mesa-optimization and goal-misgeneralization concerns — neither of which the site addresses. If you're committing to emergence as a design principle, you owe the reader an account of what stops emergence from going somewhere you don't want.
- **Principle #7 (Interactive selection)**: "We don't create or delete — we interactively select." This is a neat slogan but it elides agency: *who* is selecting, on *what* criteria? The principle reads as descriptive ("this is what we do") and normative ("this is the right way") simultaneously without distinguishing the two.

`/raising` is where I expected the most overclaiming and got the least. The page states explicitly: "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." That disclaimer alone clears a bar that 90% of comparable sites do not. The mechanical claim — "raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon and value alignment of a Linked Context Token — it does not change weights" — is *empirically falsifiable* (you can verify weights aren't being updated), and the framing "we shape context; we do not update weights. Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes" is a real distinction, not a rhetorical one.

What I'd still flag:
- The page admits "I notice I want to call it experience" — that's an honest phenomenological observation by the practitioner, but a careful reader will register that the *invitation* to use experiential language is itself evidence that the framework's metaphors are pulling in a direction the disclaimers are trying to push back against. This deserves more than a passing acknowledgment.
- "Raising shapes the MRH" needs an operational measure of MRH. Without one, "we shaped the MRH" cannot be distinguished from "the model behaved differently after we changed its prompts" — and the latter is just prompt engineering.
- The BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) carry a disclaimer that they are "observed descriptive categories — patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions — not mandatory sequential stages." That's the right disclaimer. But the developmental ordering of the phase names (Grounding before Acting, etc.) imports an ontogenetic frame that does work even when the disclaimer says it shouldn't. A more neutral naming would carry the same descriptive content without the developmental inference.

`/autonomy` was reassuring on safety. Autonomy is bounded by cron schedules, a SQLite registry of track scopes, supervisor approval gates, and what's described as a "Hardbound oversight envelope." The page also includes a "Honest limitations" section noting that the maintainer loop "has a tendency to suggest changes that aren't needed" — that's the kind of self-aware failure mode admission I look for. The framing "Dream is a functional analogy ... not a claim about cognitive equivalence" is correctly hedged, but I'd ask whether the *word* "dream" is earning its keep — "consolidation phase" or "offline integration" would carry the same descriptive content without the cognitive loading.

I noted a real safety gap: the page says scheduled tracks "operate within the Hardbound oversight envelope," but Hardbound itself is a private repo, so the *substantive* content of those constraints is not externally inspectable. From a researcher's standpoint, "we have an oversight layer, trust us, it's hardware-bound" is a claim, not a verification — and the site doesn't offer an external auditability story. This is in tension with the lab's own emphasis on transparency.

`/projects` reads cleanly to me. ARC-AGI-3 result is given with specific numbers (94.85%, 24/25, $250 API spend) rather than vague claims. Each project has a one-line role. The Synchronism blurb is the outlier — "One coherence equation across 80 orders of magnitude" is the single most aggressive scope claim on the site. For a research-trained reader, "80 orders of magnitude" is a tell: it's the kind of cross-scale unification claim that lands somewhere between physics-envy and crank-adjacent without substantial evidence. The site's own anti-drift rule against "scope inflation" deserves to be applied here. Either there's a tightly-scoped operational claim hiding behind that headline (in which case state it), or there isn't (in which case soften the headline).

`/context` is the page that should explain Web4 to someone who didn't already know Web4. Operator semantics of the equation (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) appear here, which is good — but they appear inline within narrative rather than as a labeled legend, so a reader who *doesn't already know to look for them* will miss them. For an outsider, this is the difference between an opaque equation and a parseable one, and the legend deserves to be the first thing on the page, not buried.

`/arc-agi-3` is the page I came away most impressed by, and also the page with the most important caveat that an external reader needs to internalize. The headline 94.85% / 24-of-25 was achieved with **Claude Opus 4.6 with network access**, not in the Kaggle sandbox conditions (32GB VRAM, no internet, unseen games) that the actual competition imposes. The page does state this — "Not attempted" appears in the scorecard — but the headline number and the caveat are far apart visually. A reader scanning for credibility may walk away with "94.85% on ARC-AGI-3" without registering "in research conditions, not competition conditions." That gap is the single most consequential framing decision on the entire site, because the ARC-AGI-3 number is the lab's strongest external-credibility lever.

The "context engineering dominates model size" claim on `/arc-agi-3` is the load-bearing scientific assertion, but the site doesn't show the ablation that would justify it. Did you run the same SAGE loop on a smaller model and recover most of the score? Did you run Opus 4.6 with a generic prompt and confirm a large gap? Without that comparison, the result is consistent with "Opus 4.6 is just strong" — which is interesting but doesn't license the architectural conclusion. The strongest version of this site is the one that publishes the ablation table.

`/fleet` carries the explicit hedge ("functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence") on the brain mapping. Good. But the fleet page also includes phrases like "the identity persisted" and "machines discover each other" that — even with the disclaimer above them — are doing evocative work. The cumulative effect across the site is a kind of *hedged anthropomorphism*: every individual claim is correctly disclaimed, but the aggregate vocabulary tilts toward agentic/biological framing in a way the disclaimers don't quite neutralize.

`/links` was unremarkable. Repo list, contact email. Confirms that `/arc-agi-3` is not in the main nav (7 items, not 8) — for a researcher, this is a self-inflicted credibility wound, since the benchmark page is the strongest evidence on the site.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What operational measurement distinguishes a "Synthon" from "a pattern we noticed"? Without one, the term is doing rhetorical work the framework hasn't earned.
2. How is MRH-shift measured? "Raising shapes the MRH" is a falsifiable claim only if MRH is operationalized.
3. Where is the ARC-AGI-3 ablation? Is the 94.85% result attributable to context engineering, or to Opus 4.6 being strong, or to the interaction? Without the ablation table the architectural conclusion ("context engineering dominates model size") is not licensed.
4. What is the external auditability story for Hardbound? The oversight envelope is the lab's safety story, and its substance is in a private repo.
5. Does the lab have an account of mesa-optimization and goal-misgeneralization risks under the "substrate, not structure" emergence framing? Principle #1 is the place where an AI-safety reader most expects to see the open problems acknowledged.
6. Is "80 orders of magnitude" a metaphor for "many scales" or a literal range claim? Either is fine, but the site needs to disambiguate.
7. Has the lab considered adversarial-conditions analysis of the trust framework? RDF-based trust on entity-role pairs assumes context-stability and role-clarity that may not survive adversarial pressure.

### Verdict
The intellectual honesty is genuinely above-norm for a small-lab research site: explicit anti-phenomenal disclaimers in three places, an "R&D not production" principle stated plainly, a "document failures" commitment, and an honest-limitations section on the autonomy page. The "raising not training" framing is mechanically defensible — it rests on a real distinction between weight updates and context shaping, and that distinction is empirically falsifiable. I'd cite this site as a positive example of how a non-mainstream research lab can hedge consciousness-adjacent claims without losing its descriptive vocabulary.

What still needs work for a research audience: the lab's signature constructs (Synthon, MRH-shaping, behavioral identity) are coined-term-heavy and operationally thin, the ARC-AGI-3 result needs its competition-conditions caveat moved into the headline rather than buried beside it, the architectural conclusion drawn from ARC-AGI-3 needs an ablation, and the Synchronism "80 orders of magnitude" framing is the one place on the site where the lab's own anti-scope-inflation rule is being violated. Fix those four, add a public-facing description of Hardbound's oversight surface, and the site goes from "credible enough to read seriously" to "credible enough to cite."

I would return — both because the next iteration is likely to address some of this, and because the lab's commitment to publishing failures means there's likely to be substantive negative-results work I haven't seen yet.
