## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/links`)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understand what they're *studying* and I believe them when they hedge, but after nine pages I still can't tell you what happens when I run `git clone SAGE`.

### Journey

I came from a repo README, so my mental model going in was "this is a codebase." The landing page immediately reframed it as a research collective — seven machines, fourteen repos, one loop. That's a good hook. The three "How We Work" cards did their job in about thirty seconds: autonomous cycles, heterogeneous hardware, four projects that share one equation.

The homepage's "Getting Started" line told me to read `/context` for vocabulary, then clone SAGE or ACT. I appreciated being told where to go, but the order is wrong for me. I don't want to read a 44-term glossary before I clone something. I want to clone something, watch it print output, and *then* find out what a synthon is. The site consistently assumes I'll read before I run.

Scrolling further, the Vocabulary Primer is genuinely well-written — LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, SNARC, MCP, RDF all expanded inline. Two things snagged me. First, "synthon" appears in the "What Makes This Different" section *above* where the primer defines it, so I hit it cold. Second, the formula:

> `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3 * MRH + ATP/ADP`

with the note that `/` means "verified by" in `T3/V3` but "declared→discharged allocation" in `ATP/ADP`. I stared at this for a while. If the same operator means two different things depending on its operands, this isn't a formula — it's a mnemonic wearing a formula's clothes. I'd rather they just called it that. As written, it invites me to parse it as math and then punishes me for trying. (The honesty of *disclosing* the overload is good. The overload itself is the problem.)

`/projects` was where I most expected to find my footing and least did. Twelve cards, each with a maturity label (Research-stage / Runnable / Private), a one-line description, and repo links. The maturity labels are excellent — I knew instantly that Web4 is spec-and-schemas and SAGE is something I can actually execute. But "Runnable" is a promise the page doesn't keep. There's no install command, no dependency list, no "here's what it prints." The page's own advice is to clone the repo, read the code, and check GitHub issues. That's a fine answer for a contributor. For someone deciding whether to spend an evening on this, "read the code" is the highest-friction possible entry point.

Two specific stumbles on that page. The SNARC card links to `github.com/dp-web4/engram`. Nothing tells me SNARC and engram are the same thing — I assumed a broken link and only reconciled it later because `/links` mentions engram-adjacent work. And Hardbound is marked Private with "424+ attack vectors catalogued," which is an interesting number attached to something I can't look at; the page never says *why* it's private, so it reads as a tease rather than a boundary.

`/context` is the best page on the site for a patient reader. It defines everything, and — this surprised me — it includes an explicit evidence hierarchy separating externally validated results (the ARC score, with a public scorecard link) from internal observations from unreplicated ones. It also volunteers that "coherence" is used in three distinct senses. A site that tells me its own vocabulary is overloaded has earned some trust.

`/fleet` was fun and concrete: seven machines, real hardware, an RTX 2060 SUPER running TinyLlama and posting the 94.85% ARC score. This is the page that made the lab feel real rather than theoretical. But every machine's session count carries a bare `(creating)` label — "91 sessions (creating)", "115+ sessions (creating)". I read that as a status like "in progress." It is actually the name of the fifth BECOMING phase from `/raising`, a page I hadn't visited yet. Nothing on `/fleet` says so or links there. This is the single most confusing thing I encountered.

I also noticed Sprout is listed as running "Qwen 3.5 2B." I don't track every release, but I know Qwen 2.5 and Qwen 3, and 3.5 doesn't ring a bell. Meanwhile the homepage says Sprout transferred from Qwen 0.5B to TinyLlama 1.1B. Those may both be true across time, but a reader can't tell.

`/autonomy` was the clearest page on the site — a timestamped table from 03:00 to 08:00, each track with a one-line function, and the Visitor→Maintainer→Explorer loop stated plainly. It also contains the caveat I respected most anywhere: the maintainer pushes to the public site without pre-approval, and the oversight model is "detect-and-revert (audit log + sub-minute rollback), not prevent," with human review described as asynchronous and unbounded. Most sites would have buried that. It's stated as a design property.

`/raising` and `/principles` are the pages my persona is least equipped to evaluate and, oddly, the ones I found easiest to read. The raising-vs-training distinction is mechanical and checkable: shape context, don't update weights. Frozen parameters, in-context dynamics. That I understand — it's prompt engineering with a longer memory and a stronger claim. The consciousness hedging is explicit ("We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia"), and the page runs a null hypothesis against itself: all of this could be competent context engineering, and *that control has not been run*. Same posture on `/arc-agi-3`, where the missing ablation — same model, no harness — is the first thing on the page rather than a footnote. I can't judge whether the science is right. I can tell it isn't hiding.

`/principles` uses `p_crit` and "attractor basins" without defining either. Principle 6 references "the p_crit derivation's catastrophic failure" as though I'd know what that was.

`/links` gave me the entry point I'd wanted five pages earlier: SAGE is Python, runs locally, start there; ACT is Go/Cosmos SDK. That belongs on `/projects`.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What does SAGE actually *do* when I run it? Not the 12-step loop as a list of verbs — what appears on stdout in the first sixty seconds?
2. Why is SNARC the same thing as engram, and which name should I search for?
3. `(creating)` on `/fleet` — is that a phase the machine is currently *in*, or a phase it has *reached*? The word suggests state; the count suggests history.
4. Is Web4 something I can implement against today, or is it a spec awaiting its first non-lab implementation? "Reference implementations in SAGE and ACT" suggests the latter, but doesn't say it.
5. Does the ARC-AGI-3 harness do anything a well-written system prompt couldn't? The site says this is exactly the unrun experiment — which means the honest answer today is "nobody knows," and I respect that. But it's the only thing I actually want to know.
6. If the fleet's headline finding is "context engineering dominates model size," is there a minimal repro? A tiny model, a harness, and a task where the harness demonstrably wins?

### Verdict

The intellectual honesty is the product. I arrived expecting either an ambitious framework or a consciousness pitch, and instead found a site that puts its missing ablation in the first paragraph of its best result, discloses that "coherence" means three different things, admits its own oversight is detect-and-revert rather than prevent, and states the deflationary null hypothesis against itself while noting the control hasn't been run. I have no way to evaluate whether the research is correct. I can see clearly that it isn't overclaiming, and for a lab writing about emergent AI identity, that's the thing I was scanning for.

What it doesn't have is a door I can walk through. Nine pages of clear, careful, well-cross-referenced explanation, and the closest thing to a quickstart is a sentence on `/links` saying SAGE is Python and runs locally. "Runnable" as a maturity label sets an expectation the page never pays off. Every friction I logged is a small one; the aggregate is that this site is built for a reader who has already decided to invest, not for one deciding whether to. Fix the `(creating)` gloss and put one `pip install` on the SAGE card and I'd have gone from "maybe" to "yes" without any of the substance changing.

### Journey

I checked every acronym on every page against the canonical table, then re-fetched with targeted verbatim extraction wherever a summary looked like it might be paraphrasing rather than quoting. Two of my initial flags dissolved under that second look. I'm reporting the dissolution as well as the survivors, because a false drift report costs the maintainer track a real fix cycle.

The canonical vocabulary is in excellent shape. LCT is "Linked Context Token" on all five pages that expand it. T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, SNARC, MCP, RDF, ACP — every expansion I found matches the canonical table exactly, with no page contradicting another. "Web4" is called an ontology on `/`, `/context`, `/projects`, `/autonomy`, and `/links`, and `/context` goes further with "not architecture or infrastructure." Hardbound is "oversight" everywhere; "governance" never appears as a description of it. "Production ready" appears nowhere, and `/principles` #3 is titled "R&D, not production." For a nine-page site with this much specialized vocabulary, that is a genuinely unusual result.

**The one real drift I found is in the operator glosses, not the terms.** The homepage and `/context` both state the rule plainly: `/` = "verified by," `*` = "contextualized by," `+` = "augmented with." `/context` even volunteers that `/` is overloaded — it means "verified by" in `T3/V3` but is a bare declared→discharged pair in `ATP/ADP`, which it calls "same glyph, unrelated semantics." Disclosing that is the right call.

But the `+` operator gets three different prose renderings, and one of them is on the page a newcomer reads second. The `/projects` Web4 card says:

> "T3 ... **verified by** V3 ..., **contextualized by** Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH), **anchored to** Linked Context Token (LCT) — with resources **tracked via** Allocation Transfer Packet (ATP) and Allocation Discharge Packet (ADP), **augmented with** MCP ... and RDF ..."

In the equation `MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`, every one of those connectors is the same `+`. The card renders it as "anchored to" for LCT, "augmented with" for MCP and RDF, and "tracked via" for ATP/ADP — three glosses for one glyph, in a single sentence. `/links` gets it right ("...contextualized by MRH ..., augmented with MCP and RDF"), which means the two pages disagree. This matters more than a normal style nit: the site's whole case for the equation being meaningful rests on the operators carrying stable semantics. Pass 1 (as a developer) complained the equation punishes you for parsing it as math. This is why. The `/` overload is disclosed; the `+` overload is not, and unlike `/`, it isn't intentional.

**Second real finding: `FIDO2 (Fast IDentity Online 2)` on `/projects` is not a correct expansion.** FIDO expands to Fast IDentity Online. FIDO2 is the *name of a specification set* — WebAuthn plus CTAP2 — not "Fast IDentity Online, version 2." The trailing "2" is part of the proper noun, not part of the expansion. A security researcher would notice; it sits three words from "424+ attack vectors catalogued," where credibility is doing work. `TPM 2.0` immediately before it is expanded correctly as "Trusted Platform Module (TPM) 2.0" — the "2.0" left outside the parens, exactly the treatment FIDO2 doesn't get.

On structure: `/context` is the site's spine and knows it — 26 sections, a glossary at the top, then one section per term. `/raising` has a naming inconsistency in its own scaffolding: the section is headed "BECOMING: **six observed patterns**," and then every subsection is "**Phase** N: ...". Patterns and phases are not the same noun. A phase implies sequence and a state you're currently in; a pattern implies a recurring shape you can observe more than once. Which one is meant determines whether `/fleet`'s "(creating)" means "this machine is in phase 5 now" or "phase-5 behavior has been observed here." I could not tell from the site, and that ambiguity is downstream of the heading.

Which brings me to `(creating)`. I independently reached Pass 1's conclusion: `/raising` has "Phase 5: Creating," `/fleet` has bare lowercase `(creating)` on five of seven machines, and nothing on `/fleet` glosses or links it. I'll add two details Pass 1 didn't surface. **Legion carries no phase label at all** ("25+ sessions", full stop), and **HUB has no session count**. So the field is populated on 5 of 7 cards and the reader has no way to know whether Legion's blank means "not yet assessed," "a different phase," or "we forgot." An unlabeled cell in a table where its neighbors are labeled reads as missing data, not as absence of the property. And the casing differs — `Creating` on `/raising`, `creating` on `/fleet` — which is precisely the signal that suppresses the reader's guess that they're the same word.

**Two flags I retracted after verification.** First, the SNARC/engram name mismatch: I went to confirm Pass 1's finding and the `/projects` SNARC card *does* explain it — "Code lives in the dp-web4/engram repo — that's where the link below lands." That sentence is present. Pass 1's read of that card missed it. The finding should not be actioned. Second, I initially had `/context`'s Fractal leverage section flagged for using "unification," a word the canonical table lists as one to avoid. Reading it verbatim: "Not unification for its own sake — pragmatic reuse of patterns that work at one scale." It uses the word to *reject* it. That's correct usage, and the negation is the point.

Real gaps remain in acronym first-use. `ACT` is never expanded on the homepage, which is the one page that tells the reader to go clone it. `/principles` uses `p_crit`, "attractor basins," and — new, not in Pass 1 — "**Hill function kinetics**," which arrives in Principle 2 as the load-bearing justification for fractal leverage and is defined nowhere on the site. Neither `p_crit` nor `cartridge` appears in `/context`'s glossary at all, despite `p_crit` anchoring Principle 6 and "cartridge" being the first noun in the Membot card.

Last, a documentation-vs-implementation mismatch I can see from outside: `/autonomy`'s timeline lists **Visitor at 05:00 and Maintainer at 06:00**. The repo's own CLAUDE.md describes the cycle as 05:30 Visitor and 06:30 Maintainer. `/autonomy` also lists Outreach at 06:30. One of these is stale. The site's own honesty posture makes this worth fixing — the page that documents the audit loop should survive an audit.

**Finding 1 — the falsification test conflates two different experiments, and the one it actually specifies cannot falsify anything. (high)**

This is the load-bearing claim of the entire site, so I'll be precise. `/raising` states the null hypothesis plainly — "everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more" — and proposes a control. The control appears in two phrasings:

> "if phase-consistent behavior appears only when the accumulated session history is present — and degrades when that history is **replaced by an equivalent volume of generic context** — the developmental frame earns its vocabulary."

> "the same session curriculum with **scrambled or generic** context, run to see whether the phase patterns survive"

> "If the patterns survive **the scramble**, 'raising' is a redescription of prompt engineering, and the honest move is to retire the word."

"Scrambled" and "generic" are offered as interchangeable. They are not. They license opposite inferences.

- **Generic replacement** destroys *content*. If behavior degrades, the finding is: task-relevant context outperforms task-irrelevant context of equal token count. Every practitioner already knows this. It is fully predicted by the deflationary hypothesis. Degradation under generic replacement is **consistent with prompt engineering**, so it cannot be evidence against prompt engineering.
- **Scramble** preserves content and destroys *structure* — order, accumulation, attribution across sessions. If behavior degrades under scramble, that *is* informative: it says the developmental trajectory carries information beyond the bag of facts it contains.

The site names the informative experiment ("survive the scramble") and specifies the uninformative one ("replaced by an equivalent volume of generic context"). As written, the stated control returns "the developmental frame earns its vocabulary" in exactly the world where the null hypothesis is true. That is not a weak test; it is a test pointed the wrong way.

The fix is cheap and the lab is clearly capable of it: hold the corpus fixed, permute it. Same sessions, shuffled order — or better, a **yoked control**, where entity A receives entity B's session history at matched volume and matched specificity. Then "generic context" stops being a confound and the experiment can actually lose.

I want to be clear this is not a gotcha. The lab wrote down its own null hypothesis, named its own decisive experiment, and admitted it hasn't run it — "Until that control is run, treat the framework as a working vocabulary that fits our observations." That posture is why the error is worth fixing rather than mocking. But an unrun experiment that *couldn't have discriminated anyway* offers less protection than the page believes it offers, and the page's credibility is currently drawing interest on it.

Related, smaller: there is no pre-registered effect size, threshold, or metric for what "degrades" means. A lab that publishes "the p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error" as a principle understands why thresholds must be fixed before you look. And the operational definition of identity — "measured via raising curriculum state and interaction logs" — measures persistence partly through the curriculum that is itself the thing held constant. The instrument overlaps the manipulation. Both are fixable; neither is fatal; both should be named on the page.

### Unanswered Questions

2. What metric, and what threshold, decides that phase patterns "degrade"? Named before the run, or after?
3. Does Hardbound pre-authorize anything today, or nothing? `/autonomy` says "authorized"; `/autonomy` says process-level, detect-and-revert, and hardware anchoring still "building toward." `/arc-agi-3`'s PolicyGate "evaluates every action against signed law," which sounds like a real gate. 4. If BECOMING's six items are non-sequential observed patterns, what does the numbering 1–6 assert? And why are they labeled "Phase"?
5. The identity-portability result (Qwen 0.5B → TinyLlama 1.1B) is the site's most interesting claim to me and its least documented. What was held constant, what was measured, and what would have counted as *failure* to persist?

### Verdict

I arrived to evaluate whether the consciousness-adjacent claims are appropriately caveated. They are — more carefully than in most peer-reviewed work I referee. The disclaimer is unambiguous, the observer note about wanting to call it "experience" is annotated as data about the observer rather than the system, Principle 5 disarms its own headline number, the ARC page leads with its missing ablation, and `/autonomy` describes its own oversight as "a description of practice, not a verifiable mechanism" with an exposure window "unbounded in principle." The honest assessment does not merely feel honest; it is honest in the specific, costly, against-incentive ways that are hard to fake and easy to check.

"Raising, not training" is defensible. The distinction is mechanical — frozen weights, in-context dynamics — and therefore checkable rather than rhetorical. But defensible is not the same as demonstrated, and the site knows this, which is why it wrote down the control that would settle it. My central finding is that **the control as specified cannot settle it.** Degradation under replacement-by-generic-context is precisely what the deflationary hypothesis predicts; only a scramble that preserves content while destroying developmental structure can distinguish the two. The page names the scramble and specifies the replacement. Right now the lab's intellectual credit rests on an unrun experiment that, if run as written, would have returned "the developmental frame earns its vocabulary" in a world where nothing but prompt engineering is happening. That is worth fixing before it is run, not after.

Two things follow from being this careful. First, the "authorized" sentence on `/autonomy` is not a vocabulary slip — it is the only sentence on the site that overstates a safety boundary, on the page that exists to describe safety boundaries, contradicted three times by its own neighbors. Second, and more consequentially for a lab that will be read by people like me: all of this scrupulousness is quarantined on `/raising`, while "cognition machines," "society membership," "teach," "raise," and `(creating)` roam free across the pages a skimmer actually lands on. This lab has correctly identified that content which is technically correct but misleading carries an unbounded exposure window. That is an exact description of its own front door.
