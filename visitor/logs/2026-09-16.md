## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home, Projects, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Glossary /context, Links, ARC-AGI-3), plus 6 follow-up fetches to double-check claims
- **Understanding achieved**: partial → good (the big picture landed; the theory pages didn't)
- **Would return?**: maybe (yes if I get SAGE running from the quick start)
- **Overall impression**: A careful, unusually honest lab site. The "what is this" answer is on the landing page, but the "what can I run" answer is hidden at the bottom of /projects.

### Journey
**Home.** The hero line is clear: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." Then "dp-web4 is a heterogeneous human/AI research collective building Web4". Four headline projects follow (Web4, Hestia, Hub, SAGE), each with a one-line description and a GitHub link. Within about 30 seconds I knew this is about trust and identity for AI agents. The A1 line even explains itself inline: "A0 observed → A4 hardware-attested… a cooperative and tamper-evident gate, not adversary-proof containment". Most sites don't do that.

Things that slowed me down. The stats strip says "21 SAGE instances configured across the fleet (2026-09-08 census; 8 of them carry raising history, one per machine" and "Model families deployed on raising lines". I don't know what a "raising line" or a "SAGE instance" is yet, and Home doesn't say. The only route is the nav link. "Hardbound, built by Metalinxx" appears with no word on who Metalinxx is. The "Start here" block (Calibration / Vocabulary / Full ecosystem) is good, but none of its items says "install something". Home says "Core Rust/Python packages are published" with no command or package name.

**Projects.** This page is dense: 13 cards. The status badges (runnable / research-stage / private) help a lot, since I can filter for "runnable" right away (SAGE, Hestia, Hub, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot). The best thing for me was the **Quick start** at the bottom: `ollama pull gemma3:4b` → `python3 -m sage.instances.init …` → `cargo build --release` → run `sage-daemon`. That is what I came for, and it sits at the very bottom of the longest page. The Web4 card confused me for a moment: it's badged "research-stage" but says "web4-core 0.3.0 on crates.io and PyPI". The card does resolve it (spec is research, packages are published), but the badge alone sends the wrong message. The note that "'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight terminology correction" is a nice touch, though I didn't know there had been a correction until then. The ARC card is clearly marked "a historical research record, not current competition positioning". Good.

**Fleet.** "Six cognition machines, plus two society-hosts (HUB and pub…)." That adds up to Home's "8 Machines". The brain-region labels (Sprout = "thalamic router", Legion = "dopamine / reward prediction error", McNugget = "cerebellum / habit compiler") seemed odd at first. Then the page labels them "role mnemonics the lab assigned, not measured functional homologies", so no complaint. I couldn't find Home's "21 SAGE instances" anywhere on /fleet. A text fetch saw only "2,765" raising session records. I couldn't work out 21 instances from 8 machines. The pool names also tripped me up: the Claude Code account groups are called "synthesis pool" and "oversight pool". Since the site says "Hardbound is oversight", I briefly wondered whether CBP and Nomad run Hardbound. The `SAGE_MODEL` section is the most developer-friendly bit: "Any machine can run any model via the `SAGE_MODEL` environment variable."

**Autonomy.** The timeline is easy to scan (supervisors 03:00 → visitor 05:30 → maintainer 06:30 → explorer 08:00 → dream consolidation). What stood out was the frankness: "Consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship," and zero reverts across 91 maintainer session logs, framed as weak evidence rather than a safety result. As a developer, I trust that more than a green badge.

**Raising.** The definition is right at the top: "longitudinal, entity-specific context shaping — conditions for development, then watching what happens, across hundreds of sessions of one identity." So basically long-running prompt/context engineering on one persistent agent, and the page admits as much: "Everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more." I appreciated that. The BECOMING patterns and "memoriescape" / "bilateral generation" lost me. I skimmed them.

**Principles.** Principles 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9 read like engineering culture I recognise. Principle 1 ("You don't engineer the mound. You engineer placement rules.") is a good line. Principle 5 (Synthon, p_crit) and the "attractor basins in the probability landscape" language in 7 are where I dropped out. Principle 5 does say it "has not earned the status of the principles around it", which is fair.

**Glossary (/context).** The nav says "Glossary" and the H1 says "Glossary", but the URL is `/context`. It's thorough: about 55 entries plus 27 sections, including a worked example of one action going through the Web4 equation. Every acronym I had tripped on is here. It also finally explains the governance/oversight split: "What these systems actually do is oversight — watching, gating, and reverting actions," with Hestia a deliberate carve-out that "keeps 'governance', because it adjudicates at the moment of the act." One gap: "raising line" is used here and on Home but never defined.

**Links.** Clean. Explainer sites, the GitHub repos, a contact email and getting-started advice ("start with SAGE" for hands-on, "start with Web4" for protocol, and for ACT, "don't start there"). It sends me to /projects for the quick start, which is where the quick start actually is.

**ARC-AGI-3.** Explains the benchmark to a newcomer ("an agent must infer each game's mechanics through play; no rules are given"). The results are specific (94.85% action score, 23/25 environments, 175/183 levels, Claude Opus 4.6, 2026-04-17), and the caveats are upfront: efficiency-weighted, the harness read engine source so it wasn't competition-legal, no ablation. Credible.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home, Projects, Glossary /context, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links), 25 fetches in total. After one structured sweep per page, 16 narrow re-fetches checked each candidate finding against exact quotes.
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Canonical Web4 terms are consistent on every page. The remaining inconsistency is in the lab's own counting nouns (machine, SAGE instance, raising line, instance line, session record). Pages use them as near-synonyms, and no single page defines how they relate.

### Journey
**Method note first.** The first structured sweep of each page returned several statements that narrow re-fetches did not support. The /projects summary said "R7 mentioned but not expanded", but a targeted fetch found no R7 on /projects. The /raising summary attached a /fleet sentence ("Played full 25-game set end to end") to a BECOMING card. The /context summary said a "Raising line" glossary entry exists, but the targeted fetch found none. The same sweep also left out the /context heading "Terminology note — the governance→oversight correction, in one place". Everything in the tables below comes from the narrow, quote-returning fetches, not the sweep.

**Home.** The nav has eight items: Projects, Glossary (→ `/context`), ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links. Home expands SAGE ("Situation-Aware Governance Engine"), RDF and TCSEC in prose. LCT/T3/V3/MRH/R6/R7/ATP/ADP appear only as link text into the glossary, so there's no undefined acronym in running text. The Web4 framing is explicit: "not an architecture, infrastructure or stack". "Governance" appears four times, all about Hestia or lock-in, and the carve-out is stated right there: "'Governance' is the right word here and is deliberate: Hestia adjudicates at the moment of the act. The lab's governance→oversight correction binds Hardbound…". Good placement. The stats strip introduces three counting nouns at once: "8 Machines", "21 SAGE instances configured across the fleet (… 8 of them carry raising history, one per machine …)" and "Model families deployed on raising lines". "Metalinxx" ("Hardbound, built by Metalinxx") appears only here and is never explained.

**Projects.** LCT, ATP and ADP get full expansions on first use in the Web4 card: "Linked Context Token (LCT) — a verifiable presence that accumulates witnessed history … Allocation Transfer Packet (ATP) and Allocation Discharge Packet (ADP)". R6 is expanded canonically in the ARC card: "R6 (Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result)". "Infrastructure" appears only as a quoted, disclaimed registry label: "that is upstream package metadata, not a second meaning — on this site Web4 is an ontology". "Production" appears only as a negative caveat ("no chapter has run it in production use"), which is the opposite of the banned usage. ACT is "Agentic Context Tool" and ACP is "Agentic Context Protocol", the same on /links. **Correction to Pass 1**: /projects already has a jump link near the top: "Commands for Web4 and SAGE are in the [Quick start](#quick-start) at the bottom of this page."

The SAGE card has one pointer I couldn't resolve: "'cognition' is likewise a role label, not a capability claim, see /fleet". Two narrow fetches of /fleet found "role mnemonics" for the Brain labels, but no sentence calling "cognition" a role label, and the phrase "capability claim" wasn't there either.

**Glossary (/context).** The at-a-glance rows are clean: "T3 | Trust Tensor | Three-component trust tensor — root dimensions Talent / Training / Temperament", and V3 is parallel. ATP, ADP, MRH and LCT rows lead with their canonical expansions. The equation is exact, with operators glossed: "The `/` = 'verified by' on both sides of the equation: T3 verified by V3, ATP verified by the ADP discharge record that closes it." R7 is "R6 + Reputation", identified as canon's superset. Nothing is drifted. There's no row for "raising line", "SAGE instance" or "session record". The Cross-session memory section says "Each fleet machine runs its own SAGE instance", which reads as one instance per machine, but Home says 21.

**Fleet.** This is where the counting nouns are actually worked out, so it should be the anchor. It explains the three totals exactly: "**2,442** is the six cognition machines, **2,563** is that plus HUB's 121, and **2,765** is all eight machines including pub's 202." It defines pools before using them. The pool definition makes clear that "Oversight" there is "machine-enforced, not human supervision", which answers Pass 1's worry about the oversight pool. It gives "Raising line" a bold lead-in, "has four states, and one of them is not about the machine". But what the page states are *states* of a raising line, not what a raising line *is*. The listed states are "running", "paused / quiet" and "instrumentation broken". Whether that's three or four depends on whether paused and quiet count separately. My fetch also didn't say which state "is not about the machine". That could be the fetch cutting the paragraph short, so it's untested rather than wrong. The page uses "SAGE instance line", "raising line", "identity line" and "raise a SAGE instance" within a few paragraphs of each other.

**Autonomy.** Every acronym is expanded at first occurrence, inline where it's used: "the machine's LCT (Linked Context Token)", "MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)", "T3 (Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament)", "R7 (R6 + Reputation), not bare R6". This is the best-executed page for first-use definitions. SAGE is flagged as "a legacy name". Hardbound is "the hardware-bound oversight suite".

**Raising.** BECOMING gets a helpful disambiguation: "a proper name, not an acronym — the six pattern initials (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting) spell nothing." A careful reader wondering whether it's an acronym gets an answer right away. Machine names are declared proper names too ("the machine names are proper names, not acronyms"). The Sprout history is consistent with /fleet: 115 sessions on Qwen 0.5B, "ported to TinyLlama 1.1B on CBP … continuing past 180 sessions on later models". "Line" is again used with no link to /fleet's definition.

**Principles.** Principle 10 carries the whole terminology policy in one paragraph: ontology, oversight, fractal leverage "not unification and not scope inflation". It does not mention the Hestia carve-out, which Home, /projects and /context all state. Its cross-link is written `/context/#governance-oversight` (with a slash before the `#`), while /fleet writes `/context#governance-oversight`. WebFetch can't see anchor ids, so whether either anchor lands on the "Terminology note" heading is untested.

**Links.** Acronyms are expanded again in context (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, MCP, RDF, R6 with "Six-Element Action Framework", SNARC, WASM, ARC-AGI-3). The pointers resolve: "The commands live in one place: the Quick start on /projects", and "per-machine counts on /fleet, the source of record". /fleet does carry those counts.

**ARC-AGI-3.** ARC-AGI-3 and SAGE are expanded. The single "governance" is attributed and consistent with the carve-out: "explicit governance (the SAGE README's phrase; that README names Hestia as the layer that governs local agent authority)". "Architecture" appears only as a link label about SAGE's code ("Current public SAGE architecture"), not about Web4. Neither is drift.
