## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /arc-agi-3, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I understood the *problem* in about 90 seconds and the *ecosystem* in about ten minutes, but I still can't tell whether I could run any of this on the laptop I'm sitting at.

### Journey

Landed on `/` from a repo README. The headline — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a good hook: concrete nouns, no mysticism. What actually held me was the paragraph underneath it: *"AI agents already act on their own — browsing, transacting, coordinating with other agents — and there is no general way to establish whether one will behave appropriately in a given context."* That's a problem I recognize from my own work. Framing it as three answers (platforms decide trust / cryptographic ownership / "a third answer — trust computed per relationship from witnessed interaction, by the party doing the relying") is exactly the right shape for a dev landing cold. This is the best-written thing on the site.

Then the equation appeared: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. My honest reaction was a small recoil — it *looks* like math, and it isn't. I hunted for the operator key and found it, which rescued things. But the key itself contains the problem: `/` means "verified by" in `T3/V3` and a "declared→discharged allocation pair" in `ATP/ADP`. Same operator, two meanings, same expression. The site is upfront about this, which I respect, but as a developer an overloaded operator inside something presented as a canonical form reads as a design smell, not a notation quirk.

Went to `/projects` next, since that's where a dev goes. The **status labels are the single best UX decision on the site**: `runnable` vs `research-stage`, `public` vs `private`, per card. That told me in one scan where to spend my attention (SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot are runnable) without reading a word of theory. More sites should do this.

Then I hit the wall. The getting-started block is:

```
git clone https://github.com/dp-web4/SAGE
cd SAGE
# See README for Python/conda setup
```

That comment is where the site stops helping. SAGE is described as an "on-device cognition kernel," and every machine on `/fleet` is a Jetson, a 4090, an M4, or at minimum an 8GB discrete GPU. So: **can I run SAGE on a normal laptop, or am I about to clone a repo that needs hardware I don't own?** Nothing on the site answers this. One line — "runs on X or better, CPU-only mode available/not" — would convert "interesting" into "I'll try it tonight."

`/context` was the surprise of the pass. I'd assumed from the earlier pages that terms were being thrown around undefined, and I went in expecting to find gaps. I didn't. The glossary is ~45 entries deep and every term I'd noted as bare — Hardbound, PolicyGate, cartridge, crystallization, metabolize, fractal leverage, raising, attractor — is there, defined, several with genuinely careful hedging (`attractor` is explicitly labeled "a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object"). My hypothesis was wrong, and the site is in better shape than the per-page experience suggests. That gap between "the glossary is excellent" and "the pages read as jargon-dense" is the actual finding, and it's a placement problem, not a content problem.

`/fleet` was fun — eight machines, named, with hardware and model per box. *"Heterogeneous by design — because monocultures are fragile and diversity is where emergence happens"* is a rationale I buy. `/autonomy` told me a track is "a cron entry with a declared scope and a set of repos it writes to," which is exactly the concrete answer I wanted, and then went on to admit that consequential pushes are not human-gated and that the visitor and maintainer tracks are drawn from the same model families as the tracks that write the content ("correlated blind spots"). I did not expect a project site to volunteer that.

`/arc-agi-3` is the credibility anchor: an external ARC Prize scorecard link, 94.85% action score, 96.0% solve rate (24/25), and then — unprompted — *"the ablation that would separate the harness's contribution from the base model's (same model, no harness) has not been run."* A site that links its own numbers to a third party and then names the experiment that would undercut them is a site I'll come back to. It's also the page that assumes the most: it never says what ARC-AGI-3 *is* or why 25 unknown games is a hard test.

`/principles` and `/raising` I skimmed as a dev — more philosophy than my beat — but Principle 10 ("Terminology matters") stopped me, because it says *"Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* I had just read SAGE expanded as **Situation-Aware Governance Engine** on three separate pages. See friction log.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Can I run SAGE on hardware I own?** No page states a minimum. This is the question that decides whether I clone or close the tab.
2. Where's the code for the 12-step loop? The loop is the most interesting concrete artifact described anywhere on the site and it's never linked to a file.
3. If Web4 is "the shared language everything else speaks," is there a schema/spec I can validate against — an RDF vocabulary file, a JSON schema — or is it prose at this stage? `/projects` mentions "schemas" and "specification" but doesn't link either.
4. What does ATP/ADP actually cost or meter? "Declares intended resource spend" — spend of what? Tokens? GPU-seconds? A synthetic unit? I couldn't tell whether this is an accounting abstraction or something with a real denominator.
5. Home says 21 public repos; /links shows 14. Which is the browsable set?
6. Is the ARC-AGI-3 result reproducible by me from ARC-SAGE, or does it depend on the fleet?

### Verdict

This is the rare research site where I finished understanding *why the work exists* rather than just *what it's called* — the problem statement on the home page does more work than most project READMEs, and the ARC-AGI-3 page naming its own unrun ablation bought more credibility than the 94.85% did. The terminology is in better shape than it feels: I went looking for undefined jargon and found a 45-entry glossary that had already covered every term I'd flagged, which means the fix is linking, not writing. The one place it loses me is the handoff from "interesting" to "running" — four projects are labeled `runnable`, and for none of them can I tell what hardware I need or where the interesting code lives, which leaves a `git clone` and a `# See README` as the whole on-ramp.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (/, /projects, /context, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every canonical expansion is correct on every page that prints one — zero drift, nine pages, no exceptions — so the remaining defects are second-order: a load-bearing word that's glossed once and never again, one circular pair in the glossary, and a nav label that hides the site's best asset.

### Journey

I audit for three things: are acronyms expanded on first use, are the expansions identical everywhere, and does the structure let a reader find a definition when they need one. I checked all three across nine pages, and re-fetched four pages with narrower questions when a first pass gave me a summary instead of the actual wording.

**Expansions.** I went term by term against the canonical table. The result is the cleanest audit I've produced against this site:

- LCT → "Linked Context Token" on /, /projects, /fleet, /raising, /arc-agi-3, /links, and the /context glossary row. Seven pages, one string.
- T3 → "Talent / Training / Temperament" and V3 → "Valuation / Veracity / Validity" on /, /projects, /fleet, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3, /links, /context.
- MRH → "Markov Relevancy Horizon", ATP → "Allocation Transfer Packet", ADP → "Allocation Discharge Packet" — consistent wherever they appear.
- R6 → "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result" on /projects, /arc-agi-3, /context. No R7 anywhere.
- Web4 → "ontology" throughout; /context and /principles both carry the explicit negation ("not architecture or infrastructure").
- Hardbound → "oversight" throughout, and /projects and /fleet both disambiguate it in place ("machine-enforced policy gating here, not human supervision").

Not one deviation. I looked specifically for the drift variants in my table — "Lifelong Capability Token", "Trust-Tracing-Tensor", "Minimum Response Horizon", "Attention Token Pool" — and found none. "Production ready" appears nowhere. "Unification" appears twice, both times negated in favor of fractal leverage.

**The SAGE/governance question resolves.** Pass 1 flagged, at medium, that SAGE expands with "Governance" while Principle 10 rejects the word, and suggested a parenthetical at the expansion site. That parenthetical already exists, and it exists on more pages than Pass 1 checked. I pulled the /projects SAGE card verbatim: *"'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight terminology correction; the name is retained upstream across code, papers, and the ARC Prize benchmark."* The same note appears on /context (with a `#governance-oversight` anchor), /fleet, /arc-agi-3, /raising, and /links. **Pass 1's finding should not be actioned — it's a fetch artifact, not a gap.** The pattern in use is hub-and-pointer: the full explanation lives at `/context#governance-oversight` and every page that prints the name links to it. That's the correct pattern and it's implemented.

**Same correction for Pass 1's repo-count item.** /links states *"The dp-web4 org holds 40 original repos — 21 public, 19 internal — plus 32 forks"* and links "dp-web4 (GitHub org): All public repositories" above its 14 curated entries. The subset is labeled. Nothing to fix.

**Linking, checked directly.** I expected to confirm Pass 1's "definitions don't travel" conclusion and instead found the links are already there. /projects: *"Every Web4 term the cards use (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, R6, MCP, RDF, SNARC) is defined in the /context glossary."* /fleet: *"Vocabulary used in the cards... is defined in /context#glossary."* Both are completeness claims, and I verified /projects' — all eight named terms have glossary rows. The cross-references are real, anchored, and accurate.

So where does it actually break?

**"Ontology" is the one word that doesn't travel.** It's the site's most load-bearing term, it carries a specialist meaning, and it's glossed exactly once. Home: *"a trust-native ontology — a shared vocabulary and trust model for AI agents, in the knowledge-representation sense."* That trailing clause is the whole disambiguation — without it, "ontology" reads as philosophy-of-being, which is a plausible misreading on a site that also discusses emergence and becoming. Every other instance is bare: /projects' Web4 card opens "Trust-native ontology."; /principles 10 says "it's an ontology"; /context's row says "A trust-native ontology for AI agents, devices, and people." A reader entering at /projects (first nav item) or arriving at /principles from a search meets the word with no sense-marker. This is the same defect shape as the SAGE note — and the SAGE note was solved. The fix is the same: a `#ontology` anchor, or four words of gloss inline.

**Synthon and coherence define each other.** The glossary is admirably honest that coherence has no single definition — *"Used in three related senses on this site — the SNARC-scored session property, Synchronism's theoretical quantity, and the synthon operational marker — with no single operational definition yet."* But Synthon's row reads *"Emergent coherence entity sustained by recursive interaction, not external coordination."* So: Synthon is an entity of coherence, and one of coherence's three senses is the synthon marker. A reader looking up either term is routed to the other. The honesty note sits on `coherence` and doesn't warn that `Synthon` inherits the gap — and Synthon is a Principle (#5) and a home-page term, so it gets more traffic than the caveat that qualifies it.

**T3's "Training" disambiguation isn't in the row that gets scanned.** The table prints "Talent / Training / Temperament" bare. The clarification — *"Training: capability accumulated through interaction history — not gradient training, the sense the same word carries in ML"* — lives in a narrative section below the table. "Glossary at a glance" is explicitly built for scanning, and a scanner reading only the table takes away the ML reading of the one T3 component most likely to be misread. The text exists and is good; it's ten words from where it's needed.

**Nav label.** The glossary is the strongest documentation artifact on this site — roughly sixty entries, careful hedging, per-component glosses, correction notes with anchors. It is behind a nav item called "Context." Nobody hunting for a definition clicks "Context." Pass 1 arrived there fourth and called it "the surprise of the pass"; I'd argue that surprise is the finding. Two pages' worth of cross-links point *to* `/context#glossary` by name, which means the site already knows what's there — the top-level label just doesn't say it.

**One card is unreadable.** The /projects Web4 card is a single ~70-word sentence carrying five parenthetical expansions and three em-dash asides: *"T3 (Talent / Training / Temperament) verified by V3 (Valuation / Veracity / Validity), contextualized by Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH), anchored to Linked Context Token (LCT) — with resources cycled through Allocation Transfer Packet (ATP) and Allocation Discharge Packet (ADP) — charged, spent, and recharged against V3-validated value, not a one-way log — augmented with MCP (Model Context Protocol) transport and RDF (Resource Description Framework) representation."* Every expansion in it is correct. It is also the first card on the first nav page, and it's doing the equation's job in prose form while the equation itself is already on home and /context. Terminological correctness and readability are diverging here, and correctness won.

**Equation typography drifts.** Home prints `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3 * MRH + ATP/ADP` (spaces around `*`). /fleet prints `MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` (no spaces). Trivial-looking, but the spacing is doing semantic work: whitespace is the only cue that `*` binds looser than `/`, which is the intended reading — `(T3/V3) * MRH`, not `T3 / (V3*MRH)`. The spaced form is the better one and should be the house style. CLAUDE.md itself uses the unspaced form, so this needs deciding once, upstream, and applying everywhere.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Which sense of "coherence" does Synthon use?** The glossary names three senses and Synthon's definition uses the word unqualified. If it's the "synthon operational marker" sense, the definition is circular; if it's one of the other two, saying which would break the loop.
2. **Is the unspaced `T3/V3*MRH` or the spaced `T3/V3 * MRH` the canonical rendering?** CLAUDE.md uses unspaced; home uses spaced. The site should match whatever `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` prints, and I can't see that file from here.
3. **Does upstream canon still treat R6 as the sole framework?** The site is internally consistent (R6 only, correct six elements, no R7). Worth a Pass 3 confirmation that canon hasn't added a co-canonical R7 — if it has, the site is stale rather than wrong, and CLAUDE.md's table would need updating too.
4. **Is the `/` overloading intentional in canon, or a site-side clarification?** The site's notation key is more precise than the one-line equation in CLAUDE.md. That's the site out-specifying its own source, which usually means the source needs updating.
5. **Why does /autonomy carry no glossary pointer** when /projects and /fleet both do? Deliberate (its vocabulary is genuinely small) or an oversight?

### Unanswered Questions

1. **What, specifically, is the deflationary control?** Control arm, dependent measure, scoring procedure, blinding. Until this is written down, "not yet run" is not a commitment anyone can hold the lab to — including the lab.
2. **How many times has the detect-and-revert loop actually reverted something?** Zero and "we've never needed it" are very different answers from zero and "we've never checked," and the page permits both readings.
3. **Why is none of the ~53 daily sessions allocated to either named decisive experiment?** Deliberate sequencing, or has maintenance simply absorbed the whole budget?
4. **What is the operational measure behind "behavioral identity remained continuous"** in the Sprout→TinyLlama transfer, and is n still 1?
5. **What would count as evidence against Web4?** Synchronism has refuted predictions; Web4 has no stated failure condition.
6. **At step 7 of the SAGE loop, does "execute" touch the environment?** If yes, PolicyGate does not gate it. If no, the word needs a gloss.
7. **Is there a plan to publish log samples and coding criteria**, or is the internal-logs-only posture the intended steady state? `/links` says "not yet externally auditable" — "yet" is doing a lot of work.

### Verdict

I was sent to find over-claiming around consciousness and development and I could not construct the criticism: the operational definitions are in the first paragraph, the null hypothesis is stated above the fold by the authors, the qualia disclaimer is explicit, and every borrowed scientific metaphor I chased — brain regions, dreams, attractors, emergence, Hill kinetics, metabolism — turned out to be hedged at the point of use, usually more than once. On the specific question of whether "raising not training" is defensible: the negative claim is mechanically checkable and the page volunteers the LoRA fact that complicates it, while the positive claim is one the site declines to make. That is a more disciplined epistemic posture than most preprints in this area. What's missing is on the other side of the ledger entirely — the site has saturated on hedging and published almost nothing inspectable, so an outside reader is asked to take the honesty itself on trust. The two things that would change my assessment are both cheap and neither is prose: specify the deflationary control the way `/arc-agi-3` specifies its ablation, and publish the revert log that a detect-and-revert oversight posture implicitly claims to keep.
