## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links, arc-agi-3 — 9 routes actually)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The lab clearly does something genuinely interesting with autonomous AI agents, but the homepage hands you a wall of acronyms before it earns the right to, and I bounced off the theory pages.

### Journey
I clicked through from a GitHub repo README expecting a normal "here's our project" landing page. The first thing that hit me was the tagline: *"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."* That's a good hook — concrete, a little mysterious, makes me want to know more. The claim right under it — *"trust-native ontology, autonomous AI cognition, and the theoretical frameworks that connect them"* — is where my eyes started to glaze. "Trust-native ontology" means nothing to me on arrival.

The thing that actually held my attention on the home page was the ARC-AGI-3 number: **94.85% official action score**, and the line about a fleet of machines that "self-organize, specialize, develop trust relationships, and catch each other's mistakes." *That* is the value prop I'd repeat to a coworker. The credit to my brain went: "okay, they ran a bunch of small local LLMs as a coordinated swarm and beat a hard reasoning benchmark, cheaply." If that's the pitch, lead with it harder.

To the home page's credit, it does define its acronyms inline (LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, SAGE, MCP, SNARC) — so I wasn't completely lost. But there are *eight to ten* of them stacked together, and the equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` shows up looking like real math when it's actually shorthand. As a dev, my instinct on seeing an `=` and `+` and `/` is to parse it like code, and it doesn't parse like code, which is briefly disorienting.

**Projects** was the page I found most useful. Eleven cards, each with a one-liner. SAGE ("on-device cognition kernel"), ACT ("the human interface to Web4"), SNARC ("salience-gated memory plugin for Claude Code"), Membot ("brain cartridge server for AI agents") — these I get. They sound like things I could `git clone`. Synchronism ("coherence equations," "80 orders of magnitude") lost me completely; that read like physics-crank territory until I gave it benefit of the doubt. Hardbound and Oversight Plugins were vague because they're private/security, which is fair.

**Context** is positioned as the "start here for definitions" page, and it *is* a real glossary — but it's also the densest page on the site. It explained the equation operators (`/` = "verified by", `*` = "contextualized by", `+` = "augmented with"), which actually helped — I just wish that explanation lived next to the equation everywhere the equation appears, including the home page. The acronym list here ballooned to ~20+ terms (added TPM, FIDO2, RPE, dlPFC, WM, ACP). That's a lot.

**Fleet** was fun — named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP) with hardware specs and which model each runs. As a dev this is the most relatable page after Projects: it's just "here's our hardware and what each box does." The neuroscience metaphors ("hippocampal," "dopamine RPE") were flavor I skimmed.

**Autonomy** explained the daily cron-like schedule of agent "tracks" (Visitor at 05:00, Maintainer at 06:00, etc.). I followed the workflow easily — it's a CI/CD-shaped loop where bots audit the site and other bots fix it. The honest caveat that the Maintainer "ships to production without human approval, creating higher risk" earned trust.

**Raising** and **Principles** are the philosophy. I followed the gist of raising ("we shape context, we do not update weights" — fine, that's a clear technical claim) but the BECOMING phases and terms like "memoriescape" and "bilateral generation" are past where a dev skimming for "what is this" will go. Principles had 10 entries; about 4 landed immediately (R&D-not-production, document-failures, "productively wrong > never started"), the other 6 leaned on undefined words like "synthon," "coupling density," "attractor basins."

**Links** did its job: explainer sites, GitHub repos, a contact email. It even acknowledges the jargon and points newcomers to `/context` first.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links — plus arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Terminology discipline on this site is genuinely strong — every canonical acronym expands identically across every page with zero drift; the remaining issues are first-use definitions, one overloaded operator glyph, and uneven expansion of secondary acronyms.

### Journey
I audited for one thing above all: does the same term mean the same thing on every page? I pulled every acronym expansion verbatim from all eight pages and built a consistency matrix. The short version: this site passes the hard test. The canonical Web4 vocabulary — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, SNARC — is expanded **word-for-word identically** wherever it appears. That is rare. Most multi-page sites I audit drift by page three.

I started on the home page, which now carries a **"Vocabulary primer"** section — a real improvement for first-time readers, and it expands MCP ("Model Context Protocol") and RDF ("Resource Description Framework") inline so the equation isn't naked. From there I walked Projects → Context → Fleet → Autonomy → Raising → Principles → Links, then doubled back to ARC-AGI-3.

**Context** is the canonical glossary and the one page that carries the operator legend for the equation (`/` = "verified by", `*` = "contextualized by", `+` = "augmented with"). Every other page that prints the equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` (home, projects) prints it **without** that legend. That's the single biggest structural gap: the notation appears in three places, the key to reading it in one.

The thing that gave me real pause as a writer is the **overloaded slash**. The legend says `/` means "verified by" — so `T3/V3` correctly reads "T3 verified by V3." But the same glyph is then used as a plain pairing separator in `ATP/ADP` (a transfer/discharge cycle, not a verification), in `T3/V3` written as a unit, and informally in the fleet pools. The Context page is honest about this — it explicitly notes `/` means "verified by (T3/V3) **or** allocation pair (ATP/ADP)." But a notation that needs an "or" in its own legend is doing two jobs with one symbol, and a reader who internalizes "slash = verified by" will mis-parse `ATP/ADP` as "ATP verified by ADP," which is wrong.

**Projects** and **Links** are where I checked the two ACT-family terms, a classic drift trap. They held: **ACT = "Agentic Context Tool"** and **ACP = "Agentic Context Protocol"** are kept distinct and consistent (Tool vs Protocol) — no conflation. Good.

**SAGE** expands as "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine" on home, projects, context, fleet, raising, principles, and arc-agi-3 — seven pages, one expansion. Likewise **Hardbound** is "hardware-bound oversight suite" / "oversight" everywhere, and Principles makes the canonical point explicit: *"Hardbound is not 'governance'—it's oversight."* **Web4** is "ontology" throughout, and Principles states *"Web4 is not 'architecture'."* The R&D-not-production stance is consistent and "production ready" appears nowhere except as an explicitly rejected phrase. The canonical-discipline scorecard is clean.

Where it frays is the **secondary / security / neuroscience acronyms**, which are expanded on some pages and not others:
- **FIDO2** is expanded on /projects ("Fast IDentity Online") but appears **unexpanded** on /context.
- **TPM** is "Trusted Platform Module" on /projects but appears as bare "TPM 2.0" on /context.
- **CBP** (fleet machine + role) is never expanded on /fleet.
- **PolicyGate**, **Nomic embeddings**, **R6 records** on /arc-agi-3 are used functionally without on-page definition (R6 points to /context).

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, links + arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: As someone who lives in the Web4 equation, I can certify this site is canonically faithful — zero drift on any term I own — and the few things I'd flag are upstream-notation honesty and one suspect fleet spec, not site errors.

### Journey
I went straight to the three pages I care about — Projects, Fleet, Context — then swept the rest to confirm nothing contradicts them.

**Context** is the page I came to interrogate, because a glossary is where drift hides. It holds up. The equation prints exactly as canonical: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`. Every component expands the way I'd expand it: LCT = Linked Context Token (not "Lifelong Capability" — the single most common drift, and it's correct here), T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament, V3 = Valuation/Veracity/Validity, MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, ATP/ADP = Allocation Transfer/Discharge Packet. The Web4 definition is the one I'd write: *"A trust-native ontology for AI agents, devices, and people — not architecture or infrastructure."* That explicit "not architecture or infrastructure" is exactly the guardrail we fight to keep upstream. Fractal leverage is framed as *"pragmatic reuse of patterns that work at one scale, applied at every scale"* — correct, and explicitly *not* unification.

What I respect, and what a casual auditor would miss: the /context operator legend doesn't pretend the slash is clean. It says `/` = *"verified by (T3/V3) **or** allocation pair (ATP/ADP)."* That "or" is doing honest work. From where I sit, this is **not a site bug — it's faithful reproduction of a genuine overload in our own canonical notation.** In `T3/V3` the slash means "verified by" (T3 is verified by V3). In `ATP/ADP` the slash is a *cycle pair* — the energy ledger, transfer→discharge — and it is emphatically **not** a verification relation. The site reproduces our shorthand and flags the ambiguity rather than papering over it. That's the right call. If anything, this is feedback for *us* upstream, not for the maintainer: our canonical equation overloads one glyph, and every downstream site inherits it.

**Projects** is where I check whether the lab misrepresents what I build. It doesn't. SAGE = "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine — an on-device cognition kernel" (accurate). Hardbound = "Hardware-bound oversight suite" — *oversight*, never "governance" (correct, and the word I'd defend). ACT = "Agentic Context Tool" kept cleanly distinct from ACP = "Agentic Context Protocol" — that Tool/Protocol pair is a classic conflation trap and the site doesn't fall in it. The prose Web4 gloss on /projects actually parses the equation *more* faithfully than the bare equation does: *"T3 verified by V3, contextualized by MRH, anchored to LCT — with resources tracked via ATP and ADP."* Note it says ATP/ADP are *"tracked,"* **not** "verified by" — the prose disambiguates the slash that the equation overloads. Good instinct.

One genuinely subtle thing only a contributor would catch: that same gloss says *"anchored to LCT,"* which is the correct ontological role — LCT is the identity anchor. But in the equation, LCT is joined by `+`, and the legend defines `+` as "augmented with." So read literally, the equation says Web4 is "augmented with LCT," which *undersells* LCT — it's not an augmentation, it's the binding anchor everything else hangs off. The linear `+` chain flattens real ontological roles into a single additive list. The /projects prose gets the semantics right; the equation+legend is the loose one. Not drift, but a place where our canonical shorthand is genuinely lossy.

**Fleet** is accurate to the boxes I know — Thor (Jetson AGX Thor, Qwen 2.5 14B), Legion (RTX 4090 Mobile, Phi-4 14B), McNugget (Mac Mini M4, Gemma 3 12B), Nomad (RTX 4060, Gemma 3 4B), CBP (RTX 2060 SUPER, TinyLlama 1.1B). The identity-portability framing — *"Portable identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights"* and *"The model is weather. The identity is organism"* — is consistent with how we talk about LCT-anchored identity surviving model swaps. **But one spec is suspect:** Sprout is listed as running **"Qwen 3.5 2B."** There is no Qwen 3.5 release — the line is Qwen2.5 (1.5B/3B) and Qwen3 (1.7B/4B). Thor on the same page correctly says "Qwen 2.5." A "Qwen 3.5 2B" doesn't match any real checkpoint; this looks like a transcription error for either Qwen3 1.7B or Qwen2.5 1.5B. A contributor reading the fleet page would stumble on this.

The rest — Autonomy (ATP/ADP correctly framed as the per-track resource ledger), Principles (the canonical-defense page: "Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight." stated outright), Raising (the "shape context, do not update weights" claim is technically correct and well-caveated), Links (honestly redirects to /context for vocab) — all confirm the same vocabulary without contradiction.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary

### Journey
I came in the way a researcher usually does: skeptical, scanning for the gap between what's claimed and what's evidenced. I went to the three pages that decide credibility for me — **Raising**, **Principles**, **Autonomy** — then checked the benchmark page and the glossary, and finally swept Projects and Links.

**Raising** is the page that would normally make me close the tab. AI labs that use words like "identity," "growth," and "experience" about their systems are usually overclaiming. This one isn't — or at least, it sees the trap. The disavowal is unambiguous: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia"* and *"We use the language that fits the phenomenon."* What earned genuine respect was the observer note — *"I notice I want to call it experience"* — explicitly logged as the *author's* interpretive pull, not a property of the system. That's a move I almost never see: naming your own anthropomorphic bias in-line rather than hiding it. The framing the home page leads with — *"Developmental and lifecycle terms below (identity, behavioral continuity) are functional descriptions of observed system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims"* — is exactly the disclaimer I'd want, and it's up top, not buried.

But here's the structural problem I kept hitting: **the disavowal follows the language it disavows.** The page deploys "identity persisted," "accumulated experience," "something that looks like growth" abundantly, *then* says "but we don't mean it phenomenally." Rhetorically this is effective; epistemically it's backwards. By the time the caveat lands, the developmental frame is already installed in the reader's head. A more cautious structure would lead with the operational definition ("by 'identity' we mean a measured behavioral-consistency signature across sessions") and let the evocative words be shorthand *after* that anchor.

The **"raising is not training"** claim is the load-bearing epistemic commitment of the whole site, so I pressed on it. The mechanistic core is defensible and correctly stated: *"In raising sessions, we shape context — we do not update weights. Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes... the model's parameters are fixed."* That is true and falsifiable — frozen weights is a checkable claim. What impressed me more is that **the page refuses to let that distinction imply safety**: *"Frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors—including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns—can arise from in-context dynamics without any weight update."* Most labs would use "we don't update weights" as a safety shield. This one explicitly declines to. That's the single most credible sentence on the site.

The gap I'd still press in review: **the page never addresses whether "interactive selection" is distinguishable from in-context learning at scale.** If you shape context session over session, accumulate a memory substrate, and reinforce attractors, the *mechanism* differs from gradient descent but the *functional outcome* — a system whose behavior is progressively molded toward chosen targets — may be observationally similar to training. "We select, we don't create" is a philosophically loaded claim doing a lot of work, and it's asserted more than argued.

**Principles** is where intellectual honesty is most visible and also where the evidence-to-claim ratio is thinnest. The non-deployment warning is exactly right and exactly what I look for: *"Nothing on this site is intended for or suitable for deployment in safety-critical, medical, financial, or autonomous-control settings."* Principle 6 ("Document failures") openly logs a derivation that failed and a "400x error," and Principle 5's quantitative claim — *"1% coupling gave 35% coherence gain"* — is explicitly tagged as *"preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated)."* That tagging is the right behavior. But a single-trial, unreplicated number presented as a headline figure is still doing more persuasive work than its evidence supports, even with the caveat attached. And **"synthon" is the centerpiece concept of the site without an operational definition** — "an emergent coherence entity formed when components interact recursively under the right substrate conditions" tells me what *category* of thing it is, not how I'd *measure* one or falsify its existence. For a construct this load-bearing, that's the central weakness.

**Autonomy** is where I expected hand-waving about "safe autonomous agents" and instead found unusually frank risk accounting. The page does not hide that one track ships without review: *"the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate,"* and it names the failure mode plainly — *"a wrong push deploys before review."* It also concedes the system's epistemic limits: the visitor loop misses "deep technical errors," the maintainer can "apply correct fixes to wrong contexts," and there's a documented "tendency toward pattern-match without semantic depth." The mitigation is honestly described as *technical, not human* — *"Vercel preserves full deployment history — any push can be reverted... in under a minute."* I'd note for the record that "revertible in a minute" is a recovery story, not a prevention story, and the page doesn't oversell it as the latter. That restraint is itself a credibility marker.

**ARC-AGI-3** is the empirical centerpiece, and credibility lives or dies on attribution. The headline — *"94.85% official ARC Prize action score (efficiency-weighted)... 96.0% (24/25 games)"* — is real and sourced. The attribution is honest in direction: *"the result is Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness. The harness contributes the structured world-model building, R6 action framing, and multi-agent frame-questioning; the base model contributes inference."* What's missing is what every reviewer will ask for: **an ablation.** There is no Claude-Opus-without-harness baseline, so the harness's marginal contribution is asserted, not measured. The page does disclose the confounds that matter — the result requires network access, the local-model phase is "in progress," Kaggle "not attempted," n=25 games — so it isn't hiding the ball; it just hasn't done the decomposition that would let the harness claim stand on its own.

**Context** confirmed what the other personas found: the vocabulary is internally consistent and the equation is honestly glossed (the slash overload is flagged with an "or" rather than papered over). But as an *outsider's* on-ramp it's dense, and several load-bearing terms — "attractor basins," "coupling density," "p_crit" — are *used* across the site but not *defined* on the page that bills itself as the glossary.

One thing I want to credit explicitly: on /projects, the "Oversight Plugins" card honestly notes that *"Linked repo paths carry web4-governance slugs — these names predate the terminology correction to 'oversight' and are load-bearing for existing forks."* A lab covering its tracks would have quietly used the old word; this one flags its own legacy naming and explains why it persists. That's a small thing that tells me a lot about the site's relationship to its own terminology.

### Unanswered Questions
