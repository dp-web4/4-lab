## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Interesting research collective with real distributed-systems substance, but the front door is gated by undefined jargon — I have to crawl to /context to decode what I've already read.

### Journey

I landed on https://4-lab.vercel.app/ from a GitHub README. The headline — *"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."* — hooked me. Concrete numbers, no fluff. The tagline mentions "trust-native ontology" and "autonomous AI cognition," which is enough to keep me reading.

Then the jargon wall hit. The page references **MCP, RDF, LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, SAGE** — most without expansion on the page where they first appear. I know MCP (Model Context Protocol) and RDF from elsewhere, but the rest read like an in-group dialect. I scrolled looking for a "Start here" link. Didn't find one obvious enough.

I went to **/projects** next. Eleven cards. The Web4 card uses every undefined acronym at once: "MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3 + ATP/ADP." Other cards are more accessible — SAGE (12-step loop, on-device cognition), ARC-AGI-3 (benchmark with results), Hardbound (TPM 2.0, FIDO2 — I get hardware roots of trust). I noticed LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP *are* expanded somewhere on this page in passing — but I had to hunt for the expansions among the cards.

**/fleet** is where I got lost. Six machines, each described with neuroscience metaphors: "thalamic router," "habit compiler," "reward prediction error," "metacognition," "working memory." I'm a developer, not a neuroscientist. I want to know: *what process runs on each machine, and what does it produce?* The hardware specs are concrete (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090, Mac Mini M4) but the *role* descriptions are metaphor without substrate. By the third machine I was skimming.

**/autonomy** describes a daily cycle: tracks running at 03:00, 04:00, 05:00, etc. I bounced off two confusions:
1. "Track" isn't defined — I inferred it means a scheduled automated job, but the page says "31+ autonomous tracks" without listing them or showing a cron entry.
2. **Visitor** and **Maintainer** show up in the schedule as if they were tracks, but the page later implies they're *personas* (simulated user types). Are these code or roleplay? Unclear.

**/raising** was more accessible. The "raising vs training" framing — *create conditions for development, then watch* — is a clean software-adjacent metaphor (like emergent behavior in distributed systems). I appreciated the explicit caveat: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia."* That landed as honest. The page still leans on MRH and LCT without redefining them, which is annoying after five pages of the same.

**/principles** felt the most grounded. Ten principles, with a memorable hook: *"You don't engineer the mound. You engineer placement rules."* This made the rest of the site click for me — they're treating the lab itself as an emergent system, not a planned one. The principle about failed experiments being publishable findings (*"The p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error"*) is the first thing on the site that made me think "I'd actually want to contribute here."

**/context** is the glossary I needed on page 1. Every term I'd been hunting is defined here: Web4 equation explicitly written out, LCT = "Linked Context Token," MRH = "Markov Relevancy Horizon — boundary of what an entity can know or affect," SAGE = "Situation-Aware Guidance Engine." This page should be linked prominently from the landing page, not buried sixth in the nav.

**/links** was clean. Email contact, GitHub org link, explainer sites. Good.

**/arc-agi-3** was the most concrete page on the site. Actual numbers: "24/25 games solved (96.0%); 94.85% official action score; ~$250 API spend; ranked #3 on human leaderboard." The caveats are appropriate (public game set vs private competition set; not run in actual Kaggle constraints). If I'd seen this page first, I'd have understood what the lab can *do*, not just what it *believes*.

**No outright drift detected** — when terms *are* expanded, they match canonical. The issue is **scope of expansion**: most pages assume the reader has already visited /context. A first-time visitor reading any page besides /projects and /context encounters a wall of undefined acronyms.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is a "track" — code or persona? The /autonomy page treats both as tracks. I couldn't tell from browsing.
2. Can I run any of this locally? The /projects cards describe purpose but never say "git clone X, then Y."
3. Who runs this lab? "dp-web4" is mentioned — is dp a person, an org, an agent? The /links page has a single email (dp@metalinxx.io) so I infer a person, but the site never says.
4. What does the lab *produce* on a typical day? Logs? Code? Research findings? The /autonomy cycle implies daily output but doesn't show any.
5. How does R6 actually get used? It's mentioned but never demonstrated.
6. What is the relationship between SAGE and Synchronism and Web4? They're listed as separate projects but described as if interconnected. I can't tell from the site whether they're peers, layers, or alternatives.

### Verdict

There's a real research collective here doing interesting distributed-systems work — the principles page and the ARC-AGI-3 results convinced me of that. But the site is structured for insiders: the glossary is the sixth nav item, the landing page leads with an undefined equation, and the fleet descriptions are metaphor-heavy without functional grounding. If I'd hit /principles and /arc-agi-3 first, I'd have stayed. As-is, a developer skimming for two minutes will probably leave at the Web4 equation. Worth a second visit if I cared, but the site doesn't earn that second visit on its own — I'd need a friend to point me at /context first.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (for terminology consistency purposes; not full Web4 internals)
- **Would return?**: yes — this site is unusually disciplined for an R&D collective
- **Overall impression**: Canonical terminology is **strikingly consistent** across all pages — every acronym expansion I checked matches the table in my style guide. The remaining issues are about *coverage* (which pages expand which terms) and a few minor lexical near-misses, not drift in the defined terms.

### Journey

I worked through all nine pages in the nav order, then re-traversed to cross-check expansions. My method: build a matrix of every acronym × every page, mark each cell as Defined, Used-but-undefined, or Absent. For each definition I encountered, compare against the canonical table I was given.

I started at the landing page expecting the same friction Pass 1 reported. Instead I found a **"Vocabulary primer" section** near the bottom that defines LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, SAGE, SNARC, MCP, and RDF in plain language. Either Pass 1 didn't scroll past the equation or this primer was added since (the maintainer log mentions "structural clarity fixes" yesterday). Whichever, it's there now, and it changes my read of the front door significantly: the landing page is **not** an undefined-jargon wall. The primer is the right answer to the friction Pass 1 logged. A small note: the primer is below the fold on a standard laptop — a "↓ Vocabulary primer below" affordance near the equation would surface it.

**/projects** is the most terminology-dense page on the site. Every acronym appears with a parenthetical expansion next to it (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, R6, SNARC, TPM, FIDO2 — all defined). The Hardbound card explicitly calls itself a "Hardware-bound oversight suite" *and* includes a meta-note that the repo slugs still say "governance" because the names predate the correction. That's the kind of transparency a tech writer notices and appreciates — the site is auditing itself out loud.

**/fleet** uses fewer canonical Web4 terms than I expected. T3, MRH, SAGE, SNARC, and RPE appear; the others are absent. RPE is expanded inline ("reward prediction error"). I checked the page's neuroscience vocabulary against the rest of the site — terms like "thalamic router," "hippocampal episodic index," "dlPFC," "habit compiler" — these are *the page's own metaphor system*, not Web4 canonical terms. Pass 1 flagged these as confusing for a developer; for me, they're a parallel ontology that doesn't conflict with Web4 terminology. They could use a one-sentence frame ("we use neuroscience analogues for machine roles") but they don't violate the canonical table.

**/autonomy** has a notable terminological feature: ATP and ADP are *redefined in operational context* — "each scheduled track is an ATP" and "completion or failure is recorded as an ADP." This is the same canonical expansion (Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packet) but applied to the autonomous-cycle domain. Consistency-wise this is *good* — it shows the same Web4 concept being instantiated, not redefined. **However**, the word "track" itself is used dozens of times on this page and never formally introduced. For a writer auditing structure, this is the single biggest first-use omission on the site.

**/raising** is short and clean. The single Web4 sentence — *"In Web4 terms: raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) and value alignment of a Linked Context Token (LCT) — it does not change weights"* — defines both MRH and LCT inline on first use. The explicit consciousness caveat ("not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia") is exactly the kind of hedging a documentation auditor wants to see. **One lexical near-miss**: the page says *"the action envelope is **governed** separately by [Hardbound oversight] constraints."* The link text correctly says "oversight," but the verb "governed" is from the lexicon we're deprecating. Strict reading: this isn't a violation (Hardbound is still labeled oversight); loose reading: "governed by Hardbound oversight" mixes registers. I'd rewrite as "constrained by" or "bounded by."

**/principles** contains principle #10 — literally titled **"Terminology matters"** — which is meta-validation of this whole audit. It also contains the clearest Web4/Hardbound framing on the site: *"Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight."* That's the canonical formulation in one sentence. T3, V3, and RDF are expanded on this page. **SAGE is mentioned without expansion** ("Web4, SAGE, the fleet") — minor friction.

**/context** is the glossary, and it does what a glossary should: every canonical term has its expansion, including a one-line gloss explaining what each term *is*, not just what it *stands for*. The Web4 equation appears here with the framing "shared anchor" — appropriate.

**/links** uses MRH and ATP with expansion in the descriptions of linked repos; other acronyms appear without expansion. This is acceptable for a link list — readers either know the terms or follow the link.

**/arc-agi-3** is methodologically the cleanest page on the site for term usage. It explicitly *names* the cross-reference: "LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF — see /context for full definitions." This is the right pattern for a results-heavy page that doesn't want to redefine everything. ARC-AGI-3 is expanded ("Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence, version 3"). The Web4 framing reads: *"Web4 ... is the ontology that makes signed, reviewable action records possible"* — the canonical word "ontology" is present. There's a borderline sentence — *"Web4 supplies the audit trail"* — that, read in isolation, positions Web4 as something that provides infrastructure rather than something that is an ontology. In context (the next sentence is the explicit "ontology" framing), it's fine, but a writer might still prefer "Web4's vocabulary enables the audit trail" or similar.

**Notable terminology positives:**
1. The Web4 equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` is rendered identically on /, /context, and /arc-agi-3. Same operators, same spacing, same order. This is the kind of consistency that's easy to slip on and hard to recover from.
2. The phrase "Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight." appears on /principles in exactly that form — it's effectively the site's terminology motto.
3. /raising includes the explicit consciousness caveat verbatim — this is the kind of hedge that erodes if a writer "smooths" copy. It hasn't.
4. /projects self-discloses the gap between current site copy and historical repo slugs ("names predate the terminology correction to 'oversight'"). Transparency about your own terminology debt is rare.

**Notable terminology negatives (all minor):**
1. /raising uses the verb "governed" in proximity to "Hardbound oversight" — strict reading is fine, looser reading mixes lexicons.
2. /arc-agi-3 sentence "Web4 supplies the audit trail" in isolation could read as infrastructure-framing; the surrounding context corrects it.
3. /principles drops a single SAGE without expansion.
4. /autonomy never defines "track" as a unit of work despite using it dozens of times.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is there a documented canonical-terms file the site is being audited against? CLAUDE.md mentions `web4/docs/reference/CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` — but is that file public, and does the maintainer track verify against it programmatically, or is this manual?
2. The GitHub repos still carry "governance" in their slugs (e.g., `web4-governance`). Is there a plan to rename, or is the gap deemed acceptable because the site copy is the canonical surface?
3. /context calls itself a "glossary" implicitly but isn't titled "Glossary" in the nav. Is the "Context" framing deliberate (because the page is more than definitions) or historical?
4. Is there a style-guide policy for *when* to expand an acronym vs link to /context? Right now it varies by page — /projects expands inline, /arc-agi-3 cross-references. Both work, but the choice seems author-driven, not policy-driven.
5. "Track" is the most-used undefined operational term on the site. Is there a reason it's not in the canonical terms table?

### Verdict

This is the most terminologically disciplined R&D site I've audited in a long time. **Zero drift** in canonical expansions across 9 pages and 12+ acronyms. The Web4-as-ontology framing holds throughout. The Hardbound-as-oversight reframing is propagated everywhere on the site (with honest disclosure of the gap to repo slugs). The only real issue at the *terminology* level is the verb "governed" appearing once on /raising — and even that's a near-miss, not a violation. The structural friction Pass 1 raised is real (glossary buried, no inline first-use on every page), but the words themselves are correct. Are these heuristic, or are they empirically derived from raising session logs? If empirical, what is the methodology?
2. **What is a Synthon, operationally?** Principle #5 names it; /context links to /principles. But the falsifiability conditions aren't anywhere I can see. What would the world look like if synthons *didn't* exist as entities — would the project's claims be different? If not, "synthon" may be a vocabulary choice rather than a load-bearing theoretical construct, and that distinction matters.
3. **What is "coupling," in what units, measured how?** The 35% coherence gain figure is striking. Without methods I can't evaluate it. /context discloses single-trial-no-replication; that's exactly the right disclosure but it points to absence of evidence, not presence of methodology.
4. **How is "behavioral attractor" measured?** The dynamical-systems vocabulary is precise enough to be empirically grounded — has the project actually mapped a state-space and demonstrated attractor structure, or is "attractor" being used metaphorically?
5. **What is the relationship between "raising" and standard in-context learning?** If the mechanism is "shape context, don't update weights," then "raising" is in-context conditioning with a developmental gloss. That's a defensible claim (vocabulary fits the behavior), but I'd want the site to acknowledge the equivalence explicitly so a reader can map this work onto the ICL literature.
6. **For Synchronism: what distinguishes this from the "one equation explains everything" claim shape that the physics community is rightly skeptical of?** Even with "research conjecture" hedging, an external reader will categorize this with other 80-OOM claims unless the site explicitly differentiates.
7. **Is there independent review of the autonomous-track outputs?** The honest-assessment section discloses surface-vs-depth limits. But the publisher-supervisor gating is internal to the same model family. External review of a sample of outputs would strengthen the claims significantly.

### Verdict

The intellectual honesty here is real and unusually high. The consciousness-adjacent claims are explicitly hedged and the hedge is repeated, the failure-publication principle is followed in practice (not just stated), the ARC-AGI-3 page reads like a model of disciplined results disclosure, and the lab is willing to publicly disclose its own confabulation risks on /autonomy. The "raising not training" framing is defensible because the mechanism is named: weights are frozen, context is shaped, "raising" is the vocabulary that fits the behavior. That's an explanatory-adequacy claim, not a discovery claim, and it survives scrutiny in that form.

The remaining work is at the level of *durable* caveats and *empirical scaffolding*. The BECOMING phases need either falsifiability criteria or a clearer "heuristic, not empirical" label. The 35% coherence gain figure needs its single-trial-no-replication caveat to travel with the number, not stay on /context. The synthon construct needs an operational definition or it stays a vocabulary choice. The Synchronism 80-OOM claim needs one line acknowledging the claim-shape risk. None of these are deal-breakers — they're the difference between "credible R&D collective" (current state) and "credible R&D collective that has anticipated every objection a skeptic could raise" (achievable with three edits). I'd cite this site in a paper. I'd cite it more readily after the edits.
