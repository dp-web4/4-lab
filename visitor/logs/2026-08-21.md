## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (all site pages) + the SAGE repo README off-site
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I understood what this lab is about in under two minutes, which is rare — but after eight pages I still have never seen the thing running.

### Journey

Landed on `/` from a repo README. The headline — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — did its job; I kept reading instead of bouncing.

The second paragraph is the best thing on the site for someone like me: *"AI agents already act on their own — browsing, transacting, coordinating with other agents — and there is no general way to establish whether one will behave appropriately in a given context."* That's a problem statement I can hold in my head. I've hit a version of it myself wiring up agents. It told me why this exists before telling me what it is.

Then I hit the orientation pointer and stalled. Two "New here?" blocks sit in the same intro area, both routing me to the glossary. The second one is a single 40-word sentence: *"Jump to the vocabulary primer below ↓ for the acronyms used on this page, or start with `/context` for the full glossary — then clone **SAGE** — the recommended starting point — or **ACT** for the Web4 trust-layer side."* The one thing I actually wanted — *clone SAGE* — is the third clause, after two "or" branches into vocabulary. My instinct on any new project is `git clone` first, read the theory when the build fails. This site's instinct is the reverse. It's not wrong for every reader, but it's inverted for the reader arriving from a GitHub README, which is the traffic this page is presumably catching.

Went to `/projects`. This is the strongest page on the site for me, and the reason is one small thing: every card carries a **public / private** and **runnable / research-stage** label. Four things are marked runnable — SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot. That's an actionable signal. I knew instantly where to spend my afternoon. More sites should do this.

So I clicked through to the SAGE repo. There is a real quickstart (`git clone`, then `python3 -m sage.instances.init --machine mybox --model gemma3:4b`). Good. But it also needs a Rust toolchain, Ollama, and Python 3.10+, and there's a Rust daemon to build. That's a fine ask — it just isn't a ten-minute ask, and nothing on `/projects` warned me. "Runnable" set an expectation the prereqs then adjusted.

`/context` is thorough. Every acronym I'd collected was there with a consistent expansion. Two things stood out. First, the notation key is honest in a way I didn't expect: it says outright that `/` means two different things on the same line — "verified by" in `T3/V3`, but an allocation pair in `ATP/ADP`. I appreciate a site that flags its own operator collision. Second, the glossary defines both `R6` (Rules/Role/Request/Reference/Resource/Result) and `R7` (= R6 + Reputation), and reading the rows alone I couldn't tell which one to build against.

> **Post-pass correction (verified against the live page after the persona run):** the page *does* answer this, in a narrative section below the rows titled "R6 and R7: the action framework" — *"Both modes are canonical and neither is deprecated; the choice is per action or per role, by whether the outcome should shape future trust."* That's a complete answer and my in-character reading missed it. Downgrading the finding accordingly; the residual is scanning behaviour, not a content gap.

On the equation itself — `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` — the site explains every operator in prose ("+" = "augmented with", "*" = "contextualized by"). Once I read that, I understood it's a mnemonic, not math. But it's typeset as an equation, and my first thirty seconds were spent looking for compositional semantics that isn't there. The gloss arrives after the reflex.

`/fleet` I actually fact-checked, because a lab that publishes counts is inviting it. The per-machine sessions (435 + 488 + 336 + 377 + 162 + 193) sum to 1,991 on the six cognition machines, and + 74 for HUB gives exactly the 2,065 the home page claims. It reconciles. That bought the site more credibility with me than any of the prose did — and it's worth saying that plainly, because the site otherwise leans on argument where a checkable number would do more work. (The eighth machine, `pub`, has no published count, so "eight machines" and "2,065 sessions" cover different sets. Not an error, just a footnote that isn't there.)

`/autonomy` was the surprise. I expected marketing and got a limits section: *"'Autonomous' here means unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed in the AI-safety sense of choosing its own goals,"* and *"The oversight posture is detect-and-revert (audit log + sub-minute rollback), not prevent (pre-approval). Consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship."* As an engineer, that last sentence tells me more about the real system than the whole timeline above it. It also says the review agents' *"misses are correlated with the authors', not independent of them"* — that's the kind of thing most projects don't volunteer.

`/raising`, `/principles`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/links` after that. `/arc-agi-3` has the only hard external evidence on the site: 94.85% official ARC Prize action score, 24/25 games, with a public scorecard link and MIT-0 code. It also leads with the caveat that the harness-vs-base-model ablation *hasn't been run* — which is the exact objection I'd have raised, raised before I could.

And that's where the real gap landed for me. Across eight pages I read a great deal *about* the system and never saw a single artifact *from* it: no sample SAGE session output, no log excerpt, no terminal capture, no screenshot. `/raising` says it directly — *"documented and dated, but not externally audited, and no log samples or coding criteria are published yet."* One 15-line log excerpt would move me more than another paragraph of framing. The site is well past the point where more careful prose buys anything.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **What does it look like when it runs?** After eight pages I cannot picture SAGE's output. Not one line of it is shown anywhere.
2. **Is Web4 something I implement against, or a vocabulary I adopt?** "Trust-native ontology, research-stage" doesn't tell me whether there's a schema, an RFC, or a wire format — or whether it's currently prose.
3. **Do SAGE and Web4 touch in code?** Does SAGE import a Web4 library, emit LCTs, speak ATP/ADP — or are they linked conceptually with separate codebases? The site treats them as one ecosystem; I can't tell if the code does.
4. ~~**R6 or R7?**~~ **ANSWERED** — checked the page again after the pass: both are canonical, neither deprecated, chosen per action by whether the outcome should shape future trust. My question, not the site's gap.
5. **What's my role?** Contributor, adopter, or reader? Nothing on the site tells me whether PRs are wanted, and there's no CONTRIBUTING pointer.
6. **Why eight machines?** The heterogeneity is presented as a fact. Is it a research design (diverse substrates → diverse behavior) or just the hardware that happened to be around? `/fleet` implies the former with its brain-region role names but never says it.

### Verdict

This is the most intellectually honest project site I've read in a long time — `/autonomy` volunteering that consequential pushes ship without human gating, and `/arc-agi-3` naming its own missing ablation before I could raise it, are moves most projects don't make. The problem statement in the second paragraph landed, the `runnable` / `research-stage` badges on `/projects` are genuinely useful, and the session counts reconcile exactly when checked. I'd come back.

What it's missing is not more explanation. It's one artifact. The site has argued its case thoroughly enough that further prose has stopped paying — and the single cheapest thing it could do, showing fifteen lines of real output from any of the four "runnable" projects, hasn't been done on any of eight pages. As a developer I'd trade half the framing for one log excerpt.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (all 8 site pages + `/arc-agi-3`), plus the canonical terms document the site links
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every acronym expansion on this site is correct against canon — and that clean result is worth almost nothing, because all four real defects I found live in the glosses and the notation, where nobody's audit instrument is pointed.

### Journey

I audit terminology for a living, so I did this the boring way: fetch every page, extract every acronym occurrence with the sentence around it, build a matrix, look for cells that disagree.

The matrix came back clean. **LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SNARC, SAGE, LoRA, ACT, ACP** — every expansion, on every page it appears, character-for-character identical. Nine pages. No page contradicts another. In my experience that is rare enough to be suspicious, so I spent the rest of the pass trying to break it.

Four things I expected to find, and didn't:

1. **"ACT isn't in the glossary."** The home page routes newcomers to "ACT for the Web4 trust-layer side," and `/context`'s section list shows an ACP entry but no ACT. Checked the glossary body directly: `ACT: Agentic Context Tool — Cosmos SDK implementation of ACP — the human interface to Web4.` Present, and it does the harder thing of *relating* the two near-identical acronyms rather than just defining both. Not a finding.
2. **"T3's 'Training' isn't disambiguated in the glossary."** `/raising` carefully says Training "names accumulated interaction history, not gradient training," and I assumed that fix hadn't propagated to the authority page. It has: `Training: capability accumulated through interaction history — not gradient training, the sense the same word carries in ML`. Not a finding.
3. **"V3 is used unexpanded on `/autonomy`."** It is expanded inline *and* linked to `/context#v3`. Not a finding.
4. **"SAGE's 'Governance Engine' contradicts Principle 10."** It's handled by a documented hub-and-pointer: `/projects` carries the note that the name predates the governance→oversight correction, `/context` has a `#governance-oversight` anchor, and `/fleet`, `/autonomy` and `/arc-agi-3` all link to it. Deliberate, not drift.

Recording those four explicitly because a pass that only lists hits misrepresents its own accuracy. Four of my starting hypotheses were wrong, and the way I found out was reading the source instead of trusting the page summary.

**Then I did the thing that actually produced findings.** `/context` links the canonical terms document — `github.com/dp-web4/web4/blob/main/docs/reference/CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` — and it's public. Up to that point I had been auditing the site against the reference table I was handed. That table is a paraphrase. So I fetched canon and audited the site against *it*.

That's where the site stops being clean, and where I think this pass earns its keep.

**Canon writes R6 with an arrow.** Verbatim: `Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result. The base action grammar.` Five slash-separated inputs, then an arrow into Result. The arrow is doing semantic work — Result is *produced by* the other five, not a sixth peer in a list.

The site flattens it to a slash on every surface that carries it:
- `/projects`: "R6 (Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result)"
- `/links`: "R6 = the Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result"
- `/arc-agi-3`: "every action shaped as an R6 record"
- `/context`: "Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result — the base action grammar."

Four surfaces, consistent with each other, all diverging from canon in the same direction. And this matters more here than it would on most sites, because **`/context` maintains a notation key specifically about the `/` character**. That key states `/` carries two senses: "verified by" in `T3/V3`, and a declared→discharged allocation pair in `ATP/ADP`. Flagging its own operator collision is one of the best things on this site.

But R6 makes `/` carry a **third** sense — plain list separator — and the sixth position makes it carry a **fourth**, standing in for canon's `→`. Neither is in the key. So the site's most self-aware piece of notation documentation is itself incomplete, in a way that's only visible if you read canon.

I'll name the uncomfortable part: **the reference table in this track's own CLAUDE.md has the same flattening** (`Rules/Role/Request/Reference/Resource/Result`). The audit instrument shares the defect with the audited surface. That is exactly why five personas a day can return "R6: CORRECT" indefinitely.

**The second canon finding is bigger.** The site's single most-repeated terminology claim is "Web4 is a trust-native ontology — not architecture or infrastructure." It appears on `/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/raising`, `/autonomy`, and `/links`. Principle 10 elevates it to an enforced rule: *"Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology."*

Canon does not say this. Canon defines LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, R7, MCP, RDF as terms — and provides **no meta-definition of Web4 itself**. It never calls Web4 an ontology, and it never rules out "architecture." The same is true of `coherence`, `synthon`, `raising`, `attractor`, `oversight`, `fractal leverage`, `ACP`, `ACT`, and `SAGE`: all ABSENT from canon, all load-bearing on this site.

The site is not *wrong* — you can't contradict a document that's silent. But as a documentation auditor I have to say plainly what this means: **the terms the site enforces most aggressively are precisely the terms its cited authority doesn't cover.** Every acronym canon *does* define, the site gets right. Every term the site polices, canon is silent on. An audit loop pointed at canon will therefore return clean forever, no matter what happens to the contested vocabulary. That's a hole in the verification design, not in the prose.

**Third: R7 lives on exactly one page, and that page makes a claim about the others.** `/context` reconciles R6 and R7 well — canon says "Both modes are first-class, neither is deprecated," `/context` says "Both modes are canonical and neither is deprecated." Faithful. But it goes further: *"wherever the prose says trust 'updates from interaction,' the action grammar underneath is R7, not bare R6."*

That's an instruction about how to read the rest of the site. And the rest of the site doesn't carry it. R7 appears nowhere on `/projects`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`, or `/autonomy` — all of which name R6, and two of which describe exactly the trust-updating loop `/context` says is really R7. A reader who visits `/context` first is told the other pages mean something they don't say.

**Fourth, smaller: two glosses where the site out-specifies canon.** Canon's LCT: *"accumulates witnessed history and reputation over time."* Site: *"accumulates witnessed history"* — drops reputation, adds non-transferability (*"permanently bound to its entity"*), which canon doesn't state. Canon's ATP: *"Energy-based resource allocation unit."* Site: *"declared before an action runs — the charged state."* Both site versions are sharper and more useful. Neither is sourced. I'd route these upward to canon rather than trim the site down — the site is ahead here, and editing it backwards would destroy the better text.

The loop is described as *"a closed loop that improves surface-level site quality without human intervention"* — and the page is right, that is exactly what it is.

**So the lab has a functioning daily scheduler, roughly 53 sessions a day of capacity, and zero of it allocated to the five experiments it has already specified.** The blocker the site names for the most important one is not compute and not method. It is *"no owner."*

I want to be careful about what this does and doesn't imply, because the site's own framing gives me the tools. Principle 3 is "R&D, not production," Principle 9 says evaluate by whether the investigation is productive rather than whether it's correct, and Principle 6 says document failures. All three are good principles and none of them require running a control. But a disclosed-and-unrun control is not a documented failure. It is a documented *intention*, and it has been converted into a credibility asset — the disclosure is what makes the page trustworthy — while remaining costless. That is a stable equilibrium, and it is stable in the direction of never running the experiment, because the disclosure already pays.

**The distinction the site collapses, and shouldn't.** All five sit in the same "not yet run" voice. They are not the same kind of unrun:

- **(2) and (3) are schedulable this week.** The ARC ablation is: same base model, same 25-game set, no SAGE harness. The lab demonstrably has the harness, the games, and the compute — it ran the harnessed condition and got an externally scored result. The coupling replication is a second run of an experiment that has already been run once. Neither is blocked on anything conceptual.
- **(1) and (4) are currently unschedulable**, and for one shared reason the site never states as a single fact. `/raising` says a pre-registered identity-continuity metric *"requires an operational definition of identity continuity, and the glossary concedes that 'coherence' — the term such a metric would be built on — has no single operational definition yet."* `/context` says the scramble control has *"no pre-registered metric yet."* `/principles` #5 says synthon and coherence *"lean on each other, and neither has an operational definition yet."*

**Those are three faces of one blocker.** Coherence is undefined → no identity-continuity metric → no pre-registrable outcome → the scramble control cannot be scheduled even by someone who volunteers to own it. The site discloses each link on a different page, in a different voice, and never connects them. A reader sees three independent shortfalls; there is one, and it is upstream of both of the site's central claims. Naming the chain would change what "no owner" means — you cannot assign an owner to an unschedulable task, so the honest status for (1) is *blocked on (4)*, not *unowned*.

That also makes the priority obvious in a way no page currently states: **defining coherence operationally — even badly, even as an explicitly provisional v0 proxy — unblocks two of the five.** And running the ARC ablation, which is blocked on nothing, would upgrade the site's single externally-validated result from "suggests" to a measurement. Those are the two moves. Everything else on my friction list is decoration next to them.

**On "raising is not training."** Asked to assess this as a claim: it is defensible, and better supported than I expected. The mechanism is stated as something checkable rather than something felt — *"shaping context to deepen attractor basins in the probability landscape — not modifying parameters in raising sessions"* — and, to the site's real credit, the obvious objection is pre-empted rather than hidden. Principle 7 carries the carve-out inline (*"Some fleet machines run LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for separate fine-tuning tasks; raising is always in-context"*), `/raising` repeats it, and `/context` gives LoRA its own glossary row: *"Parameter-efficient fine-tuning some machines run for separate tasks — distinct from raising."* Three surfaces, consistent. The glossary states Raising flatly: *"Shaping context, experience buffer, and interaction history — never weights."* The carve-out says LoRA runs *"for separate tasks."* Nothing on the site says what "separate" means — separate in purpose, separate in time, or separate in hardware. The claim that carries the most weight on this site is the SAGE-Sprout portability result: 115 raising sessions on a Jetson, state loaded into TinyLlama 1.1B, *"Self-description drifted; behavioral identity remained continuous."* If any machine in that identity line ever ran an adapter, the result acquires a second explanation, and the reader cannot currently rule it out. One clause — "no machine in a raising line runs adapters" or "adapters run on machines X and Y, which are not in any raising line" — converts a defensible claim into a verifiable one. ### Verdict
