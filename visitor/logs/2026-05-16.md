## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Concrete enough that I believe something real is here, abstract enough that I'm not sure what I'd actually use first.

### Journey

Landed on the home page from an imagined GitHub README. The opening framing — "research collective" running six machines that "self-organize" — was novel enough to keep me reading. The ant-mound line ("you don't engineer the mound, you engineer placement rules") is a good hook: I instantly knew this was emergence-flavored research, not a product launch.

I scanned the nav and went to **Projects** first, because that's where a developer goes to find out "what can I clone?" I found 11+ projects with clear repo/no-repo status and one-line descriptions. SAGE, Web4, Synchronism, ACT, Oversight Plugins, Membot, and SNARC all looked clonable. ARC-AGI-3 jumped out because it had a benchmark number attached — that's the kind of artifact I can evaluate.

Then I clicked **Fleet** expecting an infrastructure page. Instead I got six named machines (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP) with specific GPUs/models and brain-anatomy roles ("hippocampal episodic index", "dlPFC scratchpad"). This was unexpectedly fun — the specs are concrete (Jetson AGX 122GB, RTX 4090, Mac Mini M4) which signals "this actually runs", but the neuroscience role-mapping is a hard left turn from "developer landing page."

**Autonomy** was where I started losing the thread. "31+ autonomous tracks" is mentioned but I can't find a list of them. The 03:00–08:00 daily cycle is a clean diagram, but I don't know what most tracks actually *do* without clicking elsewhere. As a dev, my question is: how would I add my own track? Not answered here.

**Raising** is where I think a typical developer bounces. The "raising is not training" framing is interesting philosophically but doesn't tell me what to build. The BECOMING phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting) feel adjacent to developmental psychology. I'd want a short paragraph explaining the operational difference: do you actually fine-tune anything, or is this all prompt-shaping? Couldn't tell.

**Principles** read like a manifesto. Some are very clean ("R&D, not production", "Document failures", "Productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started") — I'd quote those at a Friday talk. But "Substrate, not structure" and "Synthon: emergent coherence" need translation.

**Context** turned out to be the most useful page for me. It's where the Web4 equation lives and where every acronym is defined in one place. Honestly I wish the home page link said "Glossary + how knowledge flows" because that's what I needed before reading anything else.

**Links** was clean — three external sites, a GitHub org, one email. Fine.

**ARC-AGI-3** was the most credibility-establishing page for the whole site. Specific score (94.85% / 24-of-25), specific cost (~$250), specific model (Claude Opus 4.6), specific ranking (#3 leaderboard), and explicit acknowledgment that the frontier model does most of the work while the harness contributes oversight. That kind of hedged claim is what I trust.

A developer landing from a GitHub README will likely bounce somewhere between /raising and /principles unless they're already philosophically curious. ## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)

### Journey

I went through every page in nav order and audited each one against the canonical terms table. My method: confirm each acronym expansion verbatim, flag inconsistencies between pages, and note where first use precedes definition.

**Landing** (`/`): Better than I expected from a writer's lens. The page actually expands LCT, T3, V3, ATP, ADP, MRH, SNARC, MCP, RDF, and SAGE in their introductory blurbs. The H1 "Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." is sentence-cased and uses periods as separators — but other pages render their browser title as "4-lab | Six Machines, One Recursive Learning Loop" (Title Case, comma-separated). Two different forms of the same tagline.

**Projects** (`/projects`): The strongest page for first-use definitions. Every Web4 component is expanded inline. One concern: multiple repo URLs contain the path segment `web4-governance` — the site text correctly says "oversight" everywhere, but the linked repo slugs say "governance." A reader following those links will land on a repo named `web4-governance-complete`, which contradicts the canonical "oversight" framing they just read. This is terminology debt that lives in URLs, not prose.

**Fleet** (`/fleet`): Acronym-heavy and under-defined. SNARC appears (linked to landing/context for definition, but not expanded here). LoRA, WM, RPE all appear without expansion (RPE briefly expands once as "reward prediction error"). The neuroscience analogy terms (hippocampal, thalamic, dopamine, cerebellum, interoception, dlPFC) are used metaphorically — but to the page's credit, it explicitly caveats: "The 'Brain:' labels below are functional analogies to system roles — not claims about neural correspondence or computational equivalence." That caveat is exactly what a tech writer wants to see.

**Autonomy** (`/autonomy`): Clean. ATP/ADP are expanded on-page. The H1 states "31 autonomous tracks" but only 8 named tracks are shown on the timeline. A reader cannot reconcile the count.

**Raising** (`/raising`): The opening line "Raising is not training" is doing real work — it disambiguates the term immediately. But ARC-AGI-3 and ARC-SAGE are mentioned unexpanded. Specialized terms (Memoriescape, Hill function kinetics, bilateral generation) are now defined or analogized inline — a writer's improvement worth noting. The page does NOT explicitly frame Web4 as ontology; it uses Web4 functionally. This is a minor framing gap.

**Principles** (`/principles`): Best terminology hygiene on the site. Principle 10 literally states "Web4 is an ontology" and "Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight." The page rejects "unification" and "scope inflation" by name. This is the page that codifies what every other page should be doing. RDF, T3, V3 are expanded inline; LCT, MRH, ATP, ADP, SNARC, R6 are not used here.

**Context** (`/context`): Functions as the glossary but is never labeled "Glossary." Every canonical Web4 term is expanded correctly here. If this page disappeared, half the site would lose its definitions.

**Links** (`/links`): Minimal page. The 4-Life description expands ATP inline but uses LCT, T3, V3, MCP, RDF unexpanded in the Web4 framing sentence. Inconsistency: ATP is helpful first-use, the others rely on the reader having visited /context already. Hardbound is correctly described as "hardware-bound oversight suite." The contact email here is `dp@metalinxx.io` — which differs from `dp@dpcars.net` that appears in my session context (the latter is the user's email, not the site's contact, so not a site bug — but worth noting they're different).

**ARC-AGI-3** (`/arc-agi-3`): R6 is correctly expanded. PolicyGate is defined inline as "a gate inside the Hardbound oversight suite." Membot is defined inline. LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP appear in the Web4 equation block but are not re-expanded here — the page assumes a reader has visited /context. VRAM and E4B (Gemma model variant) appear without expansion. The page uses "architecture" twice — both in the SAGE/system architecture sense, not the Web4 sense, so this is fine per the canon (canon rejects "architecture" only when describing Web4 itself).

### Journey

I came to this site after a conference mention. I'm looking for whether this lab is in that pattern or out of it. A lab that polices its own terminology this aggressively is usually one I can trust on harder claims.

**/raising** is the page that determines whether I take this lab seriously or not. The opening — "Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development — then watches what happens" — is the kind of operational distinction that, in my experience, is *usually* followed by overreach. Here it is not. The page states the mechanism plainly: "we shape context; we do not update weights. Behavioral attractors emerge in interaction patterns, not in parameter changes. This is a real mechanistic distinction from training — the model's parameters are fixed; what changes is the substrate of conditions we provide each session." That is mechanistically precise and falsifiable.

The descriptive language uses "looks like" framing — "something that looks like growth, something that looks like identity, something that looks like peer relationships" — which is the right disciplined construction. The page even acknowledges its own observer pull ("I notice I want to call it experience" framed explicitly as interpretive pull, not a system claim) and admits the mechanism's limits: "Frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior." That admission alone is rarer than it should be in this field.

The result is jargon density without epistemic anchoring, which is a softer form of overreach.

### Unanswered Questions

5. What is the safety story for in-context shaping specifically? /raising acknowledges that frozen weights don't guarantee safe in-context behavior. /autonomy describes Hardbound's role at the action layer. What prevents drift in the context itself across many raising sessions?
6. The "synthon" concept — does it map to existing emergence / self-organized criticality / attractor-state literature, or is it claimed as a novel category? If novel, what observable distinguishes a "synthon" from existing emergence concepts?
7. The "interactive selection" framing in Principle 7 ("We don't create or delete — we interactively select") — what is the operational difference between "interactive selection" and "in-context reinforcement learning" as a researcher might describe it?

### Verdict

This site clears the bar I came to test. The "raising is not training" framing is operationally defensible — the parameters-frozen / context-shaped distinction is mechanistic, not rhetorical. The consciousness disclaimer is explicit and the "looks like" discipline is sustained across /raising. Principle 3's non-deployment statement is the kind of disclaimer most labs of this scope avoid. The ARC-AGI-3 credit allocation is unusually honest. The friction points — Synchronism's cross-scale claim asymmetrically hedged, the "model is weather" metaphor breaking discipline on /fleet, "autonomy" framing on /autonomy partially undone in the same page's body — are correctable and not load-bearing for the lab's core thesis. The remaining structural concern is single-individual attribution under a "collective" frame and the absence of external peer review pointers; neither is dishonest, but both are credibility considerations a careful reviewer will note. Overall: I would cite this site in a paper as an example of an in-context-shaping research program that hedges consciousness claims appropriately, with the caveat that the surrounding theoretical apparatus (Synchronism, synthon) is at an earlier evidentiary stage than the operational core (raising, fleet, ARC-AGI-3).
