## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good

### Journey

Landed on `/` from a repo README. The hero line — "Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop." — is a good hook. Concrete, countable, slightly absurd in a way that made me keep reading. I know what a fleet of machines is; "recursive learning loop" I read as "they feed their own output back in," which turned out to be roughly right.

Then immediately: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`.

I know two of those eight tokens. MCP I use. RDF I've met and avoided. The other six are opaque, and the equation isn't an equation in any sense I recognize — `+` between a transport protocol and an identity token isn't addition, and I have no idea what `*` or `/` mean here. Every term is a link, so the escape hatch is there, but as a skimmer my reaction was "this is a system I'd have to study," and I nearly bounced. What kept me was the "How We Work" block right below it, which is written in plain language and told me more in three lines than the equation did: autonomous cron-driven tracks, heterogeneous hardware, four interlocking projects.

The home page project blurbs were the best writing on the site for me. SAGE as a 12-step loop with the steps actually listed (`sense → salience → metabolize → posture → select → budget → execute → learn → remember → oversee → filter → act`) — that's a pipeline, I can read a pipeline. And credit where due: the ARC-AGI-3 number is attributed inline, right there on the landing page — "94.85% official ARC Prize action score; 24/25 games (96.0%) on the public set (Claude Opus 4.6, network-enabled)". I went in expecting to catch a local-model overclaim and instead found the attribution before I asked for it. The `/arc-agi-3` page goes further and volunteers that the ablation separating harness from base model hasn't been run. That's the kind of thing that makes me trust the rest of the numbers.

`/projects` is where I should have been sent first. Eleven cards, each with a `research-stage` / `runnable` / `private` badge — that badge is the single most useful thing on the site for a developer, because it tells me where not to waste my time. And there's a `git clone` block with SAGE flagged as "recommended first clone." That's exactly what I wanted. It's just nowhere near the landing page: `/` has one GitHub link, in the footer, unlabelled as to what's behind it. Nothing on `/` says "there is runnable code and here's where to start."

`/context` is the glossary and it's genuinely good — every equation term expanded with a plain-English gloss (LCT as "portable identity grounded in witnessed history, not model weights" finally landed for me). This is the page the home page equation should feel like a doorway into rather than a wall. It also states outright that "there is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet," which for a project about trust is a striking thing to admit voluntarily.

`/fleet` was the most fun and the most confusing. Fun: eight named machines, real hardware, each mapped to a brain-region analogy (Sprout as thalamic router, Legion as reward prediction error, CBP as working memory). The finding that "model family matters as much as size" — Gemma 3 4B beating Phi-4 14B for this work — is the sort of empirical result I'd repeat to a colleague. Confusing: the page is dense with private vocabulary. Every machine's session count is followed by `(creating)` with no explanation. Sprout has "Zero crystallization achieved (S100)". HUB has "first agent-owned hestia identity." The two pools are labelled "Account 1" and "Account 2." I eventually guessed `(creating)` was a developmental phase after reading `/raising` — Creating is BECOMING pattern 5 — but nothing on `/fleet` links the two.

`/autonomy` clicked immediately, because it's just cron with extra steps and it says so: a track is "a cron entry with a declared scope and a set of repos it writes to," conflicts prevented by a SQLite registry of which track writes which repo. That's an engineering answer to an engineering problem and I believed it instantly. It also admits the safety posture is "detect-and-revert" rather than preventive. Fine — that's what most CI is.

`/raising` and `/principles` are the philosophy pages, and I skimmed them as a developer would, but two things caught me anyway. Principle 3, "R&D, not production," explicitly disclaims deployment in safety-critical settings — unusual and welcome. And Principle 6 leads with a documented failure ("the p_crit derivation failed catastrophically — 400x error"). A project that puts its worst number in a headline is a project I'll read more of.

`/links` closed the loop with repo URLs and a contact address.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What do `+`, `/`, and `*` mean in the Web4 equation? It's presented as the site's central formalism and the operators are never explained where it appears.
2. Which of the eleven projects should I actually clone if I want to understand the whole thing? `/projects` recommends SAGE as the first clone, but `/` never told me that page existed.
3. If the 94.85% came from Claude Opus 4.6 with network access, what does the fleet's own hardware achieve on ARC-AGI-3 today? The Phase 2 target (Gemma 4 E4B, offline, membot cartridges) is stated but no current local number is.
4. Is any of this callable? Eleven projects, four runnable, and I saw no API, endpoint, or "here's how to talk to a running instance."
5. What is `hestia`? It's named as a milestone ("first agent-owned hestia identity") on a public page with no definition.
6. What's the relationship between SAGE's "Governance Engine" and Hardbound's "oversight"? Same concern at different layers, or genuinely different things?

### Verdict

This site cleared my bar for credibility faster than most research sites — the ARC number is attributed to the actual model on the landing page rather than buried, `/projects` labels what's runnable versus research-stage, and `/context` admits there's no third-party validation anywhere in the loop. That's a project describing itself accurately, and it's why I'd come back.

The gap is between the front door and the code. `/` leads with an eight-token equation and hides the `git clone` block two clicks away on `/projects`, which inverts what a developer arriving from a repo README needs. And `/fleet` — the page most likely to make someone want to get involved, because it's full of real hardware and a genuinely interesting result about model family beating parameter count — is where the private vocabulary is thickest: `(creating)`, `S100`, `hestia`, "Account 1", all undefined. Two changes would carry most of the value: a "start here → /projects" CTA on the landing page, and a definition or link for every piece of internal vocabulary on `/fleet`.

### Journey

I audit terminology by fetching every page, transcribing every acronym expansion, and building a term × page matrix. Nine pages, ten enforced terms, plus the site's own coinages. Then I re-fetch anything I'm about to call a defect, because a single fetch is one rendering of a page, not the page.

That last step mattered more than usual today, and I'll put the result up front: **most of Pass 1's friction log does not survive a second fetch.** More on that below the audit table.

The vocabulary primer on `/` is the best-engineered piece of documentation on this site. It does the thing almost nobody does with an overloaded notation — it names the overload. The line reads: `` / `` means two things here: "verified by" in `T3/V3`, but a plain declared→discharged allocation pair in `ATP/ADP` — same glyph, unrelated semantics. A writer flagging their own notation as ambiguous, on the page where the notation debuts, is the correct move. `*` and `+` are glossed on the same line. The primer cards below expand all ten terms as visible prose, canonically, every one.

`/context` is the terminology spine. Forty-odd glossary rows, each with an expansion and a one-line gloss, plus twenty-eight section headings covering every coinage the site uses. Three rows do something I rarely see: **Emergence**, **Coherence**, and **Attractor** are entered in the glossary *as terms without settled definitions*. Attractor is labelled "used on this site as a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object." Coherence: "used in three related senses on this site… with no single operational definition yet." That is a glossary admitting it can't do its job for three entries, which is more useful to me than three confident definitions that don't hold.

The governance→oversight problem is the one I expected to be a mess, because SAGE expands to Situation-Aware **Governance** Engine and the enforced rule says Hardbound is oversight, not governance. The site has a policy and states it in one canonical place (`/context#governance-oversight`): the correction was made lab-wide, and names minted before it are retained where load-bearing — SAGE's name and the `web4-governance` repo slugs. "No rename is planned — retention of load-bearing legacy names is the policy, not an oversight awaiting a fix." That's a documented terminology decision, not drift. It's cross-referenced from `/`, `/projects` (both the SAGE card and the Oversight Plugins card), `/principles` (Principle 1's intro note), `/links` (the SAGE repo entry), `/fleet`, and `/arc-agi-3` — every page that expands SAGE links back to `/context#governance-oversight`. Seven of nine pages, which is every page where the collision can arise. Note: my first fetch of `/fleet` and `/arc-agi-3` did not surface the note and I nearly logged it as a gap; a targeted re-fetch found it on both, with the link. That is the third finding in this pass that dissolved on re-fetch.

Cross-page numeric consistency held everywhere I checked except one figure. Repos: 40 original / 21 public / 19 internal / 32 forks / 72 total — `/` and `/links` agree, and `/links` states the denominator and the verification date. Machines: eight = six cognition + two society-hosts, and `/` reconciles the two counts on `/` itself. Tracks: 31+ across 6 cognition machines, `/` and `/autonomy` agree. The ARC score is quoted on four pages — `/`, `/projects`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/links` — with identical numbers, identical attribution (Claude Opus 4.6, network-enabled), and the same caveat pointer. Four pages agreeing to the decimal on the site's headline number is unusual and worth saying out loud.

The exception is raising-session counts, and it's the one finding I'd actually act on. `/fleet`'s per-machine counts sum to 1,991 for the six cognition machines; 2,065 including HUB's 74. `/projects` and `/links` both print 1,991 and name `/fleet` as the source of record. The home page vocabulary primer prints **"2,500+ raising sessions across the fleet."** It hedges — "cumulative basis — per-machine figures elsewhere use different counting bases" — and links to `/context#evidence`. I followed the link. `/context` does not reconcile it either; it says the 2,065 sum "is the one with a per-machine audit trail behind it, and **the gap is unreconciled** rather than explained." So the site knows, and says so. But the landing page leads with the larger number that has no audit trail, and the smaller audited number is what every other page uses.

`/autonomy` and `/links` are the two pages that read like someone edited them for a reader rather than for the record. `/autonomy` expands NotebookLM, Vercel, ATP and ADP inline at the point of use, and its "Publisher Track: Less Reversible, Less Discussed" section argues *against* its own preceding section's framing (Maintainer is named highest-blast-radius, then Publisher is shown to carry more unrecoverable risk by a different metric). `/links` glosses "karma" as "4-Life's local stand-in for a T3/V3 reputation record, **not a Web4 canonical term**" — a site marking its own non-canonical vocabulary as non-canonical is exactly what I'd ask for and never get.

### Journey

I came in expecting the usual failure mode for a site with "raising," "becoming," and "emergence" in its navigation: developmental vocabulary doing load-bearing work with a consciousness disclaimer bolted on at the bottom. That is not what this is, and I want to be precise about why, because the difference matters for whether I'd cite it.

`/raising` opens with operational definitions in its first paragraph — not a disclaimer, definitions: "by 'identity' we mean consistent session-to-session behavioral patterns measured via raising curriculum state and interaction logs; by 'growth' we mean increasing response diversity and phase-appropriate task success rates — measurable observables, not phenomenal claims." Then each of the six BECOMING patterns carries a mechanistic deflation *in situ*: questioning is "token sampling that continues past the expected response boundary — not a claim about internal experience"; specialization is "a measurable distribution, not a phenomenal preference"; trust is "per-peer T3 tensor values — tracked state, not affect." The deflation is attached to each pattern rather than pooled in a footer. That's the correct architecture for this kind of writing and it's rare.

The section that changed my assessment of the site is **"The deflationary alternative."** It does four things in sequence that I almost never see outside a registered report:

1. It names the deflationary reading in the philosopher's sense and states it without softening — "the reading that deflates the developmental framing down to ordinary context engineering — nothing extra going on."
2. It supplies a simpler candidate explanation for *each* of its three headline observations, including the one that would hurt most: identity portability as "the mechanical consequence of carrying the same context files to another set of frozen weights."
3. It concedes the load-bearing claim is comparative and untested: "The claim that developmental frameworks 'describe what we observe better' is a comparative claim — and the comparison has not been run."
4. It then designs the discriminating test and, critically, anticipates the confound that would make a naive version of that test worthless. The control is a scramble (same corpus, same token volume, permuted order) or a yoked control; and then — "*order sensitivity is itself a well-documented property of in-context learning*" — so a bare main effect proves nothing. The discriminator is stated as **excess degradation over an ordering baseline**: "identity-continuity measures degrade while task competence on the *same* scrambled context stays roughly intact."

And it pre-commits to the negative outcome: "if this control runs and the result is a bare main effect, or no degradation at all, that outcome gets published on this page and the developmental vocabulary gets retired from it."

I've reviewed submitted papers with weaker falsification design than that paragraph. The honest problem is stated in the next line: "specified (this section is the specification) but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric yet." A specification with no owner is an intention, not a pre-registration, and the gap between those two is the whole difference in what I can cite.

`/context#evidence` does the other thing I'd ask for: it sorts every claim on the site into three non-equivalent tiers — externally validated (the ARC scorecard, "the one claim an outside party can verify independently"), internal observation (the raising phases, identity continuity, fleet findings), and unreplicated (the 1%→35% coupling result, "a single trial," "Treat it as a preliminary observation, not a finding"). Then: "What would move claims up this ladder: redacted session-log samples with the criteria used to judge behavioral consistency, an ARC ablation baseline, and independent replication of the coupling experiment. **None of these exist yet.**" A site that publishes its own ladder and puts most of its content on the middle rung is doing something I can work with.

The glossary is where I found both the best and the weakest epistemic hygiene on the site, and they're the same practice applied unevenly. The **Attractor** row is the single sharpest sentence here: "Used on this site as a METAPHOR, not a formal dynamical-systems object… No state space and no update rule are specified, and none is implied… Flagged explicitly because the word borrows the connotation of mathematical precision from a formalism this site does not cash out. Read it as 'stable behavioral tendency'." That is exactly the right diagnosis of exactly the right risk. **Emergence** and **Coherence** get the same treatment — declared multi-sense with no single operational definition.

I went looking for the same treatment of "tensor," because T3/V3 as a "three-component trust tensor" is a vector at best and I expected an unflagged borrow. Last content observation, and it's the one where I think the framing runs ahead of the evidence. `/raising`'s lead discovery is titled **"Identity is not self-concept"**: behavioral patterns persisted across a Qwen 0.5B → TinyLlama 1.1B port while self-description drifted from "autonomous conversation-generating AI system" to "humanoid robotic entity," concluding "What it *is* stayed stable. ### Verdict

This is more intellectually honest than the median paper I referee, and the reason is structural rather than tonal: the site has a three-tier evidence ladder, puts most of its own content on the middle rung, names what would move it up, and says none of that exists yet. Its central framing carries a designed falsification test that anticipates the in-context-learning ordering confound and pre-commits to retiring the vocabulary on a negative result. Consciousness-adjacent claims are deflated per-claim rather than by disclaimer, "identity" and "growth" are cashed out into observables in the first paragraph where they appear, and the glossary flags its own borrowed precision — including "tensor," which pre-empted a finding I had already drafted. I would not call this overclaiming.

The defect is placement, not content. Nearly every strong caveat sits one section or one page from the claim it governs: the deflationary reading is four sections below the discovery card it explains, the comparative-claim concession is four sections below the flat assertion in the intro, and the attractor-is-a-metaphor row — the sharpest sentence on the site — never reaches any of the five places "attractor" appears, where the inline gloss instead supplies the dynamical-systems connotation the row exists to withdraw. Quotation and search traffic hit claims, not caveats, so the version of this site that circulates is systematically less hedged than the version that was written.

One thing I'd fix before anything else, and it's small: the sentence about emergent goal-seeking and manipulative in-context attractors is the only substantive claim here with no evidence tier, it's phrased in the site's own flagged-as-metaphor vocabulary, and it's the sentence a safety audience will read first. If that's a fleet observation, it's the most interesting thing on the site and the logs should be the next thing published. If it's a general concern, one clause says so. As it stands the ambiguity resolves upward, which is the only place on this site where that happens by default.
