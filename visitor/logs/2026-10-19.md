### Journey
I came in from a GitHub README and landed on Home. The hero line ("AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust.") and "The idea" section, which says trust comes from "witnessed behavior, not claimed by the agent or granted by a platform", gave me the pitch fast. "Governed by our own tools … They are refused by it every day" is the most concrete line on the page, and it made me want to see a refusal.

On `/projects` I hit a first-pass jargon wall: "written as an ontology", "witnessed evidence", "scoped authority", "fails closed", "hardware-bound oversight". I can guess at "fails closed" from security work. "Ontology" is glossed on Home ("a shared vocabulary, or ontology") but not here, where it matters more. Hardbound shows up as BUILDING with no link; it's private and from Metalinxx, which is fine, but it's the only card with nowhere to click. The card I most wanted to try was Hestia ("one set of rules across the AI agents they use"), and it says "Runs on every machine in our lab; no public installer yet." I went to the repo anyway, and it has GitHub releases: `v0.0.4` (not a prerelease) with prebuilt tarballs for aarch64-apple-darwin and aarch64/x86_64 linux, plus SHA-256 files and three `v0.3.0-rc` builds from 10-12 to 10-15. The README also has an "Installing the desktop app" section (line 295). For a developer, that is a public installer. Either the site is out of date, or "installer" means something narrower here that the site doesn't spell out.

I clicked SWE-SAGE (`github.com/dp-web4/SWE-SAGE`) from `/projects` and again from `/links`, and both times got a **404** (anonymous fetch). It's presumably a private repo. From the outside it just looks like a dead link, and it shows up on two front pages.

`/fleet` is a nice quick table of eight machines (Jetson Thor, Jetson Orin Nano, an RTX 4090 laptop, a Mac mini M4, …). Without explanation, "Its being improves the SAGE code it runs on" (Legion) and "smallest being, for SWE-SAGE" (Nomad) don't mean much to me yet. The page names the model families (Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama) but not which machine runs which, and links to the notebook for that. That's acceptable.

`/raising` is the clearest page for my persona. "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" and "We do not change the model's weights" told me right away that this is context/memory engineering, not fine-tuning. The "Seen / Not yet shown" split is easy to read. One thing still isn't clear: who runs the "tutor sessions" and the "mentor" (a human? another model?). It isn't said on the page.

`/principles` reads as six one-liners and is easy to digest. `/links` is a tidy index. `/notebook` explains the two layers well ("written for people: short, plain, and current" versus the record underneath), and its 9-page list made the glossary easy to find. On `/notebook/context` I finally got SAGE expanded ("Situation-Aware Governance Engine"), along with LCT, T3, V3, and the rest. Nothing I read on the seven front pages expanded SAGE. The front mostly avoids the Web4 acronyms (no LCT/T3/ATP on the front), which is the right call for me.

External links checked by HTTP status: hestia, SAGE, web4/hub, SAGE site, Synchronism site, 4-gov.org, PyPI and crates.io (API) all return 200. Only SWE-SAGE fails (404).

A reader who learns T3 from this row learns a composite the spec forbids, with a formula it doesn't use. "When needed" reads as a general rule, not as SAGE's local choice. This isn't expansion drift, which is why the term greps pass. It is a mechanism description in canonical vocabulary that diverges from the normative spec. Maintainer-actionable.
