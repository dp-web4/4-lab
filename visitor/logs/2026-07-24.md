### Journey
Landed on the home page from a repo README. "Seven machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop." is a strong hook — I immediately got that this is a multi-machine autonomous-agent lab, not another LLM wrapper startup. The vocabulary primer on the home page saved me: LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, SNARC all got one-liners before I hit them elsewhere. The page even admits "coherence" is used three different ways and that session-count bases don't reconcile across pages; as a developer that candor reads as engineering honesty rather than sloppiness.

/projects is where I'd spend real time. Status labels (runnable / research-stage / private) are exactly what a developer wants — I know immediately that SAGE, 4-Life, SNARC/engram, and Membot will actually execute if I clone them, and that Web4 is spec-only with no runnable demo. The `+` / `*` / `/` symbol key for the equation is right there. SNARC shipping as a Claude Code plugin ("engram") is the most immediately actionable thing on the whole site for someone like me.

/fleet was fun — named machines with real hardware specs (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090, Mac Mini M4, even a TinyLlama on an RTX 2060). "Chapter ledger" and "chapter law" appeared without definition, though.

/autonomy made the whole thing concrete: a cron-style daily timeline (supervisors → archivist → publisher → visitor → maintainer → outreach → explorer). The admission that Maintainer is "the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate" and that bad pushes deploy first and get caught within ~24 hours is the kind of risk statement I never see on project sites.

/raising and /principles are where I approached my ceiling. The training-vs-raising distinction ("Training optimizes for a loss function. Raising creates conditions for development") is crisp, and they explicitly state the null hypothesis — "everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more" — which is more than most AI labs would print. Terms like "synthon" and "p_crit" went over my head, but the pages say so themselves and link out.

/arc-agi-3 has the numbers a developer wants: 94.85% official action score, 24/25 games (96.0%), ~$250 API spend, #3 on the human leaderboard. The 94.85% vs 96.0% distinction (efficiency-weighted score vs raw solve rate) took me a re-read. The unrun ablation (harness contribution vs base model) is flagged twice — honest.

/links closed the loop: 12 public repos clustered by function, setup instructions for SAGE and ACT, one contact email. Nothing looked broken.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home + all 8 nav pages; deep reads on /raising, /principles, /autonomy)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The most self-critical project site I've evaluated — it states its own null hypothesis, admits its oversight posture is detect-and-revert, and discloses that nothing is externally audited — but the transparency is doing work that published protocols should be doing, and the gap between the two is the story.

### Journey
I came in the way a researcher would: skeptical of "raising" language and expecting consciousness overclaiming. The landing page defused most of that within one screen. The disclaimer is explicit and well-placed: developmental language "is functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience," and "behavioral identity" is unpacked as "consistent session-to-session interaction patterns, accumulated experience, and raising curriculum — measurable observables, not philosophical continuity." That is the correct move, made preemptively, not defensively.

/raising is the page I came to stress-test, and it largely survives. It states the null hypothesis in terms most labs would never print: "everything on this page might be competent context engineering and nothing more. Each observed pattern has a simpler candidate explanation." And it delivers on that — each BECOMING pattern is paired with its deflationary alternative (bilateral generation ↔ continuation sampling; specialization ↔ task routing plus few-shot clustering; identity portability ↔ "mechanical consequence of carrying the same context files"). It also concedes the decisive weakness unprompted: "the comparison has not been run. No deflationary control exists yet." What it does not say is when, or whether, that control will be run. A stated-but-unscheduled control is the softest kind of rigor: the site earns credit for naming it, but the developmental frame is used across all nine pages while the experiment that would license it remains hypothetical. I also noted a genuinely good safety sentence I did not expect: "frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior. Emergent attractors — including goal-seeking or manipulative patterns — can arise from in-context dynamics." That is a correct and under-appreciated point, stated plainly.

/principles was more mixed. The p_crit derivation failure ("failed catastrophically — 400x error") presented as signal rather than buried is exactly the norm I wish the field had. The single quantified synthon result (1% coupling → 35% coherence gain) is caveated as "preliminary observation (single trial, not independently replicated)," and — unusually — the page says the number "quantifies the experiment's internal metric, not any of [the] three senses of 'coherence.'" But the page does not systematically distinguish refuted from untested claims. One derivation is refuted; nearly everything else is untested; the framing "most research leads nowhere — and that's expected" blurs the two. The house philosophy itself distinguishes refuted from untested — the page should apply it claim-by-claim.

/autonomy is the page an AI safety reviewer will read most closely, and it is the most honest autonomous-operations disclosure I have seen on a public site: "the oversight posture is detect-and-revert (audit log + sub-minute rollback), not prevent (pre-approval). Consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship." And further: for misleading-but-technically-correct content "the exposure window is unbounded in principle," and a public repo push "is not fully revertible... That gap is unaddressed, not solved." My one structural criticism: the page's top framing says tracks "operate within Hardbound-defined scope boundaries," while the fine print concedes hardware-anchored enforcement "is what the Hardbound research is building toward." Leading with the aspirational mechanism and correcting to the actual mechanism (scoped credentials + after-the-fact audit) later on the page conflates roadmap with posture. The correction exists; it should come first, not second.

/context is where the terminology layer either holds or doesn't for an outsider. It holds on honesty — it flags "coherence" as having three senses "with no single operational definition yet," admits three unreconciled session-counting bases, and states "there is no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet." It holds less well on rigor: LCT, MRH, and ATP/ADP are named and motivated but not operationally specified (what counts as "witnessed"? what test places something inside vs outside an MRH?). The T3/V3 worked example (EMA, α=0.1) is the strongest definition on the site and shows what the rest should look like.

/arc-agi-3 is the credibility anchor. External scorecard link, explicit attribution ("Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness"), and the missing ablation stated twice. This is the one externally verifiable claim on the site and it is handled almost perfectly — "demonstrates the ceiling" is the only phrase that outruns the ablation-free design. /links then closes the loop with the admission that matters most to me: "no papers or preprints are published yet" and internal claims "are not yet externally auditable," with an email for inquiries. Honest — and also the crux: every load-bearing empirical claim outside ARC-AGI-3 rests on internal session logs with no published samples or coding criteria.

### Verdict
This site is credible in an unusual way: not because its claims are established — almost none are — but because it prices its own claims honestly, up to and including "no external, blinded, or third-party check anywhere in this loop yet."
