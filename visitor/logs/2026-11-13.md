### Journey
**Home.** "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust." The idea paragraph (track record over certificate, rules that exist before anyone acts) landed immediately. Four cards with status labels. Hestia was the most concrete for me: "one set of rules for agents from any vendor, checked before each tool call, with a hash-chained record". I know what a hook and a hash chain are, so I knew what it was. Web4 is "a shared vocabulary, or ontology". That is defined inline, which I appreciated. SAGE is "Persistent AI beings… whose consequential actions go through governed tools." The front never says what SAGE stands for.

**Projects.** Clear grouping: standard, then tools, then the paid tier, then beings, then theory. A label legend sits at the bottom. Then I read the SAGE card: "their consequential actions are **meant to** go through governed tools." Home said they *do*. That stopped me (see friction).

**Fleet.** A table of 8 machines with plain-English roles, and "different on purpose" as the reason. It reads well. "Four machines do the heavy work… Two review… Two host" matches the table (and the notebook's "six cognition + two society-hosts").

**Raising.** "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" is a good line. The "Not yet shown" box ("no blind comparison against a fresh model yet") made the page more credible to me, not less. Then: "It acts in the world **only** through a small set of governed tools… **Every one** of those actions is checked against rules before it happens." That is the strongest of the three versions.

**How we work, Links, Notebook index.** All three are short and clear. The Notebook index explains the two layers well. On Links, "4-gov · governance" told me nothing. Its title (fetched) is "Computable Accountability", by Metalinxx. I only learned what Metalinxx is from the notebook ("the researcher's company"). Links has no Hub entry, although Hub is one of the four RUNNING cards on Home.

**Notebook glossary.** I came here to decode SAGE. The heading reads "SAGE: Situation-Aware Governance Engine". The front had told me SAGE is "persistent AI beings", so the name and the description point different ways. The glossary does explain it: a legacy name kept because it is load-bearing, and the upstream framing moved away from a "cognition kernel". The explanation sits ~16K words deep. Every Web4 acronym I hit was expanded canonically (details in the audit).

**Notebook projects, quick start.** As a developer I wanted to *try* something. The quick start exists, at `/notebook/projects#quick-start` ("the only copy on the site"). I found it only by reading the notebook. No front page links to it: the front SAGE card links to the SAGE site, and Hestia and Web4 link to their repos. The quick start is honest to a fault. Two notes in it would worry me as a first-time user:
- `init` ends by printing "Next steps" that start the *Python* daemon, while the page's steps use the *Rust* daemon ("the two disagree upstream").
- The daemon "has no --version: it ignores the flag and starts, and with SAGE_MACHINE unset it takes the identity 'sprout', a real fleet machine's name."

Probing with `--version` is the first thing I would have done.

**Checking the governed-tools claim.** The notebook SAGE card quotes upstream LATEST_STATUS.md: "Consequential acts are **intended** to be governed and witnessed; observational/local capabilities are separately bounded". It adds that the boundary is "not a statement that every act a being takes crosses Hestia law", with "principal separation still being hardened". Beings also have verbs that /raising's list leaves out:
- `run` executes a Python file the being wrote. It is sandboxed, and Hestia checks it once, at the start.
- `identity_edit`.
- `memory_edit`, which until 2026-10-25 could rewrite the being's identity.json.

So the /projects wording ("meant to") matches upstream. Home ("go through") and /raising ("only… every one") state it as done.

**Links.** All 20 checked URLs return 200: front externals, notebook pages, crates.io API, PyPI, and the `/glossary` and `/autonomy` redirects.
