As a developer scanning, I almost did bounce — out, not in.

Went to **Projects** next because that's where I check whether there's anything to clone. SAGE and Hardbound have actionable descriptions; Synchronism ("coherence equation across quantum-to-cosmic scales") and AI DNA Discovery felt like they belong on a different site. **Fleet** was the most reassuring page. Real hardware (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090 Mobile, Mac Mini M4), real model names (Qwen 2.5 14B, Phi-4, Gemma 3), real role assignments. This is when I started believing the lab actually exists and isn't a manifesto.

**Raising** is where I started feeling the "is this a research lab or a worldview" tension. Six phases (Grounding → Sensing → Relating → Questioning → Creating → Acting). The 94.85% ARC-AGI-3 score gets cited here too, which gives the framework something to stand on.

**Principles** finally felt like the page I should have read first. "R&D, not production," "Document failures" (and they actually do — the p_crit 400x failure is named), "Surface your instincts," "Terminology matters." Honest stance. Wish this was linked higher.

**Context** was the glossary I needed two pages ago. LCT = Linked Context Token, T3 = Talent/Training/Temperament, MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, etc. Useful, but should be one click from the landing page, not buried mid-nav.

**ARC-AGI-3** sold me on the technical credibility — 94.85% action score, #3 on the human leaderboard, ~$250 in API spend, candid about the one game they didn't solve. This is the page I'd send to a skeptical friend.

**Links** had clear paths into the repos. `dp@metalinxx.io` for contact. Three explainer sites (Synchronism, SAGE, 4-Life). Reasonable.

### Journey
I came in skeptical. Most AI sites that talk about "becoming," "identity," or "raising" are either marketing or accidentally implying things they can't defend. **`/principles`** surprised me. Principle 3 is the strongest version of an R&D disclaimer I've seen on a site like this: "Nothing here is optimized for deployment. **`/raising`** is the page I expected to bounce off and didn't. The operational distinction is the right one: *"Raising is not training. Training optimizes for a loss function. **`/autonomy`** is where my concerns concentrated. 31+ autonomous tracks across 6 machines, ~53 sessions per day, with the Maintainer track explicitly identified as "the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate." **`/arc-agi-3`** restored a lot of credibility the developmental vocabulary asks the reader to advance. 94.85% action score on the *public* set, ~$250 API spend disclosed, the unsolved game named, the Kaggle competition (32GB VRAM, no internet, private set) explicitly labeled "not attempted." The honest assessment, in the end, *does* feel honest — it just dresses better than it needs to.
