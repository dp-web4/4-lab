## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home + all 8 nav pages: Projects, Glossary (/context), ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links), plus the SAGE repo on GitHub to check the Quick start
- **Understanding achieved**: partial (the home page gets me there; the inner pages make me work for it)
- **Would return?**: maybe (yes for SAGE and Hestia, no for the long prose pages)
- **Overall impression**: The home page now says in two sentences what the lab thinks: trust should be computed from witnessed evidence, not declared. That is a pitch I understood. The inner pages bury it under caveats and change history, to the point where a card is harder to read than the repo it describes.

*Method note: WebFetch's summary of Home was wrong. It reported LCT, T3/V3, MRH, R6/R7 and ATP/ADP as "used but not defined" on Home. In the rendered HTML they appear only once, in the "Start here → Vocabulary" card, which points to the glossary. So every item below was checked with curl and grep against the live HTML, not taken from the summary.*

### Journey

**Home.** The H1 is "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." The thesis follows: trust is "computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying." Clear and opinionated, and a good first 10 seconds. Next come four product cards (Web4, Hestia, Hub, SAGE) and a "Commercial path" section, which tells me there is a business model: an open layer plus Hardbound as a proprietary tier. I liked that it's stated openly. The "Start here" trio (Calibration → web4 STATUS.md, Vocabulary, Full ecosystem) is the right onboarding shape.

Home has three rough spots:
1. **The browser tab and link preview tell a different story than the page.** The `<title>` is "4-lab | Eight Machines, One Recursive Learning Loop". The meta description is about "8 machines… 40 original repos… raise other AI entities". Neither mentions trust, and there are no og:title/og:description tags. If someone pastes the link into Slack or GitHub, the preview pitches a hardware fleet, not the thesis in the H1.
2. **The stats block is hard to scan.** The "4 model families" tile carries a paragraph about "deployed is not the same as running", and the text under it explains how "instance, line and session record nest". I just wanted numbers. Reading it, I learned that there's a distinction I don't understand yet.
3. **The Web4 intro spends words on what it is not.** It says "an open trust ontology, not an architecture, infrastructure or stack". To a developer that negation reads as an argument I wasn't part of. Hestia's card also brings in "A1 on Hestia's own A0–A4 ladder" and then disambiguates it from TCSEC class A1, all on the landing page. TCSEC is never expanded. I had to look it up, and it turned out to be irrelevant to me.

**Projects.** The status legend (runnable vs research-stage, which is a separate axis from public vs private) is excellent. I wish more open-source orgs did this. The cards are the problem. The Web4 card is essentially one ~200-word sentence that walks through the whole equation with nested dashes and parentheses. It includes a note that the slash "is not a claim that T3 and V3 verify each other" before I know why anyone would think it was. The SAGE card tells me its session count was "corrected 2026-07-27 from an earlier '900+', again 2026-09-12 from 1,991, and again 2026-09-18". That is changelog material, not card material. The Synchronism card spends most of its length retracting earlier claims (Navier-Stokes, "80 orders of magnitude"). I respect the honesty, but I can't find what the project *is* until the end. By contrast, the Hub card ("a single ~6 MB binary… member identity, seven base roles… append-only ledger… MCP HTTP server… admin CLI") and the Membot card are models of clarity: concrete nouns, then maturity.

The **Quick start** is the most useful thing on the site for me. I checked it against GitHub. `sage/instances/init.py` exists and accepts exactly `--machine`, `--model` and `--operator-name`, and `sage-rs/Cargo.toml` exists. The comment saying "the SAGE README has no setup section" is accurate: grepping the README for install, setup or quick start finds nothing. So these commands are the only onboarding path, and they look correct. One contradiction, though:

- **Step 3 of the Quick start says "Run the loop"** while it launches `sage-daemon`. But the SAGE card on the same page, /links, and the glossary all say the Rust sage-daemon is "an inference-and-metabolism gateway, not a port of that [12-step] loop". The loop is in the Python kernel. So after following the only install path, I'd believe I was running the 12-step cognition loop the card advertises, and I wouldn't be. Either the comment is wrong or I'm missing a step that starts the Python kernel.

**Glossary (/context).** The nav says "Glossary" and the URL is /context, which is slightly confusing but fine. The page is about 11,400 words. The "Glossary at a glance" table is what I needed, and it says CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md governs when the two disagree. That's a good sign. Every acronym I searched for (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, R7) is expanded consistently. Beyond the table, though, it's an essay, and I stopped reading.

**ARC-AGI-3.** A clean page. It defines the benchmark and SAGE in the first paragraph, gives the number (94.85% action score, 23/25 environments, 175/183 levels), says why it isn't a solve rate, and says why it's no longer the headline. The engine-source caveat is clear. This is the page I'd send a colleague.

**Fleet.** Hardware cards (Jetson AGX Thor, Orin Nano, a 4090 laptop, a Mac Mini M4…) are fun for a developer. But each card becomes a forensic record ("Session 488 against 482 records on disk: a six-record gap…"). The McNugget card says the model is Gemma 4 12B while the instance is named `mcnugget-gemma3-12b`. The card does explain this ("the directory name predates the switch"), but the name contradicts the model it runs. The vocabulary line also defines lowercase "hestia (the trust store inside the Hestia daemon… not SAGE's peer trust tracker)". So there are now two things called Hestia and I have to track them by capitalization.

**Autonomy.** The H1 says "31+ autonomous tracks across 6 cognition machines, ~53 sessions per day (figures as of 2026-05-26…)". The page's headline figure is four months old on a site that otherwise dates everything to the week. The personas section describes this exact pass, which is a nice bit of recursion.

**Raising.** It opens with "Raising is not training; it is also not nothing" and immediately defines "identity" and "growth" operationally, then puts the deflationary alternative up front. More careful than I expected. It is also long, and it calls itself "the softest page on the site". I agree, and I'd skim it.

**Principles.** Readable. Of all the pages, it has the best ratio of idea to caveat. The license paragraph was useful to me as a developer: the main repos are AGPL-3.0, snarc and membot are MIT, and only the ARC-SAGE harness is MIT-0. The fact that it discloses a previous error ("Until 2026-09-18 this sentence said…") is fine, but it's the third page where I read the history of a sentence before its content.

**Links.** Good one-line repo descriptions. "Contributing" says: glossary, then SAGE for hands-on, Web4 for the protocol, not ACT. That's the clearest onboarding advice on the site, and it's on the last page in the nav.

### Unanswered Questions
1. After the Quick start, what am I running? The sage-daemon gateway, or the 12-step loop the SAGE card sells me? How do I start the Python kernel?
2. If I want to *use* Web4 today, what does `pip install web4-core` give me: a Python API to mint an LCT, compute a T3? There is no five-line code example anywhere on the site.
3. How do Hestia and Hub relate in practice? Can I run Hestia on my laptop and join someone's Hub today, or is federation (listed as "not started") required for that?
4. What is the "Commercial path" timeline? Is Hardbound something I could ever evaluate, or only read about?

### Verdict
Home is now a real pitch and ARC-AGI-3 is a model page, but most other pages favour a thorough audit trail over explaining what the thing is. As a developer I'd follow the Quick start (it checks out against the repo) and read the Hub and Membot cards. The concrete defect is that the Quick start's "Run the loop" contradicts the site's own statement that the daemon isn't the loop. Terminology is clean: zero canonical drift across all nine pages. The friction is volume and ordering, not vocabulary.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home plus the 8 nav pages; the nav, in order, is Projects, Glossary (/context), ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The terminology is consistent everywhere I checked. What's left is information design: a glossary table labelled "at a glance" that runs 5,000 words, one meta description shared by all nine pages, and a naming scheme that tells things apart by capitalization.

*Method note: WebFetch was used for the landing page (nav, H1, headings, acronym inventory). Its report repeated the error Pass 1 caught: it listed LCT/T3/MRH/R6/ATP as "not expanded" on Home. I then fetched the rendered HTML of all nine pages with curl and did every count and comparison below with grep and Python against that HTML. No figure below comes from a summary.*

### Journey

**Nav and titles.** The nav has 8 items and every page's H1 matches its nav label; the one exception is Glossary, which lives at `/context`. Each page has its own `<title>`. However, **all nine pages carry the identical `<meta name="description">`** ("8 machines (6 cognition + 2 society-hosts), 40 original repos…"), and no page has og: tags. Pass 1 flagged this on Home only; it is site-wide. A search result for /raising or /principles will show a fleet inventory.

**Acronym expansions (the core check).** I counted every canonical expansion on every page:

- LCT → "Linked Context Token": 14 occurrences across 6 pages. Zero variants.
- T3 → "Trust Tensor" / V3 → "Value Tensor": 17 and 16 occurrences. The dimensions appear as "Talent / Training / Temperament" 13 times and "Valuation / Veracity / Validity" 13 times, in the same order every time, always as root dimensions and never as the expansion. The worked-example slot form "T3 (Talent / …)" that earlier logs flagged is gone.
- MRH → "Markov Relevancy Horizon": 11. No "Relevance" or other variants.
- ATP/ADP → "Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packet": 11 each. Zero variants.
- R6 → "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result": 5 instances, all byte-identical. R7 is given as "R6 + Reputation" throughout.
- Drift strings (Lifelong, Trust-Tracing, Attention Token, Minimum Response, production-ready): **0 hits on 9 pages.**
- "unification" and "scope inflation" appear only as negations beside "fractal leverage".

The short definitions that follow the expansions agree too. MRH is "the boundary of what an entity can know or affect given its position, history, and context" on /context and /raising. LCT is "verifiable (digital) presence that accumulates witnessed history" on /projects, /context and /fleet.

**Definition on first use.** On each page I measured how far the first bare use of each acronym comes before its first expansion. What turned up:
- /projects, /raising and /links each open with an explicit notice ("Web4 vocabulary on this page … is expanded inline on first use"). I tested that claim by checking the first *card or prose* use after each notice. It holds: R6's first card use (ARC-SAGE card) carries the full expansion, and SNARC's first card use is followed at once by "(Surprise / Novelty / Arousal / Reward / Conflict)".
- /links has one small exception. The Links repo list says "4-Life's local stand-in for a T3/V3 reputation record" about 300 characters *before* the Web4 entry that expands T3/V3. The pointer notice sits between them, so it's defensible, but a strict first-use rule is broken.
- /context (Glossary) uses SAGE, T3/V3, LCT and MRH in its intro ("every relationship carries T3/V3 tensors bound to LCTs and scoped by MRH") between 9,000 and 16,000 characters before their glossary rows. SAGE comes 16.5k characters earlier. On a glossary page this is acceptable only because the rows can be linked to directly, and now they can (below).
- Home uses LCT/T3/MRH/R6/ATP only as a list inside the "Vocabulary →" card. That is a pointer, not usage. Fine.
- TCSEC appears on Home without expansion. The expansion ("Trusted Computer System Evaluation Criteria") exists only in the /context A0–A4 row.

**Structure has improved a lot since the structural-layer audit.** All 57 glossary rows now have `id` anchors (`/context#t3`, `#mrh`, `#governance-oversight`…). I found 60 fragment links across the site and **checked all 60 against the target pages' ids: 0 broken.** /principles now has 10 anchored headings. Links whose visible text is a bare URL are down to 2 (one on /context, one on /projects).

**What's still structurally off:**
1. **/context heading outline is flat and skips a level.** It is one H1 followed by 26 H3s, with no H2. "Glossary at a glance" is an H3 at the same level as "The CLAUDE.md pattern", "Threat model", "What doesn't flow well (yet)" and "Evidence & limitations". So a screen reader or a generated table of contents sees the glossary as 1 of 27 equal sections of a page titled "Glossary". /fleet has the same H1→H3 skip once.
2. **"Glossary at a glance" is not at a glance.** The table is 57 rows and **5,135 words**, 45% of an 11,374-word page. The median row is 86 words and the longest are V3 (272), Substrate (262), hestia (220) and MRH (212). Nine rows contain correction history ("Until 2026-…", "An earlier version of this row…", "withdrawn"). The rows aren't alphabetical either: Web4, Ontology, Trust-native, Trust, hestia, Hestia, A0–A4, Autonomous, Emergence, MCP, Substrate, RDF, LCT… For a lookup table, the reader can't guess where a term is.
3. **Three capitalization-only name pairs are in the glossary itself:** `hestia` (trust store) / `Hestia` (the daemon), `HUB` (a machine) / `Hub` (the project), and, on /raising, the curriculum-phase names versus the BECOMING pattern names ("which borrow the same names but are descriptive categories, not stages"). Add A1 (Hestia's second-lowest rung) versus A1 (TCSEC's highest class), which /context acknowledges "inverts". Each collision is honestly annotated. But the annotation *is* the fix the site keeps choosing, and each one costs every reader a footnote. In documentation, when two referents share a name, you rename one.

**Glossary coverage gaps.** I checked which framework terms are used on nav pages but have no glossary row:
- **"Interactive selection"** is the subtitle in /raising's `<title>` ("Raising — Interactive Selection"), a heading on /raising, and Principle 7. It has no glossary row, so the site's name for its own method is undefined in its own glossary.
- **BECOMING** is used 5× on /raising, 2× on /fleet and once on /context. It has no row. /raising defines it well ("a proper name, not an acronym… Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting"), but only there.
- **4-Life** is used on /projects, /principles and /links (2×). It has no row.
- **TCSEC** is expanded only inside the A0–A4 row, which is fine, but Home uses it bare (see above).

**The governance / oversight split is now handled in one place.** "governance" appears 46 times site-wide. I checked every occurrence that has "Hardbound" within ~110 characters (5 cases). All five are intentional: two are the /context explanation that the oversight rule binds Hardbound and that Hestia's function is legitimately called governance, one is Principles' statement of the rule, and two name SAGE's upstream expansion. Nowhere is Hardbound itself called governance. The per-page "not governance" disclaimers that a 09-17 log counted at 14 on 7 pages are down to 2 on 2 pages. The rest link to `/context#governance-oversight`, which is the right pattern. One residual for a newcomer: Home's first paragraph calls Hestia, Hub and SAGE "running governance systems", and Hestia is "the governance daemon" on /projects, /context and /links. A reader who then hits the site's own oversight rule on /principles ("Hardbound is not 'governance'") has to reach the /context note to learn that the rule is Hardbound-specific. /principles doesn't link that anchor.

**Cross-page figures agree.** Machines: "eight (six cognition + two society-hosts)" is consistent on Home, /fleet, /projects and /context. /autonomy's "6 cognition machines" and ARC's "6 machines at benchmark time" are both correct in context. Session records are 2,499 / 2,620 / 2,846 on /projects, /context, /links and /fleet, with /fleet named as source of record, and the named owner does hold the figure. The claim that the Quick start is "the only copy on the site" is true: install and clone commands appear only on /projects. The ARC figures (94.85%, 23 of 25 environments, 92.0%, 175 of 183 levels, Opus 4.6, 2026-04-17) are identical on all 7 pages that state them. Formats vary ("23/25" on /arc-agi-3, "23 of 25" elsewhere), but the values agree. Every date on the site is ISO (138 of them, none in any other format).

One small arithmetic inconsistency: /projects gives Synchronism's total as "~3,360 sessions (~678 core + ~2,671 chemistry)". The parts sum to 3,349, and /context repeats "~3,360" twice. The tildes cover it, but a reader who adds the parts will wonder why the total is 11 higher.

### Verdict
The consciousness question is closed on this site. Every experiential term is disowned or operationalized, and the deflationary alternative is stated more rigorously than most published work manages. "Raising, not training" is defensible on the narrow point it makes (current lines don't update weights, with the LoRA history disclosed). The open problem is the positive claim underneath it. The lab's name for its method, "interactive selection", describes a contingent probe-and-reinforce loop, while the site's own records describe a fixed script with unconditional praise and a separate model doing the consolidating. The next move shouldn't be another caveat. Draw that conclusion on the page, and run the one drift measurement the site says public data already supports.
