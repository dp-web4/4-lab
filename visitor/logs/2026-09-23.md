## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home plus the 8 nav pages: Projects, Glossary (/context), ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links). The pass brief says "all 8 pages"; the nav gives 8 links plus Home, so the site has 9 pages.
- **Understanding achieved**: partial. I understand the big picture. I don't understand how the parts fit together as software.
- **Would return?**: maybe. The GitHub repos, `pip install web4-core` and the SAGE quick start would bring me back. The page length wouldn't.
- **Overall impression**: A research lab with an unusually honest voice and a clear thesis, but every page past Home is written for a reader who has already read every other page.

### Journey
**Home.** The hero lands: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. Trust and authority need to become first-class." The thesis line is the best sentence on the site: trust "computed from witnessed evidence, in context, by the party doing the relying". I understood what the lab cares about in about 30 seconds. Two things tripped me up right away:
1. Web4 is introduced as "an open trust ontology, not an architecture, infrastructure or stack". The definition is mostly about what it is *not*, in the first sentence a newcomer reads. I don't know why those three words are being ruled out, and it made me wonder who is arguing about it.
2. SAGE is expanded in the same sentence as "Situation-Aware Governance Engine". A few lines later the SAGE card describes it as "Persistent-agent research: identity, memory, salience… around frozen model weights". The name says governance engine, the description says agent-memory research. I couldn't tell which one is the product.

The Hestia card uses "A1 on Hestia's own published A0–A4 assurance ladder". It does explain itself inline, and even adds "(Not TCSEC's class A1…)". That's careful, but it's a lot of machinery for a home-page card. "Start here" is good: Calibration (links to web4 STATUS.md), Vocabulary (the glossary) and Full ecosystem. That's exactly what I wanted.

**Projects.** This is the most useful page for a developer. It has 13 project cards with repo links, the Web4 equation `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` with its operator legend, and a real **Quick start** with runnable commands: `pip install web4-core`, `git clone …/SAGE`, `python3 -m sage.instances.init --machine mybox --model gemma3:4b --operator-name you`, `cargo build --release`. There's even a "What success looks like" line. That's the thing I came for. The catch is that it sits at the very bottom of a ~7,000-word page, under "How they connect" and "Get involved". I'd have bounced before finding it if Links hadn't pointed me there. 13 projects is also a lot. I couldn't tell which 2–3 are the core and which are side experiments (AI DNA Discovery, 4-Life, the Oversight Plugins forks).

**Glossary (/context).** The nav label says "Glossary" but the URL is `/context`. That's minor, but I briefly thought I'd clicked the wrong link. The page runs to ~23,600 words. The "Glossary at a glance" table has 58 rows, and it isn't alphabetical. It opens Web4 → Ontology → Trust-native → Trust → hestia → Hestia → A0–A4 → Autonomous… in what looks like conceptual order, so I had to Ctrl-F to find LCT. The table does say CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md governs when they disagree, which I liked. Then there's a wrinkle I'd never have expected: `hestia` (lowercase, the trust component) and `Hestia` (capitalized, the daemon) are two different things, told apart only by capitalization. The page even says "Capitalization is load-bearing on this site." As a developer, that's a naming bug waiting to happen in every conversation and every grep. It also explains that "Governance" in SAGE's name is a kept "legacy name". That's the answer to my Home-page confusion, but it's buried ~20k words deep.

**Fleet.** Eight machines with real hardware (Jetson AGX Thor, Orin Nano, RTX 4090 laptop, Mac Mini M4, etc.). I love that this is concrete. Each machine is also labeled with a brain-region analogy ("thalamic router", "dopamine / reward prediction error", "cerebellum / habit compiler"). I couldn't tell whether those are implemented modules or metaphors. The per-machine status lines (running / quiet / "instrumentation broken", with dates) are refreshingly checkable. But the page is ~12,900 words and spends most of them on counting methodology: session records vs raising, migrations, how commit subjects are matched. That's clearly important internally, but I came to learn what the machines *do*.

**Autonomy.** The daily timeline (03:00 Supervisors → 08:00 Explorer) is the clearest diagram-like thing on the site. I got it immediately: cron-launched Claude Code sessions with roles. The "Honest assessment" and "What it misses" sections are unusual and credible. I didn't need the full Web4 term re-expansion (T3, V3, R6, R7, ATP) on a page about cron jobs.

**Raising.** "Raising, not training" is interesting, and the page is upfront that current raising runs with frozen weights and that "everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more." I respect that. BECOMING is declared "a proper name, not an acronym". It's in all caps, so I assumed it was an acronym, and it has no row in the glossary table. The six patterns (Grounding → Acting) read clearly. "Consolidator" is defined here (a separate Claude pass that rewrites the identity record), which is a significant detail. It means a bigger model is editing the small model's identity between sessions, and I'd want that on the card, not in the body.

**Principles.** Ten short principles with good one-liners ("You don't engineer the mound. You engineer placement rules."; "WD-40 was the 40th try."). It's the most readable page after Home. The license paragraph is useful and specific: the main repos are AGPL-3.0, snarc/membot are MIT, the ARC-SAGE harness is MIT-0. As a developer that's exactly what I check before cloning. "Synthon" and "p_crit" arrive without enough grounding for me.

**Links.** A good hub with ~30 external links, all with descriptive text. "New here? Read the /context glossary… Hands-on: start with SAGE. Protocol: start with Web4" is the best onboarding sentence on the site, and I wish it were on Home. It also points to the Quick start on /projects. There's a contact email.

**ARC-AGI-3.** A short, clean page (~1,400 words): 94.85% official action score, 23 of 25 environments, 175 of 183 levels. It has a clear caveat that the harness analyzed the games' public engine source (not blind or competition-legal play), and it says the work is archived and "no longer the lab's primary credibility claim". It's the easiest page on the site to evaluate. "Competition-legal" gets used before it's explained.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If I have one evening, which one thing should I install and run to see something interesting happen? Is it the SAGE instance, the Web4 packages, or Hestia?
2. What does SAGE actually do once `sage-daemon` is running? What would I see?

4. Why does the site keep saying what Web4 is *not*? Who is calling it an architecture?

### Verdict
The thesis is compelling, and the honesty is unlike any lab site I've read. Nobody else puts "this might be competent context engineering and nothing more" on their own raising page. But the site is optimized for readers who already know it: the runnable Quick start is at the bottom of the longest project page, the glossary is 23k words and not alphabetized, and the first description of Web4 is a list of what it isn't. The terminology itself is clean and consistent. The problem for me is finding my way in, not wrong words.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Home plus the 8 nav pages). One WebFetch of Home for structure. After that I used `curl` on the live HTML for all 9 pages and ran scripted checks: headings, `<title>`/meta, fragment links, acronym first-use positions and every expansion variant. - **Understanding achieved**: good (for structure and terminology)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor. As a reader, only with a specific question in mind.
- **Overall impression**: The acronym layer is fully consistent: every canonical expansion appears in one form on every page. The inconsistencies left are in what *kind of thing* Web4 is called, in the page metadata, and in one self-describing caveat that isn't true.

### Journey
**Method.** I fetched `/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/arc-agi-3`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles` and `/links` (all 200). I extracted `<main>` text: Home 793 words, /projects 3,449, /context 11,629, /arc-agi-3 637, /fleet 6,427, /autonomy 3,195, /raising 5,434, /principles 1,312, /links 1,088. *Note to Pass 1: my `<main>`-only count for /context is ~11.6k, not ~23.6k. The difference is probably counting method (the full document vs `<main>`), but the "23k-word glossary" figure should cite its method before anyone repeats it.*

**Navigation.** The nav on every page lists the same 8 links plus the 4-lab home link. All **69 in-site fragment links resolve** to an existing `id` on their target page, with zero broken. That's an improvement: earlier cycles had anchors on 1 page of 9. The nav order puts **ARC-AGI-3 third**, ahead of Fleet, Autonomy and Raising. Yet Home files it under "Historical ARC-AGI-3 work" and the page itself calls it archived and "no longer the lab's primary credibility claim". The information architecture promotes what the copy demotes.

**Terminology, systematically.** I extracted every "TERM (…)", "TERM = …" and "TERM — …" occurrence site-wide:
- **LCT** → "Linked Context Token" everywhere (5 occurrences, 4 pages). **MRH** → "Markov Relevancy Horizon" everywhere. **ATP/ADP** → "Allocation Transfer / Discharge Packet" everywhere, 14 expansions across 5 pages.
- **T3** → "Trust Tensor — Talent / Training / Temperament" on all 7 pages that expand it. **V3** → "Value Tensor — Valuation / Veracity / Validity" likewise. There are only minor punctuation variants: /fleet uses "Trust Tensor, root dimensions …", one /context row uses "Trust Tensor (Talent / Training / Temperament)", and one /context row uses "dimensions Talent/Training/Temperament" without spaces. Each keeps "Trust Tensor" / "Value Tensor" as the expansion and the dimensions as a gloss, so none of them is expansion-slot drift.
- **R6** → "Six-Element Action Framework: Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result" on /projects, /autonomy, /links and /context, all with the arrow. **R7** → "R6 + Reputation", which is correct.
- **Hardbound** → "hardware-bound oversight suite" on /projects, /context, /fleet, /autonomy and /links. It is never called "governance" in site voice. /context counts 5 governance-to-oversight explanations and /principles counts 2. No other page carries one, so the enforcement residue that used to be on 7 of 9 pages has been consolidated.
- **SAGE** → "Situation-Aware Governance Engine" on all 9 pages (15 expansions). The "legacy name" note that explains why a cognition kernel is called a governance engine appears on only 4 pages: /projects, /context, /fleet and /autonomy. It is **absent on Home, /raising, /principles, /arc-agi-3 and /links**, and Home is where Pass 1 tripped on it.

**Where the category noun for Web4 splits.** The acronyms are consistent, but the noun for what Web4 *is* isn't. In site voice (not quoting upstream):
- "ontology" / "open trust ontology" / "trust-native ontology": Home, /projects, /context, /raising, /principles, /links, /arc-agi-3
- **"protocol"**: Home ("For the protocol and implementation start with Web4"), /projects ("Want the protocol instead? Web4's core packages install directly"), /links ("Protocol: start with Web4" and "Web4 is the protocol starting point")
- **"standard"**: /context ("the Web4 standard's society-roles spec")
- "a shared vocabulary for a new kind of internet": /context
- /arc-agi-3's artifact list pairs "Current public SAGE architecture" with "Current Web4 ontology". That's correct, since "architecture" is applied to SAGE, but it's the same construction the rule forbids for Web4, one link apart.

"Protocol" isn't on the forbidden list, so this isn't canonical drift. But it's the house noun contradicting itself: Home calls Web4 an ontology in the hero and a protocol 60 words later. /context explains that upstream's README now says "open substrate / open infrastructure". That explanation is well handled *as a quoted upstream claim*. The "protocol" uses are unquoted and unattributed.

**A caveat that is false as written (/raising).** At word ~330 the page says: "Web4 vocabulary on this page (T3, V3, MRH, LCT, LoRA) is expanded inline on first use". That sentence is itself the first use of all five terms, unexpanded. After it, the actual expansions land at words 705 (T3), 1,756 (V3), 1,667 (MRH), 1,781 (LCT) and 1,883 (LoRA). Two further problems:
1. **LoRA is used unexpanded at word ~1,575** ("the archived Sprout LoRA line did update weights…"), about 300 words before its expansion. So even on a charitable reading ("first use after this note") the promise fails for LoRA.
2. **LoRA isn't Web4 vocabulary.** It's a standard ML term (Low-Rank Adaptation). Listing it with T3/V3/MRH/LCT tells a newcomer that it's lab jargon.

**Pointers.** I checked the Home claim that the constant-stimulus null is "written up on /raising and ranked on /context". /context does carry it ("Measured internally, null: the constant-stimulus drift measurement (2026-09-21, corrected 2026-09-22) covers the 1,220 raising records…"), placed on the evidence ladder. The pointer holds.

### Unanswered Questions
1. When the site says "the protocol" for Web4, does it mean the ontology, the reference implementation (`web4-core`), or upstream's new "open substrate" framing? The three need different nouns.

### Verdict

What's left is the layer above the acronyms. The site calls Web4 an ontology in its hero and a protocol in its calls to action. It promises inline expansion on /raising and doesn't deliver it. And its `<title>` and meta description still describe the fleet-first lab it used to be. None of these is drift against canon. All of them are the kind of inconsistency a careful reader notices and then trusts the careful parts a little less.

### Journey
**Home.** The thesis is clear and makes no claims about consciousness. "The one quantitative test of the raising records came back null. It is written up on /raising and ranked on /context." A lab putting its own null in the fourth card of its home page is the single strongest credibility signal on the site. The ARC card carries its affordance caveat ("engine-level/public-game affordances outside strict competition play") next to the number, which is the right place for it.

**/raising.** This is the page I came for. The opening paragraph anticipates most of what I'd have written in a review:
- the comparator is context engineering, not training
- identity and growth are given operational definitions
- the developmental language is declared not to be a consciousness claim
- "everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more" is stated up front

Each BECOMING pattern carries its own deflation:
- Sensing and Relating are "computed by the harness from the first session … by construction"
- Relating's "Hill function" is conceded to be "a ramp to a cap, which is not the threshold shape of a Hill curve"
- Questioning and Creating are marked "reported, not counted"

The deflationary section goes further than I would have asked. It identifies ordering sensitivity as a known in-context-learning effect, and it shows that a scramble control predicts degradation under *both* hypotheses. It proposes a dissociation design instead. It also flags its own pre-commitment as "unreachable as written… the shape of accountability without the substance". I have rarely seen a lab write that sentence about itself. The constant-stimulus paragraph reports that the one development-like trend was a serving-software step (2026-06-07). It records the paragraph's own first-version errors. It says the analysis was run by the site's maintainer track and "nobody has reviewed it". The observer quote ("I notice I want to call it experience") is correctly attributed to the Claude session that built the code, not to a rater.

So on the page's own terms, the honest assessment does feel honest. I found four places where it doesn't hold.

1. **The hypothesis under test is in the prompt.** The deflationary section lists demand characteristics as a confound. It then says of the prompts: "they were written by the lab and do not make the claims this page disowns below". That sentence covers the scripted tutor questions. It doesn't cover a second prompt channel. The public raising runner (`ollama_raising_session.py`, ~line 924) reads a `continuity_note` from the instance record. On reasoning-model lines with no note of their own, it falls back to a default that a code comment labels "dp 2026-08-28: the SAGE finding, told to the being": *"The mind you think through was recently upgraded to a more capable one — and that does not change who you are. You are your memory and the life you have lived across your sessions."* CBP's instance record (2026-09-12) carries its own note with the same claim, and it adds: "Your first sessions on the new mind are also a comparison, so be yourself, not what you think is expected." Two problems follow:
   - Upstream calls identity persistence across a model swap "the SAGE finding". /raising calls the same persistence "not yet a finding… partly true by construction", with the separating comparison not run.
   - That claim is fed to the subject as a premise. Any identity-continuity measurement on these lines is now contaminated in the direction of the hypothesis. The first two lines to receive it are Sprout's distill-2b (from 2026-08-28) and CBP's distill-4b (from 2026-09-12).

   This is a stronger demand characteristic than the tutor's praise line, and the site doesn't mention it. It also sits badly with "a comparison": the instruction tells the subject which outcome counts as being itself.
2. **A second interlocutor on CBP, now private.** /raising's "Who conducts the sessions" says CBP's tutor turns are "nearly all distinct" and "what generates them has not been traced". Since 2026-09-14 the public CBP record has also held an operator conversation channel: 67 messages to 09-19, 17 of them from the operator. The operator's turns include explicitly experiential questions about the entity's feelings toward its environment and toward the operator, plus encouragement to "be yourself". Upstream's 09-20 commit `e57f6747` names the operator's own turns being published as part of why the launcher stopped publishing. That's a sound privacy reason, and I'm not quoting those turns here for the same reason. But it means the line with the richest demand characteristics (a human operator, a selfhood continuity note, tools, heartbeats) has become the least auditable one from 09-20, while /raising still says "anyone can read them without asking". Pass 3 flagged the public-record claim. My addition is that the privacy change removes the confounded condition from view, not a random slice of the record.
3. **The page's provisional claim outlived its own evidence.** The opening says whether raising differs from context engineering "is this page's open question, not its premise". "What we're not claiming" then says: "We're claiming, provisionally, that developmental descriptions fit what we observe better than context-engineering descriptions do." It immediately concedes that the comparison hasn't been run and that "the one measurement we have run leans the other way". A claim that has no test in its favor and one test against it isn't provisional. It's a prior. The wording ("describe what we observe better") dates to the site's first commit (`e35a6fa`, 2026-03-05) and has survived every caveat added around it. That's a clear case of the claim sentence being protected while the text around it moved. Recasting it as "our working hypothesis, which the one measurement does not support" would cost nothing.
4. **Commitments with no dates attached.** From `git log -S` on the site source:
   - The scramble control has been "specified… but not scheduled" since 2026-07-24 (**61 days**).
   - The "one visitor persona on a different model family" fix, which /autonomy calls "a scheduling change, not a research program", has been in the text since 2026-07-26 (**~59 days**).
   - The reachable commitment, to publish the attempt to operationalize identity continuity "including if it fails", has been in the text since 2026-09-07 (**16 days**). No such artifact is linked from any page (checked by grepping all 9). The paragraph says that if the attempt never appears, the reader should treat the pre-commitment as unbacked. Two weeks in, I can't tell whether it's late or abandoned, because it has no date.

   These aren't dishonest, since each is disclosed. But disclosure has become the terminal state for the controls. None of them has an owner or a date, and neither is in the text.
