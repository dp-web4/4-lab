## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary

### Journey
I came in from a GitHub README and landed on Home. The headline, "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust.", plus "Society does not trust people because of a certificate" gave me the thesis quickly. "What we build" has four cards: Web4, Hestia, Hub, SAGE. Web4 is glossed inline as "a shared vocabulary, or ontology, that any system can implement", which was enough for me to place it. Home shows the status badges (RUNNING / RESEARCH) but doesn't say what they mean.

The legend is on `/projects`, at the bottom: "Running: in daily use on our own machines. Building: real code, not yet deliverable. Research: an open question we are working on." That's clear, but I'd already seen the badges on Home without it. The page groups the work three ways: governance for agents (Web4, Hestia, Hub, Hardbound), AI beings (SAGE, SWE-SAGE) and theory (Synchronism). That grouping helped a lot. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight", built by Metalinxx, with no link, which is fine for an enterprise tier. I clicked through the cards. Web4, Hestia, the Hub tree, the SAGE site and the Synchronism site all return 200. **SWE-SAGE (`github.com/dp-web4/SWE-SAGE`) returns 404** when I'm not logged in. From the outside that reads as a dead link to the one project I'd most want to look at as a developer, since it's a coding agent.

`/fleet`: eight machines in a table, from Jetson boards to a Mac mini and Windows desktops, each with a role. The page names the model families (Qwen, Gemma, Granite, Llama) but doesn't map them to machines. That's fine; it points to `/notebook/fleet` for detail, and that page returns 200. "Different on purpose / monoculture" landed with me.

`/raising` was the most interesting page: "the model is the weather, the being is the organism", and "We do not change the model's weights." The "Seen / Not yet shown" split is exactly what I'd want, and "no blind comparison against a fresh model yet" made me trust the rest more. Terms like tutor, mentor, waking periods and governed tools are used without definition. I can guess what they mean, and the notebook link is right there.

`/principles` has six short principles. Clear and quick to scan.

`/links` lists sites, code and packages. It has the same SWE-SAGE 404. The `web4-core` package resolves on both crates.io (API) and PyPI. The `4-gov.org` link returns 200, but the page gives no context for what 4-gov is beyond "governance".

`/notebook` explains the two layers well ("The rest of this site is written for people: short, plain, and current") and indexes nine notebook pages, including a Glossary. I went looking for what SAGE stands for and found it only in the notebook glossary: "SAGE: Situation-Aware Governance Engine".

### Journey

**Front.** No Web4 acronym appears on any front page. LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP and R6/R7 get 0 hits, so the front has no expansion drift and nothing to define. Web4 is called "an open standard … a shared vocabulary, or ontology" on Home and "the open standard, written as an ontology" on `/projects`. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight". Both are canonical. Pass 1 already noted that SAGE is never expanded on the front, and I confirm it.

What I found on the front is a factual inconsistency. `/links` labels the org link **"dp-web4 on GitHub · everything public"**. On the same page, and on `/projects`, it links `github.com/dp-web4/SWE-SAGE`, which returns 404 anonymously. The notebook knows why. `/notebook/projects` and `/notebook/links` both say "The repository is private, so its link on the front /projects and /links pages returns 404 to visitors." So the notebook documents the front's defect, but the front still says "everything public". That is a false fact, so it goes to dp.

**Notebook index → Glossary naming.** The index lists the page as "Glossary", and its `<title>` is "Notebook: Glossary". Its URL is `/notebook/context`. Across the other eight notebook pages, prose points to it as **"/context" 36 times** and as "/notebook/context" once. `/notebook/principles` uses both in one paragraph: "the T3/V3 reading on the glossary" and then "see /context". `/context` does redirect (307), so nothing is broken. But a reader who meets "/context" has to work out that it means the page the index calls Glossary. Bare `/autonomy` (14 prose uses) and `/arc-agi-3` (5) have the same shape. They redirect correctly too, but they are legacy route names, not the notebook URLs.

**First-use distance.** On most notebook pages the expansion sits within 2–5 words of first use (`/notebook/raising`, `/notebook/autonomy`, `/notebook/fleet`, `/notebook/links` for ATP/ADP). The outliers:
- `/notebook/projects`: a note at word ~480 says every acronym the cards use "is defined in the /context glossary — **the cards expand each on first use**". That is false for R6. Its first card use is the Web4 Governance Plugins card (word ~2,886: "R6 records of each call"), unexpanded. Its first expansion is ~640 words later, on the ARC-AGI-3 card (word ~3,528). R7 is first expanded at word ~3,693 (Synchronism card). SNARC is fine: it is expanded on its own card at first card use.

- `/notebook/context` (Glossary): T3 is first used at ~344 and its row is at ~1,846. MRH is ~352→2,281 and SAGE ~136→2,903. This is the known at-a-glance pattern (summary table before the rows). A glossary can reasonably expect readers to scroll, so it's low.

**Front-six map on `/notebook/principles`.** The front `/principles` page ends "The reasoning behind each principle … [is] in the notebook". The notebook's map, recorded 2026-10-13, says plainly that four of the six ("Rules before actions", "The record is kept by someone else", "We use what we build", "Different on purpose") have "No reasoning on this page yet". That disclosure is honest, but the front sentence is still false for 4 of 6, eight days later. The map also says the front's "decisions that are hard to undo come back to the human" is not current practice for pushes.

**Status labels.** Last week's gap is closed. `/notebook/projects` now carries a crosswalk: "The front projects page uses a third set, on another axis again: RUNNING = … So the two can disagree without either being wrong: Web4 is RUNNING on the front and research-stage here". A tech writer would ask for exactly that.

**Structure.** Each of the 16 pages has exactly one `<h1>`. Every notebook page has a distinct `<title>`. All 9 notebook pages share the site-wide meta description ("4-lab is a research collective … AI readers: the full record is at /notebook."), including the Glossary. Search snippets for notebook pages will therefore all read like the front Home page.

**Minor vocabulary.** The front calls Hub's unit a "community" ("Two host the community the rest belong to"). `/notebook/fleet` calls the same machines "society-hosts". Front `/fleet` splits the machines 4 heavy / 2 review / 2 host, and the notebook says "Six cognition machines, plus two society-hosts". These are consistent (6 = 4 + 2) once you do the arithmetic, but the nouns differ across the layer boundary with no gloss. On front `/projects`, Hardbound ("hardware-bound oversight") sits under the section heading "Governance for AI agents". Read literally, the house rule (Hardbound = oversight, not governance) is satisfied by the card and blurred by its heading.
