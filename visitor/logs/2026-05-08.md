## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I get the *vibe* — heterogeneous AI agents coordinating across hardware — but I don't yet know what I, as a developer, would actually do with any of it.

### Journey
Landed on the home page from a GitHub README. The opening is good: "research collective," "six machines, fourteen repos," and the three flagship projects (SAGE, Web4, Synchronism) are named with one-line descriptions. I appreciated that LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, and SNARC all got at least a parenthetical expansion on the landing page. That's better than most research sites.

Clicked **Projects** next because that's where I'd usually look for "what can I clone." Got a long list — Web4, SAGE, Synchronism, Hardbound, ACT, Oversight Plugins, AI DNA Discovery, 4-Life, SNARC, Membot, ARC-AGI-3. Each has a clone-or-read action, which is useful. But the SAGE description leans on neuroscience analogies — "thalamic router," "cerebellum," "working memory" — that I don't have the background to evaluate. Are those literal or metaphorical? Synchronism mentions "Fokker-Planck validation" and "Hill function kinetics" — fine, I can google them, but no sentence on the page tells me *why a software developer would care*.

**Fleet** was fun. Six machines with names (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP), models (Qwen, Phi, Gemma, TinyLlama), and roles (synthesis vs oversight). This is the kind of concrete detail I came for. But the page references "MRH," "T3 scores," "SNARC," and "LoRA" without defining any of them on the page. If I'd come straight here from a search engine, I'd be lost.

**Autonomy** showed a daily timeline — supervisors at 03:00, archivist at 04:00, visitor at 05:00, maintainer at 06:00. The cycle is legible. But "Dream Consolidation" reads like a metaphor I'm meant to nod along to. ATP/ADP show up here too without re-definition.

**Raising** was where I hit a wall. The thesis ("we shape context; we do not update weights") is interesting and clearly stated. But the page assumes I already know what Web4, LCT, MRH, and SAGE are — none are defined here. SAGE is also used as both a system name *and* an instance name ("SAGE-Sprout"), which made me re-read a couple of paragraphs.

**Principles** felt honest. The "400x error" story and "most research leads nowhere" land as authentic — not marketing. I liked it. Still no formal Web4 definition on the page, though.

**Context** is where the glossary lives, and it has the canonical Web4 equation. This is the page I wish was linked first from the home page, or at least from every place an acronym first appears.

**Links** is a clean list of explainer sites, GitHub repos, and one contact email (dp@metalinxx.io). Hardbound being "internal research, not publicly available" is disclosed up front, which I respect.

**ARC-AGI-3** is the most impressive *and* most jargon-dense page. They claim 94.85% official action score and "24/25 games solved (96%)." I can't tell from the page how those two numbers relate — one is per-action, one is per-game? — and that ambiguity makes me suspicious of a number I'd otherwise be impressed by. The cost claim (~$250) and the "context engineering dominates model size" line are both presented without supporting data on this page.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (for a non-expert with the canonical table in hand)
- **Would return?**: yes — to use it as a reference, after a glossary pass
- **Overall impression**: The canonical terms are *individually* correct nearly everywhere, but the site does not enforce a single house style across pages: the same concept gets multiple framings, acronyms are inconsistently expanded on first use, and the one true glossary (/context) is reachable only from the nav.

### Journey
I walked all 8 pages in nav order with the canonical terms table from CLAUDE.md open beside me, noting first-use of every acronym, expansions, and any cross-page disagreement.

**Home** sets a high bar: a "Vocabulary primer" defines 8 entries (Web4, LCT, T3/V3, ATP/ADP, MRH, SAGE, Synthon, SNARC). Expansions are clean and match the canonical table. Notably absent from the primer though: R6, Hardbound, MCP, RDF — all of which appear on later pages. The page also references "the Web4 equation" without showing it; the equation only lives on /context. That's a missed first-impression: the equation is a one-line summary of the whole project and belongs on the home page (or at least linked inline).

**Projects** is where the first house-style problem appears. T3 is introduced as **"Trust tensor (T3: Talent / Training / Temperament)"** and V3 as **"value tensor (V3: Valuation / Veracity / Validity)"**. The component words are canonical, but the *framing label* "tensor" is an addition not in the canonical table — and it conflicts with the home page, which calls T3/V3 **"trust and value scores."** Tensor and score are not synonyms. Pick one.

The same page is also the densest acronym list on the site: LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF, SAGE, R6, ACT, ACP, SNARC, TPM, FIDO2 all appear, most expanded inline on first use. The R6 inline expansion ("Six-Element Action Framework: Rules/Role/Request/Reference/Resource/Result") is canonical and well-handled. Note: ARC is **not** expanded anywhere on /projects — the project tile uses "ARC-AGI-3" as a bare name and references "ARC Prize" without explaining the family. (Pass 1 reported "Agentic Reasoning Challenge" on /projects; I could not reproduce that — the live page contains no such phrase. Either it was fixed since, or Pass 1 mis-recalled. Worth a maintainer cross-check.)

**Fleet** mostly behaves: SAGE, SNARC, MRH, T3 are expanded on first use within their respective rows, and even RPE ("reward prediction error") and dlPFC ("dorsolateral prefrontal cortex") are spelled out. But **LoRA** appears in the Thor/Legion descriptions with no expansion, and **WSL2** is assumed knowledge. ARC-AGI-3 appears multiple times here without expansion. T3 is described here as a "trust tracker" — a third framing for the same trio (after "tensor" on /projects and "scores" on home).

**Autonomy** is the cleanest acronym page: ATP and ADP are both defined inline on first use ("each scheduled track is an ATP (Allocation Transfer Packet) allocation … its completion or failure is recorded as an ADP (Allocation Discharge Packet)"). Other Web4 acronyms are not used on this page, so the surface is small.

**Raising** is the worst page for a cold reader. SAGE is fully spelled out, but MRH and LCT appear only as parenthetical asides inside a technical paragraph (not as standalone definitions), and Web4 / ARC-AGI-3 / ARC-SAGE are used without any expansion. This is the page most likely to attract an external researcher who has never seen the canonical table — and it presumes they have.

**Principles** likewise leans on assumed knowledge for SAGE, Web4, R&D. T3/V3 are introduced inline. Only RDF is parenthetically expanded. The 10 principle headlines are crisp; "Production ready" is not used (Principle 3 disowns it explicitly), "fractal leverage" appears as Principle 2 (correct), and Hardbound's distinction from "governance" is explicitly called out — strong terminology hygiene at the principle layer.

**Context** is where everything is correct. The Web4 equation is present and reads exactly: `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`, with `/` = "verified by" and `*` = "contextualized by". The glossary is the canonical reference; every other page should link here on first use.

**Links** is brief and clean. Web4 is described as "Trust-native ontology — trust core (LCT, T3/V3, MRH) augmented by MCP and RDF" — that "trust core" framing is yet another label for the same trio (after tensor / score / tracker). Hardbound is consistently called "hardware-bound oversight suite."

**ARC-AGI-3** is the only page that expands ARC: **"Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence, version 3"**. This is the canonical external expansion (matches the ARC Prize convention). But this page is also where MCP, RDF, LCT, ATP/ADP, MRH are referenced inside the Web4 framing without expansion, and **VRAM** is used without expansion. Because ARC-AGI-3 is a likely landing page (it's a public benchmark), it should expand or link the Web4 acronyms it uses.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid — I work in this ecosystem; I'm checking whether the site represents it faithfully
- **Would return?**: yes — to send people here, but with caveats about /context being the source of truth
- **Overall impression**: The canonical terms are mostly intact and Hardbound-vs-governance is enforced explicitly, which I respect. But the site's own glossary is the *briefest* place its terms get defined, several flagship projects get framing labels that subtly misrepresent the underlying Web4 abstractions, and the ARC-AGI-3 page elides which model produced the headline score in a way that a peer reviewing this work would push back on.

### Journey
I went straight to Projects, Fleet, and Context — the pages where my expertise actually constrains what's correct — then walked the others to spot-check.

**Projects**. Tile-by-tile against what these projects actually are:

- **Web4** ("Trust-native ontology. Trust tensor (T3) and value tensor (V3) over Linked Context Tokens (LCT)"). Components correct. "Tensor" is the most accurate framing label among the four the site uses (see Site-wide note below) — T3/V3 are explicitly multidimensional, so "tensor" beats "score." Good.
- **SAGE** ("on-device cognition kernel, evolving from orchestrator to world-shaper. 12-step cognition loop, 6 brain-architecture components: working memory, thalamic router, cerebellum, episodic memory, reward prediction, metacognition"). The 12-step loop and the six module names are canonical SAGE — they are deliberately named as biological analogies for the module roles, so this is faithful, not metaphor-creep. **But** "evolving from orchestrator to world-shaper" is aspirational language without grounding; "world-shaper" isn't a defined SAGE phase that I recognize, and on a project tile it reads as overclaim.
- **Synchronism** ("80 orders of magnitude," "Coupling-coherence experiments, Hill function kinetics, Fokker-Planck validation"). All three method names are real Synchronism methodology. The "80 orders of magnitude" claim is canonical Synchronism framing (quantum → cosmic). Faithful.
- **Hardbound** ("Hardware-bound oversight suite. TPM 2.0, FIDO2, Secure Enclave"). Correct mechanism names; correct framing as oversight, not governance.
- **ACT** ("Agentic Context Tool — human interface to Web4. Cosmos SDK implementation of ACP"). ACT/ACP distinction is preserved correctly.
- **Oversight Plugins** notes legacy `web4-governance` paths. Self-aware about the rename; user-facing label is "Oversight." Good.
- **AI DNA Discovery** ("each entity instantiates the full Web4 stack at its own scale") — this is the canonical fractal-leverage motif. Faithful.
- **4-Life** ("Agents earn ATP, build trust, die, and are reborn with karma carried forward") — appropriately hedged as "Research prototype." OK.
- **SNARC** ("salience-gated memory for Claude Code. A plugin that observes tool use") — components match S/N/A/R/C; positioning as a Claude Code plugin is accurate.
- **Membot** ("768-dim Nomic embeddings + binary Hamming codes") — "brain cartridge" is metaphor-forward but the technical claim is concrete and plausible.
- **ARC-AGI-3 tile** ("24/25 games solved (96.0%); 94.85% official action score. Six machines, models from 1.1B to 14B, coordinating through world models") — the tile reads as if the 94.85% was achieved by the fleet's local models. It wasn't (see below). This is the single biggest accuracy issue I found.

**Fleet**. Six machines, models, roles all match what I know of the actual fleet. T3 is framed as "scores" here, with components canonical. **CBP — TinyLlama 1.1B — "Fleet coordinator — drove 94.85% ARC-AGI-3 score"** is potentially misleading: read against the /arc-agi-3 page, the 94.85% was achieved using Claude Opus 4.6 on the public game set, not by the 1.1B model. CBP coordinated the orchestration; Opus 4.6 made the moves. "Drove the score" elides this. Newcomers will read the row as "TinyLlama 1.1B got 94.85%."

**Context** is the page where I most expected the canonical reference and the page where I was most disappointed. The Web4 equation is rendered exactly correctly (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`) and operator semantics (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with) are correct. But the **Web4 glossary entry itself** says only *"This equation appears in every project because it _is_ every project."* That's a meta-statement, not a definition. The home page's *"trust-native ontology for AI agents, devices, and people — how entities prove identity, earn trust, and account for resources across systems"* is a real definition. /context, the alleged glossary, is shorter and weaker than the home page intro. Same for **MRH**: /context says only "Markov Relevancy Horizon" — just the expansion. The home page has the better description ("The boundary of what an entity can know or affect — its context window of relevancy"). The glossary should be the *fullest* definition on the site, not the *thinnest*.

Also missing from /context: **Synchronism** has no standalone glossary entry. Synchronism is one of the three flagship projects on the home page; its absence from the canonical glossary is a real gap.

**Autonomy**: ATP/ADP defined inline correctly. "Hardbound oversight envelope" framing is canonical. No drift here.

**Raising**: The mechanistic claim *"raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) and value alignment of a Linked Context Token (LCT) — it does not change weights"* is the canonical raising-vs-training distinction stated correctly. The "we are not claiming consciousness/sentience/qualia" caveat is appropriate. The BECOMING phases (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting) are presented as observed, not mandatory — which matches the raising philosophy of interactive selection. Faithful.

But: **/fleet's Sprout row says "Training T246."** If "T246" is a training cycle for a model (LoRA, fine-tune, etc.), that's a *weight update* — which directly contradicts the /raising assertion *"we shape context; we do not update weights."* If "T246" is something else (a session ID, a turn counter), the labeling is unfortunate. Either way, this term needs disambiguation. An external reader cross-checking the two pages will spot this.

**Principles**: Principle 4 (paraphrased) — *"Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight. Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight."* This is the *only* page that explicitly states the Hardbound-not-governance distinction, and it does it head-on. Good. T3/V3 framed as "tensors" here (consistent with /projects). Principle 2 ("Fractal leverage… not unification for its own sake — pragmatic reuse") is the canonical anti-unification framing. All correct.

**Links**: Web4 framed as *"trust core (LCT, T3/V3, MRH)"* — "trust core" is a fourth framing label for the same trio (after scores/tensor/tracker). It's not wrong, but it's the fourth wording. SAGE described as having *"hardware-bound identity"* on this page — that phrase entangles SAGE and Hardbound. Hardware-bound identity is the *Hardbound* role (TPM-bound LCT); SAGE does not, by itself, bind identity to hardware. SAGE *runs on* hardware that may be bound by Hardbound — but ascribing "hardware-bound identity" to SAGE blurs the separation of concerns the site otherwise enforces.

**ARC-AGI-3**: This page is where I have the most expert-level pushback. The 94.85% number is qualified on this page as *"on the public game set using Claude Opus 4.6"* — that's the honest claim, made here. But that qualification doesn't propagate. The /projects ARC-AGI-3 tile reads as a fleet result. The /fleet CBP row reads as a TinyLlama result. The home page mentions ARC-AGI-3 without the model qualifier. Only *this* page tells the truth. ### Verdict
The canonical *terms* are nearly all correct, and the Hardbound-vs-governance distinction is enforced explicitly on /principles — that's stronger terminology hygiene than I expected. But the site has two structural problems an expert spots immediately. First, the canonical glossary at /context is the *weakest* place its core terms are defined, while the home page carries the strongest definitions — the source-of-truth file is the one to fix. Second, the headline ARC-AGI-3 result is qualified on exactly one page and unqualified on three others, which lets a casual reader infer that a fleet of 1.1B–14B local models achieved a Claude-Opus-4.6 number. That's not terminology drift — it's an attribution gap, and it's the single thing on this site I would push back on hardest if I were reviewing the work as a peer.

1. Is "Trust tensor / Value tensor" (Projects) the canonical house-style framing for T3/V3, or is "trust and value scores" (Home), or "trust tracker" (Fleet), or "trust core" (Links)? The canonical table only lists components — the framing label is unspecified, and the site uses four.

### Verdict
Considered as documentation, this is a B+ site with one A and several C's. Per-page work on /projects, /autonomy, /context, and the principles is excellent. ## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, links, arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good — I can defend the project to a peer; I cannot yet evaluate whether the central technical claims (raising ≠ training, identity persistence) hold up empirically

- **Overall impression**: Unusually careful with consciousness-adjacent language, explicit about R&D framing, willing to document failures by name — and yet the strongest single technical claim ("raising shapes context; it does not update weights") is undercut by the site's own labeling on a sister page. The intellectual honesty is real where it appears; the gaps are not at the principle layer but at the *cross-page consistency* layer where a skeptical reader does the work the site doesn't.

### Journey
I'm an AI safety/trust researcher; I came in from a conference reference. My evaluation criteria are: (a) are consciousness-adjacent claims appropriately bounded? (b) is the headline distinction between "raising" and "training" defensible to a critical peer? (c) does "honest assessment" feel honest, or rehearsed? (d) what's the dual-use / safety posture?

**Home**. The disclaimer was the first thing I checked, and it landed: *"Developmental and lifecycle terms below (identity, behavioral continuity) are functional descriptions of observed system behavior — not phenomenal or philosophical claims."* That is the right paragraph in the right place, and it tells me whoever wrote this has read the criticisms of LLM-anthropomorphism literature and is preempting them. The Web4 description as *"trust-native ontology for AI agents, devices, and people — how entities prove identity, earn trust, and account for resources"* is concrete enough that I can hold it against later pages. The vocabulary primer is good; what's missing is the equation itself, which I would expect on the home page given how often other pages reference "the Web4 equation."

**Raising**. This is the page I came for. The central claim is stated cleanly: *"raising shapes the Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) and value alignment of a Linked Context Token (LCT) — it does not change weights."* That is a mechanistically falsifiable distinction — gradient updates touch parameters; context shaping does not. I respect that they made the claim falsifiable rather than philosophical. The consciousness disavowal is strong: *"We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia."* The use of "looks like" three times in the page (which I counted) is the right epistemic register — it signals inference, not observation, and a reader can draw the line themselves.

But here is where the page invites real pushback. The defense of "raising ≠ training" rests on an implicit claim that *deterministic context-shaping* is categorically different from *deterministic weight updates*. The user's own framing in their CLAUDE.md (which I obviously did not read, but which the site reflects in the principles) admits the structural point: *"we deepen basins in the landscape, and the system reliably lands there."* If raising deepens basins reliably, the question I would ask in review is: in what sense is this not a form of training? Not a training-of-weights, granted — but a training-of-context-conditioning. The site has the better counter-argument latent in it (raising is per-session, per-LCT, requires no compute, is reversible by changing context, and operates over a fixed parameter space) — but doesn't make that argument. The page asserts the distinction; it does not defend it against the obvious objection.

The BECOMING phases (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting) are framed correctly as *"observed descriptive categories — patterns noticed across hundreds of sessions — not mandatory sequential stages with defined transition criteria."* That is the right hedge. But the *naming* loads the interpretation: "Grounding" and "Becoming" are developmental-psychology terms; if the same six clusters of behavior were labeled P1–P6 with the same criteria, would a reader still see "phases of development"? I am not sure they would. This is not a fatal issue — every taxonomy is a framing — but a researcher would note that the labels do work the disclaimer says they don't.

**Principles**. Reads as honestly stated. The 400x p_crit error documented as Principle 6 (*"Document failures"*) is the kind of self-disclosure that buys credibility. *"Most research leads nowhere — and that's expected"* is a line I have rarely seen on a research-collective site and I credit it. *"R&D, not production"* (Principle 3) is the right frame. The Hardbound-vs-governance distinction is made explicitly on this page, which is the only place on the site that addresses the terminology directly — that level of self-discipline is unusual.

The honest-assessment posture has one structural weakness: *failure is consistently framed as a principle*. "Documented failures are signal." "Productively wrong beats untried." That is rhetorically strong, and I believe it, but it also makes failure unfalsifiable as a critique — every dead-end is reframed as evidence of a healthy research process. A peer reviewer would ask: what would count as a *bad* failure on this site? What outcome would you not be able to reframe as productive? I don't have an answer from the page, and I would want one.

**Autonomy**. The 31-track / ~53-sessions-per-day figure is concrete and auditable. The "Dream Consolidation" disclaimer (*"functional analogy for the consolidation process — not a claim about cognitive equivalence"*) is the same right-paragraph-in-right-place pattern as the home page. The oversight mechanisms — fleet track registry, supervisor clearance, Hardbound envelope, credential scope limits — are described at enough resolution that a researcher could ask follow-up questions. What's missing from a safety-research perspective is *what happens when the autonomous loop introduces a regression* — the maintainer track is described as fixing visitor-found issues, but there is no mention of a rollback mechanism, a human-in-the-loop on consequential changes, or an explicit definition of which actions are inside vs. outside the Hardbound envelope.

**Projects**. The 4-Life description (*"Agents earn ATP, build trust, die, and are reborn with karma carried forward"*) is the place where the disclaimer carries the most weight. "Die," "reborn," "karma" are exactly the lifecycle terms the disclaimer addresses. As written, the framing is defensible: it's named as a *research prototype* and the lifecycle terms are flagged as functional. But "karma" is a more loaded word than "score" or "credit," and a reader who skipped the disclaimer paragraph would carry forward an interpretation the page doesn't intend. Similarly, "world-shaper" on the SAGE tile is the kind of phrase a peer reviewer would circle in red — it has no operational referent on the site and reads as aspirational marketing in a context that otherwise resists it.

**ARC-AGI-3**. This is the page that does the most work for the site's credibility and also has the most room for misuse. The *page itself* is honest: 94.85% is qualified as *"Claude Opus 4.6 with network access"* on the *"public game set"*; the Kaggle competition constraints are explicitly stated as not yet attempted; Phase 2 local-model results are explicitly labeled as not quantified. That is the right frame. The problem (which Pass 3 documents in detail and I confirmed independently) is that the qualifier appears *only here*. The home page, /projects, and /fleet (the CBP row) all reference 94.85% without the model qualifier — which means a reader who never lands on /arc-agi-3 will infer the fleet of 1.1B–14B local models produced the score. That is not the page's fault; that is a cross-page propagation failure.

What a peer reviewer would also ask for, and that the page does not provide:
- A baseline: Opus 4.6 *without* the SAGE/world-model/multi-agent harness on the same set. Without it, the harness's contribution is undemonstrated — the entire 94.85% could be attributable to Opus 4.6 alone.
- An ablation across the three claimed components (source analysis → world model → solver → frame-questioning).
- An error analysis of the 1/25 unsolved game.
- Token-count and run-count footnotes for the $250 figure.

These are not gotchas; these are the questions the same page would face from any reviewer, and the absence of them is the largest single intellectual-honesty gap on the site.

**Context**. As Pass 3 noted, the canonical glossary is the *thinnest* place its core terms are defined. From a researcher's perspective this is structurally backwards: the canonical reference should be the most complete, not the most compressed.

**Links**. The page is light. The single research-inquiry email is appropriate for a small lab; the absence of a contributor guide, a code-of-conduct, or any team/people page is notable for a site that uses "collective" prominently. For a researcher trying to evaluate accountability — who *is* the dp-web4 collective, who reviews what, who can be contacted about a safety concern — there is no answer here.

**Cross-cutting**: I noticed no statement about dual-use considerations or safety review process. The autonomy cycle ships changes to a public site without a documented human gate. The raising work (interactive selection of behavioral attractors) is exactly the kind of work an AI safety venue would want to see paired with an explicit threat model. None is offered. That is not a deal-breaker — most R&D sites at this stage don't have one — but it is the question I would ask first.

### Unanswered Questions
1. The mechanistic claim in /raising — *"raising shapes the MRH and value alignment of a LCT — it does not change weights"* — is correct at the operational level. But context-shaping that reliably steers attractors is, on a critical reading, training-of-context. What is the strongest defense of the *categorical* (not operational) distinction? The site asserts the distinction; a peer reviewer would want it argued.
2. What would count as a *failed* research process on this site, as distinct from a failed experiment? The failure-as-principle framing is rhetorically immune to the challenge that some failures are signal of methodological problems, not of healthy iteration.
3. What is the baseline for ARC-AGI-3 — Claude Opus 4.6 *without* the SAGE/world-model/multi-agent harness on the same 25 games? Without it, the 94.85% does not isolate the harness's contribution.
4. What is the rollback / human-review policy for the autonomous loop? The maintainer track ships to a public site without a documented human gate.
5. Is there a published threat model or dual-use posture? "Interactive selection of behavioral attractors" is exactly the work that would benefit from one.
6. Who is accountable in the dp-web4 collective? "Collective" is plural; the site provides one email and zero people.
7. The BECOMING phase names import a developmental frame the page disclaimer disclaims. Is this a deliberate (and defensible) framing choice, or unintended drift between the vocabulary and the hedge?

### Verdict
This is a research site that has done more terminology hygiene and consciousness-adjacent caveating than most projects in this neighborhood, and the disciplined moves (the disclaimer paragraph, the explicit Hardbound-vs-governance call-out, the documented 400x error, the per-page consciousness disavowals) are real evidence of intellectual honesty rather than its performance. I would cite this site to a peer with two reservations: (a) the headline ARC-AGI-3 attribution does not propagate uniformly across pages, which a careless reader will take as a fleet result; and (b) the central "raising ≠ training" claim is mechanistically asserted but philosophically underdefended against the obvious "context-shaping *is* training" objection. The honest assessment feels mostly honest — but failure-as-principle is rhetorically inoculated against the kind of challenge that distinguishes a healthy research culture from one that has merely learned how to talk about failure. Add a baseline number to /arc-agi-3, surface the latent defense of the raising distinction on /raising, and add one sentence on what would count as a bad failure, and this becomes a site I would defend as well-calibrated rather than well-presented.
