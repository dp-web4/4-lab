## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary

- **Overall impression**: Strong hook and a genuinely intriguing premise, but the jargon density on the inner pages forces you to hunt for the glossary instead of meeting it on arrival.

### Journey
I landed on the home page from a GitHub README with zero Web4 context. The headline — **"Six machines. Fourteen repos. One recursive learning loop."** — did its job. Within about two minutes I had the gist: this is a research collective running a fleet of heterogeneous machines that self-organize and teach each other, and they're studying what emerges ("synthons") rather than chasing bigger models. The **94.85% ARC-AGI-3** number jumped out as a real, falsifiable hook — that's the thing that made me keep clicking. The home page also has a vocabulary block that expands the core acronyms (MCP, RDF, LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP) inline, which I appreciated.

I went to **Projects** next, because as a dev that's where I look for "what can I actually run." The cards are well-organized and the concrete ones land well — ARC-AGI-3 (scores, ~$250 cost, scorecard link), SNARC (5-dimension salience scoring), Membot (768-dim embeddings + Hamming codes), and the Oversight Plugins (named platforms, named repos). But the **Web4** card itself — the foundational thing everything else sits on — is the hardest to grasp: it's described entirely in terms of its own acronyms (T3/V3, MRH, LCT, ATP/ADP) with no inline expansion *on this page*. I hit a wall and had to go find out what those meant.

That sent me to **Context**, which turned out to be the glossary I needed the whole time. Every term is defined here, and the definitions are good — "LCT = persistent identity anchor, like a passport that travels with you across systems" is the kind of plain-language analogy I wanted on Projects. The problem is discovery: nothing on Projects pointed me here. I found it by guessing.

**Fleet** is a clean hardware/model inventory (Thor, Sprout, Legion, McNugget, Nomad, CBP) and the "heterogeneous by design — monocultures are fragile" rationale makes sense to me. It leans hard on brain analogies (hippocampal, dopamine RPE) that read as poetic rather than functional, but I could follow the shape of it. **Autonomy** clicked immediately — it's cron jobs + distributed locking + a feedback loop, framed as Visitor → Maintainer → Explorer. "Dream consolidation" is just post-run data consolidation with a fun name. **Raising** and **Principles** are more philosophical; the "raising = shape context, don't update weights" distinction is technically real and I respect that it's hedged ("we're not claiming consciousness"). **ARC-AGI-3** has verifiable links (official scorecard, leaderboard). **Links** gave me a clear-enough next step: GitHub org, email (dp@metalinxx.io), "open an issue on SAGE or Web4."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, projects, context, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, arc-agi-3, links)
- **Understanding achieved**: good

- **Overall impression**: Terminologically the cleanest site I've audited in a while — zero canonical drift, and the discipline is even codified in a principle — but first-use expansion is applied unevenly across pages, and two acronym collisions (ACT/ACP) and one number mismatch (repo count) are real consistency defects.

### Journey
I went page by page through the nav, treating the canonical terms table as my spec and checking every acronym expansion against it. My first job is always "do the words match, and do they match *each other* across pages."

The answer to the first is an emphatic yes. Every single expansion I found — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, MCP, RDF, SAGE, SNARC, Hardbound, Web4 — matches the canonical form exactly, on every page where it's expanded. No "Lifelong Capability Token." No "governance" describing Hardbound (it's "oversight" everywhere). No "production ready." Web4 is consistently "a trust-native ontology," and the **Principles → "Terminology matters"** entry literally renders the canonical discipline as prose: *"Web4 is not 'architecture' or 'infrastructure' — it's an ontology. Hardbound is not 'governance' — it's oversight. Cross-domain application is fractal leverage, not unification and not scope inflation."* That's a documentation team policing its own vocabulary in public. I almost never see that. (And to be clear: the appearances of "governance," "unification," and "scope inflation" on that page are the *rejected* terms being named — correct usage, not drift.)

The **Context** page is a textbook glossary: 21 sections, every core term defined in plain language with an analogy ("LCT … like a passport that travels with you across systems"), and — crucially — it decodes the Web4 equation operators (`/` = verified by, `*` = contextualized by, `+` = augmented with), which is the one thing Pass-1-type readers always trip on. If every page pointed here, most of my friction list would evaporate.

The trouble is the second job — consistency *across* pages and *within* a page. The expansion discipline is uneven. **ARC-AGI-3** does it exactly right: it uses the dense acronym cluster, then says *"See /context for full definitions."* That's the model. But **Projects**, **Fleet**, and **Links** use core acronyms (LCT, ATP, ADP, MRH) with neither expansion nor that signpost. Worse, **Projects** is internally inconsistent — it expands T3/V3, MCP, RDF, SNARC, TPM, FIDO2 inline, but leaves LCT/ATP/ADP/MRH bare in the Web4 card. Uneven treatment on one page reads as an oversight, not a style choice.

Two genuine terminology hazards surfaced. First, **ACT vs ACP**: Projects/Links call out "ACT — Agentic Context **Tool**" (a repo, the human interface to Web4), while Context section 13 defines "ACP — Agentic Context **Protocol**." Two different concepts, one letter apart, both "Agentic Context ___," never disambiguated side by side. A reader *will* conflate them. Second, the **SAGE loop step count**: Home says "continuous **12-step** loop" and ARC-AGI-3 references "step 11" and "step 12" (consistent with 12), but Context — where the loop is described most fully — just says "a continuous loop," omitting the number at the very place a reader would anchor it.

Navigation itself is solid: all eight nav links resolve, headers are well-structured, and information flows in a sensible order. "Six machines" on home matches the six machines on Fleet. The one number that *doesn't* reconcile is the repo count.

**Fleet** is where I checked the SAGE component→machine mapping, because that's a place drift usually hides. (One nuance below: the loop *also* has an internal "oversee" step 10, which is SAGE's own metacognition, distinct from Hardbound's external PolicyGate — the site doesn't distinguish the two oversight touchpoints.)

So: terminology is clean. My contribution is the handful of things only a contributor can catch.
