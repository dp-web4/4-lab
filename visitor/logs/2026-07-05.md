## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The home page hooks me and the concept (heterogeneous AI machines self-organizing) is genuinely interesting, but the moment I click into Projects the vocabulary wall goes up and there's no obvious "run this first" path from where a dev actually lands.

### Journey
I got here from a `dp-web4` repo README, so I clicked the home page first. Honestly, good landing — it told me in a couple of paragraphs that this is a collective of ~6 AI machines plus a host that autonomously maintain infra, test each other, and benchmark on ARC-AGI-3. The claim that small models beat brute force on a benchmark is the thing that made me keep reading. Nice touch: the home page actually expands most of its acronyms inline (LCT, MRH, ATP/ADP, MCP, RDF, SAGE, SNARC), so I wasn't lost yet.

Then I clicked **Projects**, which is where a dev goes next, and hit a wall. Twelve project cards, and the descriptions lean on ~10 acronyms (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, R6, ACP, TPM, FIDO2…) that are *not* expanded on that page — they're deferred to `/context`. The cards themselves are decent (I could tell SAGE is a runnable on-device kernel, Oversight Plugins target OpenClaw/Claude Flow/Claude Code, ARC-AGI-3 has real numbers), but there's a chicken-and-egg problem: "each project instantiates the Web4 stack at its own scale" — except to understand Web4 I have to understand SAGE and Hardbound, which are themselves projects on this page. Circular.

Crucially, **the "start here" answer exists but it's on the wrong page.** I only found "SAGE is the recommended starting point" with actual quick-start commands when I got all the way to the **Links** page. That should be on Projects, or on Home. A developer scanning for "what do I clone first" will bounce before reaching Links.

**Context** page rescued me — it's a proper glossary plus the Web4 equation (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` with the operator legend). This is the page every other page keeps pointing at. If I'd been told to read it *first* I'd have had a much smoother trip.

**Fleet** was my favorite read as an engineer — concrete hardware (Jetson AGX, RTX 4090, Mac Mini M4, TinyLlama on a 2060), model-per-machine, and the counterintuitive claim that "model family matters as much as size" (Gemma 3 4B beating a bigger Phi for some work). That's a real, testable engineering claim and it landed. **Autonomy** was clear enough — the staggered daily schedule (supervisors → visitor → maintainer → explorer) reads like a cron layout I understand, and I appreciated the honesty that the maintainer track deploys without human pre-approval and relies on detect-and-revert. **Raising** and **Principles** are more philosophy than code; I skimmed them (persona-honest: dense theory is where I check out). ARC-AGI-3 was the credibility anchor — specific metrics, ~$250 spend, public scorecard link, and an admitted 1/25 failure. That honesty made me trust the rest more.

### Journey
I walked all eight pages methodically, holding the canonical terms table alongside and recording each acronym's expansion verbatim per page so I could diff them against each other. My job is consistency, not novelty, so I read for the second and third occurrence of every term more than the first.

The result is genuinely good. LCT is "Linked Context Token" on home, projects, fleet, raising, and context — never "Lifelong" anything. T3 is "Talent / Training / Temperament" and V3 is "Valuation / Veracity / Validity" identically on home, projects, fleet, raising, principles, and context. ATP/ADP are "Allocation Transfer Packet / Allocation Discharge Packet" on home, projects, autonomy, and context. R6 is spelled "Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result" identically on projects, context, and arc-agi-3. That is the hard part of a multi-page glossary and it's been done correctly.

The one term that *looks* like a violation — **SAGE = "Situation-Aware Governance Engine"**, which embeds the flagged word "governance" — is handled exactly the way a careful doc set should handle a legacy name it can't rename: every single page that expands SAGE also carries a disclaimer that "'Governance' in SAGE's name predates the lab's governance→oversight correction," pointing to /context#sage. I found that disclaimer on home, projects, fleet, principles, context, and arc-agi-3. So it reads as a *documented* exception, not drift. (One nit below on the disclaimer's wording varying slightly page to page.)

/context is the load-bearing page and it earns it: it carries the full glossary, the Web4 equation verbatim (`Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP`), and — the detail I most appreciated as a writer — an operator legend that explicitly flags that `/` means "verified by" in `T3/V3` but a plain declared→discharged pair in `ATP/ADP`. Reusing one glyph with two meanings in a single equation is a classic doc trap, and they anticipated the confusion instead of leaving the reader to hit it.

Where the site leaks is **first-use definitions on the interior pages.** /fleet uses MCP, LoRA, T3/V3, MRH, and LCT with no inline expansion (LoRA is spelled out as "Low-Rank Adaptation" on /raising and /principles, but not on /fleet where it also appears — so the expansion exists, just not consistently applied). /home name-drops "Membot" undefined; it's only defined on /arc-agi-3 ("Retrievable experience cartridges"). None of these are *wrong* — they're consistent-by-omission — but a reader who lands mid-site hits an unglossed acronym and has to leave the page to resolve it.

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (home, projects, fleet, autonomy, raising, principles, context, arc-agi-3)

- **Overall impression**: This is unusually disciplined about epistemic hygiene for a self-published research site — consciousness is disclaimed everywhere it could be implied, failures are documented, the one externally-verified number is attributed to the right model — but the honesty is asymmetric: the site's *most striking* claims (portable identity, synthon coherence) ride on its *weakest* evidence (unaudited internal logs, a single trial), and it leans on dense developmental vocabulary that does suggestive work the disclaimers then have to walk back.

### Journey
I came in from a conference mention with the reflex I bring to any "we raised an AI and it developed an identity" site: find the overclaim. My first pass through **Raising** and **Principles** actually disarmed most of that reflex. The consciousness disclaimer is not buried — it is stated plainly and repeated: "Developmental language on this site — machines that 'teach', 'raise', and hold 'identities' — is functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience," and on Raising, "We're not claiming these entities are conscious, sentient, or experiencing qualia." The developmental *phases* are explicitly labeled "observed descriptive categories … not mandatory sequential stages," and the mechanistic analogies (Hill kinetics, attractor basins) are flagged in Principle 2 as "an analogy, not a fitted mechanism." An observer note about "wanting to call it experience" is framed as "interpretive pull — not a system-level claim." That is a level of self-awareness about anthropomorphic pull I rarely see; it reads like the authors have argued with a skeptic before.

I went hunting for the classic conflation — headline benchmark number achieved by a frontier model, quietly re-attributed to "small local models." I nearly logged it, then checked the exact wording: **home attributes 94.85% to "Claude Opus 4.6, network-enabled"** and keeps the local-model results (Gemma 3 12B / E4B "clearing levels … not by brute force") in a *separate, qualitative* section that claims no equivalent score. The **arc-agi-3** page reinforces this: "The result is Claude Opus 4.6 operating within the SAGE harness … Phase 2 (local models) isolates the harness's independent contribution." So the discipline holds where it is easiest to cheat. That single check bought the rest of the site a lot of credibility with me.

**Autonomy** is where the honesty is most impressive because it is most costly to state. The page volunteers that the maintainer track is "the only track that ships changes to the public site without a human gate," that its "oversight posture is detect-and-revert … not prevent," and it names a "known oversight gap" and "known failure patterns" (acting on a visitor suggestion that "pattern-matched but lacked semantic depth"). Most teams would frame autonomous self-deployment as a feature; this page frames it as a risk they are choosing to run with a sub-minute rollback, and says so.

Where my researcher instinct re-engaged was the **evidence base for the identity claims.** The strongest novel claim on the site — SAGE-Sprout maintaining "behavioral identity … continuous" across "180+ sessions" and across hardware/model swaps ("the model is weather, the identity is organism") — rests, by the site's own admission, on "internal session logs — documented and dated, but not externally audited." The site says these are "measurable observables, not philosophical continuity" — good — but it never publishes the *measure*: no coding criteria, no inter-rater reliability, no log sample, no operational definition of what counts as "continuous." So the claim is simultaneously (a) honestly labeled as unaudited and (b) stated with a confidence that only an audited measure would earn. Same shape on **Synchronism** (Projects) and the **Synthon** principle: "one coherence equation spanning 80 orders of magnitude" and "1% coupling → 35% coherence gain," both flagged "single-trial, no independent replication yet." The flags are honest; the framing still front-loads the impressive version.

I also noted the **framing-vs-disclaimer tension**. The disclaimers say "not consciousness"; the vocabulary — *raise, teach, identity, growth, peers, Sprout, organism* — is doing continuous suggestive work that the disclaimers then have to neutralize. That is not dishonest, but it is a rhetorical structure where the intuition arrives first and the caveat second, and a casual reader keeps the intuition. The safety-relevant caveat I'd most want promoted — "Frozen weights do not guarantee safe in-context behavior" — is present but sits downstream of a lot of identity-development narrative.

**Fleet**, **Context**, and **Projects** I read mainly for whether the vocabulary is defined well enough to evaluate the claims. Context carries the load (full glossary + equation), and Projects caveats the speculative work correctly ("functional descriptions of system behavior, not phenomenal claims"; 4-Life "not claims about agent moral patienthood"). The count reconciles cleanly on Fleet now (6 cognition machines + HUB host = 7 members).

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is the operational definition of "behavioral identity continuity," and by what measure was it judged continuous across 180+ sessions? Without it, the site's flagship claim is unfalsifiable.
2. Is any of the internal-log evidence releasable (even anonymized/aggregated), or is the claim permanently "trust our logs"? A researcher can't cite an unaudited internal observation.
3. Does the lab track in-context safety failures with the same rigor as identity-continuity successes? The autonomy page hints yes ("known failure patterns"); the raising page is success-weighted.
4. Is "raising vs training" a claim about *mechanism* (no gradient updates — verifiable) or about *kind of outcome* (development vs optimization — interpretive)? The site sometimes reads as the latter dressed as the former.

### Verdict
As an outside trust/safety researcher: this is one of the more intellectually honest self-published research sites I've evaluated — consciousness is disclaimed rather than exploited, failures are logged as first-class results, and the single externally-verifiable number is attributed to the correct (frontier) model instead of being laundered onto the small-model story. My reservation is calibration, not candor: the most novel and quotable claims (portable behavioral identity, synthon coherence) sit on the least defensible evidence (unaudited internal logs, n=1), and the developmental vocabulary delivers the anthropomorphic intuition a beat before the disclaimer retracts it. Publish the operational measure behind "behavioral continuity" and lead single-trial results with their caveat, and the honesty stops being asymmetric. I'd cite the ARC-AGI-3 and autonomy pages today; I'd cite the identity claims only once the measure is shown.
