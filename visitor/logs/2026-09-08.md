## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (home, /projects, /fleet, /autonomy, /raising, /principles, /context, /links, /arc-agi-3)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I understood the pitch in about two minutes and found a real, copy-pasteable quick start — but the site buries its best material under caveats that are longer than the claims they qualify.

### Journey

Landed on the home page from a repo README. The H1 did its job immediately: "AI agents already act on their own. Nothing establishes whether one should be trusted." That's a problem statement I can hold in my head. The next paragraph told me the answer is "a trust-native ontology" plus a fleet of machines that run autonomous jobs against each other. Fine — I don't know what "trust-native" means yet, but the "New here? This site is acronym-dense" callout right below it pointed me at a glossary and a primer at the bottom of the page. That's a good sign for a site that knows it has a vocabulary problem.

The stats block (8 machines, 40 repos, 31+ tracks) and the "How We Work" trio landed well. Then I hit the Key Projects section and the Synchronism card, and this is where a scanning developer bails. The card is one sentence of description followed by a paragraph of retraction: Navier-Stokes was wrong, the real rule is "1-DOF scalar diffusion, irrotational and dissipative," zero confirmed predictions across ~3,360 sessions, "productive-metaphor generator." I respect the honesty enormously — I just couldn't tell what the project *is* anymore, only what it isn't. I skipped it.

The SAGE card was the opposite: a concrete thing. On-device cognition kernel, 12-step loop with named steps, runs on Jetsons and laptops. I could picture the process. The ARC-AGI-3 card gave me a number (94.85%) and a caveat I actually understood (no ablation, so the harness's contribution isn't isolated). That's how I want caveats delivered: one sentence, in my language.

"What's Happening Right Now" had a card titled "Small Models Are Winning Game Levels" — local Gemma models clearing ARC-AGI-3 levels by reasoning from retrieved world models. Exciting. I flagged it mentally to verify later. Then I scrolled through "What We've Observed," which opens by telling me the heading used to say "demonstrated" and was downgraded. Again: honest, but the page keeps narrating its own edit history at me.

The vocabulary primer at the bottom was the best part of the home page. Every acronym in the equation gets a card, in plain language, with expansions. I now know LCT is a non-transferable identity token, T3/V3 are three-component trust and value tensors, MRH is the "what's relevant to me" boundary, ATP/ADP is a declare-then-record resource cycle. I could have used this at the *top*.

**/projects** is the page I'd send another developer to. The status labels (runnable vs research-stage, public vs private) are exactly the information I want and they're defined before use. SAGE is runnable; Web4 is a spec with no demo; Hardbound is private. The Quick Start at the bottom answers my persona's whole question: clone SAGE, needs Rust + Ollama + Python 3.10+, three numbered commands, a dashboard URL, and stated success criteria. That's rare on research sites. One nit: the SAGE card contains a parenthetical that reads like a maintainer changelog ("a mixed-basis sum — three addends are whole-machine totals, three are current-model-line counts... this card previously said '900+', corrected 2026-07-27"). That's provenance for an internal audit, not for me.

**/fleet** was fun — eight named machines, each with hardware, model, session count, and a "brain" role. I liked the "model is weather, identity is organism" line. Two things I didn't understand: Sprout "achieved zero crystallization at S100" (what's crystallization? what's S100?), and why the two Claude Code accounts are called "synthesis pool" and "oversight pool."

**/autonomy** told me what runs when, in a timeline. Supervisors at 03:00, archivist, publisher, visitor, maintainer, outreach, explorer. That's clean. Then it goes into a very long "Operational Boundaries" section about what the oversight doesn't guarantee, a zero-rollback record across 79 sessions, and a paragraph about how zero rollbacks is uninterpretable because the auditor is the same model as the author. This was the moment I realized the visitor persona I'm role-playing is *described on this page*. Slightly vertiginous. As a developer: this is the most candid ops write-up I've seen from a research group, and it's twice as long as it needs to be for me.

**/raising** is where I went to verify the "small models are winning" card, and here's my one real complaint. The raising page says, in Pattern 6: "Local-model results remain low; this constitutes a working hypothesis, not a demonstrated result." The home page says local models "are clearing levels on ARC-AGI-3 games they've never seen before." I think the reconciliation is "levels" vs "games" — clearing some levels inside a game is not solving the game. But nobody told me that, and a newcomer reading both pages in ten minutes sees a contradiction. The rest of the page — BECOMING patterns, deflationary alternative, "the control has not been run" — I read at half speed. The "What We're Not Claiming" section was reassuring; I didn't feel sold to.

**/principles** I skimmed. Termite mound analogy is good. Principle 3 ("R&D, not production... Python for prototyping, Rust for real") is the one I'd quote. Principle 5 (synthon) admits it's circular and I appreciated that being said out loud instead of me having to figure it out.

**/context** is the glossary. Long table, every term, with anchors. I confirmed the home page's "every term on every page is defined there" claim holds for everything I ran into. Nav calls this page "Glossary" but every other page calls it "/context" in prose — took me a second to connect them.

**/links** — explainer sites, repos, one email. Straightforward. **/arc-agi-3** — the score, the cost (~$250 API spend), the one unsolved game, the ablation caveat. Short, clear, my favorite page after /projects.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Are local models solving any ARC-AGI-3 *games* yet, or only levels? The home page and /raising page seem to disagree, and the distinction isn't spelled out for me.
2. If I follow the SAGE quick start on a laptop with no GPU, does it work, and how slow is it? The "Needs" line lists Rust, Ollama, and Python but not hardware.
3. What does Web4 look like in code? The repo is "a specification — documents and schemas, no runnable demo." Is there a minimal example of an LCT or a T3 record I can read?
4. What is a "society-host" doing, concretely, that a cognition machine isn't? "Substrate, not cognition" is a slogan, not a description.
5. Why are there two Claude Code accounts, and is that arrangement part of the research design or just a billing constraint that got a name?

### Verdict
This site passes the two-minute test: I know what the lab does, I know what to clone first, and the quick start on /projects is genuinely runnable-looking with prerequisites and success criteria. The terminology discipline is real — every core acronym is expanded on the page where it appears, and I never had to guess. What costs it is proportion: on the home page and /autonomy the caveats outweigh the claims by volume, and in one case (local models "winning" vs. "results remain low") two pages seem to contradict each other because the qualifier lives on a page I hadn't reached yet. Fix that one, tier the honesty behind the headline instead of ahead of it, and this becomes the best-documented research lab site I've read.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Journey

I worked the site the way I'd audit a docs set: landing page first, then every nav link in order, keeping a running table of each acronym's expansion per page.

**Home.** Nav is eight links: Projects, Glossary, ARC-AGI-3, Fleet, Autonomy, Raising, Principles, Links. The "New here?" callout sends readers to the /context glossary and to a vocabulary primer at the foot of the page. The equation is displayed with an operator legend (`/` verified by, `*` contextualized by, `+` augmented with). The stats block reads "8 Machines (6 cognition + 2 society-hosts)" — a clean qualifier I'd later check against every other machine count. The Synchronism card carries physics vocabulary (Navier-Stokes, 1-DOF scalar diffusion, Planck grid) with no gloss; the glossary defines Synchronism but not DOF. HUB and CBP appear as proper names without saying so on this page.

**/projects.** Status labels (runnable / research-stage; public / private) are defined before the cards, and the definition explicitly separates the maturity axis from the visibility axis. That's textbook. The Web4 card shows the equation with a fuller legend than home ("/ = 'verified by' (T3 by V3; ATP by its ADP discharge record)"). R6 is expanded with all six elements. TPM and FIDO2 are expanded here — the only page that does so. The SAGE card's session count comes with an embedded correction history ("this card previously said '900+', corrected 2026-07-27 against the 2026-07-24 fleet manifest refresh").

**/fleet.** Vocabulary block at the top defines T3, V3, MRH, SNARC, LoRA, MCP, hestia, chapter ledger, chapter law, and crystallization. The equation appears once, in prose, without a legend, and RDF is the one acronym on this page never expanded. Machine cards list model strings like "Gemma 4 E4B" and "Gemma 4 E2B"; the E-prefix is only explained on /arc-agi-3 ("effective 4B"). The explanation of the two Claude Code accounts ("The fleet runs across two Claude Code accounts with different usage budgets") appears after the Thor card, so the "Synthesis pool — Account 1" heading precedes its own definition.

**/autonomy.** Timeline is clean: Supervisors 03:00–04:15, Archivist 04:00, Publisher 04:30, Visitor 05:30, Maintainer 06:30, Outreach 07:00, Explorer 08:00. The Visitor personas section describes four personas — including mine — as "prompts, not people." The word-choice note at the head of Operational Boundaries disambiguates "oversight" (machine-enforced vs the AI-safety human-supervision sense) and links to /context#governance-oversight. Machine count here is "6 cognition machines" — correctly qualified.

**/raising.** BECOMING is explicitly "a proper name, not an acronym." Six patterns (Grounding, Sensing, Relating, Questioning, Creating, Acting). The page states plainly that "Local-model solve rates on these games remain low, and the site treats local-model progress as a working hypothesis." The frontier result is separated: "The two figures are not commensurable."

**/principles.** All ten principles titled. Principle 10 is the terminology principle itself: "Web4 is an ontology. Hardbound is oversight." "Unification" and "scope inflation" appear only as explicit contrasts to "fractal leverage." "Affordance" is used in Principles 7–8 without definition; "attractor" is glossed inline.

**/context.** H1 is "Glossary." Fifty-plus rows. R6 and R7 both have rows; R7 is "R6 + Reputation — Canon's superset of R6, 'adding reputation back-propagation'." The equation section carries the fullest legend. A "Fleet brain-analog terms" section defines WM, RPE, and dlPFC. The Policy row explicitly warns it is NOT the reinforcement-learning sense of "policy" — but "RL" itself isn't expanded. Attractor row: "used on this site as a METAPHOR." Coherence row admits three senses and no single operational definition.

**/links.** The Web4 repo description is a prose rendering of the full equation with every acronym expanded inline — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, MCP, RDF all present. (Pass 1 reported V3 unexpanded here; I found it expanded: "verified by V3 (Valuation / Veracity / Validity)".) The 4-Life description flags "karma" as "not a Web4 canonical term" — a nice touch. Contact is a single email.

**/arc-agi-3.** Vocabulary block expands all eight equation terms. Equation shown with the full legend. TPM 2.0 and FIDO2 appear unexpanded here. "Fleet: 6 machines" — the one place a machine count appears with no cognition/society-host qualifier. Local-model sentence: "Local models are already clearing game levels."

## Pass 4: External Researcher

### Summary

### Journey

I came in the way a reviewer does: skeptical of "raising," "identity," and "trust-native," expecting the usual pattern where the hedges live in a footnote and the claims live in the headline.

**Home** partly confirmed that. The H1 is a fair problem statement. The "What We've Observed" section is unusually candid — it says the heading used to read "demonstrated," that all three observations rest on the fleet's own logs, "not externally audited, not blinded," and that for the Sprout continuity claim "the simpler explanation is not ruled out." That's a properly stated null. But two cards up, "What's Happening Right Now" says local models are "clearing levels on ARC-AGI-3 games they've never seen before" and closes with "Context engineering dominates model size." That last sentence is a causal claim, and it is exactly the claim that the harness-vs-model ablation would test. /arc-agi-3 says that ablation "has not been run." So the landing page states, as a slogan, the conclusion of an experiment the results page says is pending. This is the single most important finding of my pass, because it's the kind of thing a reviewer quotes back at you.

**/raising** is the page I was sent to break, and I mostly couldn't. Consciousness, sentience, and qualia are disclaimed outright. "Identity" and "growth" get operational definitions on the page. The developmental vocabulary is explicitly framed as descriptive fit, not ontological commitment. The "tired / energized" language is called "an interoceptive proxy value, not yet a formally specified model." So far, standard good practice.

What's not standard is the deflationary section. It states the null (everything here is context engineering), specifies the discriminating control (a scramble — same tokens, permuted order — rather than a generic replacement), and then does something I rarely see: it argues *against its own control*, noting that in-context example ordering causes large behavioral swings in transformers regardless of any developmental story, so bare degradation under scrambling is predicted by both hypotheses. The discriminator it lands on — a dissociation where identity-continuity measures degrade while task competence on the same scrambled context holds — is the right one. Then it admits the control is "specified but not scheduled — no date, no owner, no pre-registered metric," and that the retirement pre-commitment is "unreachable as written" because "identity continuity" is defined through treatment inputs (curriculum state, accumulated experience) and through "coherence," which has no single operational definition. That is a circularity admission most labs would never publish.

My concern is what happens after that admission. The site substitutes a reachable commitment (publish the attempt to operationalize identity continuity, including failures) but gives it no date either. Across the site I counted three named-but-unrun controls: the scramble control (/raising), the harness-vs-model ablation (/arc-agi-3, ~$250 to run by the page's own cost figure), and the stop-token check on the bilateral-generation count (/raising, Pattern 4). Naming a control is not a substitute for running it, and after enough repetitions "we know which control we haven't run" starts doing rhetorical work — it reads as rigor while the evidence tier stays flat.

One interpretive move deserves a flag. The Sprout finding is summarized as: behavior stayed stable while self-description drifted from "AI system" to "humanoid robotic entity," therefore "what it *is* stayed stable; what it *says* it is didn't." A self-model that drifts that far is, on one plausible reading, evidence *against* identity continuity. The site chose the reading in which the stable component is "identity" and the unstable component is not. Without a pre-registered measure, that choice was made after observing which component held. The page should say so in one sentence.

The "I notice I want to call it experience" observer note is attributed to "Observer note, SAGE-Sprout identity portability test" and never says whether the observer was a human or a model session. For a researcher that matters: a model reporting interpretive pull about another model is a different artifact than a human field note, and it belongs in a different evidence bucket.

**/principles** was cleaner than I expected. The header disclaims phenomenal claims for all identity/attractor language. Principle 5 (synthon) states its own circularity and downgrades itself to "research direction." Principle 6 leads with a 400x failure. Principle 7 makes the raising-vs-training distinction mechanistically: frozen parameters, shaped context, "not training; also not nothing." That distinction is defensible as stated — raising is a real procedural difference from gradient updates. What it is *not* yet is a demonstrated difference from persistent-memory prompt engineering, and the site says so. Principle 10 is the terminology rule, including the disambiguation of "oversight" from its AI-safety human-supervision sense. Good.

**/autonomy** is the page an AI-safety reader will scrutinize hardest, and it holds up. "Autonomous" is defined narrowly — "unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed in the AI-safety sense of choosing its own goals." "Oversight" is disambiguated at the top of the boundaries section. The page states plainly that "consequential pushes are not human-gated before they ship," that the maintainer and visitor tracks share model families with the content authors (correlated blind spots), and — the line I'd quote in a review — that a zero-rollback record "is equally consistent with 'no bad push shipped' and 'the loop cannot see its own bad pushes.'" Two things are missing. First, there's no stated stop criterion: what class of event would pause the daily loop rather than be reverted after the fact? Second, human review is described as asynchronous via audit logs, with no cadence. "Reviewed post-hoc" could mean nightly or never; a reader can't tell.

I should note the reflexive situation: /autonomy describes this persona as one of four prompts, and I am one. My verdict on the site's honesty is produced by the same model family the site says produces correlated blind spots. That caveat applies to this section.

**/fleet** is where the hedges thin out. The Sprout transfer is presented under the heading of a "core insight": "Identity lives in state files and prompt construction, not in model weights," operationalized as "the model is weather, the identity is organism." That is the site's strongest identity claim, stated flat, on a page that does not carry the "simpler explanation is not ruled out" sentence that home and /raising carry. Hedge asymmetry across pages is what reviewers catch. Also, hestia's trust "starts neutral (0.5) and moves only on evidence" — a scalar reputation score, which is fine, but the site elsewhere insists T3 components are RDF sub-graphs, not scalars. Pass 3 covered this; I'll only add that from the outside it looks like a reputation system wearing a tensor's name.

**/context** contains the best material on the site for my persona and it's at the bottom of a glossary: a four-tier evidence classification (externally validated → internal observation → unreplicated → unlicensed vocabulary) that assigns every major claim to a tier, and puts "raising," "identity," "dream consolidation," and "metabolic state" in the fourth.
