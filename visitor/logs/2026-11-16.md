## Pass 1: Curious Developer

*Method note: this session had no WebFetch tool, so the live site was read with `curl` and parsed to text (HTML, not a summarizer). No local source was read. Pages: the 7 front pages in the nav, plus `/notebook/projects` (the page a developer goes to next), `/notebook/context` and `/notebook/links` for spot checks. Outbound links and registry versions were checked live.*

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (7 front + 3 notebook)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The front explains in two minutes what the lab does: governance tools for AI agents, plus "beings" as the research bet. But getting from "this looks interesting" to "I ran something" takes four hops and ~6,500 words of notebook text.

### Journey
**Home.** Clear pitch: "AI agents are becoming actors in the world. We are building how they earn trust." Four project cards with status labels (Web4 RUNNING, Hestia RUNNING, Hub RUNNING, SAGE RESEARCH). Web4 is defined inline as "a shared vocabulary, or ontology", which is the right amount of theory for me. I read RUNNING as "I can run this". Home doesn't say what the labels mean. The legend is only at the bottom of /projects, where RUNNING means "in daily use on our own machines". "Agents do the work… run daily tracks" uses "tracks" without explaining it here; /principles does.

**/projects.** Good structure: governance tools, then AI beings, then theory. Hardbound is "hardware-bound oversight… built by Metalinxx". Each card links to a repo. As a developer I wanted a "try it" link. The cards go to GitHub, and the page's one notebook link goes to `/notebook/projects`, not to its `#quick-start` anchor.

**/fleet.** A table of eight named machines (Jetson Thor and Orin Nano, a 4090 laptop, a Mac mini and others). Nice and concrete. Then "Four machines do the heavy work… Two review, plan and coordinate. Two host the community the rest belong to." I tried to map that onto the table. Nomad and CBP review and coordinate. HUB and pub must be the "two host" pair, yet the table says pub is "Staging for the first public Hub", not the host of this community. By elimination the four heavy machines include Sprout, an Orin Nano "edge device: a being with a camera and microphone", which doesn't read like "running large experiments".

**/raising.** The clearest page on the site. "The model is the weather, the being is the organism" works for me. "Not yet shown: that this continuity is more than well-kept memory files fed back to a model" is exactly the skeptical question I'd ask, and they ask it first. Two things were unclear: who the "tutor" and the "mentor" are (human? another model?), and SAGE is never expanded on any front page.

**Cross-page snag.** The three front pages describe how SAGE beings act with three different strengths:
- Home: consequential actions "go through governed tools".
- /projects: they "are meant to go through governed tools".
- /raising: a being "acts in the world only through a small set of governed tools… Every one of those actions is checked against rules before it happens".

The notebook SAGE card quotes upstream ("Consequential acts are intended to be governed and witnessed") and says outright that the diagram is "not a statement that every act a bein[g]…". So /projects matches the evidence, and Home and /raising state more than it.

**/principles, /links, /notebook.** /principles reads well. "Agreement between different systems means more than agreement within one" is a good line. On /links, "4-gov · governance" told me nothing. Its own `<title>` and meta say it's a Metalinxx explainer of Web4 ("Computable Accountability"). `/notebook/links` describes it properly, but the front label doesn't. /links lists Web4, Hestia and SAGE code but not Hub, which is the one RUNNING project without its own repo line there. /notebook explains the two layers well.

**/notebook/projects (trying to run something).** The page is 7,229 words. Near the top it says "Commands… are in the Quick start at the bottom of this page", which helps, and the in-page link works. Before you get there, the Web4 card is one paragraph of roughly 700 words. Its first "sentence" runs the whole equation through nested parentheticals and em-dash asides. I gave up on it and jumped to Quick start, which is good: honest (each step says what was and wasn't run), explicit about the SAGE `init` vs Rust-daemon disagreement, and it warns about the fail-open `matches` bug in `web4-policy` 0.1.0.

**Checks I could run as a developer:**
- All 13 outbound links from the front pages return 200: GitHub org, web4, web4/hub tree, hestia, SAGE, the SWE-SAGE write-up, the SAGE site, the Synchronism site, 4-gov.org, PyPI, crates.io.
- Registry claims hold exactly via the APIs: web4-core 0.4.0 on crates.io (2026-10-15), 0.3.0 on PyPI; web4-policy has one version, 0.1.0; web4-trust-core 0.3.0.
- Hestia v0.3.0 is still "Latest" (`gh release list`). Main is now **118** commits ahead of v0.3.0; the notebook's "64 commits… counted 2026-11-04" is dated and so not false, just aged. 4-hub still has no releases.
- In a fresh `/tmp` venv, `pip install web4-core web4-trust` succeeded. `import web4_core` gives 0.3.0 and exposes PyLct, PyKeyPair, PyT3, PyV3, PyCoherence, PyInMemoryLedger and others, matching the Quick start's list. `web4_trust` imports as **0.2.0**; the Quick start doesn't give its version (no error, just unstated).
- I tried to run the PyPI listing's first usage block. This machine's own Hestia gate refused the interpreter call (scope request opened, not routed around). So that example was **not re-run today**; the notebook's 2026-11-09 run stands untested by this pass, not refuted.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

I took the page list from the live nav (7 front pages) and the live `/notebook` index (9 pages). No local source was read. All counts below come from scripts run over that HTML and text. "First use" means the first time the token appears in the page's rendered text.*

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (7 front + 9 notebook) + `/llms.txt`
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Web4 acronym expansions are fully consistent across every page that uses them, and the site's internal links are clean. The weak points are structural: a front pointer whose target disclaims half of it, a capitalization rule that covers two of the three spellings in use, and a glossary "at a glance" table that isn't.

### Journey
I started on Home and walked the nav: Projects, Fleet, Raising, How we work, Notebook, Links. Then I opened all nine pages listed on `/notebook`. The front is tight: 111–373 words a page, one H1 each, no skipped heading levels. None of the Web4 acronyms (LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, R6, R7) appears on any front page, so expansion drift there is impossible by construction. Home defines Web4 inline as "a shared vocabulary, or ontology".

The notebook is a different scale: about 62,000 words across nine pages. The Glossary (17,230 words) and Raising (15,715) are each longer than the whole front put together many times over. I ran four mechanical checks before reading for sense:

1. **Every acronym expansion on every page**, grepped against the canonical table: details in the audit below. Zero drift.
2. **First use vs first expansion per page, and per card on `/notebook/projects`**: the page's own note promises "each card expands an acronym where that card first uses it". The Web4, Hestia, Hardbound and ACT cards keep that promise. The Hub card uses "PRD" inside a quoted phrase ("not implemented by this PRD") and a file name before it expands it. The SAGE card uses "ARC Prize" with no expansion. Both are minor.
3. **Every internal `href` and `#fragment`** across the 16 pages, including the `/context`, `/glossary`, `/autonomy` and `/arc-agi-3` redirects, resolves to an existing page and id. I found no broken anchors and no duplicate ids. The Glossary has 84 ids.
4. **Every "verified by" gloss** (14 occurrences on 5 pages): after the 2026-11-15 fix they all say the same thing. The T3/V3 reading is upstream's (whitepaper §3, web4-standard GLOSSARY.md), it is not in CANONICAL_TERMS_v1, and the ATP/ADP half and the feedback reading are the site's own. I found no page still calling the whole legend "the site's gloss".

Then I read for sense, as a writer would.

**Front "How we work" → notebook Principles.** The front closes: "The reasoning behind each principle, and the daily tracks in detail, are in the notebook". I followed the link. The notebook page's own map, "The front's six principles, mapped to this page" (recorded 2026-10-13), says three of the six have **"No reasoning on this page yet"**: *Rules before actions*, *We use what we build*, and *Different on purpose* ("the nearest is Principle 5"). The map is honest. It also records practice that cuts against two of those three: pushes are detect-and-revert, not checked before they act, and all four visitor personas run on one model family. But the front sentence pointing there is half false, and it has been since the map was written 34 days ago. This is a front fact, so it goes to dp.

**Capitalization rules.** The Glossary makes capitalization meaningful in two places. For hestia, lowercase is the trust component and capitalized is the daemon. For HUB, all-caps is the machine and capitalized is the software. I tested both against the other eight notebook pages.
- *hestia*: the rule holds. Every lowercase prose use I found is a defined component reference, a command (`hestia init`), a file path, or a quotation (dp's "retire the whole hestia thread"). The one borderline case is the Glossary's own "The ladder's source is hestia docs/PRD_ASSURANCE.md". That is a repo path, written without the word "repo" that the hestia row requires.
- *HUB / Hub*: no page uses HUB for the software. But **a third spelling, lowercase "hub", appears 37 times in prose on 5 notebook pages**: 14 on Fleet, 10 on Glossary, 10 on Projects, 2 on Autonomy, 1 on Links. Examples: "the fleet's hub serves a read-only MRH projection", "a hub member id", "hosts the society (hub daemon, chapter ledger)", "legion-sage on the hub". Most uses read naturally as "one running instance of Hub". But the Glossary defines only HUB and Hub. After the hestia rule a careful reader expects case to carry meaning, and "the hub" on the Fleet page could be either the machine or the instance. The HUB row itself says HUB is "running the Web4 hub daemon", using the undefined third spelling inside the definition of the other two.

**Glossary at a glance.** The heading promises a scannable table, and the page already admits it no longer is one ("a median of about 90 words a cell… until that date this sentence promised one-line definitions"). I measured it again: 78 rows, median 88.5 words per definition cell, 24 cells over 150 words. The LCT cell is 607 words, Substrate 400, V3 393, MRH 381. 17 cells carry correction history ("until 2026-…", "fixed", "row added"). In the hestia row, the definition of the component starts only in the third sentence, after the case rule and a 10-26 correction note. The expansion column is the part that can be scanned, and it is correct. The definition column has become a second narrative section.

**Page names.** The Autonomy page has three names: "Autonomy" (index and nav), "Autonomy — The Daily Cycle" (`<title>`) and "Autonomous Cycles" (H1). The Glossary is "Glossary" in the index, `<title>` and H1. In notebook prose, though, it is referred to as "/context" 35 times and as "/notebook/context" 4 times, which carries the old URL as the page's name. Projects is "Projects" in the index but "The Ecosystem" in its H1. Each of these is minor. Together they mean a reader can't match a cross-reference to a page title.

**/llms.txt.** I checked the sizes it states against the HTML: Glossary ~109K characters (stated "about 108,000"), Projects 45.9K ("46,000"), Fleet 57.7K ("58,000"), Raising 96.2K ("96,000"), and Quick start at character 35,850 ("about 36,000"). All match.
