## Pass 1: Curious Developer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understand what this lab believes and how it's organized, but after nine pages I still can't see the software do anything.

### Journey

Landed on `/` from a repo README. Headline: **"Eight machines. Forty original repos. One recursive learning loop."** My first reaction is that this tells me the *size* of the thing before it tells me what it is. The very next paragraph does better — "builds a trust-native ontology — a shared vocabulary and trust model for AI agents, in the knowledge-representation sense" — and the problem statement underneath is the sentence that actually hooked me: *"AI agents already act on their own — browsing, transacting, coordinating with other agents — and there is no general way to establish whether one will behave appropriately in a given context."* That's a problem I recognize from my own work. It should be higher than the machine count.

Home is dense but well-glossed. Every acronym I hit — LCT, T3, V3, MRH, ATP, ADP, SAGE, SNARC, MCP, RDF — had an expansion sitting right next to it. I went looking for the usual failure mode (coined term dropped in cold) and mostly didn't find it. "trust-native" is glossed in the same sentence it appears in; "Hardbound" gets "(hardware-bound oversight suite)"; "raise/raising" is used in the first paragraph before it's explained, but there's an explicit hedge — *"machines that 'teach', 'raise', and hold 'identities' — is functional description of observed system behavior, not a claim about consciousness or experience"* — plus a link to `/raising`. I'd call that adequate, not a gap.

Home told me to "Start with the /context glossary". I clicked. `/context` is **26 sections long**. The glossary table is section 2. This is a good page but it is not a glossary, and the nav label ("Glossary") plus the home CTA both set me up to expect a lookup table and hand me an essay. I bounced out after skimming.

`/projects` is the page I actually wanted first. Eleven project cards, each with a one-liner and a repo link, and — the thing I was hunting for — an actual **Quick start** block:

```
git clone https://github.com/dp-web4/SAGE
cd SAGE
# See README for Python/conda setup
```

And then it stops. That's a clone, a directory change, and a comment telling me to go read the README. The one place on the whole site aimed squarely at me delegates the moment it gets interesting. I don't need the site to duplicate the README, but I need to know before I clone: what are the deps, how long does it take, what does success look like on stdout. "SAGE runs on a single machine — no Jetson required" is exactly the right kind of sentence and it's the only one of its kind.

`/fleet` was the most fun page and the least useful one. Eight machines with named roles — Thor as episodic memory, Sprout as "thalamic router", McNugget as "cerebellum / habit compiler" — and a disclaimer I appreciated: *"The 'Brain:' labels below are role mnemonics the lab assigned, not measured functional homologies."* Good. Someone anticipated the eye-roll. Session counts per machine, totals that are explicitly scoped (*"1,991 / 2,065 totals, which cover the six cognition machines plus HUB"* — pub excluded, and they say so). The only copyable thing on the page is the string `SAGE_MODEL`.

`/autonomy` is where I got most frustrated, and it's the page I find most *interesting*. Eight scheduled tracks on a daily clock, 31+ across the fleet, all running unattended. The honesty is there — *"unattended and self-scheduled, not self-directed in the AI-safety sense"*, *"task definitions are authored in advance, not chosen by the track itself"*, and a stated detect-and-revert posture with scope enforced at the process level via scoped access, a track registry, and after-the-fact audit. As a developer, this is the single most reproducible-sounding idea on the site. **And there is not one line of scheduler syntax, one config schema, or one example track definition anywhere on the page.** I can't copy the idea. I can only be told it works.

`/raising` I read faster than intended because the framing ("Raising is not training") is the kind of claim I'd normally close the tab on. I didn't, because of this: *"everything below might be competent context engineering and nothing more — the control that would discriminate raising from that alternative has not been run yet."* That's the site conceding its own headline might be a relabel. It bought back the credibility the section title spent.

`/principles` reads as ten aphorisms. "Productively wrong is infinitely more valuable than never started" and "Failed experiments are signal" are the ones I'd repeat to a colleague. Principle 10 does something I've never seen a site do — it flags its own naming debt: *"Governance in SAGE's name predates Principle 10's governance→oversight correction."* They know their acronym contradicts their own rule and they say so instead of quietly renaming.

`/arc-agi-3` is where the numbers live: 94.85% official action score, 24/25 games, ~$250 API spend, #3 on the human leaderboard with the fewest actions of the top 3, and an external ARC Prize scorecard link I can verify without trusting them. The caveats are stronger than I expected — the missing same-model-no-harness ablation is stated *up front*, not buried, and *"The loop is the capability — stated as a working hypothesis, not a demonstrated result."* What's missing for me is provenance of the harness code: the page names the components (SAGE, Membot, Web4) but never says whether the ARC game-playing agent was written here or adapted from the ARC Prize starter kit. For a benchmark claim, that's the question I'd ask second, right after the ablation.

`/links` gave me five external links, an org link, an email, and the repo accounting (40 original / 21 public / 19 internal / 32 forks / 72 total). Honest arithmetic, but no answer to "which 3 of the 21 public repos should I open first?"

The acronym hygiene here is better than any project site I've read. The terminology problem this site has is not *drift* and not *missing expansions* — it's **volume**: correct definitions arriving faster than a first-time reader can absorb them.

### Unanswered Questions

2. How do I set up one autonomous track on my own machine? Schedule entry, scope declaration, access boundary — none of it is shown.
3. Was the ARC-AGI-3 agent code written here, or adapted from the ARC Prize starter kit?

### Verdict

The terminology is tighter than I expected and the honesty is unusual — this site tells me its ARC ablation wasn't run, its coherence definition isn't settled, its attractor language is a metaphor, and its own project name violates its own principle. I trusted it more at page nine than at page one, which is backwards from most research sites.

But I came here as a developer and I'm leaving without having run anything. The Quick start punts to a README, `/autonomy` describes 31 daily agent jobs without showing me one config, and nothing on the site shows the software producing output. The lab has clearly spent its effort on saying true things precisely. The next unit of effort should go somewhere else entirely: one transcript, one config file, one thing I can copy.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/projects`, `/context`, `/fleet`, `/autonomy`, `/raising`, `/principles`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`) + the canonical terms source document
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every acronym expansion on this site is correct, so the remaining drift has moved into notation and glosses — and the site's own cited source of truth defines seven terms the site never uses at all.

### Journey

I did this audit differently than an expansion check, because an expansion check on this site returns clean and has for several cycles. Instead I fetched the source the site itself names — `CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` in the public web4 repo, linked from `/context` ("Source of truth: the canonical terms reference in the public web4 repo") — and audited the site against **that**, not against a crib-sheet table. Canon is v1.0.0, last updated 2026-02-12, status **Authoritative**, and defines **14 headwords**. That reframing is what produced everything below.

The first thing canon told me is that the site's expansions are not just correct, they're *more* correct than I could have verified from a table. Canon's headword lines are `### T3 (Trust Tensor)` and `### V3 (Value Tensor)` — full names that don't appear in the terminology table I was given. The site says "three-component trust tensor" and "value tensor". Match. Same for the component glosses, MRH, ATP, ADP, LCT. Nine pages, zero wrong expansions. I'm stating that plainly so it isn't mistaken for a light pass: I looked hard.

The drift is one level down, in **notation**.

Canon writes R6 as: **`Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource → Result. The base action grammar.`** Five inputs, an arrow, one output. The site writes it as `Rules / Role / Request / Reference / Resource / Result` — flat slashes, six peers — on **four pages** (`/context`, `/projects`, `/links`, `/arc-agi-3`). The arrow is gone everywhere.

I checked whether that was an artifact of my own fetch pipeline flattening arrows, because that would make the finding worthless. It isn't: canon's `→` came through intact in the same pipeline, and `/fleet` renders "Allocation Transfer Packet → Allocation Discharge Packet" with the arrow preserved. Arrows survive. The slashes are real.

What makes this more than pedantry is that `/context`'s R6 *prose* has the asymmetry canon encodes — "what it consumes (Resource), and what it produces (Result)" — so the page knows Result is an output. Only the notation flattens it. And the flattening lands on the one glyph this site has already documented as overloaded. `/context`'s notation key is explicit and, honestly, better than most specs I audit: "'/' means two different things on this line: 'verified by' in T3/V3, but a plain declared→discharged allocation pair in ATP/ADP — same glyph, unrelated semantics." That's two senses declared. R6 is a **third** sense — plain element separator — and it's undeclared, on a page whose entire pitch is that it tracks this glyph's senses. A site this careful about `/` shouldn't be the place canon's only arrow goes to die.

Then I ran the reverse audit: not "does the site say the right thing about term X," but "what does canon define that the site never mentions?" Canon's 14 headwords include four relationship verbs — **Witnessing, Binding, Pairing, Delegation** — plus **Federation** and **Society**. The site's glossary covers Witnessing. Federation appears exactly once, on `/context`, as a passing usage ("using federation for distributed consolidation — is designed but not built"), never as a defined term. **Pairing and Delegation appear nowhere on any of nine pages.**

Binding is the one that bites. Canon: **"Binding — Permanent attachment of identity to hardware or cryptographic proof. Irreversible."** The lab ships a project called **Hardbound**, described site-wide as the "hardware-bound oversight suite." That is canon's Binding, by name and by function — and the site never once connects them, never uses the headword, and canon's load-bearing property **"Irreversible" appears nowhere on the site**. Meanwhile `/fleet` uses the word in a completely unrelated sense: memory that "binds what+where+when for pattern-completion retrieval." So the site has a homograph collision on a reserved canonical term, and the reserved sense is the one missing.

Two smaller structural notes from reading canon directly. First, canon ships **`NOT:` anti-definitions** — "NOT: Attention Token Pool, Adaptive Trust Points, Alignment Transfer Protocol", "NOT: Minimum Response Horizon, Memory Retention Heuristic", "NOT: Trust-Tracing-Tensor, Trust-Transfer-Token" — and the site copies **zero** of them. For a site whose stated job is stopping drift before it reaches newcomers, that's free ammunition sitting unused in a document it already links. Second, and this is a warning to whoever audits next: canon's Society entry reads "A **self-governing** group of entities with treasury, membership rules, policies, and roles." Canon itself uses *governing*. The governance→oversight rule is a lab-local convention that is **stricter than the source of truth it cites** — so do not "correct" a canon quotation to match Principle 10.

On Web4-as-a-noun, the site is disciplined in prose and slips in headers. `/context` negates the structural framing twice ("not architecture or infrastructure", "not a blockchain, not a platform"), `/principles` #10 states the rule outright, and `/`, `/projects`, `/autonomy` all say "ontology." But `/arc-agi-3` lists system components and gives Web4 the header **"The ontology layer"** — Web4 slotted into a stack beside SAGE and Membot, which is precisely the mental model `/context` spends two sentences rejecting. `/links` does it once more: "Web4 trust-layer / Cosmos SDK side." The banned nouns ("architecture", "infrastructure", "stack") are all clean at zero occurrences. The claim came back wearing a word nobody banned. The test that catches this is grammatical role — *is Web4 a component in an enumeration?* — not string matching, and "layer" itself is fine everywhere else it appears (`/autonomy`'s "bookkeeping layer", `/fleet`'s "trust layer" are different referents and read correctly).

The ATP/ADP finding is a propagation gap, not an error, and I nearly filed it wrong. `/context` carries canon's full closed loop — "discharged packets are charged back to ATP against *validated value creation*", "allocate, spend, recharge" — which matches canon's "regenerated through contribution" and "can be recycled back to ATP through validation." Complete and correct. But the three surfaces a first-time reader hits first present ATP/ADP as one-way bookkeeping: `/` says "ATP declares intended resource spend before an action runs; ADP records the actual outcome", `/autonomy` says "issued an ATP against its declared resource budget; an ADP records actual spend", and `/fleet` prints a literal one-directional "ATP → ADP". Nothing false; the recharge half just doesn't travel off `/context`. A reader who never reaches section 13 of a 26-section page learns a strictly weaker ATP than canon defines.

Navigation and cross-page consistency held up. All eight nav links resolve, the nav order is stable across pages, and the governance→oversight disclosure has genuinely propagated — near-identical sentences on `/projects`, `/fleet`, `/raising`, `/principles`, and `/links`. That's what a fix that traveled looks like, and it's worth noting as the counterexample to everything above. Two IA notes: the nav label "Glossary" points at a 26-section essay whose glossary is section 2 (Pass 1 hit this too), and the canonical terms document — the site's declared source of truth — is linked from exactly one page, and it is not `/links`. The page whose entire job is links omits the most important link on the site.

### Unanswered Questions

### Verdict

The disclosure is real. This site pre-registers a control that would retire its own central vocabulary, sorts its claims into evidential tiers and puts its flagship framing in the bottom one, documents a 400x failure by name, and admits that its automated auditors share failure modes with its authors. I would cite `/autonomy`'s correlated-auditor sentence in a paper about automated oversight. As an exercise in honest self-description this is at or near the ceiling, and further hedging is now a cost with no return.

What it doesn't have is verifiability. The one number offered for independent checking sits behind a JavaScript-rendered page and arrives welded to a second number the site's own ladder never licensed and its own repo contradicts — on five pages. Nine pages name eight external bodies of work and cite none of them. The control that would license the vocabulary has no date. Every remaining credibility problem on this site is of the same kind: the lab has said the true thing and not yet shown the checkable thing.

**So what**: disclosure and verifiability are different currencies, and this lab has spent one to saturation without opening an account in the other. Disclosure is free — it costs a sentence, and the sentences here are excellent. Verifiability costs a scheduled run, a redacted log, a quoted scorecard figure, six citations. The next unit of effort should buy none of the first and all of the second. And the sharpest instrument the site already owns — the evidence ladder — should be wired to the claims it governs, because a tiering system that only exists on the page nobody finishes is a rigor artifact rather than a rigor practice.
