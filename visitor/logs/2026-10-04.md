### Journey
I started on Home through WebFetch. The nav has six items (Projects, Fleet, Raising, How we work, Notebook, Links), each page has one h1, and the headings are clean. The front pages use **zero** Web4 acronyms. I counted every page: LCT/T3/V3/MRH/ATP/ADP/R6/R7 appear only under `/notebook/*`. Then I walked the notebook index ("Every term and acronym used in the work, with its canonical definition" → **Glossary**) and every subpage. I checked first use against first expansion per page, cross-page naming, titles, breadcrumbs and anchors, and the glossary entries that front-page terms depend on.

Three structural things stood out:

1. **One page, three names.** The nav and index call it "Glossary". Its `<title>` and h1 say "Glossary", and its route is `/notebook/context`. Yet the notebook text calls it "/context" **37 times** across all 9 subpages ("defined in the /context glossary", "see /context"). A reader who meets "/context" in prose has no way to know it means "the Glossary". (This overlaps Pass 1's 64 stale route labels. 2. **The breadcrumb skips a level.** On `/notebook/projects` the breadcrumb reads "Home / Projects", and "Home" links to `/` (the front page). There is no "Notebook" crumb, so the trail misstates where the reader is. The same template is presumably on every subpage.
3. **The glossary's governance rule no longer matches the front pages.** The glossary has a single "governance→oversight" note. Other pages are told to link to it rather than re-explain it, which is good practice. It says the vocabulary "was corrected lab-wide", with "one deliberate carve-out: Hestia keeps 'governance'" (citing Home and /projects), plus SAGE's legacy name. The new front pages use "governance" more broadly:
   - /projects and Home describe **Hub** as "Governance a community or organization owns" and "The same governance for a community". Hub is not in the carve-out.

   - /principles: "The lab's agents work under our own governance"; /links: "4-gov · governance".

   Either the carve-out has grown (Hub, the umbrella category) and the glossary note is stale, or the front pages break the rule the glossary says the site follows. As a writer I can't tell which, and a reader can't either. Pass 1 rated the heading adjacency low. I rate the whole pattern medium: the site's single source of truth for a term now contradicts its most-read pages.

Smaller items:
- **/fleet heading vs body.** "Two kinds of work" introduces **three** groups: four machines do heavy work, two review, two host.
- **Anchors.** The Glossary has 67 ids (good, and it has entries for being, raising, hub, sage and governance-oversight). The front pages, `/notebook/fleet`, `/notebook/autonomy` and `/notebook/home` have **0** ids. No front page links into any glossary entry. Front-page terms like "being", "raising" and Hub/HUB have good definitions one hop away, but nothing points there.
- **Hub/HUB is defined, but not where readers meet it.** The glossary rule is clear: "All-caps HUB is always this machine; capitalized Hub is the software it runs". Front /fleet uses both in one row ("HUB … Hosts the fleet's own community on Hub") without a link or a note.
- **The glossary has no entry for terms the front pages lean on**: "tracks"/"daily tracks" (Home, How we work), "tutor", "mentor" (/raising), "notebook"/"front pages" (the site's own two-layer model), SWE-SAGE, 4-gov, web4-core. ("Interactive selection" does have an entry.)

- **Model families.** Front /fleet's "Qwen, Gemma, Granite and Llama" agrees with the notebook ("Phi and TinyLlama lines are archived"). Consistent; noted as a pass.

## Pass 3: Web4 Contributor

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (7 front pages and 9 notebook pages). Landing page via WebFetch; every page also fetched as rendered HTML and stripped to text. Front-page claims were checked against the notebook and against the public upstream repos: web4 `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `docs/reference/CANONICAL_TERMS_v1.md` (last changed 2026-04-28) and `RELATED_REPOS.md`; SAGE `AGENTS.md` (refreshed 2026-10-01) and `sage/docs/LATEST_STATUS.md` (As of 2026-09-26); hestia `README.md`.
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Canonical vocabulary is clean everywhere. But the new front pages describe Web4, Hestia, Hardbound and SAGE more strongly than their own repos, and more strongly than the notebook the front pages say they summarize.

### Journey
I went straight to /projects, since that's where my repos are described. The cards are short and mostly right. Badges match the web4 README's stack table: Hestia "Running daily", Hub "Running Rust reference implementation", Hardbound "Private/proprietary; building". I compared each card sentence by sentence with its notebook entry and its upstream README. A consistent pattern appeared: **the redesign kept each project's nouns and dropped each project's assurance limits.** The notebook still carries every one of those limits. Only the front copy lost them.

1. **Synchronism → Web4 (HIGH).** Front /projects: Synchronism "supplies the equations that Web4's trust model builds on." Every other source says the opposite:
   - `/notebook/projects`: "no Web4 primitive (LCT, T3/V3, MRH, ATP/ADP, R6/R7 …) is defined from a Synchronism equation".
   - The glossary: "lineage (not a derivation)".
   - `/notebook/links`: "conceptual lineage for Web4, not an engineering dependency".
   - web4 `AGENTS.md`: "Blue-sky conceptual lineage; not an engineering dependency".
   - web4 `docs/reference/RELATED_REPOS.md`: Synchronism "influenced Web4's MRH framing … not an engineering dependency and its physics claims remain conjectural".

   For a Web4 contributor this is the worst misstatement on the site. It tells every newcomer that T3/V3 rests on a physics conjecture, one whose own Navier-Stokes identification the notebook says was refuted. The same card also drops "conjecture": "A theoretical framework in which coherence, not substance, is fundamental."

2. **Hestia: "a record the agent cannot edit" (medium-high).** Home: "checked before each action, with a record the agent cannot edit". /projects: "Every action is checked first and recorded where the agent cannot change it." The hestia README's first boxed warning is "**The gate stops accidents, not adversaries**". It calls the gate an early prototype ("two environment variables suffice today" to bypass it, issue #49), and says that at A1 "the gate is cooperative and same-UID: the record is unforgeable-ish and *inspectable*". `/notebook/home` gets this right: "a cooperative and tamper-evident gate, not adversary-proof containment". Tamper-evident ≠ cannot-edit, and "every action" ≠ a cooperative hook.

3. **Hardbound: present-tense hardware binding (medium).** Front /projects: "Identity bound to hardware, enforcement that fails closed, and audit evidence an outside party can verify." The BUILDING badge helps, but the sentence describes those properties as present. `/notebook/principles` and the glossary both say "by design; enforcement is process-level today, not hardware-anchored". The hedge was dropped in summary.

4. **SAGE: "every action … through governed tools" (medium).** Home says beings "act only through governed tools". /projects says "every action they take goes through governed tools". /raising says "Every one of those actions is checked against rules before it happens, and recorded." SAGE `LATEST_STATUS.md` (2026-09-26) is narrower: "Consequential acts are **intended** to be governed and witnessed; observational/local capabilities are separately bounded". ### Journey
**Home.** It is honest about scale ("one human and a fleet of AI agents") and modest in tone. One line stopped me: "The lab notebook holds every claim with its evidence, dates and caveats ... **AI readers should start there.**" The notebook index repeats it: "The rest of this site is written for people: short, plain, and current." As a design choice that is a **caveat gradient**. Machines get directed to the hedged version and humans to the unhedged one. Scholarly practice usually runs the other way: the abstract carries the limitations, because it is the only part most people read. I kept this in mind for every front page that followed.

**Raising (front), my main target.** The page has a "Seen / Not yet shown" split. That is the right shape, and Pass 1 rightly praised it. I then read `/notebook/raising` (6.7k words) against it sentence by sentence:

1. **The tutor.** Front: a being develops through "guided sessions with a tutor".
